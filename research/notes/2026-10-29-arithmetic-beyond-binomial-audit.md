# Independent audit: arithmetic descent on a larger plane source

Date: 2026-10-29. Reviewer: `arithmetic/jet_gap_audit`.

**Verdict: PASS for the higher-jet obstruction and its application to the
literal twenty-three-parameter source below.** The admissible locus of the
larger source is not computed by this audit. The result is a conditional
family obstruction, with a known nonempty subfamily of actual non-binomial
carriers, and is not an all-pairs lower bound for the geometric minimum
`delta(p)`. No canonical record or inherited proof is changed.

## Literal source and exact hypotheses

Over an algebraically closed field use `P2_(s:t:z)` and let

```
U0 = s^4 + s^2 z L0,
U1 = s^3 t + s^2 z L1,
U2 = s t^3 + alpha z^4 + s z C2,
U3 = t^4 + z B3.
```

Here `alpha != 0`, `L0,L1` are arbitrary homogeneous linear forms,
`C2` an arbitrary homogeneous quadratic, and `B3` an arbitrary homogeneous
cubic. There are `1+3+3+6+10=23` scalar parameters. The restriction at
`c=(z=0)` is the literal parametrization
`[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]` of `C0`.

The asserted obstruction retains only systems that are basepoint free and
whose full reduced inverse support of `C0` is exactly `c`. Basepoint
freeness makes the resulting morphism finite onto its integral image,
since its pullback of `O(1)` is the ample bundle `O_P2(4)`. Birationality
is an additional hypothesis when the image is called a degree-sixteen
normalization; it is not needed for the pullback obstruction.

This larger coefficient source genuinely varies both formerly fixed
coordinates, but its admissible parameter locus has not been classified.
In particular no assertion is made that a generic choice in the source is
admissible. The inherited subfamily `L0=L1=C2=0` is nonempty and admissible;
its birationality criterion is recorded in [R-0017](../results/singleton_plane_conductor_descent.md)
and its source evidence. Its primitive member `alpha=1, B3=s^3` has the
explicit non-binomial irreducible image equation

```
F = (x0^3 x3-x1^4)^4 - x0^13 (x0^2 x2-x1^3).
```

That identity is a genuine degree-sixteen carrier rather than a disguised
binomial defining equation. This audit uses the inherited full-support
and birationality result as an accepted premise and does not rerun it.

## Independent higher-jet argument

Let `r` be a uniformizer of a source divisor, `E` its residue field, and
let `A` be the target local ring inside the source local ring. Assume
that, modulo `r^e` with `e>=2`, every element of `A` has the form `R(w)`
for `R(W) in k(W)`, where the residue `w0` of `w` is transcendental over
`k`. Suppose a local unit has expansion

```
q = c + a r + O(r^2),   c in k*,   a in E*,
```

and `q^b` belongs to `A`, for a positive integer `b`.

Its residue is `c^b`. If `q^b=R(w) mod r^e`, injectivity of
`k(W)->E`, `W->w0`, implies `R(W)=c^b`. Consequently

```
q^b = c^b mod r^e.
```

In characteristic zero its coefficient of `r` is the nonzero scalar
multiple `b c^(b-1) a`, a contradiction. In characteristic `p>0`, write
`b=p^v n` with `p` not dividing `n`. Frobenius gives

```
q^b = c^b + n c^(b-p^v) a^(p^v) r^(p^v)
       + terms of higher r-order.
```

The displayed coefficient is nonzero, so necessarily `p^v>=e`.
This uses a Hasse-order/Frobenius calculation, rather than repeated
ordinary differentiation, which would miss higher powers of `p`.

The rational-function assertion includes localized denominators. If the
target local ring is generated by `a0,a1,w`, with `a0=a1=0 mod r^e`, any
localized element is `N(a0,a1,w)/M(a0,a1,w)`. A denominator nonzero at the
target prime has a nonzero polynomial restriction `M(0,0,W)`. Its value
at the transcendental element `w0` is nonzero, so it is invertible modulo
`r^e`. Reduction therefore has the claimed form `R(w)`. This check is
needed; a polynomial-only argument would not establish local descent.

## Application and boundary checks

At the generic point of `D=(s=0)`, use `z=1`, `r=s/z`, `y=t/z` and
the target frame `X2`. Then

```
u = alpha + r [y^3+C2(r,y,1)],
a0 = (r^4+r^2 L0(r,y,1))/u,
a1 = (r^3 y+r^2 L1(r,y,1))/u,
w  = (y^4+B3(r,y,1))/u,
q  = 1/u.
```

Both `a0,a1` vanish modulo `r^2`. The residue
`w0=(y^4+B3(0,y,1))/alpha` is transcendental because its numerator has
degree exactly four in `y`; the cubic perturbation cannot cancel the
leading term. The residue of `q` is `1/alpha`, and its linear coefficient
is

```
-alpha^(-2) [y^3+C2(0,y,1)],
```

which is nonzero: `C2(0,y,1)` has degree at most two. Thus the higher-jet
lemma applies with `e=2` on the entire literal source whenever a sole-
support mate exists.

Indeed such a mate `G_b` has pullback with sole zero support `z=0`.
Unique factorization on `P2` forces `G_b(U)=lambda z^(4b)`, `lambda!=0`.
After scalar normalization and division by `U2^b`, this is exactly
`q^b` descending to the target local ring. Hence characteristic zero
allows no positive mate degree, while in characteristic `p>0` every
mate satisfies `p | b`. This is independent of the number of terms of
both defining equations.

On the linear sublocus `L0(0,t,z)=L1(0,t,z)=0`, equivalently
`L0=l0*s, L1=l1*s`, both `a0,a1` vanish modulo `r^3`. The same proof
uses `e=3` and gives `p^(v_p(b))>=3`. This includes the inherited
admissible subfamily. In particular characteristic two requires `4 | b`;
for odd primes it implies `p | b`.

For an actual birational degree-sixteen carrier, degree exhaustion gives
`m_C=4b`, so `p | m_C` for every odd prime and every supported mate.
The degree bound `max(16,b)>=p` follows. This is a family lower bound,
not a lower bound for `delta(p)`, which minimizes over all carriers.
An all-pairs result would require proving that every possible supported
pair satisfies an analogous jet gap or another independent mechanism.

## Limits of this verdict

The argument proves a necessary condition and the characteristic-zero
exclusion in the stated source. It does not prove that any positive-
characteristic mate degree is attained, compute the conductor ideal,
classify the larger admissible locus, or reduce arbitrary carriers to
this source. In particular the stronger claim that `b=p` is attained
for all sufficiently large primes requires separate conductor descent
or explicit polynomial membership evidence.
