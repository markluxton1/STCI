# Opposite torus limits: retained support and endpoint defects

Date: 2026-10-30. Status: **independently reviewed provisional**, with the
[independent proof audit](2026-10-30-four-streams-degeneration-audit.md).
No canonical promotion and no exclusion for the fixed quartic are
claimed. This note investigates arbitrary defining pairs, rather than another
bounded family of carrier equations.

## 1. Question and exact literature input

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic zero,

\[
C=C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3,
\qquad R=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3],\quad P=I_C.
\]

Assume hypothetically that \(J=(F_a,G_b)\subset R\) is a homogeneous
complete-intersection ideal with \(\sqrt J=P\). The torus action has weights

\[
\lambda\cdot(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)
=(x_0,\lambda x_1,\lambda^3x_2,\lambda^4x_3).
\]

The exact external input inspected is Hellus–Hübl, *A result on Macaulay's
curve*, [Theorem 1.2 and Remark 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.7661), pp. 4–5
of the PDF. For a hypothetical pair, the ideals generated by its two lowest
and its two highest weight components each have only height-one minimal
primes. Their greatest common divisors are nonunits in \(P\). Each defining
form has at least three weight components. These are necessary conditions;
the paper does not establish nonexistence.

The [existing arithmetic investigation](2026-10-28-stci-search-arithmetic.md),
Section 4, already explains why a common surface in the two displayed
limiting equations prevents reduction to a proper binomial pair. The new
content here is a precise projective description of the possible defects in
the **full** flat limits and an all-degree control showing that two opposite
endpoint defects are compatible with genuine supported complete
intersections.

The two-equation special fibers in the Hellus–Hübl construction must not be
identified with Hilbert-scheme limits. Their vertical surfaces belong to a
nonflat two-equation family. Parameter saturation, equivalently using all
initial elements of \(J\), removes those extra components and may introduce
additional generators.

## 2. An all-degree flat-limit statement

Write \(J_-=\operatorname{in}_w(J)\) and
\(J_+=\operatorname{in}_{-w}(J)\), using full weight-initial ideals. Let
\(Z_\pm=\operatorname{Proj}(R/J_\pm)\). Equivalently these are the two
projective Hilbert limits of the orbit of \(Y=\operatorname{Proj}(R/J)\).
The homogeneous ideals \(J_\pm\) need not be saturated; Proj or subsequent
irrelevant-ideal saturation is understood when speaking of the projective
schemes.

**Proposition 2.1.** Both projective flat limits have reduced support exactly
\(C\), degree \(ab\), and generic multiplicity \(m=ab/4\) along \(C\).
Their non-Cohen–Macaulay loci, if nonempty, are contained in the two fixed
points \(p_0=[1:0:0:0]\) and \(p_\infty=[0:0:0:1]\).

**Proof.** Since \(\sqrt J=P\), some \(P^N\subset J\subset P\). Both \(P\)
and \(P^N\) are weight homogeneous. Taking either full initial ideal gives

\[
P^N\subset J_\pm\subset P.
\]

Hence \(\sqrt{J_\pm}=P\), including before projectivization. Flatness preserves
the Hilbert polynomial. A one-dimensional scheme with sole reduced support
\(C\) has degree \(4\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3,\eta_C}}
\mathcal O_{Z,\eta_C}\), giving the stated multiplicity.

For the last assertion one can either use invariance of the finite embedded
point locus, or see the local geometry explicitly. On \(x_0x_1\ne0\), put

\[
u=x_1/x_0,\quad
A=(x_2/x_0)u^{-3}-1,\quad
B=(x_3/x_0)u^{-4}-1.
\]

This ambient chart is
\(\operatorname{Spec}k[u,u^{-1},A,B]\); the torus scales \(u\) and fixes
\(A,B\), and \(C\) is \(A=B=0\). Every invariant ideal in this chart is

\[
k[u,u^{-1}]\otimes K
\quad\text{for an ideal }K\subset k[A,B].
\]

Indeed take its weight components and use the invertibility of \(u\) to
shift each component to weight zero. Here \(\sqrt K=(A,B)\), so \(k[A,B]/K\)
is Artinian of length \(m\). Thus each \(Z_\pm\) on the open orbit is the
product of \(\mathbf G_m\) with a fixed transverse Artin scheme. It is
Cohen–Macaulay there. Only the two endpoints remain. No claim that this
Artin algebra is Gorenstein, or is generated by two relations, is made.
\(\square\)

For either limit let \(T_\pm\subset\mathcal O_{Z_\pm}\) be its maximal
zero-dimensional subsheaf, and define the Cohen–Macaulay hull by

\[
\mathcal O_{\overline Z_\pm}=\mathcal O_{Z_\pm}/T_\pm.
\]

For a projective curve with sole irreducible reduced support this removes
the embedded points and leaves a Cohen–Macaulay curve with the same degree
and generic multiplicity. The construction is invariant under the torus,
so

\[
\ell_\pm:=\operatorname{length}T_\pm
=\operatorname{length}(T_\pm)_{p_0}
 +\operatorname{length}(T_\pm)_{p_\infty}.
\]

The complete-intersection Hilbert polynomial gives the exact relation

\[
\boxed{\quad
\chi(\mathcal O_{\overline Z_-})+\ell_-
=\chi(\mathcal O_{\overline Z_+})+\ell_+
=-\frac{ab(a+b-4)}2.
\quad}                                                     \tag{1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
p_a(\overline Z_\pm)=1+\frac{ab(a+b-4)}2+\ell_\pm.          \tag{2}
\]

These statements concern **projective embedded points**. Possible torsion
of a homogeneous initial quotient supported only at the irrelevant vertex
is removed by Proj and is not counted in \(\ell_\pm\).

Geometrically, the limiting scheme keeps the full multiplicity on \(C\).
Discarding its endpoint torsion increases the genus of its pure curve by
exactly the discarded length. Thus a high-genus hull is not, by itself, a
violation of the genus of the original complete intersection.

## 3. A scalable control with both opposite degenerations

This example is on a **line**, not on \(C_0\). Its purpose is to test proposed
general implications about opposite degenerations. It is not a construction
for the quartic and cannot disprove an obstruction using its particular
embedding.

Fix an integer \(d\ge2\), let \(L=(X,Y)\subset\mathbf P^3_{X,Y,Z,W}\), and
set

\[
F=X^d+Y(Z^{d-1}+W^{d-1}),\qquad G=F+Y^d.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

Then

\[
(F,G)=(F,Y^d),\qquad \sqrt{(F,G)}=(X,Y).
\]

Since \(F\) is not divisible by \(Y\), this is a genuine complete
intersection of type \((d,d)\), supported on \(L\), in characteristic zero
(in fact the assertion holds in every characteristic). Give \(X,Y,Z,W\)
torus weights \(0,0,-1,1\). Each of \(F,G\) has exactly three weight
components. Their two lowest components are both \(YZ^{d-1}\), and their
two highest components are both \(YW^{d-1}\). Both extreme ideals have
principal radicals and gcds in \(I_L\). Thus even that full package of
extreme-component conditions is compatible with a supported complete
intersection.

**Proposition 3.1.** The two full flat-limit ideals are

\[
\begin{split}
I_-&=(YZ^{d-1},Y^d,X^dY^{d-1},X^{2d}Y^{d-2},\ldots,
                      X^{d(d-1)}Y,X^{d^2}),\\
I_+&=(YW^{d-1},Y^d,X^dY^{d-1},X^{2d}Y^{d-2},\ldots,
                      X^{d(d-1)}Y,X^{d^2}).
\end{split}                                                     \tag{4}
\]

Both are saturated projective ideals. Both Cohen–Macaulay hulls are

\[
H=(Y,X^{d^2}),                                                   \tag{5}
\]

the same plane multiple line. The sole embedded point of \(I_-\) is
([0:0:0:1]), and that of \(I_+\) is ([0:0:1:0]). Their equal lengths are

\[
\boxed{\ell=\frac{d^2(d-1)^2}{2}.}                             \tag{6}
\]

**Proof of the full-limit calculation.** For the negative-weight limit,
write the actual torus parameter as \(t\). After multiplying the transformed
equation by \(t^{d-1}\), it is

\[
YZ^{d-1}+t^{d-1}X^d+t^{2(d-1)}YW^{d-1}.
\]

Put \(s=t^{d-1}\) and first work over \(k[s]\). Define

\[
f_s=Y(Z^{d-1}+s^2W^{d-1})+sX^d,
\qquad M_i=X^{di}Y^{d-i}\quad(0\le i\le d).
\]

The parameter-saturated ideal of \((f_s,Y^d)\) is

\[
\mathcal K=(f_s,M_0,M_1,\ldots,M_d).                            \tag{7}
\]

First, all \(M_i\) belong to the saturation. Inductively use

\[
X^{d(i-1)}Y^{d-i}f_s
 -(Z^{d-1}+s^2W^{d-1})M_{i-1}=sM_i
\quad(1\le i\le d).
\]

Second, choose a monomial order with \(Z\) first, over the coefficient
ring \(k[s]\). The listed generators form a **monic** Gröbner basis with
leading monomials \(YZ^{d-1},M_0,\ldots,M_d\). The S-polynomial of \(f_s\)
with \(M_i\), \(i<d\), has remainder

\[
sM_{i+1}+s^2W^{d-1}M_i,
\]

which reduces to zero. For \(i=d\) both remaining terms are multiples of
\(M_d\); the pairs of monomial generators have zero S-polynomial. Hence
standard monomials give a free \(k[s]\)-basis of the quotient.

Consequently \(\mathcal K\) is \(s\)-saturated. After inverting \(s\) it
equals \((f_s,Y^d)\), proving (7). Its fiber at zero is \(I_-\). Flat base
change \(s=t^{d-1}\) gives exactly the original torus degeneration, with
the same special fiber. Reversing the torus exchanges \(Z,W\), giving
\(I_+\). This proof uses all initial equations and no assumption that the
displayed two limiting forms remain a regular sequence.

**Proof of the hull and length.** In \(I_-\), \(W\) occurs in no generator,
so \(W\) is a nonzerodivisor on its quotient. Thus the ideal is saturated
with respect to the projective irrelevant ideal. Away from \(Z=0\), its
localization is exactly \(H=(Y,X^{d^2})\). The quotient \(H/I_-\) is supported
at the single projective point ([0:0:0:1]). Since \(R/H\) is a plane
hypersurface, it is Cohen–Macaulay, so \(H\) is the hull ideal.

On the chart \(W=1\), a basis of \(H/I_-\) consists of

\[
X^iY^jZ^k,
\quad 1\le j\le d-1,\quad
0\le i<d(d-j),\quad 0\le k<d-1.
\]

Its length is

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}d(d-j)(d-1)=\frac{d^2(d-1)^2}{2}.
\]

The same proof, exchanging \(Z,W\), applies to \(I_+\). \(\square\)

As a separate numerical consistency check, the original \((d,d)\) curve
has Euler characteristic \(d^2(2-d)\). The plane hull of degree \(d^2\)
has Euler characteristic \(d^2(3-d^2)/2\). Their difference is precisely
(6). This check is not being used in place of the module calculation.

For \(d=2\), the limit is

\[
(YZ,Y^2,X^2Y,X^4),
\]

with a length-two embedded point and plane-quartic hull. A genus-one
complete intersection can therefore degenerate to a genus-three pure hull
together with the length-two endpoint torsion required by flatness.

## 4. What the control rules out, and what it leaves open

The example proves that none of the following statements can be used as
a general geometric contradiction for supported complete intersections:

- both extreme pairs have common divisors containing the support;
- both full limits require extra generators;
- both full limits have positive endpoint defects;
- the two limits have the same Cohen–Macaulay hull;
- their pure hulls have larger genus than the original complete intersection;
- their endpoint defects grow without bound as the equation degrees grow.

All these features occur together in (3)–(6). The vertical surfaces of the
unsaturated equation family produce no residual curve in the general fiber.
The actual geometric mechanism is concentration of nilpotent structure into
embedded points while a more elongated transverse multiple structure remains
on the line.

The example does **not** show that the full equivariant ideals forget all
information: they retain the entire endpoint modules and their attachment.
It rules out the listed implications, not every possible degeneration
argument and not a \(C_0\)-specific invariant. In particular it says nothing
against an argument retaining the nonnormal homogeneous coordinate ring of
the quartic, its specific conormal extension, or the ambient equation-lifting
data.

## 5. A precise quartic-specific bridge still missing

One embedded global datum absent from the line control is the quartic's
one-dimensional projective-normality defect. On \(C\simeq\mathbf P^1\),

\[
R_1=\langle s^4,s^3t,st^3,t^4\rangle
\subset H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(1)),
\]

with missing section \(e=s^2t^2\), of torus weight two. The full Rao module
is a copy of \(k\) in degree one; this inherited computation and its liaison
uses are in [the global containing-intersections note](2026-10-27-global-containing-intersections.md),
Section 4, and its
[independent audit](2026-10-27-global-containing-intersections-audit.md).
Only the elementary degree-one defect is needed below.

For an actual complete intersection \(Y\) with both degrees at least two,

\[
H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(1))=R_1.
\]

Indeed the Koszul resolution gives \(H^1(\mathcal I_Y(1))=0\), and there
are no linear equations. Set
\(\mathcal N=\mathcal I_C/\mathcal I_Y\), considered as the kernel of
\(\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal O_C\). The connecting map in

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal N(1)\longrightarrow
\mathcal O_Y(1)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_C(1)\longrightarrow0
\]

satisfies

\[
\delta_Y(e)\ne0\quad\text{in }H^1(\mathcal N(1)).               \tag{8}
\]

Geometrically, the missing linear coordinate of the rational normal quartic
does not lift as a section to the proposed complete-intersection thickening.
This statement is about its actual embedded extension, not merely the degree
of its normal bundle.

Passing to a Hilbert limit does not preserve (8) automatically:
\(H^0(Z_\pm,\mathcal O_{Z_\pm}(1))\) may jump, even though the homogeneous
weight-initial quotient has the same graded Hilbert function as \(R/J\).
The homogeneous degree-one quotient and the full space of sheaf sections
must be distinguished. The following exact statement isolates which part
of the limit can change the connecting class.

**Proposition 5.1 (the pure-hull lifting class).** Let \(Z\) be either torus
limit, let \(T\) be its endpoint torsion, and let \(\overline Z\) be its
Cohen–Macaulay hull. Define

\[
\mathcal N_Z=\ker(\mathcal O_Z\longrightarrow\mathcal O_C),\qquad
\mathcal N_{\overline Z}
 =\ker(\mathcal O_{\overline Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_C).
\]

There is a natural isomorphism

\[
H^1(\mathcal N_Z(1))
 \simeq H^1(\mathcal N_{\overline Z}(1))                         \tag{9}
\]

carrying \(\delta_Z(e)\) to \(\delta_{\overline Z}(e)\). Moreover,
writing \(M_W(1)=H^1(\mathcal I_W(1))\), one has the exact sequence of
torus representations

\[
0\longrightarrow H^0(T(1))
\longrightarrow M_Z(1)
\longrightarrow M_{\overline Z}(1)
\longrightarrow0.                                           \tag{10}
\]

The natural map \(M_Z(1)\to M_C(1)\simeq k\) factors through
\(M_{\overline Z}(1)\). In particular the endpoint contribution
\(H^0(T(1))\), whose dimension is \(\ell\), maps to zero. The following
are equivalent:

1. \(\delta_Z(e)=0\).
2. \(M_{\overline Z}(1)\to M_C(1)\) is surjective.
3. The missing coordinate \(e\) lifts to a section of
   \(\mathcal O_{\overline Z}(1)\).
4. The pure hull has a torus-equivariant embedding in \(\mathbf P^4\)
   restricting to the rational normal quartic on its reduced support,
   such that projection from the omitted coordinate point, disjoint from
   the embedded hull, recovers its given embedding in \(\mathbf P^3\).

In (4), the coordinates of \(\mathbf P^4\) have weights \(0,1,2,3,4\),
and the four coordinates of weights \(0,1,3,4\) are the prescribed
ambient sections. An arbitrary unrelated embedding in \(\mathbf P^4\)
does not satisfy this condition.

**Proof.** The image of \(T\) in the torsion-free curve sheaf
\(\mathcal O_C\) is zero. Thus the quotient map to \(C\) factors through
the hull and produces the commutative diagram with exact rows

\[
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
0&\to&\mathcal N_Z(1)&\to&\mathcal O_Z(1)&\to&
       \mathcal O_C(1)&\to&0\\
&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\Vert\\
0&\to&\mathcal N_{\overline Z}(1)&\to&
       \mathcal O_{\overline Z}(1)&\to&
       \mathcal O_C(1)&\to&0.
\end{array}
\]

The kernels of the first two vertical maps are both \(T(1)\). A
zero-dimensional sheaf has no positive-degree cohomology. Therefore
the first vertical map induces (9), the second is surjective on global
sections, and naturality gives the asserted equality of connecting
classes. This proves that deleting endpoint torsion does not itself
change whether \(e\) lifts.

Apply cohomology to
\(0\to\mathcal I_Z(1)\to\mathcal I_{\overline Z}(1)\to T(1)\to0\).
Both ideal sheaves have no linear sections because they are contained
in \(\mathcal I_C\), and \(C\) is nondegenerate. The resulting exact
sequence is (10). The factorization to \(M_C(1)\) is induced by
\(\mathcal I_Z\subset\mathcal I_{\overline Z}\subset\mathcal I_C\).
The exact sequence
\[
0\to\mathcal I_{\overline Z}(1)\to\mathcal I_C(1)
\to\mathcal N_{\overline Z}(1)\to0
\]
then shows that the connecting image of the one-dimensional
\(M_C(1)\) is zero precisely when the preceding map is surjective.
This proves equivalence of (1)–(3).

When \(e\) lifts, the torus's diagonalizability lets one choose a lift
of weight two. The four prescribed ambient sections, together with
that lift, give a morphism to \(\mathbf P^4\). The first four already
have no common zero, so the image avoids the projection center. In
the complement of that center, which is the total space of
\(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3}(1)\), this morphism is the graph of the
chosen section over the embedded hull. It is a closed immersion
there; properness of the hull makes it a closed immersion into
\(\mathbf P^4\). Its restriction to \(C\) is the complete degree-four
series. The converse follows by restricting the extra coordinate.
\(\square\)

In particular,
\[
h^1(\mathcal I_Z(1))
=\ell+h^1(\mathcal I_{\overline Z}(1)),                         \tag{11}
\]
but the entire \(\ell\)-dimensional endpoint contribution is irrelevant
to killing this particular missing-coordinate class. The exact
specialization question has become a question about a **pure**
multiple curve and its prescribed lift to four-space. This improves
on simply attributing all failure of the class to embedded points.

A potentially decisive next theorem would constrain these two pure-hull
lifts, or the two surviving nonzero classes, for limits of one actual
two-generator ideal in characteristic zero. It would need an
incompatibility with that common complete-intersection lift, or a positive
residual conclusion. Propositions 2.1 and 5.1 do not establish such an
incompatibility, and no inference from a \(\mathbf P^4\) lift to a quartic
STCI construction is intended.

**Subsequent resolution of one proposed test.** The independently reviewed
[first-neighborhood investigation](2026-10-30-four-streams-linear-series.md)
proves that whenever both equations have normal order at least two, both
limits and both pure hulls necessarily retain the nonzero class. They all
contain the same obstructed first infinitesimal neighborhood. Thus ordinary
nonvanishing is automatic on this entire branch and cannot distinguish a
hypothetical STCI from other thickenings. The stronger compatibility problem
above remains open; pairs with nonzero first normal image are separate.

The nonsplit quadric/conormal sequence in characteristic zero is another
specific embedded datum; its exact accepted scope is
[R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md). This note does
not infer an all-degree obstruction from that sequence or from its relation
to the missing coordinate.

## 6. Review and frontier disposition

The independent audit reconstructs Proposition 2.1 and the projective
endpoint identities, the exact all-degree flat-limit computation (3)–(7),
the degree-one connecting-class statement (8), and Proposition 5.1,
including its prescribed \(\mathbf P^4\) interpretation, without a material
correction. The proposed next
compatibility theorem for the two limits is explicitly unproved. The example was not
located in selective searches of the current notes; no literature novelty
claim is made.

There was no numerical search, no uncompleted computation claimed as a
result, and no canonical or navigation change. The unrestricted
characteristic-zero STCI question for \(C_0\) remains open. The contribution
is an all-degree description of a possible degeneration mechanism and a
counterexample to several tempting bridges, together with a more precise
global datum that a successful opposite-limit argument must control.
