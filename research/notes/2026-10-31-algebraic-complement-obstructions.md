# Algebraic Euler classes on complements of smooth space curves

Date: 2026-10-31. Status: **independently reviewed provisional deductions**;
see the [scoped audit](2026-10-31-algebraic-complement-obstructions-audit.md).
No canonical promotion or claim of literature novelty. The unrestricted
characteristic-zero STCI problem for
\(C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\) remains open.

This continues the [continuous-section comparison](2026-10-31-complement-sections-and-topology.md).
The bounded question is whether ordinary algebraic characteristic classes,
or their primary quadratic refinement, retain a further obstruction on
the complement. The answer below is uniform in the curve and the degrees;
it does not require choosing normal forms or a carrier.

## 1. Statement and geometric meaning

Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from
two, let \(C\subset\mathbf P^3_k\) be a smooth connected curve of degree d
and genus g, and put \(U=\mathbf P^3_k\setminus C\). For every line bundle L
on U, the forgetful map is an isomorphism:
\[
\boxed{\quad
\widetilde {CH}^{\,2}(U,L)\xrightarrow{\sim}CH^2(U)=\mathbf Z/d.
\quad}                                                               \tag{1}
\]
Consequently, for every algebraic rank-two vector bundle E on U,
\[
e_{\mathrm{cw}}(E)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_2(E)=0\text{ in }CH^2(U),               \tag{2}
\]
where the characteristic Euler class has twist \(L=\det E^\vee\).
In particular,
\[
e_{\mathrm{cw}}\bigl(\mathcal O_U(a)\oplus\mathcal O_U(b)\bigr)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\mid ab.                               \tag{3}
\]
A nowhere-zero algebraic section forces this class to vanish. The
converse is **not** asserted on the nonaffine U.

Chow theory records the rational-equivalence class of a curve of zeros.
Chow–Witt theory refines cycle classes by quadratic-form data. Equation
(1) says that in this degree and on this complement the refinement has
no additional information: forgetting it loses nothing. This closes the
specified characteristic Euler detector, not all algebraic or motivic
obstruction theory. It does not construct a pair with the required
common-zero support.

## 2. Ordinary cycles and the virtual bundle

Localization for Chow groups gives
\[
CH^2(U)=\mathbf Z h^2/(d h^2),\qquad CH^3(U)=0.                        \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the fundamental class of C maps to \(d h^2\), while a point of C
maps to the point generator \(h^3\). No assertion about a moving pair of
equations enters this calculation.

There is a parallel integral calculation in algebraic K-theory, valid
in every characteristic. With \(t=1-[\mathcal O(-1)]\),
\[
K_0(U)=\mathbf Z[t]/(t^3,d t^2).                                     \tag{5}
\]
To see this, use \(K_0(\mathbf P^3)=\mathbf Z[t]/t^4\) and coherent-sheaf
localization. Smoothness identifies K-theory with coherent G-theory here.
The Hilbert polynomial determines classes in \(K_0(\mathbf P^3)\), and
\[
[\mathcal O_C]=d t^2+(1-g-d)t^3,\qquad[\mathcal O_p]=t^3.
\]
Every coherent sheaf on C of generic rank r has pushforward Hilbert
polynomial \(rd\,n+\chi\), and therefore pushforward class
\(rd\,t^2+(\chi-rd)t^3\). The image of \(G_0(C)\) is exactly the subgroup,
and ideal, generated by \(d t^2\) and \(t^3\). This proves (5), including
the absence of a hidden genus or degree-zero-Picard contribution.

If the desired section exists, its exact sequence requires
\[
[\mathcal O_U(a)]+[\mathcal O_U(b)]-[\mathcal O_U]-[\mathcal O_U(a+b)]
=-ab\,t^2=0.                                                       \tag{6}
\]
The equality follows by expanding \([\mathcal O(n)]=(1-t)^{-n}\) modulo
\(t^3\). Thus this necessary virtual-bundle condition is again exactly
\(d\mid ab\). An equality in K-theory is not an isomorphism of middle
bundles and does not supply an exact sequence with the desired maps.

## 3. The refined comparison without an affine hypothesis

Write \(\mathbf I^j(L)\) for the twisted unramified sheaf of powers of
the fundamental ideal of the Witt ring. Since k is algebraically closed,
its 2-cohomological dimension is zero. On a smooth threefold,
\[
\mathbf I^4(L)=0,\qquad
\mathbf I^3(L)\simeq\mathbf K^M_3/2
\simeq\mathcal H^3_{\mathrm{et}}(\mu_2^{\otimes3}).                   \tag{7}
\]
The first statement is [Asok–Fasel, Proposition 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.0770).
The ensuing identifications are the twisted fundamental-ideal quotient
and the norm-residue comparison, as in their proof of Proposition 5.2.
The action producing the line-bundle twist becomes trivial modulo the
next fundamental-ideal power. Thus (7) holds for every L, without choosing
a square root or orientation of L.

For completeness, the two cohomology vanishings needed on this
**nonaffine** U can be proved directly. The Bloch–Ogus spectral sequence
\[
E_2^{p,q}=H^p_{\mathrm{Nis}}
 (U,\mathcal H^q_{\mathrm{et}}(\mu_2^{\otimes3}))
\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathrm{et}}(U,\mu_2^{\otimes3})
\]
has no rows q greater than three, by the cohomological-dimension bound,
and no columns p greater than three. The terms \((3,3)\) and \((2,3)\)
have no incoming or outgoing differentials from the second page onward:
incoming terms have q at least four and outgoing terms have p at least
four. Hence its upper edges give
\[
H^6_{\mathrm{et}}(U,\mu_2^{\otimes3})
 \simeq H^3_{\mathrm{Nis}}(U,\mathbf I^3(L)),
\qquad
H^5_{\mathrm{et}}(U,\mu_2^{\otimes3})
 \twoheadrightarrow H^2_{\mathrm{Nis}}(U,\mathbf I^3(L)).              \tag{8}
\]
These are precisely the edges used before the final affine step in the
proof of Asok–Fasel Proposition 5.2. We use that argument, **not** the
proposition's affine vanishing conclusion.

Purity for the smooth codimension-two embedding \(C\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^3\)
and trace-normalized Gysin maps give the segment below; see
[Milne, Theorems 16.1 and 24.1, Remark 24.2](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/LEC.pdf)
for these standard ingredients.
\[
0\longrightarrow H^5_{\mathrm{et}}(U,\mu_2^{\otimes3})
\longrightarrow H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(C,\mu_2)
\xrightarrow{i_*}H^6_{\mathrm{et}}(\mathbf P^3,\mu_2^{\otimes3})
\longrightarrow H^6_{\mathrm{et}}(U,\mu_2^{\otimes3})
\longrightarrow0.                                                   \tag{9}
\]
Both middle groups have order two, and \(i_*\) is an isomorphism: a point
on the connected smooth proper curve maps to the point class of
\(\mathbf P^3\). This is multiplication by **one**, not by d; the map
from \(H^0(C)\) to \(H^4(\mathbf P^3)\) is the different map that measures d.
Therefore both outer groups in (9) vanish. Equations (7)–(8) now yield
\[
H^2_{\mathrm{Nis}}(U,\mathbf I^3(L))=
H^3_{\mathrm{Nis}}(U,\mathbf I^3(L))=0.                              \tag{10}
\]

Finally use the exact sequence of sheaves
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbf I^3(L)
\longrightarrow\mathbf K^{MW}_2(L)
\longrightarrow\mathbf K^M_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Its cohomology sequence and (10) prove (1). The characteristic Euler
class is defined for bundles on smooth schemes and forgets to the top
Chern class; a nowhere-zero section makes it vanish. These are the
characteristic-class statements in [Asok–Fasel, *Comparing Euler classes*,
Introduction and §§2–3, 5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.5250).
We do not need an affine splitting theorem or an unqualified application
of their oriented comparison theorem. This proves (2)–(3).

The argument uses neither rationality, genus zero, the marked planes nor
the quadric containing C0. Its field restriction is characteristic
different from two; it therefore cannot distinguish characteristic zero
from odd-characteristic STCI constructions.

## 4. What the checked analytic realization results supply

Over the complex numbers, Hartogs gives \(H^0(U,\mathcal O_U)=\mathbf C\).
In particular U is not 1-convex: its proper Remmert reduction to a Stein
space would have constant global functions, hence point image, forcing
U compact. [Stopar, Corollary 8.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.4256)
does provide a non-Stein Oka theorem for sections, but requires a
1-convex base along with further domain and submersion hypotheses.
The source hypothesis already fails; testing sprays would not repair it.

[Fornæss–Sibony–Wold, Theorems 1.6–1.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.5408)
give U an exhaustion with corners having two positive Levi directions.
Their convention counts positive eigenvalues. Their line-bundle
extension theorem with corners needs at least three in dimension three;
moreover its conclusion extends a line bundle from an end, rather than
embedding the trivial bundle into our prescribed rank-two bundle.
Neither its stronger positivity hypothesis nor the desired section
conclusion follows from the two-direction result.

[Forstnerič, Proposition 1.2(a) and Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110201)
also makes U a subelliptic **target** for maps from Stein sources. This
does not turn U into a Stein source for a theorem about sections over U.

There is an immediate global control against an unqualified realization
principle. If C is nonplanar, \(\mathcal O_U(1)\oplus\mathcal O_U(d)\)
passes (3), (6), and the complete continuous-section test. A holomorphic
nowhere-zero section would extend across C to two homogeneous forms with
common-zero support C. Its linear form would contain C, contradicting
nonplanarity. The linear form cannot be identically zero either: a single
positive-degree form has a hypersurface of zeros on U. Thus passing
all these tests is not sufficient, even for positive split bundles.
This control is not an STCI exclusion in other degrees.

## 5. Consequence for the unrestricted question and stopping

For C0 every pair satisfying \(4\mid ab\) passes the ordinary Chow,
virtual-bundle and twisted characteristic Euler conditions, as well as
the previous complete continuous test. These invariants impose no
additional degree restriction and no positive residual-degree bound.
They remain valid necessary conditions, with no new construction or
inference favoring nonexistence.

An extension class in \(H^1(U,\mathcal O_U(-a-b))\) constructs a middle
bundle, but does not identify it with \(\mathcal O_U(a)\oplus\mathcal O_U(b)\).
The identification and its actual section are still the missing data.
For an independently supplied class, checking its reflexive extension
and eventual splitting is a candidate verification method; it is not a
source of such a class. Affine replacement still requires strict descent.

Stop this primary Euler calculation uniformly. Do not turn its vanishing
into a generic secondary-obstruction campaign or a degree search. Reopen
with an actual realization/descent operation, or a specified algebraic
obstruction whose nontrivial evaluation has a precise implication for
arbitrary pairs. No such next experiment is selected by the checked
literature. Higher motivic data and geometric information from actual
surfaces and thickenings remain uncomputed by this note.
