# The marked embedding through its equation filtration and normal deformation

Date: 2026-10-24. Status: **research proposals with local proofs and independent agent reconstruction; no canonical promotion**.

This investigation starts from `main` at `ce22436`, after the merged [ideal-filtration reconciliation](../RECONCILIATION_2026-10-24-IDEAL-FILTRATIONS.md). Its immediate question is the geometry of the marked embedding \(C\subset Z=X\cap Y\). There is **no assumption that \(Z_{\mathrm{red}}=C\)**. Additional components are permitted; identifying or classifying them is not a separate objective here. The supported punctual-defect question in the predecessor remains a special open question, rather than the organizing restriction of this continuation.

The two most useful advances are an elementary, scheme-theoretic annihilator of \(\Delta\), and an intrinsic realization of \(\Delta\) as the special fibre of conormal torsion during deformation to the normal cone. Together with separate criteria for the carrier square, regularity of the cone embedding, and transverse specialization, they distinguish several geometric failures that the single invariant cannot conflate.

## 1. Exact hypotheses, retained inputs, and authority

For the geometric statements, \(C\) is a smooth integral curve in a smooth threefold \(P\), over a field of arbitrary characteristic. Locally
\[
R=\mathcal O_{P,p},\quad I=I_C=(x,y),\quad B=R/I,
\quad A_X=(f),\quad A_Y=(g),\quad J=A_X+A_Y\subset I,
\]
where \(f,g\) form a regular sequence. Distinct integral hypersurfaces give this situation; several algebraic results below have weaker hypotheses, stated in their individual notes. No rational-quartic classification, sole-support theorem, or STCI exclusion is a premise.

The inherited [comparison construction](2026-10-23-ideal-filtrations-independent-investigation.md) and [normal-Koszul continuation](2026-10-24-delta-singular-locus-continuation.md) are reviewed but **unpromoted** evidence. Their exact claims are retained as premises, without replaying inherited certificates. In particular,
\[
S=\operatorname{gr}_I R=\operatorname{Sym}_B E,\quad E=I/I^2,
\quad H=\operatorname{in}_I J,\quad T=\operatorname{in}_I(IJ),
\]
\[
D=J/IJ,\quad F^nD=((J\cap I^n)+IJ)/IJ,
\quad Q=H/S_+H,\quad G=\operatorname{gr}_F D,
\]
\[
0\longrightarrow\Delta=T/S_+H\longrightarrow Q
\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow0. \tag{1}
\]
All initial ideals are **full** initial ideals. A list of carrier symbols cannot replace \(H\) or \(T\).

The new results below are proved in separate proposal notes, with independent reconstruction recorded there or in linked reviews. This report is a synthesis and continuation guide, not an ID-bearing theorem record. No canonical result or question is promoted by it.

## 2. Retain the entire embedding and its maps

The data remain the actual schemes and inclusions
\[
C\subset Z\subset X,Y\subset P,
\]
including the original Cartesian square \(Z=X\times_PY\), all composites, and both labelled carrier ideals. The nine ideal-power filtrations and seven transitivity sequences are retained as specified in the predecessor, section 2. In particular, on \(C\),
\[
M_X=A_X/IA_X,\qquad M_Y=A_Y/IA_Y,
\qquad D=M_X\oplus M_Y,
\]
\[
\phi=(\alpha,\beta):D\longrightarrow E,
\qquad
0\to F^2D\to D\xrightarrow{\phi}E
\to N^*_{C/Z}\to0. \tag{2}
\]
The quotients \(E/\operatorname{im}\alpha\), \(E/\operatorname{im}\beta\), and \(E/\operatorname{im}\phi\) describe the paths through \(X,Y,Z\); their maps and image intersections remain part of the diagram. For projective carriers of degrees \(d,e\), the source lines in (2) are \(\mathcal O_C(-d),\mathcal O_C(-e)\). None is replaced by an abstract pair of unlabelled generators in questions about the original carriers.

The full quotient \(R/J\), its multiplication, and its Rees algebra retain the embedded scheme and extension information. The normal algebra \(S/H\), the filtration quotients, or \(\Delta\) alone do not reconstruct that scheme. Changing generators preserves the marked ideal \(J\subset I\), but can change the labelled intermediate hypersurfaces and their comparison maps.

## 3. A stronger intrinsic annihilator

Put
\[
\mathfrak a=\operatorname{im}(E^\vee\otimes D\xrightarrow{\phi}B)
=\operatorname{Fitt}_1(N^*_{C/Z}),
\]
where the first map includes evaluation. In frames, \(\mathfrak a\) is the ideal of entries of the first conormal map. The [annihilator proposal](2026-10-24-delta-conormal-annihilator-proposal.md) proves
\[
\boxed{\mathfrak a\Delta=0.} \tag{3}
\]
This holds for **any** coherent \(J\subset I\) when \(I\) is locally generated by a regular sequence of length two; \(J\) need not be a complete intersection. It is stronger than set-theoretic support containment: \(\Delta\) is an actual module on the subscheme where the conormal map vanishes.

The proof is elementary. For \(h\in IJ\cap I^n\), write
\(h=xc+yd=xu+yv\), with \(c,d\in I^{n-1}\), \(u,v\in J\). Regularity gives \(c-u=-yw,d-v=xw\). For \(f=xa'+yb'\in J\),
\[
a'd-b'c=a'v-b'u+fw\in J\cap I^{n-1}.
\]
If \(a,b\) are its coefficient residues and \(\ell=ad^*-bc^*\), then \(\ell\in H_{n-1}\) and
\[
ah^*=c^*f_1+Y\ell,\qquad bh^*=d^*f_1-X\ell.
\]
Both belong to \(S_+H\). This proves (3) without filtered convergence assumptions.

At a DVR point, \(\mathfrak a=(t^r)\ne0\) implies that each elementary-divisor exponent of \(\Delta_n\) is at most \(r\), and
\[
\operatorname{length}_B\Delta_n\le r\,\mu_B(\Delta_n).
\]
The bound is sharp: \(J=(t^rx+y^2,t^ry+x^2)\) has \(\Delta_3=B/(t^r)\). Thus the nonreduced conormal scheme matters. This bounds coefficient thickness, not normal degree or the number of defect generators. It does not assert that \(\mathfrak a\) annihilates all higher \(Q_n\): surviving higher equations can remain free.

The [independent annihilator review](2026-10-24-delta-annihilator-review.md) reconstructs the proof, its broader hypotheses, and the intrinsic Fitting ideal.

## 4. Delta is the special fibre of conormal torsion

Introduce an independent deformation parameter \(u\) and retain the actual extended Rees algebra and induced ideal
\[
\mathscr R=R[u,I/u],\qquad
\mathscr J=\bigoplus_n(J\cap I^n)u^{-n},\qquad
L=(I/u)\mathscr R.
\]
The canonical quotient \(\mathscr R/L=B[u]\) gives the marked family \(C\times\mathbf A^1\). No splitting \(B\to R/J\), or retraction \(Z\to C\), is assumed. The conormal module of the deformed \(Z\), restricted to this family, is
\[
\mathscr N=\mathscr J/L\mathscr J,
\qquad\mathscr N|_{u=1}=D,\quad\mathscr N|_{u=0}=Q.
\]
The [Rees proposal](2026-10-24-delta-rees-specialization-proposal.md) and its [independent review](2026-10-24-delta-rees-specialization-review.md) establish
\[
0\to\mathscr E\to\mathscr N\to\operatorname{Rees}_F(D)\to0,
\qquad
\mathscr E=H^0_{(u)}(\mathscr N),
\]
\[
\mathscr E_n=\frac{IJ\cap I^n}{I(J\cap I^{n-1})},
\qquad\boxed{\Delta\simeq\mathscr E/u\mathscr E.} \tag{4}
\]
These identities require only noetherian \(R\) and \(J\subset I\). Over a field,
\[
\Delta=0\iff\mathscr E=0
\iff\mathscr N\text{ is flat over }k[u].
\]
Flatness over \(B[u]\) is a stronger condition. Parameter torsion of this conormal module must also be distinguished from the algebra of the deformed \(Z\), which is already \(u\)-torsion-free.

Combining (3) and (4) gives a further elementary consequence:
\[
\mathfrak a\mathscr E\subset u\mathscr E,
\qquad u^N\mathscr E=0\ \Longrightarrow\
\mathfrak a^N\mathscr E=0.
\]
Indeed iterate the first inclusion, using the commuting curve and deformation-parameter actions. Agent `audit_rees` independently checked this synthesis consequence. The exponent is local unless a uniform one has been chosen. Unlike \(\Delta\), the full persistence module is not asserted to be annihilated by \(\mathfrak a\) itself.

The full torsion module contains information lost by its special fibre. For
\[
J=(x^a+y^b,xy),\qquad 2\le a<b,
\]
one has
\[
\mathscr N\simeq B[u]e_F\oplus B[u]e_G
\oplus(B[u]/u^{b-a})e_T,
\quad\deg(e_F,e_G,e_T)=(a,2,b+1).
\]
At fixed \(b\), the graded defect \(\Delta=Be_T\) stays unchanged while the torsion duration \(b-a\) varies. The note proves the full ideal and complete syzygies, and supplies distinct integral algebraic carriers with the same local ideal. The examples are local marked-line models, not global constructions for the fixed rational quartic.

## 5. Different geometric questions have different tests

The [normal-geometry proposal](2026-10-24-delta-normal-geometry-proposal.md) proves the following distinctions in the smooth-curve, complete-intersection scope.

| Geometric question | Exact local test or comparison |
|---|---|
| Does normal degeneration preserve the original labelled carrier fibre product? | \(H=(f^*,g^*)\), equivalently the original symbols form a regular sequence. Its failure is \(H/(f^*,g^*)\). |
| Is the actual normal cone regularly embedded in the ambient normal bundle near its zero section? | \(Q\) free of rank two; equivalently \(\Delta=0\) **and** every \(F^nD\) saturated in \(D\). |
| Is the restricted conormal module flat in the deformation parameter? | \(\Delta=0\), equivalently \(\mathscr E=0\). |
| Is the normal cone flat over the marked curve? | Each piece of \(S/H\) is \(B\)-torsion-free. This is different from the preceding tests. |
| Is \(C\hookrightarrow Z\) itself a regular immersion? | In this same-dimensional local complete-intersection setting, precisely \(I=J\). |

For the whole deformed diagram, induced carrier ideals satisfy
\[
\mathscr J=(\mathscr A_X+\mathscr A_Y):u^\infty.
\]
The deformation of the actual intersection is the flat closure of the generic intersection of the carrier deformations. The extra saturation is measured by
\(\mathscr J/(\mathscr A_X+\mathscr A_Y)\), a different module from \(\mathscr E\). The arrows and triangles commute even when the special square ceases to be Cartesian.

Three examples keep these distinctions concrete:

- \(f=x,g=x+y^m\), \(m\ge2\): \(\Delta=0\), and the actual cone has ideal \((X,Y^m)\), but the labelled carrier cones intersect with ideal \((X)\). Changing the carrier to \(g-f\) changes the labelled square.
- \(f=y,g=y+tx\): \(Q=B^2\) and \(\Delta=0\), so the cone embedding is regular; its algebra \(B[X]/(tX)\) is not flat over the marked curve.
- The inherited supported quadruple \(J=(x^2,y^2+tx)\) has \(\Delta=0\), but retained torsion in \(G_2,G_3\) prevents regularity of the cone embedding. This uses the predecessor's reviewed computation as a premise.

Vertical structure in these degenerations is not, by itself, evidence for an additional component downstairs.

## 6. Transverse specialization retains the neighborhood tower

Choose formal coordinates \(R=B[[x,y]]\), \(B=k[[t]]\), with a specified lift of the curve parameter. Write \(M=R/J\), \(K=IM\), \(\bar M=M/tM\), and
\[
U_n=\operatorname{Tor}_1^B(M/K^n,k).
\]
The [base-change proposal](2026-10-24-delta-base-change-proposal.md) proves the natural exact sequence
\[
U_{n+1}\to U_n\to
(K^n/K^{n+1})\otimes_Bk
\to\operatorname{gr}_{\bar K}^n\bar M\to0. \tag{5}
\]
The specialization kernel is \(\operatorname{coker}(U_{n+1}\to U_n)\), measuring torsion in the neighborhood tower that fails to lift one stage deeper. This construction is natural for the compatible filtered maps in the full diagram. A transverse slice depends on the specified parameter lift; the cone over \(C\) itself is intrinsic.

Assume \(t\) is a nonzerodivisor on \(M\), and \(M/K=B\). Then compatibility of the cone with the actual slice **in all degrees** is equivalent to normal flatness over \(B\), equivalently flatness of every \(M/K^n\). The converse uses Artin--Rees, not a degreewise dimension guess. The nonzerodivisor hypothesis cannot be omitted: \(J=(x,ty+x^2)\) has nonflat algebra and cone but all its cone comparisons with \(t=0\) are isomorphisms.

The proposal first gives a sufficient criterion for \(\Delta\) to commute with the slice, retaining both \(J\) and \(IJ\): flatness of \(R/J\) and normal flatness of both \(\operatorname{gr}_I(R/J)\) and \(\operatorname{gr}_I(R/IJ)\). It then resolves the further implication in this codimension-two complete-intersection setting: normal flatness of \(S/H\) **alone** forces every \(F^nD\) to be saturated and \(G\) flat. Thus it suffices for all those specialization conclusions.

The additional argument uses a field lemma: the two weights of the filtered equation module of a transverse complete intersection are the first two minimal initial-generator degrees, and are determined by its Hilbert function. Normal flatness makes these weights agree generically and in the actual slice; strict lifting of \(J\cap I^n\) then forces saturation by Smith normal form. Lemma 4 and Proposition 5 of the base-change note give the proof and independent audit.

This conditional locus has a material limitation. Normal flatness also forces \(K^N=0\) locally: the generic normal algebra is artinian, so high-degree graded pieces have rank zero and flatness makes them zero; Nakayama finishes. Consequently the local support is then \(C\), as a **deduced conclusion**, not an assumption of the investigation. Points where additional components meet \(C\) remain covered by (5). Unlike deformation in \(u\), specialization in \(t\) changes the actual transverse embedding and requires the neighborhood tower. No flatness or vanishing of \(Q\) or \(\Delta\) follows from the corollary alone.

## 7. Next directions and precise open boundaries

The most promising next object is the sheaf \(\mathscr E\) with its \(u\)-power filtration, together with the original carrier source maps. Three focused questions emerge:

1. **Persistence and multiplication.** Identify exactly how the \(u\)-power filtration of \(\mathscr E\) compares with the incoming normal-Koszul differential filtration. The explicit family proves its own page/duration correspondence; a general sheaf-level identification, compatible with changes of coordinates and extension data, is not established here.
2. **Specialization where normal flatness fails.** Use the joint torsion profiles of the neighborhood towers for \(R/J\) and \(R/IJ\) to determine the actual kernel and cokernel of specialization for \(\Delta\). Normal flatness already implies equation saturation in the present scope; the useful broader problem is the interaction of the two failures outside that strong conditional locus, without excluding points where further components meet \(C\).
3. **Global realization of the local maps.** Carry the labelled source lines, the intrinsic annihilator scheme, and the filtered Rees equation module through overlaps. Determine the extension and gluing data needed for adapted local equations to arise from the actual global hypersurface forms. Local models alone do not supply those global forms.

The older question about punctual \(\Delta\) with \(\sqrt J=I\) and \(\Delta_\eta=0\) remains open in this record. It is a special test case, not a premise of this investigation or the required next objective. Nothing here resolves the unrestricted characteristic-zero STCI problem or reconstructs an intersection from \(\Delta\) alone.
