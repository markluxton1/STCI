of the source author. Scope: Sections 1–6, conclusions 1–4 and equations

defect found; independently reviewed provisional deductions, no promotion**.
The unrestricted characteristic-zero STCI question remains open.

The tested curve is exactly
`C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]`, over an algebraically closed field. The construction,
index identity, local bounds and two-divisor lemma work in every
characteristic. Strict movement, and therefore the application excluding
two global Cartier divisors upstairs, use characteristic zero. The input
is a proper homogeneous CI `Z=V(F_a,G_b)` with **sole reduced support C**.
Residual components are outside these conclusions.

## 1. Full closure, flatness and the finite image

I reconstructed the morphism from the formulas in the independently
reviewed [tropical-model note](2026-10-31-tropical-model-characteristic.md).
It is an isomorphism on the dense three-dimensional torus. Its restriction
to the reduced section `P1 × {(1,1)}` is the given embedding of C. Its
ambient nonproperness causes no problem in the following argument.

On the torus, the local algebra at the generic point of C is Artinian of
length `m=ab/4`, by the CI cycle degree. Its maximal ideal is generated by
U and V and its m-th power vanishes. The sheaf `(U,V)^m O_Z` is therefore
generically zero. Since a CI curve is Cohen–Macaulay and has no embedded
associated points, this subsheaf must vanish everywhere on the torus.
This step uses sole support and purity, rather than an unjustified
extension of generic vanishing across special points.

Consequently scheme-theoretic closure lies in the **fixed** finite ambient
scheme `P1 × Spec(k[U,V]/(U,V)^m)`. This proves finiteness over P1 and
excludes extra endpoint support. On an endpoint chart the closure ideal
is contraction after inverting its parameter: if `t h` belongs to the
contracted ideal, so does h. Its quotient thus has no parameter torsion.
On the interior the same torsion freeness follows from CM purity.
Finite torsion-free modules over the local DVRs of P1 are free. Their
rank is m. Thus W is finite flat, including both endpoints, and B is a
quotient of the constant vector bundle with fiber `k[U,V]/(U,V)^m`.
Global generation, nonnegative degree and `H^1(B)=0` follow.

Every pulled-back relation of Z vanishes on W by contraction. The induced
map `rho:W→Z` is proper because W is proper over k and Z is separated.
Its topological map identifies the two reduced curves, so it is
quasi-finite and hence finite. It is an isomorphism on the torus.
The kernel of `O_Z→rho_*O_W` is supported at the two endpoints;
CM purity again makes it zero. The cokernel has exactly the two finite
lengths denoted by delta0 and deltaInfinity. In particular, the
scheme-theoretic image is the **entire nonreduced Z**.

This construction uses contraction of the whole ideal. Independently
dividing two chosen equations would not prove any of these assertions.

## 2. Genus and strict movement

For a finite map, Euler characteristic is preserved under pushforward.
Riemann–Roch gives `chi(O_W)=m+e`; CI adjunction downstairs gives
`chi(O_Z)=-ab(a+b-4)/2`. Their difference is exactly

`delta0+deltaInfinity = e+m(2a+2b-7)`.

The finite quotient of the fixed Artin algebra gives a morphism from P1
to its length-m quotient scheme. The algebra-ideal conditions cut out a
closed subscheme of the quotient Grassmannian. The determinant of B is
the pulled-back Plücker bundle. Thus e measures the actual embedded
punctual-family movement.

If e is zero, global generation and splitting of vector bundles on P1
give `B≅O^m`. In such a trivialization the unit, multiplication and the
images of U and V are constant: their coefficients are global regular
functions on P1. The quotient algebra map, including its kernel, is
therefore constant. This proves embedded constancy, not merely constancy
of the isomorphism type of its fibers. Conversely an embedded constant
family has e zero.

Scaling t and fixing u,v preserves a constant W. Equivariance of phi and
the already established scheme-theoretic image imply invariance of Z
under weights `(0,1,3,4)`. The homogeneous CI ideal J is saturated: its
graded quotient is CM of dimension two and has no irrelevant-ideal
torsion. Thus invariance of the projective subscheme implies invariance
of J itself. The two-dimensional minimal-generator space `J/R_+J` has
a basis homogeneous both in ordinary degree and torus weight. Weight
projection supplies lifts in J; graded Nakayama makes those two lifts
generators. Height two excludes a common nonunit factor.

I checked the primary text of
[Hellus–Hübl, Theorem 1.2(a)–(b)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.7661),
including its introductory hypotheses, rather than relying on its
abstract. It concerns this exact quartic, algebraically closed
characteristic zero, and homogeneous pairs whose radical is its ideal.
For each extreme bihomogeneous pair it gives a nonunit gcd lying in the
curve ideal. Ordinary degree together with weights `(0,1,3,4)` is its
bigrading: the other coordinate is `4d−weight`. For the two eigenvector
generators every extreme is the generator itself, contradicting height
two. This proves `e>0` for every assumed defining pair in characteristic
zero. No assertion about variation of the abstract generic Artin algebra
is required.

## 3. Every global two-Cartier presentation is covered

The two standard affine charts of Y are factorial. Their overlap units
are `c t^r u^i v^j`; chart units remove precisely the Laurent u,v factors
and constants. Hence `Pic(Y)=Z`, with class pulled back from P1. A
nonzero section of `O(d)` is a Laurent polynomial in u,v whose
coefficients belong to `H^0(P1,O(d))`. It follows that every effective
Cartier divisor has `d≥0`.

For any two such divisors cutting out this W scheme-theoretically,
their local sections have height two in a regular ambient threefold,
so form a regular sequence. Adjunction gives
`omega_W=pi^*O(d1+d2−2)`. Finite duality over P1 gives independently

`pi_*omega_W=B^vee⊗O(−2)`.

Projection formula applied to adjunction gives
`pi_*omega_W=B⊗O(d1+d2−2)`. Equating determinant degrees yields
`2e=−m(d1+d2)`. Since B is globally generated and both d's are
nonnegative, all three degrees vanish, forcing an embedded constant
family. The proof applies to **any** two effective Cartier divisors,
without tying them to divided pullbacks. For the lemma's arbitrary
finite-flat W with the prescribed support, global generation follows by
the same generic nilpotence and parameter-torsion-freeness argument as
in Section 1 above.

This is a global presentation obstruction. It neither proves failure of
the local complete-intersection property nor excludes a section of a
nonsplit rank-two bundle.

## 4. Endpoint algebras, changed parameter and the coarse bound

At zero the target affine coordinates pull back to
`t, t^3(1+U), t^4(1+V)`. Thus the image of its completed local ring is
exactly `A0=k[[t]][t^3U,t^4V]`. This algebra is finite over `k[[t]]`,
as a submodule of the finite module B0; hence it is complete and no
additional topological-closure term is missing from this notation.

At infinity set `z=τu/v`, `1+U'=u^(−3)v^2`, `1+V'=u^(−4)v^3`.
The exponent matrix has determinant minus one; its inverse is the same
integer matrix. Explicitly,

`u=(1+U')^(−3)(1+V')^2`,
`v=(1+U')^(−4)(1+V')^3`,
`τ=z(1+V')/(1+U')`.

These formulas give an invertible change of the completed transverse
coordinates, not just a first-order normal change. Since `z/τ` is a
unit, z and τ define the same adic topology and special fiber and z is
a nonzerodivisor. Complete Nakayama makes BInfinity finite over
`k[[z]]`; torsion freeness makes it free, of rank the common fiber
length m. The displayed inverse also proves algebra generation by
U',V' over `k[[z]]`. Finally the target affine coordinates are exactly
`z, z^3(1+U'), z^4(1+V')`, giving the asserted AInfinity.

For either special fiber choose a standard-monomial basis of its
polynomial quotient in the two transverse coordinates. Its exponents
form an order ideal E of cardinality m. The same monomials lift to a
DVR basis of B. The diagonal lattice with respective powers
`t^(3i+4j)` lies inside A, so `length(B/A)≤sum_E(3i+4j)`.
A monomial of total degree d has a divisor chain of d+1 elements in E;
therefore its position r in total-degree order satisfies `d≤r`.
Summing gives `sum_E(3i+4j)≤2m(m−1)`.

The example `B=k[[t]][V]/(V^m), U=0` attains this local bound. It does
not establish simultaneous endpoint attainability or projective CI
realizability. Combining the two inequalities with the genus identity
gives exactly `0<e≤m(4m−2a−2b+3)` in characteristic zero. No hidden
equalities or stronger endpoint restrictions were used.

## Geometric effect and verification boundary

A characteristic control comes from the inherited pair
`F=x1^4−x0^3x3`, `G=x2^7−x0x1x3^5`. On the dense torus its
pullbacks, after removing the invertible t powers, are `1−v` and
`u^7−v^5`. The full ideal is therefore `(v−1,u^7−1)` and already
defines its closure in Y. In characteristic seven it is
`(V,U^7)`, so W is a constant length-seven family with e zero.
On `x0=1`, eliminating x3 gives `(x2−x1^3)^7`; on `x0=0`
the equations force x1=x2=0. This is indeed a supported CI on C.
In characteristic zero the same closure instead consists of seven
disjoint constant reduced sections, one for each seventh root of
unity in u. Its projective image has seven distinct translated
quartic supports meeting at the endpoints, so the sole-support
hypothesis fails. This checks the characteristic restriction
geometrically without extrapolating a local example to a new
characteristic-zero defining pair.

The object measured is the full embedded finite transverse scheme,
continued flatly across both endpoints by saturation. The positive
degree records its nonconstant position in a fixed punctual quotient
space. The endpoint indices measure the functions gained by reversing
the projective contractions. The duality calculation says that such
positive movement cannot coexist with two global effective Cartier
equations in this product ambient.

This supplies a uniform necessary geometry for hypothetical defining
pairs. It does not show that moving families with these contracted
endpoint lattices cannot have a homogeneous CI image. The upper bound
leaves that interface open. Full formal-pair algebraization claims in
the source's final paragraph were not needed for, and were not
independently re-audited by, this bounded review. Its repository/literature
priority observations are likewise not theorem inputs. No computer
algebra, inherited certificate rerun, or canonical promotion was
performed. No material mathematical correction to Sections 1–5 was
required.
