## 1. Objects and basic hypotheses reconstructed

Let \(C\) be a smooth connected closed curve over an algebraically closed field \(k\), regularly embedded in \(\mathbf P^3\), and \(Y=V(F_a,G_b)\) a proper complete-intersection curve. Since a smooth connected curve has disjoint irreducible components, connectedness implies that \(C\) is integral. Write

\[
I=I_C,\qquad L=I_Y,\qquad J=I_T
\]

for the ideal of the **entire isolated \(C\)-primary component** \(T\) of \(Y\). This component is intrinsic: it is obtained by localizing at the generic point of \(C\) and contracting. In particular \(J_{\eta_C}=L_{\eta_C}\).

A complete-intersection curve is unmixed. Taking its isolated primary component leaves a pure one-dimensional scheme, hence a locally CM curve: at a closed point there is no embedded maximal associated prime, so depth is one. The residual \(D\), with ideal \(K=L:J\), is locally CM by complete-intersection linkage. It has the complementary minimal components, including their actual generic multiplicities; \(C\) is not a component of \(D\). This is different from linking to reduced \(C\) once.

The generic ideal of \(T\) has two minimal generators because it equals the ideal of the actual complete intersection \(Y\) there. No curvilinearity, normal-flatness, or binary initial-CI hypothesis is needed.

## 2. Support and the torsion correction

Set

\[
\mathcal E=J/IJ,\qquad
\mathcal Q=J/(L+IJ),\qquad
P=\mathcal O_C(-a)\oplus\mathcal O_C(-b).
\]

The actual equations give \(P\to\mathcal E\), with cokernel \(\mathcal Q\). At the generic point this is an isomorphism, so \(\mathcal E\) has rank two and \(\mathcal Q\) is torsion.

The support assertion has a short independent proof:

\[
\mathcal Q_p=0
\Longleftrightarrow J_p=L_p+I_pJ_p
\Longleftrightarrow J_p=L_p
\Longleftrightarrow (L:J)_p=\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3,p}.
\]

The middle equivalence is Nakayama on \(J_p/L_p\); the last follows directly from the colon definition. Thus

\[
\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal Q=C\cap\operatorname{Supp}D.
\]

For the global equivalence \(\mathcal Q=0\iff\operatorname{Supp}Y=C\), projective CI connectedness is necessary: it excludes a nonempty residual union disjoint from \(C\). The statement does not assert that every residual component individually meets \(C\).

Let \(\mathcal T=\operatorname{tors}\mathcal E\) and \(\overline{\mathcal E}=\mathcal E/\mathcal T\). Since \(C\) is smooth, \(\overline{\mathcal E}\) is locally free of rank two. The induced map \(\bar\rho:P\to\overline{\mathcal E}\) is generically an isomorphism; its kernel is a torsion subsheaf of a locally free sheaf and therefore zero. Consequently the image of \(P\to\mathcal E\) meets \(\mathcal T\) trivially, proving

\[
0\to\mathcal T\to\mathcal Q\to\operatorname{coker}\bar\rho\to0.
\]

Smith normal form over each \(\mathcal O_{C,p}\) gives

\[
\operatorname{length}\mathcal Q_p
=\operatorname{length}\mathcal T_p+
\operatorname{ord}_p\det\bar\rho.
\]

This also establishes the claimed non-lci interpretation. If \(\mathcal T_p=0\), then \(\mathcal E_p\) is free of rank two over the DVR. Its residue fiber is \(J_p/\mathfrak m_pJ_p\), so \(J_p\) has two generators. A height-two ideal with two generators in a regular local ring is a regular sequence. Conversely, if \(J_p\) is generated by a regular sequence \(f,g\), its only first syzygy is \((g,-f)\), whose coefficients vanish modulo \(I\). Thus \(\mathcal E_p\) is free of rank two. The torsion support is exactly the non-lci locus of \(T\).

## 3. The non-Gorenstein length equality is valid

Classical linkage gives

\[
J/L\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_Y}(\mathcal O_D,\mathcal O_Y)
\simeq\omega_D\otimes\omega_Y^{-1}
=\omega_D(4-a-b).
\]

Reduction modulo \(I\) therefore yields

\[
\mathcal Q\simeq\omega_D(4-a-b)\otimes\mathcal O_C.
\]

The critical step is comparison with \(\mathcal O_D\otimes\mathcal O_C\). It is not an isomorphism claim. It is the following exact local length calculation, which does not assume \(D\) Gorenstein.

For a point in \(C\cap D\), let \(B=\mathcal O_{D,p}\). The ideal \(IB\) is maximal-primary and has two generators. Since \(B\) is one-dimensional CM and \(k\) is infinite, a \(k\)-linear combination \(f\) of these generators avoids the finite set of associated primes of \(B\). Complete it to an invertible change of generators, \(IB=(f,g)\). Put \(A=B/fB\), an Artinian local algebra.

The canonical-module reduction \(\omega_A\simeq\omega_B/f\omega_B\) may also be proved directly in this setting. Over the ambient three-dimensional regular local ring \(R\), the CM module \(B\) has projective dimension two. Apply \(\operatorname{Ext}_R^\bullet(-,R)\) to
\[
0\to B\xrightarrow{f}B\to A\to0.
\]
The vanishing \(\operatorname{Ext}_R^2(A,R)=0=\operatorname{Ext}_R^3(B,R)\) gives
\[
0\to\omega_B\xrightarrow{f}\omega_B\to\operatorname{Ext}_R^3(A,R)\to0,
\]
and the last term is the canonical module of \(A\).

Artinian duality identifies \(\omega_A\) with \(\operatorname{Hom}_k(A,k)\), up to a noncanonical isomorphism. Dualizing the quotient by \(g\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_k(\omega_A/g\omega_A,k)\simeq\operatorname{Ann}_A(g).
\]
The two endomorphisms given by multiplication by \(g\) on the finite-dimensional vector space \(A\) have equal kernel and cokernel lengths. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{length}(\omega_B/IB\omega_B)
=\operatorname{length}(\omega_A/g\omega_A)
=\operatorname{length}\operatorname{Ann}_A(g)
=\operatorname{length}(A/gA)
=\operatorname{length}(B/IB).
\]

There is no completeness gap: the final algebra \(A\) is Artinian and already complete; finite-length modules also agree with those over a local completion. Local invertible twists do not change length.

Since a finite subscheme of smooth \(C\) is determined by its pointwise lengths as an effective divisor, the following identities are valid:

\[
C\cap D=\operatorname{div}\mathcal T+\operatorname{div}\det\bar\rho,
\]
\[
I_{C\cap D,C}=\operatorname{Fitt}_0(\mathcal Q)
=\operatorname{Fitt}_0(\mathcal T)\,(\det\bar\rho).
\]

The final product equality can be seen from the divisor lengths over a DVR; it does not require an unwarranted general multiplicativity statement for Fitting ideals in arbitrary exact sequences.

## 4. Independent local negative control

\[
L=(x^2,xy+ty^2),\quad
J=L+(y^3),\quad K=(x+ty,t^2)
\]

in \(k[[t,x,y]]\). Put \(h=x+ty\). Then
\[
L=(hy,h^2+t^2y^2).
\]
For lexicographic order \(h>t>y\), a monic Gröbner basis is
\[
hy,\quad h^2+t^2y^2,\quad t^2y^3.
\]
It gives \(\operatorname{Ann}_{R/L}(y^3)=(h,t^2)=K\), hence \(J/L\simeq R/K\). Since \(y^3\) is regular on \(R/K\), one obtains \(J\cap K=L\). The quotient \(R/J\) is free over \(k[[t]]\) on \(1,x,y,y^2\), so \(J\) is the full marked primary component.

The displayed Hilbert--Burch matrix has minors \((-x^2,xy+ty^2,-y^3)\), up to sign. Reducing the syzygies modulo \((x,y)\) gives
\[
\mathcal E\simeq k[[t]]^2\oplus k[[t]]/(t^2).
\]
The actual two equations give the free basis vectors, so the torsion-free determinant is a unit, while \(\mathcal Q\simeq k[[t]]/(t^2)\). The determinant-only residual detector therefore fails exactly as claimed. These identities use no division by a characteristic-dependent integer.

## 5. Full attachment versus reduced-marked attachment

The additional combined identity requested during review is also correct. Let \(H=T\cap D\) be the full finite attachment scheme. Since \(J\subset I\), there is an exact sequence

\[
0\to I\mathcal O_H\to\mathcal O_H\to\mathcal O_{C\cap D}\to0.
\]

Thus, with \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{nil}}=\operatorname{length}(I\mathcal O_H)\),
\[
\operatorname{length}H
=\operatorname{length}(C\cap D)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{nil}}.
\]
The ideal \(I\mathcal O_H\) is nilpotent because \(T\) is supported on \(C\). It should not be called the entire nilradical: \(C\cap D\) can itself be nonreduced in the direction along \(C\).

Writing \(s=a+b-4\), linkage gives
\[
0\to\omega_T(-s)\to\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal O_D\to0.
\]
Use also the scheme-union exact sequence
\[
0\to\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal O_T\oplus\mathcal O_D\to\mathcal O_H\to0.
\]
Duality and the Hilbert polynomial of \(\omega_T\) yield
\[
\chi(\omega_T(-s))=-\chi(\mathcal O_T)-s\deg T,
\]
and hence
\[
\operatorname{length}H=s\deg T-2p_a(T)+2.
\]
Combining this with the audited module identity proves
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{length}\mathcal T+
\deg(\det\bar\rho)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{nil}}
=(a+b-4)\deg T-2p_a(T)+2.
\ }
\]
This calculation uses standard liaison, so it does not depend on transferring a theorem about reduced curves to a nonreduced primary component.

The local control separates the terms concretely:
\[
\mathcal O_H\simeq k[[t,y]]/(t^2,y^3),\quad
\operatorname{length}H=6,\quad
\operatorname{length}(C\cap D)=2,\quad
\epsilon_{\mathrm{nil}}=4.
\]
Thus a literature formula for \(T\cap D\) must not be identified with the reduced-marked attachment divisor \(C\cap D\).

## 6. Literature, comparison and scope

Primary references freshly inspected:

- [Hartshorne, Experiments with Gorenstein Liaison, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0106250v1), for the linked-ideal Hom formulation and usual locally CM curve setting.
- [Stacks, Fitting ideals, §15.8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07Z6), especially presentation independence, base change and the support of \(\operatorname{Fitt}_0\).
- [Stacks, finite-length duality, Lemma 47.16.4](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A7Q) and [Matlis duality, Proposition 47.7.8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08Z9). The Artinian specialization and the explicit Ext reduction above avoid any unspoken complete-ring assumption.

The [one-step boundary note](2026-10-27-one-step-boundary-obstruction.md), §5, uses a lower curvilinear thickening \(T\) and tests whether a larger actual intersection has greater generic length. Its determinant may vanish identically. Here \(T\) is the **full primary component** of the supplied actual intersection; generic equality forces a nonzero determinant, and its divisor plus module torsion measures attachment. Thus this is a complementary application, not a restatement of that contact test.

[R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md) concerns the filtered comparison module \(\Delta\). Its torsion defects are not identified with \(\mathcal T=\operatorname{tors}(I_T/I_CI_T)\) here. [R-0019](../results/quasiprimitive_residual_cycles.md) assumes a saturated quasiprimitive flag and a proper residual intersection on an auxiliary carrier; no assertion about its cycle being the full-primary attachment divisor is made.

Primary extraction can fail to commute with specialization. The result is fiberwise; neither the module nor either side of the divisor identity is asserted flat in an arbitrary family. It supplies no residual-absorption deformation, degree bound, or characteristic-zero STCI conclusion by itself. It does not identify attachment with sums of individual branch contacts, with a total-curve conductor, or with vertical exceptional components.
