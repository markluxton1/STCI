# Residual curves recover the equations: liaison and the correct deformation space

Date: 2026-10-28. Branch: `codex/intersections-containing-quartic-2026-10-27`.
Status: **provisional contribution, not canonically promoted**. This note
applies classical linkage and a literature theorem to the general
containing-intersection question. The [independent audit](2026-10-28-residual-liaison-audit.md)
includes the relative-family and Artinian recovery addenda. The unrestricted
characteristic-zero STCI question remains open.

## 1. Question, inputs, and the new point

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Fix

\[
C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3,
\qquad V_d=H^0(\mathcal I_C(d)).
\]

Let \(F_a,G_b\in I_C\) have no common surface component, with
\(4\le a\le b\), and set

\[
Y=V(F_a,G_b),\qquad I_D=(F_a,G_b):I_C.
\]

Here \(D\) is the **once-linked curve**, whether or not the full intersection
is reduced along \(C\). If the generic multiplicity of \(Y\) on \(C\) is
larger than one, \(D\) retains thickness on \(C\). It is then different from
the residual after removing the full \(C\)-primary component.

Retrieval used [INDEX](../INDEX.md), [MAP](../MAP.md), and the
[contribution protocol](../CONTRIBUTING_AGENT.md), followed by:

- [R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md):
  \(N_{C/\mathbf P^3}=\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(7)^2\).
- [The all-degree containing-intersection note](2026-10-27-global-containing-intersections.md),
  §§2 and 4, and its [audit](2026-10-27-global-containing-intersections-audit.md):
  degree, genus, the Rao module, and generic smooth residuals are already
  established in reviewed provisional work. They are premises here, not new
  discoveries.
- [The absorption synthesis](2026-10-27-arbitrary-degree-absorption-synthesis.md)
  and [attachment module](2026-10-27-absorption-attachment-module.md): full
  primary extraction must be distinguished from linkage by the fixed
  reduced \(C\).

The additional conclusion is that the residual remembers the equations
very rigidly, even though it can have many geometric deformations. Its
minimal resolution gives a precise way to distinguish deformations that
preserve the construction from deformations in the ambient Hilbert scheme.
In particular, a degree-five carrier creates an explicit exceptional
deformation direction; it is not a reason to favor or exclude quintics.

## 2. The residual uniquely remembers the complete intersection

Put \(A=a+b\). Standard complete-intersection linkage gives the sheaf exact
sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal I_Y(n)
\longrightarrow\mathcal I_D(n)
\longrightarrow\omega_C(n+4-A)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1}
\]

The Koszul resolution of \(Y\) gives \(H^1(\mathcal I_Y(n))=0\) for every
integer \(n\). Since \(\omega_C=\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(-2)\),

\[
H^0(\omega_C(n+4-A))
=H^0(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(4n+14-4A))=0
\quad\text{for }n\le A-4.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{(I_D)_n=(I_Y)_n\quad(n\le a+b-4).}\tag{2}
\]

Both defining degrees lie in this range because \(a,b\ge4\). Thus the ideal
generated by the pieces of \(I_D\) in degrees at most \(b\) is exactly
\((F_a,G_b)\). This recovers \(Y\) from \(D\), and double linkage then
recovers the marked partner \(C\).

For \(a=b=d\), the entire degree-\(d\) equation space of \(D\) is exactly
the original pencil. For \(a<b\), the degree-\(a\) equation is unique up to
scalar, while the degree-\(b\) equation is unique modulo its multiples.
This removes precisely the familiar redundancy
\(G\mapsto G+HF\).

The conclusion applies to the **embedded** residual, not merely its
abstract isomorphism class, normalization, degree, genus, or Rao module.
It also explains why unrestricted residual deformation is too large a
construction space: losing one of these low-degree equations destroys the
particular linkage presentation.

In families that keep \(C\) fixed, (1) and the vanishing above persist over
an Artinian base. Thus recovery also proves injectivity on tangent vectors:
an infinitesimal change in the pencil cannot be invisible in the embedded
residual. The dimension counts below therefore count actual embedded
residual freedom, rather than redundant choices of generators.

### 2.1. The once-linked curve is flat through multiplicity jumps

There is a particularly useful family statement, with no assumption that
\(C\) and its residual have disjoint components. Let \(T\) be a locally
Noetherian base, and let \(Y_T\subset\mathbf P^3_T\) be a family of
degree-\((a,b)\) complete intersections defined locally on \(T\) by
equations containing the constant family \(C_T=C\times T\). Assume that
every geometric fiber is a proper intersection, equivalently the defining
equations have no common surface component on that fiber. Define

\[
\mathcal I_{D_T}=(\mathcal I_{Y_T}:\mathcal I_{C_T}).
\]

Then \(D_T\) is flat over \(T\), its fibers are locally Cohen–Macaulay,
and its formation commutes with every locally Noetherian base change.
In particular its fiber at \(t\) is the once-linked curve
\((F_t,G_t):I_C\), including when that fiber shares support with \(C\).

Here are the base-change details. Fiberwise properness and the relative
Koszul complex make \(Y_T\) a flat relative complete intersection. Its
relative dualizing sheaf is \(\omega_{Y_T/T}=\mathcal O_{Y_T}(A-4)\),
locally after trivializing the equation line bundles on the parameter
space. Both \(C_T\) and \(Y_T\) are regular immersions of codimension
two in \(\mathbf P^3_T\). Change of rings for their Koszul complexes
therefore identifies

\[
\mathcal Hom_{Y_T}(\mathcal O_{C_T},\mathcal O_{Y_T})
\simeq\omega_{C_T/T}\otimes
\omega_{Y_T/T}^{-1}|_{C_T}.
\tag{2a}
\]

The higher relative Hom sheaves here vanish: ambient Koszul duality is
concentrated in codimension two, and the two equal codimensions cancel.
Thus (2a), including its natural map to \(\mathcal O_{Y_T}\), commutes
with base change. Its source is a line bundle on the flat \(C_T\), so it
is flat over \(T\). Evaluation at one identifies it with the annihilator
of \(\mathcal I_{C_T/Y_T}\) inside \(\mathcal O_{Y_T}\). Hence there is
an exact sequence

\[
0\to \omega_{C_T/T}(4-A)
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{Y_T}
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{D_T}\to0.
\tag{2b}
\]

For every geometric fiber, the first arrow is injective: it is still the
evaluation map \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal O_C,\mathcal O_Y)\to
\mathcal O_Y\). The local flatness criterion applied to (2b), whose first
two terms are \(T\)-flat, makes the quotient \(T\)-flat. Pulling (2b)
back therefore remains exact. Repeating (2a) after the base change
identifies its quotient with the new colon ideal. Classical fiberwise
linkage gives the locally Cohen–Macaulay assertion. These arguments also
work over an Artinian base with nilpotents; they are not an inference from
equality of numerical Hilbert polynomials alone.

The duality facts are standard; for the general relative-dualizing-sheaf
flatness and base-change framework see
[Stacks, §109.19](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0E6N).
The application here also has the elementary local description
\(I_C=(u,v)\), \(F=\alpha u+\beta v\),
\(G=\gamma u+\delta v\), with linked ideal
\((F,G,\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma)\), whose Hilbert–Burch presentation
is compatible with the same base changes.

It follows that the fixed polynomial of the once-linked curve is a
construction space **through absorption**, not just on the multiplicity-one
open locus. If a fiber has cycle \([Y_t]=m_t[C]+R_t\), then

\[
\boxed{[D_t]=(m_t-1)[C]+R_t.}\tag{2c}
\]

In the normal two-dimensional regular local ring at \(\eta_C\), this
subtracts one from the colength of a zero-dimensional Gorenstein complete
intersection: the annihilator of the maximal ideal is its
one-dimensional socle. At every other curve generic point, linkage to
\(C\) changes nothing. This proves (2c). The degree and genus of \(D_t\)
stay fixed while its \(C\)-supported part grows and genuine residual
degree decreases. Extracting the **full** \(C\)-primary component is a
different operation and need not commute with specialization. Its
nonflatness does not obstruct the flat family (2b).

## 3. The minimal resolution in every such bidegree

Write \(S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\). The linked ideal has the minimal resolution

\[
\boxed{
0\to S(-(A-1))
\to S(-(A-2))^5
\to S(-a)\oplus S(-b)\oplus S(-(A-3))^3
\to I_D\to0.
}\tag{3}
\]

This holds for every proper pair in the stated degree range. No generic
smoothness or reducedness hypothesis on \(D\) is used.

Here is a reconstruction that also fixes the shifts. Let

\[
K_C=\bigoplus_n H^0(\omega_C(n)).
\]

It is generated in degree one by \(s^2,st,t^2\), with presentation matrix

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
x_1&0&x_2&x_3&0\\
-x_0&x_1&0&-x_2&x_3\\
0&-x_0&-x_1&0&-x_2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The relations among these five columns are the two columns of

\[
N=\begin{pmatrix}
x_3&x_2^2\\
-x_2&-x_1x_3\\
0&x_0x_3-x_1x_2\\
-x_1&-x_0x_2\\
x_0&x_1^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The matrices satisfy \(MN=0\). The rank-drop locus of \(N\) is \(C\): on
\(x_0\ne0\), proportionality of its columns gives
\(x_0x_3=x_1x_2\) and \(x_0^2x_2=x_1^3\); the \(x_3\ne0\) chart is
symmetric; if \(x_0=x_3=0\), proportionality forces \(x_1=x_2=0\).
Thus the maximal minors of \(N\) have height two. The usual rank-and-grade
exactness criterion, together with the nonzero maximal minors of \(M\),
gives an exact complex

\[
0\to S(-3)\oplus S(-4)\xrightarrow{N}
S(-2)^5\xrightarrow{M}S(-1)^3\to K_C\to0.
\tag{4}
\]

To identify its cokernel with \(K_C\), the indicated monomials give a
surjection: their products with the coordinate monomials span all binary
forms of degree \(4n-2\). The Hilbert numerator of (4) is
\(3t-5t^2+t^3+t^4\), yielding \(h^0(\omega_C(n))=4n-1\) for \(n\ge1\).
The surjection has equal Hilbert functions, hence is an isomorphism.

Taking all sections in (1) gives

\[
0\to I_Y\to I_D\to K_C(4-A)\to0.
\]

Combine (4) with the Koszul resolution of \(I_Y\). A horseshoe resolution
has one common summand \(S(-A)\) in its last two terms. It cancels: by
graded local duality, the last free term in the **minimal** resolution of
the saturated curve ideal is determined by the minimal generators of
\(M_D^\vee\), where the already known Rao module \(M_D\) is \(k\) in
degree \(A-5\). There is exactly one last generator, in degree \(A-1\).
After cancelling \(S(-A)\), the resolution is (3). All remaining maps have
strictly positive degree, since \(a,b\ge4\), so it is minimal.

The only genuinely new finite algebra check here is reproduced in
[the Macaulay2 script](../computations/verify_residual_liaison_resolution_2026_10_28.m2).
It verifies the matrices over \(\mathbf Q\) and checks the predicted linked
resolution on five deterministic proper pairs over \(\mathbf F_{32003}\).
The finite examples do not prove the all-degree statement; (1), (4), and
local duality supply that proof. They assert no smoothness or
characteristic-zero lifting of the sampled residuals.
The [saved PASS output](../computations/residual_liaison_resolution_2026_10_28.txt)
records the new run, including the residual degrees and genera.

## 4. Literature gives a precise deformation interpretation

A focused primary-source search on 2026-10-28 located Jan Oddvar Kleppe,
[*The Hilbert scheme of space curves of small diameter* (2006)](https://www.numdam.org/article/AIF_2006__56_5_1297_0.pdf).
Theorem 1.3 characterizes Hilbert-scheme obstructions for a diameter-one
Rao module by three products of graded Betti numbers. Proposition 1.4
compares the dimension of the fixed-postulation stratum with the full
Hilbert scheme. These are existing theorems, not new mechanisms discovered
here. The source uses algebraically closed characteristic zero and locally
Cohen–Macaulay curves, exactly the setting in which it is used below.

Set \(c=A-5\). In (3),

\[
\beta_{1,c+4}=\beta_{2,c+4}=\beta_{2,c}=0,\qquad
\beta_{1,c}=\mathbf1_{a=5}+\mathbf1_{b=5}.
\]

All three obstruction products vanish. Hence:

**Deformation deduction.** Every once-linked residual in (3) is a smooth
point of its Hilbert scheme. Its fixed-postulation stratum is also smooth,
and has codimension

\[
\boxed{\mathbf1_{a=5}+\mathbf1_{b=5}}
\tag{5}
\]

in that Hilbert scheme. This concerns deformations of the residual in
\(\mathbf P^3\); it does not assert smoothness of an absorption stratum,
flatness of full-primary extraction, or preservation of the fixed partner.

An explicit local dimension is useful for checking what space is being
used. Define

\[
s_n=\begin{cases}\binom{n+3}{3}&n\ge0,\\0&n<0,\end{cases}
\qquad v_n=h^0(\mathcal I_C(n)).
\]

Here \(v_n=0\) for \(n\le1\), and
\(v_n=s_n-4n-1\) for \(n\ge2\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\dim_{[D]}\operatorname{Hilb}
=4(ab-4)+v_{a-4}+v_{b-4}-s_{b-a-4}.
}\tag{6}
\]

For completeness, the application of the dimension theorem can be checked
directly. Duality in (1) gives

\[
h^1(\mathcal O_D(n))
=h^0((\mathcal I_C/\mathcal I_Y)(A-4-n)).
\]

At the three additional generator degrees \(A-3\), and at all syzygy
degrees in (3), this group is zero. Therefore
\(\operatorname{Hom}_S(I_D,H^1_*(\mathcal O_D))_0\) has dimension
\(h^1(\mathcal O_D(a))+h^1(\mathcal O_D(b))\), namely
\(v_{b-4}-s_{b-a-4}+v_{a-4}\). Substitution in the cited theorem gives
(6). This also allows \(b\) to be much larger than \(a\); the subtraction
of multiples of \(F_a\) is essential in that range.

## 5. A construction space inside the residual Hilbert scheme

For fixed \(C\), distinct proper complete intersections of type \((a,b)\)
form a smooth parameter space of dimension

\[
L_{a,b}=\begin{cases}
2(v_a-2),&a=b,\\
v_a+v_b-s_{b-a}-2,&a<b.
\end{cases}\tag{7}
\]

For unequal degrees, this is the projective bundle of the second equation
modulo multiples of the first. For equal degrees it is the proper open
subset of \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_a)\).

Allow the linked rational quartic to move in its smooth Hilbert
neighborhood. Its dimension is sixteen by R-0020, and the equation spaces
in degrees at least four form vector bundles because \(H^1(\mathcal
I_C(a))=H^1(\mathcal I_C(b))=0\). The family of pairs and their linked
residuals thus has dimension \(L_{a,b}+16\). Equation recovery in §2 makes
this a family of distinct embedded residuals. Algebraically, (5)–(7) give

\[
\dim_{[D]}\operatorname{Hilb}
-(L_{a,b}+16)=\mathbf1_{a=5}+\mathbf1_{b=5}.
\tag{8}
\]

The fixed-postulation neighborhood is the natural incidence-preserving
space. More precisely, take the local constant-postulation Hilbert
functor \(H_\gamma\) of the residual, and restrict to the open neighborhood
where fibers are locally Cohen–Macaulay and the recovered low equations
remain a proper regular sequence. On this functor the requisite spaces
of sections and their quotients are locally free and commute with base
change: this is the fixed-postulation condition, including over Artinian
bases, not a consequence of a numerical equality just on closed points.
For equal degrees the rank-two degree-\(a\) bundle reconstructs \(Y\).
For unequal degrees its degree-\(a\) line and the quotient line in
degree \(b\) reconstruct \(Y\) uniquely.

Relative linkage then reconstructs a flat degree-four genus-zero family
\(C'\). In this reverse direction the residual need not be lci, but its
flat locally Cohen–Macaulay fibers have a relative dualizing sheaf flat
over the base and commuting with base change (the framework cited in
§2.1). Its evaluation exact sequence gives flatness of the partner in
exactly the same way as (2b). Relative Cohen–Macaulay biduality makes the
link involutive. At the original point the recovered partner is the
smooth \(C\); smoothness remains open and also holds for its Artinian
deformations. Shrinking the neighborhood yields the stated equivalence
with incidence data for a moving smooth rational quartic, without
asserting that all Hilbert deformations preserve postulation.

Requiring that reconstructed quartic to be **this fixed embedded \(C\)**
cuts out the fiber of the sixteen-dimensional partner map. A smooth
rational quartic with the same Rao module is not automatically the fixed
marked curve.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \((4,4)\) | \(12,16\) | 32 | 48 | 48 |
| \((4,5)\) | \(16,30\) | 47 | 63 | 64 |

| \((5,6)\) | \(26,77\) | 88 | 104 | 105 |
| \((6,6)\) | \(32,112\) | 114 | 130 | 130 |

The degree-five discrepancy is a useful falsification control. A general
Hilbert deformation of a quintic-linked residual can lose a required
degree-five equation. Such a deformation is legitimate curve geometry,
but it cannot guide the original construction without retaining the
equation/partner incidence. In all degrees, the sixteen partner motions
must also be excluded when \(C\) is fixed.

## 6. What changes in our interpretation, and a next test

Residual geometry is an effective construction parameter, not just a
degree remainder. In the multiplicity-one open locus, its low equations
recover both hypersurfaces, while its normalization, attachment, and
singularities can vary. The positive-dimensional attachment fibers from
October 27 therefore contain distinct embedded residual curves; their
freedom is not exhausted by choices of equation generators.

The useful moduli problem is a flag: **residual curve, its recovered
complete intersection, and its marked linked partner**. Full residual
Hilbert deformations, constant-postulation deformations, and deformations
with fixed partner are different spaces. Equations (5)–(8) make the
difference explicit instead of treating all residual motions as available
for constructing a pair containing \(C\).

A bounded next experiment is to work inside a fixed-partner,
fixed-attachment fiber and produce an explicit residual node or splitting
deformation there, using its low equation space to recover the actual
forms throughout. The chosen degrees should test a proposed geometric
mechanism, not define a degree-by-degree agenda. The quintic case supplies
a particularly useful control: the two transverse postulation-changing
directions must be distinguished from incidence-preserving directions.
For absorption, retain the once-linked curve's fixed Hilbert polynomial
and allow it to acquire a \(C\)-supported part; separately recompute the
full primary component and genuine residual curve. Section 2.1 proves that
this is an actual flat, base-change-compatible construction space for
every proper family of pairs, even when generic multiplicity jumps.

No argument here decides whether a higher-degree family can reach sole
support. It supplies an explicit construction coordinate system and a
guard against following residual deformations that have forgotten the
two hypersurfaces or the particular embedded quartic.
