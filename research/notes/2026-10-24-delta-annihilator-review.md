# Independent review: the conormal entry ideal annihilates Delta

Date: 2026-10-24. Reviewer: `audit_annihilator`, independently reconstructing the argument assigned by the primary agent and cross-checking it with `annihilator_probe`.

**Status:** independently reconstructed proof within the scope below; provisional research evidence, not a canonical promotion or a human referee report.

## 1. Exact statement reviewed

Let (R) be a noetherian local ring, (I=(x,y)) an ideal generated by a regular sequence, and (J\subset I). Put

\[
B=R/I,\qquad S=\operatorname{gr}_I R=B[X,Y],\qquad
H=\operatorname{in}_I(J),\qquad A=S/H,
\]
\[
D=J/IJ,\quad E=I/I^2,\quad
\phi:D\longrightarrow E,\quad
\Delta=\operatorname{in}_I(IJ)/(S_+H).
\]

Define the entry ideal intrinsically by

\[
\mathfrak s=\operatorname{im}\bigl(D\otimes_B E^\vee
\xrightarrow{\phi\otimes1} E\otimes_B E^\vee
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{evaluation}} B\bigr).
\]

The reviewed claim is

\[
\boxed{\mathfrak s\Delta=0.}
\]

There is no assumption that (J) is a complete intersection, that (R/J) is flat over a parameter ring, or that (\sqrt J=I). The regular-sequence hypothesis on (I), including the identification (S=B[X,Y]), is retained. In the geometric application (C\hookrightarrow P) is a smooth codimension-two curve embedding, and the actual carrier ideal (J=(f,g)) is a complete intersection; the statement therefore applies in particular to the requested embedding problem with additional support allowed.

## 2. Independent elementary reconstruction

The canonical identification

\[
Q=H/S_+H\simeq\operatorname{Tor}_1^S(A,B)
=H_1(K_A(X,Y))
\]

sends the class of an initial equation (h_n=X\bar c+Y\bar d\in H_n) to the normal Koszul cycle (z=(\bar c,\bar d)). This is the connecting homomorphism for (0\to H\to S\to A\to0); it fixes the representative and its direction, rather than using an arbitrary isomorphism of modules.

Take (q\in\Delta_n\subset Q_n). For (n=1), (\Delta_1=0), since (IJ\subset I^2). For (n\ge2), choose

\[
h\in IJ\cap I^n,\qquad
h=xc+yd=xu+yv,
\]

where (c,d\in I^{n-1}) and (u,v\in J). Such choices exist because (I^n=xI^{n-1}+yI^{n-1}) and (IJ=xJ+yJ). The regular-sequence syzygy of (x,y) gives (w\in R) with

\[
c-u=-yw,\qquad d-v=xw.
\]

Consequently, in (M=R/J), the actual normal Koszul cycle ((c,d)) is the boundary ((-yw,xw)).

For any (f\in J), write (f=xa'+yb'), and let (a,b\in B) be the reductions of (a',b'). Thus (\phi([f])=aX+bY). The element ((a',b')) is a cycle in (K_M(x,y)), and its wedge with the displayed boundary is zero **as an actual chain**:

\[
a'd-b'c=w(xa'+yb')=wf=0\quad\text{in }M.
\]

Taking its homogeneous part of normal degree (n-1) gives

\[
a\bar d-b\bar c=0\quad\text{in }A.
\]

No assertion about the order of (w) is needed. The equality takes place in (M) before passing to the associated graded algebra.

The degree-one Koszul cycle (v_f=(a,b)) represents (\operatorname{in}_1(f)) in (Q_1). The determinant equality implies identities of cycles

\[
az=\bar c\,v_f,\qquad bz=\bar d\,v_f.
\]

Both (\bar c,\bar d\in A_{n-1}\subset A_+). Multiplication by (X) and (Y) is null-homotopic on the Koszul complex, so (A_+) annihilates its homology. It follows that (aq=bq=0) in (Q_n). Letting (f) range through (J) proves (\mathfrak s\Delta=0).

This proof does not need convergence of a filtered spectral sequence, a completion, a coefficient field, or a division by an integer. It works in every characteristic.

## 3. Checks against possible gaps

- **The class being annihilated is the actual defect.** Its representative is chosen in (IJ\cap I^n), which is the full initial ideal numerator; no chosen list of initials replaces it.
- **The quotient direction is preserved.** The argument takes (\Delta\) as a submodule of (Q\), not as a quotient of (\operatorname{gr}_F D).
- **No strictness of a chosen presentation is used.** The expressions for (h) are available for every element of (IJ\cap I^n).
- **The wedge vanishes at chain level.** This avoids the additional bookkeeping needed to show that products of accumulated incoming spectral-sequence boundaries lift to zero on the first page.
- **Positive normal degree is essential.** For (n\ge2), the coefficient classes have degree (n-1>0), so the Koszul homotopies apply. The excluded degree-one case vanishes directly.
- **No carrier or support reduction is hidden.** The syzygy used is that of the normal parameters (x,y), not that of (f,g). The argument applies without a sole-support hypothesis.
- **The codimension-two hypothesis is substantive.** The determinant calculation uses that degree two is the top term of this normal Koszul complex. This review certifies no higher-codimension generalization.

## 4. Intrinsic and geometric consequences

The definition of (\mathfrak s) is independent of coordinates and of generators of (J). Because (E) is locally free of rank two,

\[
\mathfrak s=\operatorname{Fitt}_1(\operatorname{coker}\phi)
=\operatorname{Fitt}_1(N^*_{C/Z}).
\]

Indeed a finite free module surjecting onto (D) provides a presentation of (\operatorname{coker}\phi) with two target generators; its (1\times1) minors are exactly the entries of (\phi). The local annihilator theorem therefore sheafifies. It refines the predecessor's support containment to a scheme-theoretic annihilation statement along the rank-zero subscheme of the conormal map.

At a DVR point with (\mathfrak s=(t^r)\ne0), every invariant factor of (\Delta_n) has exponent at most (r). In particular

\[
\operatorname{length}_B\Delta_n
\le r\,\mu_B(\Delta_n).
\]

The already reviewed punctual family in [the predecessor, section 5](2026-10-24-delta-singular-locus-continuation.md) has (\phi=\operatorname{diag}(t^r,t^r)) and (\Delta_d=B/(t^r)). It witnesses sharpness; that inherited computation was not replayed here. If (\mathfrak s=0), the new theorem supplies no numerical bound, as expected for a generically singular pair.
