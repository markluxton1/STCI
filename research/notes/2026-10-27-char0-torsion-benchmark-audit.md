# Audit of a characteristic-zero torsion benchmark and a pinching limitation

Date: 2026-10-27. Author: targeted independent literature/audit agent under
the characteristic-conductor lane. Status: explanatory verification of
classical examples and a scoped elementary pinching observation; no novelty
claim, canonical promotion, or fixed-quartic STCI conclusion.

## 1. A known characteristic-zero construction beyond Frobenius

This section concerns the **twisted cubic**, not the fixed rational quartic:

\[
T=[s^3:s^2t:st^2:t^3]\subset\mathbf P^3.
\]

The classical equations are

\[
Q=x_0x_2-x_1^2,\qquad
G=x_2^3-2x_1x_2x_3+x_0x_3^2.
\]

The exact pair appears in [Oprea's original UCSD problem set, problem
4(iv)](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~doprea/203f22/203hwk4F22.pdf).
It was verified through indexed text from that primary course PDF;
direct PDF retrieval of the associated 2018 solution returned HTTP 502.
[Murayama's author-hosted local-cohomology notes, Example 1](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~murayama/LocalCohomology.pdf)
give equivalent equations after permuting variables and cite Hartshorne's
exercise I.2.17. Its indexed primary text was inspected; a direct PDF retry
returned HTTP 403. Thus this is a classical benchmark, not a new example.

The compact algebraic verification also exposes the mechanism. Put
A=x0*x3-x1*x2 and B=x1*x3-x2^2. Then

\[
x_0G=A^2+x_2^2Q,\qquad
x_2G=B^2+x_3^2Q.
\]

Hence Q=G=0 forces A=B=0 over an algebraically closed field, while all
three quadrics Q,A,B vanish on T. On x0=1 the quotient equations are
x2=x1^2 and (x3-x1^3)^2=0; on x0=0 their reduced support is the single
endpoint [0:0:0:1]. The proper degree-six complete intersection therefore
has cycle 2[T] and reduced support T.

The cross term with coefficient 2 is retained in characteristic zero.
The construction arranges a global square through correction by the
carrier equation; it does not require Frobenius to delete mixed terms.

## 2. Divisor-class interpretation and its exact boundary

The normal quadric cone Q has class group Z generated by a ruling class
ell; its hyperplane class is 2*ell and T has class 3*ell. Thus 2[T]=3[H].
The class of T has local Cartier index two at the vertex. This is Weil
class torsion modulo H, not torsion of a globally Cartier curve class.
Locally the cone has the familiar characteristic-zero quotient form
k[a^2,ab,b^2] inside k[a,b], with the sign involution. Its finite quotient
and index account for the possible double structure without inseparable
sheet coalescence.

The independent primary reference [Hartshorne--Polini, Proposition
7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1301.3222) identifies fixed-carrier STCI with
positive APic proportionality when the curve meets the carrier's singular
locus finitely. Their Lemma 7.6 also says Pic(X)/Z*H is torsion-free for
surfaces X in P3 in characteristic zero. Thus any torsion-based alternative
here must retain non-Cartier Weil/almost-Cartier or conductor information.

That full primary HTML and its PDF opened successfully in this audit.
The quadric-cone statement above can also be reconstructed on its Hirzebruch
resolution: the negative section contracts, H is the sum of that section
and twice a fiber, and quotienting the divisor group by the contracted
section gives H=2*ell. The degree-three curve has class 3*ell.

This example proves that a characteristic-zero sole-support construction
can use singular-carrier divisor classes and explicit polynomial correction.
It gives no carrier or defining pair for C0; the known low-index and
low-degree boundaries for C0 still apply literally.

## 3. Why an unramified nodal pinching is not automatically suitable

Consider a projective nodal integral rational curve E0, with normalization
P1 identifying two distinct points a,b, and the abstract projective surface
X=E0 times P1. Its normalization is S=P1 times P1. The two disjoint
conductor curves Gamma_a={a} times P1 and Gamma_b={b} times P1 are
identified pointwise. Every point of their image has two distinct
normalization preimages.

Suppose c is a smooth ample Cartier curve on S, its image C is smooth,
the map c->C is an isomorphism, and the **full reduced inverse image** of
C is exactly c. These conditions cannot all hold. Ampleness makes c meet
Gamma_a. Choose q in that intersection, and let q' on Gamma_b be its
distinct partner. Full inverse support puts q' in c as well. The map
c->C then identifies q and q', contradicting its assumed isomorphism.

This is an exact degree-independent failure of a naive gluing recipe.
One may obtain torsion phases by identifying reduced conductor branches,
but that alone does not preserve the smooth marked curve and its required
full inverse support. The example is an abstract pinched surface, with no
assertion of an embedding as a hypersurface in P3.

The corresponding established primary results are
[Hartshorne--Polini, Proposition 7.3 and Corollary 7.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1301.3222):
for a smooth STCI curve at a singular point, the normalization has one
point over it; on an ordinary surface the curve can meet the singular
locus only at pinch points. Example 7.12 constructs twisted cubics on a
ruled cubic with 2C a complete intersection. These results already record
the needed ramification qualification; it is not a new exclusion here.

## 4. Use in the characteristic-conductor lane

The useful positive contrast is a **corrected global pure power** on a
singular carrier with non-Cartier divisor-class torsion. The useful
negative control is that prescribing local phase torsion or an abstract
pinching does not supply a smooth fixed curve, full inverse support, or
an admissible P3 hypersurface. The exact missing bridge for C0 is the
construction of such an actual carrier and homogeneous correction while
retaining those marked conditions. No accepted fixed-quartic proof was
rerun, and no source or navigation record was edited.
