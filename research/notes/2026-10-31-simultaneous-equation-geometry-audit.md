## Independently reconstructed argument

Work over the stated algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. The
quadric \(Q=V(x_0x_3-x_1x_2)\) is smooth and integral. Expanding the determinant
of (1) gives \(q^2\); the entries are homogeneous quadratics, so the displayed
map \(\mathcal O(-2)^2\to\mathcal O^2\) and its adjugate factorization have the
claimed twists.

On \(Q\), the rank-zero ideal is

\[
(x_1^2,x_1x_2,x_2^2)=(x_1,x_2)^2.
\]

Its reduced support consists of the two displayed points. On either affine
chart \(x_0=1\) or \(x_3=1\), the quadric equation eliminates the remaining
coordinate; \(x_1,x_2\) are regular parameters at the corresponding point.
The rank-zero scheme is consequently two length-three fat points, total
length six. The source correctly distinguishes this scheme from the reduced
two-point scheme \(T\). These are precisely the two marked endpoints of the
parametrized quartic.

Suppose \(M=AB\) through any rank-two vector bundle, with each determinant
divisor exactly the reduced \(Q\). At a point of \(Q\), rank zero of either
factor would put its determinant in the square of the ambient maximal ideal.
A unit times a local equation of the smooth divisor \(Q\) does not belong to
that square. Each restricted factor therefore has constant rank one,
including at the two special points. This argument is local and does not
assume that the intermediate bundle splits.

Thus \(L=\operatorname{im}(B|_Q)\) and \(K=\ker(A|_Q)\) are line subbundles.
The composite is the surjection onto \(L\), followed by

\[
L\longrightarrow E|_Q/K,
\]

followed by a line-subbundle inclusion into the target. Locally those outer
maps have unit entries. Hence the composite's rank-zero ideal is precisely
the zero ideal of this line-bundle morphism. On the integral smooth surface
\(Q\), a nonzero such morphism has empty zero scheme or an effective Cartier
divisor; the zero morphism vanishes everywhere. Neither possibility has
nonempty finite support. This proves the contradiction scheme-theoretically,
and also proves the stated invariance under source/target isomorphisms.

## Cokernel and quadric layers

The determinant is nonzero in the polynomial ring, so the displayed free
presentation is injective. Its graded cokernel has projective dimension one
and depth three; locally the same argument gives maximal Cohen–Macaulay
depth over \(q^2=0\). The long cohomology sequence gives vanishing of
\(H^1(\mathcal M(t))\) for every \(t\). Away from \(T\), an entry is a unit,
so elementary local row/column operations reduce the presentation to
\(\operatorname{diag}(1,\text{unit}\cdot q^2)\).
The cokernel is a line bundle there and generically free of rank one on
\(2Q\). At either point of \(T\), its residue-field fiber has dimension two,
which excludes local freeness of rank one.

The column relations give exactly (8). For the layer twists, the kernel of
\(M|_Q\) is generated by \((-x_2,x_1)\), giving \(\mathcal O_Q(-3)\): these
are the ordinary Koszul syzygies of the regular sequence \(x_1,x_2\).
Tensoring the resolution by \(\mathcal O_Q\) identifies that kernel with
\(\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal M}(q)(-2)\).
Under this identification its generator corresponds to
\(u=x_1e_1+x_2e_2\). This proves
\(\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal M}(q)=\mathcal O_Q(-1)\).
The relations \(qe_1=-x_2u,\ qe_2=x_1u\) then identify
\(q\mathcal M=\mathcal I_{T,Q}(-1)\). Quotienting by \(u\) gives
\(\mathcal I_{T,Q}(1)\), with quotient map \(e_1\mapsto x_2,\ e_2\mapsto-x_1\).
Thus (9)–(10), including all twists, are correct. Finally
\(H^1(Q,\mathcal I_{T,Q}(-1))=k^2\), from the ideal sequence of the reduced
two-point scheme and \(\mathcal O_Q(-1)=\mathcal O_Q(-1,-1)\).

Geometrically the ACM sheaf on the doubled surface contains a quadric layer
with a punctual ideal defect. Smoothness of the reduced support does not
remove that defect. A product of the stipulated two simple-determinant maps
would instead measure alignment of two line subbundles, whose vanishing is
divisorial. This is the precise obstruction; the finite nonfree locus and
the length-six rank-zero scheme are related, distinct objects.

## Literature and exact consequence

[Hartshorne, *Liaison with Cohen–Macaulay modules*, Appendix, pp. 430–431](https://seminariomatematico.polito.it/rendiconti/64-4/419.pdf)
was checked directly. It supplies the ACM/MCM correspondence and the
matrix-factorization background used by the note. It does not supply the
new counterexample or a simple-quadric product theorem; those are assessed
by the direct argument above. No classification theorem for the nonreduced
quadric is needed or inferred.

The reviewed claim refutes universal splitting of a determinant-\(q^N\)
matrix into \(N\) rank-two maps having determinant divisor \(Q\), already for
\(N=2\). It does not refute the ordinary adjugate matrix factorization of
\(q^N\), which exists here. Its first-column support is the union of the
lines \(V(x_1,x_0)\) and \(V(x_1,x_3)\), not the quartic. For an actual
supported pair, a unit in the distinguished first column makes the
cokernel locally free on \(V(q^N)\), so the nonfree locus is confined to
\(C\). This example satisfies containment of its nonfree locus in \(C\), but its
first column does not vanish along the interior of \(C\), so it does not
satisfy the actual column-support condition. Whether the
actual column-support and homogeneous conditions yield a stronger theorem
remains unresolved. No step changes the unrestricted characteristic-zero
STCI status. No characteristic extension is claimed by this audit.
