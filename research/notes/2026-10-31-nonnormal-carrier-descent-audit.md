## Findings

No material mathematical finding remains for the claims within the note's
stated scope. The following checks support that disposition.

1. **Normalization and index lemma.** Under the actual-mate hypothesis the
   mate does not vanish identically on the integral carrier. Its pullback to
   the normal surface is therefore a nonzero effective Cartier divisor. On a
   normal surface such a divisor has pure codimension-one support. Generic
   smoothness of the carrier along the smooth curve gives one degree-one
   inverse branch; its finite birational map to the normal curve is an
   isomorphism. Purity excludes extra isolated support points. Writing the
   pullback divisor as `mc ~ bH`, intersection with `H` gives `m=ab/d`; then
   `c^2=d^2/a` is well-defined because `mc` is Cartier. Let `r` be the global
   Cartier index of `c`, the least positive integer such that `rc` is
   Cartier; equivalently, it is the order of `[c]` in `Cl(X)/Pic(X)`, not in
   `Cl(X)`. Since `mc` is Cartier, `r|m`. Also `(rc).c=rd^2/a` is integral
   on the smooth curve `c`, so `a/gcd(a,d^2)|r`. For
   `(a,b,d)=(5,8,4)`, these give `m=10`, `c^2=16/5`, and `r∈{5,10}`.
   In particular the normalization is singular somewhere on `c`. This
   argument does not assume a Gorenstein normalization.

2. **Torsion and descent.** The octic gives `2(5c-4H)=0` upstairs, while
   the class may be zero. If it is zero, the resulting section with divisor
   `5c` has square proportional to the pulled-back octic, since the ratio of
   sections with equal divisors is a global unit on the integral proper
   normalization. That root cannot be an ambient quartic: if it descended to
   the quintic carrier, the hypersurface restriction sequence lifts it to an
   ambient quartic, contrary to R-0001. Its square does descend by the
   assumed actual mate. If the class has order two, the note correctly makes
   no ADE or resolution claim. R-0003's formula gives `4ktE` for mate
   degrees; degree eight and the R-0001 lower-degree factor-reduction
   consequence imply `ktE=2`, and rule out `tE=1, k=2`. Thus the stated
   alternatives `(t,E)=(1,2)` or `(t,E)=(2,1)` follow. This uses R-0001 in
   addition to R-0003.

3. **Exact local family.** For
   `A=k[a,y,z]/(ay^ell-z^(r*ell)) ⊂ B=k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^r)`, with
   `a=x^ell`, the inclusion is finite and birational; `B` is the normal
   `A_(r-1)` surface. In its monomial realization
   `x=u^r, y=v^r, z=uv`, the exponent semigroup for `B` is
   `p,q≥0, p≡q (mod r)`. The semigroup generated by
   `(r*ell,0), (0,r), (1,1)` gives exactly the membership test stated in
   the note. It yields `x^j∈A` iff `ell|j`, and the conductor consists
   exactly of the monomials with `q≥r(ell-1)`, namely `y^(ell-1)B`.
   For `1≤n<r`, the symbolic power of `c=(x,z)` is `(x,z^n)`, with two
   minimal generators at the origin; `rc=(x)` locally, so the index is
   `r`. Pulling back `(a,z^(r*ell))` gives reduced support `c`, with
   order `r*ell`. The stated conductor map and nonreduced inverse scheme
   are consistent with these calculations.

4. **Ordinary projective control and scope.** The homogenized equation in
   (7) is homogeneous of degree `r*ell`, primitive and linear in `a`, hence
   irreducible. Intersecting with the plane `a=0` gives the line `a=z=0`
   as its sole reduced support, and the chart `w=1` recovers the affine
   local model. The example establishes compatibility of arbitrary local
   index and power descent with an actual projective-space complete
   intersection; it does not produce the fixed quartic or a quintic carrier.
   The note preserves that limitation.

5. **Inherited A4 source and section-image criterion.** The cited structural
   audit's Gorenstein conclusion is conditional: for a hypothetical
   nonnormal quintic with a mate and Gorenstein normalization, the sectional
   genus is one, yielding the degree-five Gorenstein del Pezzo normalization
   with one A4 point, the interior contact, and an actual section
   `Q∈H^0(X,4H)` with divisor `5c`. It does not classify arbitrary
   non-Gorenstein normalizations or prove a mate exists. The integrated note
   accurately labels this as inherited and scoped. In the explicit A4 model
   `Q=T^5` on the complete affine open. For an actual basepoint-free finite
   birational projection defined by four sections `X_i`, an ambient octic
   pulls back to the span of degree-eight monomials in those sections.
   Equality with `Q^2=T^10` on the dense affine chart is equivalent to
   equality of the global sections. Its divisor is `10c`; finiteness and
   surjectivity then give sole support `C` downstairs. The note correctly
   keeps projection admissibility as a separate requirement and does not
   substitute a chart identity for it.

## Order-two cover check

In the exact setup of the integrated note, the reflexive algebra is already
normal; normalization is not needed. Let `L=O_X(U)`, with a chosen
isomorphism `L^[2]≅O_X`, and form

    A=O_X⊕L

using the induced reflexive multiplication. Since both summands are reflexive,
they are `S2` as `O_X`-modules. The nontrivial class remains nontrivial on the
regular locus of the normal surface, so the associated degree-two algebra
there is a connected finite étale cover. Its generic algebra is therefore a
field, making `A` a domain. The finite map `Spec(A)→X` is étale at every
codimension-one point, so `A` is regular in codimension one. The `S2` module
depth gives `S2` for this finite domain (local dimensions agree under the
integral extension), and Serre's criterion gives normality. Thus the
integrated note's claim of a connected normal quasi-étale double cover is
supported for this construction.

This cover conclusion still supplies no ADE classification, minimal
resolution type, or conductor descent for the root. It remains separate from
the unresolved degree-eight section-image question.

## Supplemental review: global descent in an integral hyperplane twist

This check covers §4's later ordinary-projective construction (7a)–(7d),
including its explicit example. It does not reopen the earlier audit findings
or promote the construction beyond the stated special family.

Let `d=ell*n`, `s=r-n-1`, and take the stated smooth irreducible plane curve
`G(X,Y,W)=0`, with `G` not divisible by `Y` or `W`. Then
`F=G Y^ell W^(ell*s)-Z^(r*ell)` is homogeneous of degree `r*ell`. As a
polynomial in `Z` over `k[X,Y,W]`, it is Eisenstein at the prime `G`: its
leading coefficient is a unit, every intermediate coefficient is zero, and
its constant coefficient is divisible by `G` but not `G^2`. Hence the carrier
is integral. Modulo `G`, the pair ideal is `(G,Z^(r*ell))`; its actual scheme
has sole reduced support `C=V(G,Z)`. The mate surface `V(G)` is smooth along
`C`, and `Z` cuts `C` simply there, so the restriction is the actual primitive
`r*ell`-fold Cartier thickening along the whole curve. The Fermat choice in
the note satisfies the required transversality: its restrictions to `Y=0`
and `W=0` have distinct roots and avoid their common point.

The rational section `q=Z^r/(Y W^s)` has degree `n` and satisfies
`q^ell=nu*G`. In each local frame of `H`, its coefficient satisfies a monic
equation with regular coefficient; it is therefore integral over the carrier
and regular on its normal finite normalization. This proves regularity even
at apparent denominator loci. The §2 purity argument supplies the full
reduced inverse `c` globally: normalization is an isomorphism at the generic
point of `C`, giving its unique divisorial inverse; the finite birational map
`c→C` is an isomorphism because `C` is normal; and the pullback of the
nonzero mate section is an effective Cartier divisor on the normal surface,
whose support is pure codimension one, so no isolated inverse points occur,
including over `W=0`.

At the generic point of `C`, `Y` and `W` are units and the equation gives
`G=unit*Z^(r*ell)`. Thus `div(nu*G)=r*ell*c` and `div(q)=r*c`; the latter
also makes `r*c` Cartier. At a transverse point of `C∩{Y=0}`, with `W=1`,
the local equation is exactly `a y^ell=z^(r*ell)` and `q=z^r/y`. Its
normalization is `x y=z^r`, with `x=q`; the local Cartier index is exactly
`r`, and `x^j` fails to belong to the downstairs local ring whenever
`ell` does not divide `j`. Consequently the global index is exactly `r`,
and `q^j` descends precisely when `ell|j`: divisible powers equal
`G^(j/ell)`, while nondivisible powers already fail locally. For
`(ell,n,r)=(2,1,5)`, this verifies square-only descent in an integral
hyperplane twist on a degree-ten carrier with smooth conic support and a
quadratic mate.

Finally, for forms of matching degrees,
`(G A^ell-F0^(r*ell),G)=(G,F0^(r*ell))`, so the radicals agree with
`sqrt((G,F0))`. This operation preserves all pre-existing residual
components and cannot convert a merely containing pair into a sole-support
pair. The construction is a global projective control for index and descent,
not a fixed-quartic or quintic-octic example; the original fixed embedding
and global section-image problem remain open.
