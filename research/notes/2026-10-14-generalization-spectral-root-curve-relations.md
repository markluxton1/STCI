# Root-curve descent and the geometry of moving binary relations

Date: 2026-10-14. Status: **new structural theorem, independently reviewed**.
This note generalizes the relation-bundle viewpoint, rather
than enumerating further carrier degrees. The accepted moving-power
second-relation obstruction and its independent audit are premises. No
accepted calculation is repeated.

## 1. The root divisor remembers the entire first binary relation

Let `C` be a smooth integral projective curve, and let the actual conormal
bundle in a smooth threefold be

    E=H^-1 tensor V,             dim V=2,

where `V` is constant. Suppose the first saturated generic initial relation
is a line subbundle

    R=H^-r tensor M subset Sym^r E,        r>=2.

Thus `M subset Sym^r V tensor O_C` is a line subbundle. Fixing the constant
determinant factors does not affect the following statements. Put
`P=C x P(V^*)`, with projections `p` and `q`. The universal binary form
defines a divisor

    Z=V(f) subset P,
    O_P(Z)=p^*M^-1 tensor q^*O(r).

The subbundle condition says that no fiber polynomial is zero. Hence
`pi=p|Z` is finite flat of degree `r`, including at repeated roots. No
reducedness or irreducibility of `Z` is assumed in the general theorem.

For `j>=r-1`, pushing forward the divisor sequence gives

    0 -> M tensor Sym^(j-r)V
      -> Sym^j V tensor O_C
      -> pi_*O_Z(j) -> 0,                              (1)

where the first term is zero for `j=r-1`. The vanishing needed here is
`H^1(P1,O(j-r))=0`. In particular the saturated quotient by the first
relation at a later degree `s>r` is

    A_s=Sym^s E/(R Sym^(s-r)E)
       =H^-s tensor pi_*O_Z(s).                        (2)

The notation `O_Z(j)` always means restriction of the **direction** ruling
`q^*O(j)`, not the base ruling.

## 2. Exact criterion for a second fiberwise coprime relation

Let `T` be a line bundle on `C`. By adjunction, a morphism

    T -> A_s

is the same as a section of the line bundle

    O_Z(s) tensor pi^*(H^-s tensor T^-1).               (3)

In each fiber, the proposed second binary relation is coprime to the first
one precisely when this section is a unit on the finite root scheme. This
statement includes a repeated root with its full scheme structure: an
element of its Artin local algebra is a unit exactly when its value on the
reduced root is nonzero. Consequently the second relation is coprime in
**every** fiber if and only if (3) has a nowhere-vanishing section, or
equivalently

    O_Z(s) is isomorphic to pi^*N,
    N=H^s tensor T.                                    (4)

This is both necessary and sufficient for a full **graded** family. Given
an isomorphism in (4), it gives a line `T subset A_s` whose restriction is
coprime on every fiber. In the graded algebra `Sym E/(R)`, take the ideal
generated by this line in degree `s`, and take its inverse image in
`Sym E`. Locally on the base the quotient line lifts to a binary form, so
the inverse image is locally generated by a regular sequence `(f_r,g_s)`.
Every fiber is a graded Artin complete intersection of degrees `(r,s)`.
The Hilbert function is constant, and the graded layers are locally free
over `C`.

It is essential that the second generator lives **modulo multiples of the
first generator**. A global lift `T -> Sym^s E` is not needed. Such a lift
can be impossible even when (4) holds. The graded family constructed here
also has not been lifted into the actual formal neighborhood of `C`.

## 3. The criterion becomes a precise torsion problem on a rational base

Now let `C=P1`, `M=O(-epsilon)`, with `epsilon>=0`. The root divisor has
bidegree `(epsilon,r)`, where the first entry is the base degree. Its
direction ruling has degree `epsilon`, and its base ruling has degree
`r`. If (4) holds, taking total degrees on `Z` gives

    r deg N=s epsilon.                                 (5)

Put

    d=gcd(r,epsilon),     r0=r/d,     epsilon0=epsilon/d,
    L=O_Z(r0) tensor pi^*O(-epsilon0).

The convention `gcd(r,0)=r` covers the constant relation case. Condition
(4) is equivalent to

    r0 divides s,     L^(s/r0) is trivial in Pic(Z).      (6)

Indeed the only possible descended bundle in (5) is
`N=O(s epsilon/r)`. Thus, if `L` has finite order `t`, the possible later
degrees are exactly the positive multiples of `r0*t`, subject to `s>r`.
If `L` has infinite order, no such uniform graded second relation exists.
This uses `Pic(Z)`, including all nilpotent information when `Z` is
nonreduced. It does not replace the root scheme by its support.

For `epsilon>0`, any such `N` has positive degree, whereas `Sym^s V` is
constant. Thus

    Hom_C(N,Sym^s V tensor O_C)=0.

The second generator line in (4) cannot lift globally to `Sym^s E`.
The generator extension is necessarily nonsplit. Discarding that
extension would incorrectly exclude the valid graded families supplied
by (6).

The previously proved pure-power obstruction is consistent with (6).
For a moving power, `Z` is a nonreduced multiple of its moving graph.
The obstruction lies in the full nonreduced Picard group. Passing merely
to the graph loses the decisive information. General root curves need
not have the same obstruction.

## 4. A smooth elliptic root curve defeats a naive pure-power extension

Let `B` be an elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero. Choose a line bundle `A` of degree two and a
nontrivial torsion line bundle `eta` of exact order `m>2`. Put
`D=A tensor eta`. Both complete degree-two systems define maps to `P1`.
Their product embeds `B` in `P1_base x P1_direction`.

For completeness, the product map has degree dividing two onto its image.
If it had degree two, its image would project with degree one to each
factor, so would be a `(1,1)` graph and would imply `A=D`. This contradicts
`eta!=O`. Thus the map is birational onto its integral `(2,2)` image. That
image has arithmetic genus one; its smooth normalization also has genus
one. The normalization quotient therefore has length zero, so the image
is smooth and the map is an isomorphism.

Take this image as `Z`. Then

    O_Z(1)=D,     pi^*O_base(1)=A,
    O_Z(-1,1)=eta.

Here `r=epsilon=2`, and (6) says exactly `m divides s`. Taking `s=m`
gives a graded complete-intersection family of degrees `(2,m)` with a
moving first binary relation and no loss of coprimality at any fiber.
The order `m` is arbitrary. Thus the exclusion for a moving pure power
cannot be extended to an arbitrary moving binary relation.

This is a structural counterboundary, **not** an STCI example. On `C0`,
where `H=O(7)`, this graded algebra has length `2m`, top degree `m`, and
socle line `O(-6m-2)`. The general socle formula is

    Soc=R tensor T tensor (det E)^-1.

Its layers are Gorenstein symmetric, and its full normal-layer defect is
`Delta=m(m-2)`. In particular, some members can even match the accepted
numerical socle constraints for projective complete intersections. The
missing condition is realization in the actual ambient neighborhood and
then by two projective equations; a graded construction alone supplies
neither.

## 5. The actual ambient obstruction lives naturally on the root curve

Suppose no independent relation has yet appeared by degree `r+k`, with
`k>=1`. The first-relation correction bundle is

    Q_k=Hom(R, Sym^(r+k)E/(R Sym^k E))
       =pi_*[O_Z(r+k) tensor pi^*(H^-k tensor M^-1)].   (7)

The identity follows directly from (1), the projection formula, and the
definition of `R`. Finite pushforward gives the exact cohomology identity

    H^1(C,Q_k)
      =H^1(Z,O_Z(r+k) tensor pi^*(H^-k tensor M^-1)).    (8)

There is a geometric interpretation. Blow up the actual threefold along
`C`. Its exceptional divisor is `P=C x P1`, and its conormal bundle is

    O_P(-Exceptional)=p^*H^-1 tensor q^*O(1).

Since `Z` is a Cartier divisor in `P`,

    N_(Z/P)=O_Z(r) tensor pi^*M^-1.

The line bundle in (8) is consequently

    N_(Z/P) tensor (O_P(-Exceptional)|Z)^k.              (9)

It is the obstruction bundle for extending the Cartier divisor `Z` into
successive neighborhoods of the exceptional divisor. In coordinates, the
first class is the restriction to `f=0` of the nonlinear ambient normal
transition applied to `f`. The differential operator includes the change
of the base coordinate as well as the quadratic change of the normal
coordinates. A pointwise evaluation of a constant-direction obstruction
would omit the base derivative and is not this class.

For a smooth root curve of bidegree `(epsilon,r)`, its genus and the degree
of the line bundle in (8) are

    genus(Z)=(epsilon-1)(r-1),
    degree=2r epsilon+k(epsilon-r lambda),

where `H=O(lambda)`. For the elliptic `(2,2)` example on `C0`, the first
obstruction space is

    H^1(Z,O_Z(3) tensor pi^*O(-5)).

This line bundle has degree `-4`; the obstruction space therefore has
dimension four. A nonzero obstruction group does not show that the actual
ambient class is nonzero. Conversely, torsion of the ruling difference
does not make this class vanish.

Adjunction also gives a uniform dual description:

    omega_Z tensor L_k^-1
      =O_Z(-k-2) tensor pi^*(omega_C tensor H^k),

where `L_k` is the line bundle in (8). The first relation's coefficient
bundle `M` cancels from the formula. Its geometry still enters through
the actual root divisor `Z`; the cancellation does not make the
obstruction independent of the first relation.

The first class has a coordinate-free description. Write
`J=O_P(-Exceptional)`. The actual first neighborhood of `P` in the blowup
has a square-zero extension class

    kappa in Ext^1_P(Omega_P,J).

Restricting to `Z` and pulling back along its conormal differential
`df:O_Z(-Z)->Omega_P|Z` produces the first obstruction in
`H^1(Z,N_(Z/P) tensor J|Z)`. In local splittings of the square-zero
extension, their differences are derivations; applying those derivations
to local equations `f` and restricting to `Z` gives exactly this cocycle.
This also proves that the base-coordinate derivative must be included.

For `C0`, the two tangent directions of `P=P1 x P1` decompose the
ambient class into

    H^1(P,O(-5,1)) and H^1(P,O(-7,3)).

For a smooth root divisor of bidegree `(2,2)`, the divisor exact sequence
identifies the four-dimensional target as

    H^1(Z,L_1)
      =coker[ H^1(P,O(-7,1)) --multiply f-->
                                      H^1(P,O(-5,3)) ].

The two spaces have dimensions twelve and sixteen, and the map is
injective because `H^0(Z,L_1)=0`. This gives a small intrinsic obstruction
incidence for the entire moving quadratic family, rather than one
calculation for each projective degree.

This identifies the next broad research problem: relate the ambient
formal-neighborhood extension class of `Z` to the torsion-descent condition
(6), while retaining the second-generator extension. Solving that bridge
would impose geometry beyond all graded Hilbert-function and socle-degree
tests at once.
