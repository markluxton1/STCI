# The cone-vertex fiber and ambient-section defects of ideal powers

Date: 2026-10-31. Status: **provisional deductions, independently
reconstructed; written-artifact review recorded in assessment §31**.
No canonical promotion or unrestricted STCI conclusion.

## 1. Exact input and what is inherited

Let \(C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3\) over an algebraically
closed field k of characteristic zero, \(S=k[a,b,c,d]\),
\(\mathfrak m=(a,b,c,d)\), and \(I=I_{C_0}\).
Write \(X=\operatorname{Bl}_{C_0}\mathbf P^3\), with hyperplane class H
and exceptional divisor E.

The [October 5 theorem, §2](2026-10-05-literature-new-routes.md#2-uniform-theorem-for-integral-curves-on-a-smooth-quadric),
with its [independent audit, §3](2026-10-05-foundational-frontier-audit.md),
already proves
\[
I^r=(I^r)^{\mathrm{sat}}=I^{(r)}\qquad(r\ge1).
\tag{1}
\]
Its scope is every integral divisor on a smooth quadric, in every
characteristic. This result is reused, not rediscovered here.

[Derenthal–Hausen–Heim–Keicher–Laface, Theorem 4.5(22)](https://arxiv.org/html/1409.0799v3#S4)
gives the Cox ring of this exact X. The case is marked as a computed
representative; the proof's Case X22 explicitly uses the equations
of C0, so no general-family identification is required. The nine
generator classes are
\[
H,H,H,H,\quad 3H-E,3H-E,3H-E,\quad 2H-E,\quad E.
\]
Use their variables \(T_1,\ldots,T_9\), with
\[
Q=T_8,\quad U=T_5,\quad V=T_6,\quad W=T_7,\quad e=T_9.
\]
The first four variables are \(a,b,c,d\). The order-one generators
represent \(bc-ad,\ b^3-a^2c,\ ac^2-b^2d,\ c^3-bd^2\).

Equation (1) identifies the Cox ring, with its Rees grading retained,
with the extended ordinary Rees algebra \(S[It,t^{-1}]\).
Here e has Rees degree -1, the four ideal generators degree 1, and
the ambient coordinates degree 0. The total homogeneous degree in S
is a separate grading.

## 2. The special fiber is a quadric cone

Specializing all nine published relations at \(a=b=c=d=0\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Cox}(X)/\mathfrak m\operatorname{Cox}(X)
=
\frac{k[Q,U,V,W,e]}
 {(eQ,eU,eV,eW,\ UW+V^2)}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular the four annihilation relations must be retained.
The last published relation alone would give the wrong dimension.

For Rees degree \(r\ge0\), the extended algebra's component is
\(I^rt^r\), and after quotienting by \(\mathfrak m\) it is
\(I^r/\mathfrak m I^r\). In (2), e kills every positive-degree
generator. Consequently its nonnegative Rees-degree subalgebra is
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal F(I):=\bigoplus_{r\ge0}I^r/\mathfrak m I^r
  \simeq k[Q,U,V,W]/(UW+V^2).
\quad}
\tag{3}
\]
The four generators in (3) all have degree one for this grading.

Thus the analytic spread of \(I_{\mathfrak m}\), defined as the
dimension of its special-fiber algebra, is three. Passing to
\(I^r\) passes to the r-th Veronese of (3), preserving dimension.
It follows that **no positive ordinary or symbolic power of
\(I_{\mathfrak m}\) has a two-generated reduction**. Indeed a
two-generated reduction J of such a power would make its Rees
algebra finite over the Rees algebra of J, hence its special fiber
finite over an algebra generated by two elements, of dimension at
most two. This contradicts (3).

Geometrically, \(\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal F(I)\) is a quadric
cone with vertex the Q-direction. It is the exceptional fiber over
the vertex of the affine cone in
\(\operatorname{Bl}_I\mathbf A^4\to\mathbf A^4\).
It is not the ruled exceptional divisor over the projective curve
in \(X\to\mathbf P^3\). In particular, it is not a residual curve
outside C0.

## 3. A uniform ambient-section defect on every ordinary thickening

There is an elementary geometric explanation of the failed
Cohen–Macaulay condition in the symbolic-Rees criteria below.
It also generalizes beyond C0.

Let C be an integral curve of type \((a,b)\) on a smooth quadric,
with \(1\le a\le b\) and \(b\ge3\), over an algebraically closed
field of any characteristic. Write \(\mathcal I=\mathcal I_C\).
For every \(r\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
h^1(\mathbf P^3,\mathcal I^r(br-2))
   =\max\{(b-a)r-1,0\}.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]

To prove this, use the inherited exact sequence
\[
0\to\mathcal I^{r-1}(d-2)\xrightarrow{\ q\ }
\mathcal I^r(d)\to\mathcal O_Q(d-ar,d-br)\to0.
\tag{5}
\]
Induction on r gives \(H^1=H^2=0\) for
\(\mathcal I^r(d)\) when \(d\ge br-1\): both quadric entries
are at least -1, so their \(H^1,H^2\) vanish by Künneth, and
\(d-2\ge b(r-1)-1\) handles the kernel. The base \(r=0\)
is the vanishing of intermediate cohomology of line bundles on
\(\mathbf P^3\).

At \(d=br-2\), the kernel twist is \(br-4\ge b(r-1)-1\);
this is where \(b\ge3\) is used. The long exact sequence gives
\[
H^1(\mathbf P^3,\mathcal I^r(br-2))
\simeq
H^1(Q,\mathcal O_Q((b-a)r-2,-2)).
\]
Künneth yields (4), including \(r=1,b=3\).

For C0, equation (4) becomes
\[
h^1(\mathbf P^3,\mathcal I_{C_0}^r(3r-2))=2r-1.
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(Z_r=V(\mathcal I_C^r)\subset\mathbf P^3\). Restriction from
ambient polynomials to \(H^0(Z_r,\mathcal O_{Z_r}(br-2))\) has
cokernel (4). These are missing ambient sections of the
codimension-two thickening Zr, not of the Cartier multiple rC
inside Q. For C0 every such thickening is therefore non-ACM.
This is an explicit all-power extraction from the inherited
quadric sequence and Hilbert-function work; it is not a newly
discovered exclusion of arbitrary supported thickenings.

## 4. Exact literature consequences and stopping boundary

[D'Cruz–Mandal–Verma, Theorems 1.1–1.2 and 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.05886)
clarifies the symbolic-Rees criteria. Theorem 1.1, attributed to
Cowsik, gives at most \(d-1\) local defining elements from finite
symbolic-Rees generation for a radical ideal other than the
maximal ideal. Here \(d=4\): it gives three local elements, without
a homogeneous projective conclusion. The printed Theorem 1.2
requires prime height \(d-1\), failing for height two in dimension
four; its Huneke antecedent is the three-dimensional theorem.
Theorem 2.3 applies in arbitrary positive height but additionally
requires eventual Cohen–Macaulayness of symbolic-power quotients.
Equation (6) proves that this hypothesis fails here.

The construction implication of finite generation is therefore
limited. A hypothetical homogeneous pair with radical I need not
be a reduction of any power of I. Radical equality does not imply
equality of integral closures or the eventual power recurrence
defining a reduction. Equations (3) and (6) do not exclude such a
pair and give no positive residual-degree bound.

The generalization in (4) isolates the ruling imbalance as a
source of missing global sections on ordinary ambient thickenings.
It does not force the same sections to survive in an arbitrary
complete-intersection thickening. No power, fiber, chamber or
normal-form classification is selected. The
[strategy assessment §31](2026-10-31-unrestricted-strategy-assessment.md#31-the-exact-cox-fiber-closes-a-reduction-shortcut-not-the-stci-question)
records the independent challenge, review state, and reopening
conditions.
