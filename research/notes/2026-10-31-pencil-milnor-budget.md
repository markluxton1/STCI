## 1. Statement and relation to arbitrary defining pairs

Let C be a smooth connected curve of degree d and genus g in P3
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Suppose
P,Q are homogeneous forms of the same positive degree D with
\[
V(P,Q)_{\rm red}=C.
\]
Their proper CI has generic transverse length
\[
m=D^2/d.
\]
Let mu be the generic transverse Milnor number along C of a
general member of the pencil generated by P,Q. Allow mu=0 when
that member is generically smooth along C. Then
\[
\boxed{\quad dD(m+1-\mu)\ \ge\ (4d+2g-2)m
       =(\deg N_{C/\mathbf P^3})m.\quad}                 \tag{1}
\]
For the fixed rational quartic C0, this says
\[
\boxed{\quad \mu\le {D^2\over4}+1-{7D\over8}.\quad}       \tag{2}
\]

This covers every hypothetical STCI pair F_a,G_b: choose any
common multiple D of a,b and take P=F^(D/a), Q=G^(D/b).
The powers change the thickening and its generic length; they
preserve its reduced support. No normality or irreducibility of
the original carriers, balanced normal bundle, prescribed
transverse type, or normal-flatness hypothesis is used.

Over C, mu is the usual Milnor number of a general transverse
plane-curve germ. Over another algebraically closed characteristic-zero
field, use the corresponding finite Jacobian-algebra dimension.
The proof over C transfers by descending the finite equations,
the curve and witnesses of radical equality to a finitely generated
subfield, embedding it into C, and extending algebraic closures.
Degree, genus, generic intersection length and generic transverse
Jacobian dimension are unchanged.

## 2. Two primary inputs, retaining all special points

First work over C. For a reduced surface S and a hyperplane H
general for S,
\[
\chi(S\setminus H)=1+\operatorname{pol}(S),              \tag{3}
\]
where pol is the degree of the gradient map, zero if that map
is not dominant. This is the Dimca–Papadima Euler-characteristic
formula, also proved algebraically in
[Aluffi, Corollary 3.6](https://www.math.fsu.edu/~aluffi/archive/paper432.pdf).
The polar degree depends only on reduced support.

For a reduced degree-e surface singular precisely along smooth C,
write nu for its generic transverse Milnor number and Q_S for
the finite set of special points on C. The formula of
[Siersma–Tibăr, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05743)
becomes
\[
\operatorname{pol}(S)
=(e-1)^3-(de+d+2g-2)\nu-\Delta(S),                       \tag{4}
\]
\[
\Delta(S)=\sum_{q\in Q_S}\bigl(\nu+\chi(A_q)-1\bigr),
\]
where A_q is the local Milnor fiber of the surface germ.
Smoothness of C makes the local branch count at every q equal
to one; it does not make the surface germ equisingular there.

Each summand is nonnegative. Indeed, the Milnor fiber is connected
with reduced rational homology only in degrees one and two.
[Siersma–Tibăr, Milnor Fibre Homology via Deformation,
Theorem 4.4(c)](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~siers101/SiTi2015DefHomology.pdf)
gives b1(A_q)<=nu for a one-branch critical curve. Hence
\[
\nu+\chi(A_q)-1=\nu-b_1(A_q)+b_2(A_q)\ge0.              \tag{5}
\]
This retains monodromy and arbitrary special-point behavior;
it does not use an isolated-singularity formula for the surface.

## 3. Unions of general pencil members

A general pencil member is reduced and smooth off C by Bertini:
the base has codimension two, so a repeated surface component
would contradict smoothness on its complement. Let p be its
polar degree. These members have common generic transverse
Milnor number mu, and their polar degree is constant on a
nonempty open of the pencil.

For any integer k>=2, select k distinct general members and let
S_k be their reduced union, of degree kD. Every pair has common
zero scheme V(P,Q), since its two independent linear combinations
generate the same ideal. Thus the union is singular precisely
along C, and each transverse pair has intersection length m.

Choose H general for the finite collection and C. Since
chi(C minus H)=2-2g-d, inclusion–exclusion in (3) gives
\[
\operatorname{pol}(S_k)=kp+(k-1)(d+2g-1).              \tag{6}
\]
The reduced plane-curve union formula gives
\[
\mu_k=k\mu+k(k-1)m-k+1
     =mk^2+(\mu-m-1)k+1.                              \tag{7}
\]
One obtains (7) from mu=2delta-r+1, additivity of delta with
pairwise intersection terms, and addition of branch counts;
see [Dimca–Greuel, Theorem 2.1(1)](https://www.journalofsing.org/volume18/dimca-greuel.pdf).
The transverse curves have no common branch because their pairwise
intersection is zero dimensional. In particular, the intersection
term is the full m, not the reduced length one.

Put alpha=d+2g-2 and n=4d+2g-2=deg N. Substitute (6)–(7)
into (4). The cubic term cancels using dm=D^2, giving
\[
\Delta(S_k)=A k^2+B k,                                \tag{8}
\]
\[
A=dD(m+1-\mu)-nm,\qquad
B=(3-d)D+\alpha(m-\mu)-p-1.
\]
For C0 this is explicitly
\[
\Delta(S_k)=
k^2[4D(m+1-\mu)-14m]
+k[2m-2\mu-D-1-p].
\]
By (5), the left side is nonnegative for arbitrarily large k.
Thus A>=0, proving (1) and (2). The selected members and special
points can vary with k; their total correction is still (8).

The normal degree in (1) follows from
deg(T_P3 restricted to C)-deg(T_C)=4d-(2-2g).
No splitting of N is involved. The line with P=x^D,Q=y^D
attains equality: d=1, g=0, m=D^2 and mu=(D-1)^2.

Further power equalization does not strengthen the inequality
artificially. Replacing P,Q by P^r,Q^r gives
\[
D'=rD,\quad m'=r^2m,\quad
\mu'=r\mu+r(r-1)m-r+1,
\]
because a general new member is a union of r general old members.
Thus m'+1-mu'=r(m+1-mu), and both sides of (1) multiply by r^2.

## 4. Geometric content and stopping decision

The actual geometric objects are reduced surfaces from one
global pencil, all meeting in the same full thick base curve.
Their generic transverse Milnor numbers record vanishing cycles
of plane slices. At each special point, (5) measures the deficit
of first homology from its generic upper allowance, plus the
second homology there. It is not a residual degree downstairs.
The leading coefficient in (8) is the asymptotic total of these
nonnegative special-point defects. Pairwise contact supplies m,
while the embedded curve contributes its normal degree.

This necessary condition was new to the inspected project record
at extraction, without a claim of literature priority or logical
independence from earlier budgets. The subsequent §6 identifies
its exact transverse cluster meaning and proves that it adds no
restriction for C0. Comparison with the full global normal-layer
degrees and torsion remains separate.

The inequality does not force a contradiction. Base length alone
does not force mu close to m: in the local parameter ideal
(x^2+y^r,y^N), with N>r>=2, the length is 2N and a general
pencil member has mu=r-1. This is a transverse control only,
not a projective construction for C0.

The bounded selection ends with this proof. No spectrum,
special-point or local-pencil classification follows. Reopening
requires a specified global comparison forcing mu above (1),
or a construction of ambient equations satisfying the full support
condition. A generic Milnor number compatible with (1) is not
evidence that those equations exist. The unrestricted
characteristic-zero STCI question remains open.

## 6. Cluster interpretation and a stronger local bound

This continuation assesses the actual force of (1), rather than
classifying transverse types. Independent proof, strategy and
written-preservation reviews pass at provisional scope.

Work at the geometric generic point of C, after completion and
extension of k(C) to its algebraic closure. Let I=(P,Q) be the
resulting parameter ideal in a two-dimensional regular local ring R,
and let J be its integral closure. Then
\[
m=\ell(R/I)=e(I)=e(J).
\]
Principalize by point blowups, and write n_p for the positive
base-cluster multiplicities, including the origin. If b is the
number of geometric branches of a general pencil member, then
\[
m=\sum_p n_p^2,\qquad
\mu=\sum_p n_p(n_p-1)-b+1,\qquad
T:=m+1-\mu=\sum_p n_p+b.                              \tag{9}
\]
Here m is the multiplicity of J, not its colength:
the latter is sum binomial(n_p+1,2).
[D'Cruz, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0603211)
states the residue-weighted Hoskin–Deligne formula.
[Bondil, Proposition 2.2, formula (3), Lemma 3.4 and Remark 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0304040)
give the general-element and reduction comparison. They justify
using a general member of the actual two-section pencil, without
replacing it by an unrelated complete linear system.

There is an intrinsic formulation on the same resolution:
\[
I\mathcal O_X=\mathcal O_X(-Z),\quad
Z=\sum_i\rho_iE_i,\quad E_{\rm red}=\sum_iE_i,\quad
\varepsilon_i=-Z\cdot E_i\ge0.
\]
Nonnegativity holds because the resolved pencil generates
O_X(-Z). Its restriction to E_i has degree epsilon_i when
nonconstant; these are the dicritical components. A general
member meets them in b=sum epsilon_i transverse points.
With K=K_{X/R} and A_i=1+coeff_Ei(K), intersection gives
\[
\boxed{\quad
m=-Z^2=\sum_i\varepsilon_i\rho_i,\qquad
T=-Z\cdot(K+E_{\rm red})=\sum_i\varepsilon_iA_i.
\quad}                                                \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, -Z.K=sum n_p and -Z.E_red=b, equivalently Bondil's
Milnor formula gives the second equality directly. T measures
log discrepancies weighted by the degrees of the maps from
exceptional divisors to the pencil. It is not a residual degree.

If c=lct(I), the log-resolution formula and (10) imply
\[
c=\min_i{A_i\over\rho_i}\le {T\over m}.
\]
The minimum retains divisors with epsilon_i=0; they can compute
the threshold even though they do not contribute to (10).
[de Fernex–Ein–Mustaţă, Theorems 0.1 and 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0205171)
give mc^2>=4 in this smooth two-dimensional local setting.
Their treatment includes generic points, completion and
residue-field extension. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\quad 4\le mc^2\le T^2/m,\qquad
T=m+1-\mu\ge2\sqrt m.\quad}                            \tag{11}
\]
This is a universal local statement, independent of a projective
curve or the existence of global equations.
For an arbitrary primary ideal the same statement uses m=e(I)
and a general two-generator reduction, not m=length(R/I).

For geometric interpretation in the point cluster, the proximity
excess is epsilon_p=n_p-sum_{q proximate to p} n_q.
A nonorigin point is free if proximate to one predecessor and
satellite if proximate to two. Thus
\[
b=n_0-\sum_{\text{satellite }p}n_p,\qquad
T=2n_0+\sum_{\substack{p\ne0\\p\ {\rm free}}}n_p.
\tag{12}
\]
Satellite multiplicities still affect m and mu; they cancel in
this expression for T. Without geometric generic base change,
the sums require the corresponding residue degrees. Variation
and ramification of these centers over C are additional global
data, not terms silently included in this generic formula.
The discriminant multiplicity of the transverse finite map
(P,Q) is mu+m-1=2m-T, not T; see Bondil, Lemma 4.1 and
Corollary 4.4.

For the projective setup, (11) gives T>=2D/sqrt(d).
It already implies (1) whenever
\[
\deg N_C\le2d^{3/2}.                                  \tag{13}
\]
For C0, 14<16, so T>=D is strictly stronger than T>=7D/8.
The polar inequality therefore supplies no additional restriction
on hypothetical supported quartic pencils.

There is an immediate comparison with the inherited global graph
bound. Choose two general members, both of order q=n_0. Their
contact surviving the first blowup is
\[
m-q^2=\sum_{p\ne0}n_p^2.
\]
The [existing bound, §8](2026-10-28-residual-degree-blowup-bounds.md#8-october-31-extraction-a-uniform-amount-of-contact-survives-the-first-blowup)
requires this to be at least 0.6524027... times m for a supported
quartic pair. It constrains a quadratic cluster total, whereas
T is a linear total. Neither formula identifies T with the
global normal-filtration degrees or point torsion.

For completeness, equality in (11) forces J to be a power of
the maximal ideal by de Fernex–Ein–Mustaţă, Theorem 1.4.
For C0 it would give q=D/2 and m=q^2, contradicting the
inherited graph bound. Hence a hypothetical supported quartic
pencil has T>D, and T>=D+1 since D is even. This strictness is
a combination with the already known global result; it is
not an independent new obstruction.

The continuing strategy decision is to stop this Milnor-budget
lane. Preserve the transverse valuation interpretation and the
valid global special-point identity, but do not use the weaker
polar inequality to motivate a local classification. Further
progress needs control of actual homogeneous realization or of
global cluster/branch variation beyond these generic formulas.
