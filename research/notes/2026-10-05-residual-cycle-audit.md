# Independent audit of the pointwise residual cycle

Date: 2026-10-05, resumed sustained-research phase.

Status: **PROVED under the stated hypotheses.** This independently verifies
the pointwise cycle statement previously marked a proof candidate in
`2026-10-05-residual-cycle.md`. It supplies no existence assertion and no
identification of the underlying zero-dimensional schemes.

## Local determinant statement

Let R be a complete DVR, K its fraction field, and B a finite flat
R-algebra whose reduced quotient is R and whose generic fiber is
K[y]/(y^m). Let B_i be the saturated order flag

    B_i = B intersect (y^i) B_K,  B_0=B, B_m=0.

Suppose its successive quotients are invertible R-modules, globally denoted
E_i=L^i(D_i), with D_0=D_1=0. These are the hypotheses used by the
repository's quasiprimitive BF filtration; an unsaturated I-adic flag is
insufficient. Take q in B_1 whose class in E_1 is nonzero over K. Write its
first-class vanishing order as t_q.

Choose an R-basis b_0=1,b_1,...,b_(m-1) adapted to the saturated flag.
This is possible because every quotient is free over the DVR. Multiplication
by q has zero row indexed by b_0 and zero column indexed by b_(m-1).
The remaining square block, rows 1,...,m-1 and columns 0,...,m-2, is
triangular with nonzero diagonal. Its i-th diagonal entry is the leading
map E_i -> E_(i+1), and therefore has valuation

    t_q + ord(D_(i+1)) - ord(D_i).

All other maximal minors vanish. Thus the cokernel is one free copy of R
plus a finite torsion module whose length is the valuation of this
determinant. Summing proves

    length_R tors(B/qB) = (m-1)t_q + ord(D_(m-1)).       (RC)

Higher coefficients of q affect off-diagonal entries but do not affect
this determinant. No assumption that q itself generates the quotient
direction at the closed point is needed.

## Residual intersection statement

Let C be a smooth curve on a smooth surface Q in a smooth threefold, and
let X=V(F,G) be a complete-intersection multiple curve with reduced support
C satisfying the preceding quasiprimitive hypotheses. Suppose both restrictions
vanish along C and the first class of the equation of Q is nonzero in L
over the function field of C. Locally write

    F|Q=h rho,  G|Q=h sigma,

where h defines C in Q. Assume rho and sigma have no common curve component.
Their finite intersection Z is supported on C, because X has that support.
There is an exact sequence on Q

    0 -> O_Z(-C) -> O_(X intersect Q) -> O_C -> 0.     (EX)

Indeed its kernel is the ideal generated by h in
O_Q/(h rho,h sigma), and cancellation of the nonzerodivisor h identifies
that kernel with O_Q/(rho,sigma), with the indicated twist.

At a point of C choose a completed parameter t on C and lift it to the
ambient smooth threefold. The complete-intersection local algebra B is
finite flat over R=k[[t]]: it is finite because its radical is the ideal
of C, and torsion-free because the complete intersection is CM and has
only that one minimal prime. The completed middle algebra in (EX) is B/qB,
where q is the local equation of Q. Its quotient O_C is R, while its kernel
is finite R-torsion. Consequently that kernel is exactly the torsion
submodule, with its length equal to the local intersection length of Z.
Equation (RC) therefore proves, coefficientwise as cycles on C,

    [Z] = (m-1) T + D_(m-1),                         (CY)

where T is the zero divisor of the first class of q in
L tensor O_C(Q). Twisting the finite kernel does not change its lengths.
This proof allows special-point normal-order jumps of q and nonprimitive
fibers of X. The identity is of length cycles, not of ideals or schemes.

## Application and precise scope on C0

For a hypothetical characteristic-zero pair (F_4,G_b) on C0, the auxiliary
quadric is smooth and the quartic restriction has order exactly one along
C0. This proves that q has nonzero first class in L over the function field.
The mate may have higher order on Q: then sigma_b contains a further copy
of C, but rho does not, and the same cancellation and cycle identity still
apply. One must retain that further copy in sigma_b until explicitly
subtracting its intersection cycle; no order-one assumption on the mate is
necessary. Properness of the residual intersection follows from the support
condition and the fact that rho has no C component.

In that application m=6, T has degree e+1, and D_5=D_2+D_3, giving

    [rho intersect sigma_6] = 5 T + D_2 + D_3.

The other local inequality in the preceding note is also valid. Choose an
ambient normal parameter y whose image generates L over R and a kernel
parameter x. In B/yB, the torsion nilradical T' has length ord(D_(m-1))
by (RC). Its quotient T'/T'^2 is the torsion conormal quotient in the
x direction, of length ord(A), where A is the first-normal Fitting
divisor. Thus

    A <= D_(m-1)

coefficientwise. This is an inequality; the local example
(t^a x-y^2,x^3) proves equality is not universal.

The pointwise residual constraint is stronger than its degree equality,
but does not by itself exclude the nonregular pole strata or determine
the higher transverse jets. These remain the necessary next data.
