### Final addendum: reducible and powered carriers

The subsequently added factorization paragraph in synthesis §1 also
**PASSES**. Properness of \((F,G)\) implies that no irreducible factor
\(F_i\) divides \(G\), so each \((F_i,G)\) is a proper intersection.
Intersection with the Cartier divisor \(V(G)\) is additive on the actual
surface cycle \(\sum_i u_i[V(F_i)]\). At \(\eta_C\), a factor not
containing \(C\) is a unit, while a containing factor contributes
\(u_i m_i\); hence \(m=\sum_{C\subset V(F_i)}u_i m_i\).
Subtracting this full \(C\)-coefficient from the additive intersection
cycle gives exactly the displayed residual-degree formula. Coincident
residual curves retain summed multiplicities; no scheme disjointness is
assumed. If each containing integral factor is smooth everywhere along
\(C\), its inherited residual estimate is \(\deg R_i\ge b\deg F_i/3\).
Every factor missing \(C\) contributes the larger full degree
\(b\deg F_i\). Summing, with \(a=\sum_i u_i\deg F_i\), proves
\(\delta\ge ab/3\) for this stated reducible/powered class. This does
not extend the smooth-along-\(C\) premise to arbitrary singular factors.
