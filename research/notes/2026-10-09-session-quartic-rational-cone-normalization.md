# Quartic rational-cone normalization: a proposed all-mate exclusion

Date: 2026-10-09. Characteristic zero. Status: **PROOF WRITTEN;
INDEPENDENT ADVERSARIAL AUDIT REQUESTED**. This note does not assert
that every remaining nonnormal quartic has this normalization.

Proposed theorem: let X be an integral quartic in P3 with finite
normalization `(S,H)`, where S is the rational normal quartic cone and
`H=nu^*O_X(1)` is its hyperplane polarization. Let C be a smooth rational
curve of degree four on X. Then no positive-degree homogeneous form G
has intersection support `X intersect V(G)=C`.

The proof uses full normalization fibers and a first-jet descent test
on an actual product chart. It assumes neither a reduced conductor nor
inclusion of a ramification subscheme in the lifted curve.

## 1. Divisor classes force a global hyperplane equation

The cone is the surface `P(1,1,4)` embedded by O(4), with weighted
coordinates s,t,z of degrees 1,1,4. Its resolution is F4, contracting
the negative section E, with `E^2=-4`, `f^2=0`, `E.f=1`, and

```
pi^*H=E+4f.
```

The standard weighted-cone and ruled-resolution descriptions are
recorded by Dolgachev, section 1.2.3 of
[Weighted projective varieties](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/idolga-new/wp-content/uploads/sites/1467/2024/08/weighted82.pdf).
The class computation here is direct: Weil divisor classes on S are
classes on F4 modulo the contracted E. Consequently

```
Cl(S)=Pic(F4)/Z[E]=Z[f],    [H]=4[f].
```

In particular Cl(S) has no torsion. Also
`H0(S,H)=k*z + H0(P1,O(4))`, since on F4 the hyperplane sections have
pushforward O plus O(4).

Suppose a mate G of degree b exists. An integral quartic is generically
regular along a smooth degree-four curve C: otherwise a general
integral plane quartic would have four distinct singular points,
contradicting arithmetic genus three. Full inverse support and purity
on the normal surface S therefore give a sole reduced lifted curve c,
with c isomorphic to C and every entire fiber above C a singleton.

Write `div(nu^*G)=m*c`. Intersect with the ample Cartier H:

```
m*(H.c)=b*H^2,    H.c=4,    H^2=4,
```

so m=b. Thus `b[c]=b[H]` in Cl(S). Torsion freeness yields `c~H`.
The curve c is therefore Cartier. A smooth Cartier curve on a normal
surface cannot pass through a singular point: in a local ring A,
if A/(f) is regular of dimension one, then the maximal ideal of A is
generated by f and one lift of its curve parameter, so A is regular.
Hence c avoids the cone vertex.

Choose its global hyperplane section `f=alpha*z+phi4(s,t)`. Avoidance
of the vertex implies alpha nonzero; scale to alpha=1. The divisors
of `nu^*G` and f^b agree. Their ratio has zero Weil divisor, hence on
the normal projective S is a nonzero global constant. Therefore

```
nu^*G=lambda*f^b,    lambda in k*.
```

This global equality is essential. There is no freely chosen affine
unit multiplying f^b in the subsequent descent test.

## 2. Every such normalization map is a projected cone

The map nu is given by four independent sections of H. Since it is
basepoint free at the vertex, one has nonzero z coefficient. By a
projective coordinate change on the target and subtraction of that
section from the others, its four sections have the form

```
z+psi4(s,t),    beta1(s,t), beta2(s,t), beta3(s,t),
```

where beta_i are binary quartics. They have no common zero on P1:
at such a zero one could choose z=-psi4 and obtain a base point of
nu on S.

Let `beta:P1->P2` be this basepoint-free map and E its image. The
surface image X is the projective cone over E, since on each source
ruling z varies freely and the target vertex coordinate has degree
one. Its degree is `deg(E)=deg(X)=4`. Since beta pulls O_P2(1) back
to O_P1(4), it has degree one onto E. Thus E is an integral rational
plane quartic, with beta its normalization. In particular E is
singular: its arithmetic genus is three and its normalization genus
is zero.

The lifted c intersects every source ruling once, away from the vertex,
by the equation z=-phi4(s,t). A point of X away from its vertex has
one normalization preimage on each ruling indexed by a point of the
corresponding beta fiber.

If some beta fiber contains distinct u,v, consider the image of
c intersected with the u ruling. Its other normalization preimage on
the v ruling must also belong to c by full inverse support. Then
`nu|c` identifies its distinct u,v points, contradicting `c isomorphic
to C`. Hence a mate would require beta to be injective on points.

## 3. A unibranch singular base point gives the jet contradiction

Choose a singular point of E. By the preceding paragraph its beta
fiber would have one point u0. At a unibranch singular point the
normalization map has zero differential. Indeed, if one local plane
coordinate pulled back with a nonzero linear coefficient, it would
form a formal parameter in k[[u-u0]]. The completed local plane-curve
ring would then equal k[[u-u0]], making E regular. Thus both local
coordinate derivatives of beta vanish at u0.

Choose beta_j nonzero at u0 and work in the target affine chart
`Y_j!=0`. Put

```
w=(z+psi4)/beta_j,
q1=beta_i/beta_j,    q2=beta_k/beta_j,
d=(phi4-psi4)/beta_j.
```

The target affine coordinate ring pulls back to
`k[w,q1(u),q2(u)]`. The source is smooth along this ruling, with
independent coordinates w and u. At u0, `q1'=q2'=0`.
The hyperplane equation trivialized by beta_j is

```
f/beta_j = w+d(u).
```

Dehomogenize the degree-b target form G by Y_j^b. It belongs to the
target coordinate ring and has pullback

```
G/Y_j^b = lambda*(w+d(u))^b.
```

Every target polynomial has zero derivative with respect to u at u0
while w is held fixed, because q1'=q2'=0. The displayed pullback has
that derivative

```
lambda*b*(w+d(u0))^(b-1)*d'(u0).
```

As an identity in the independent ruling coordinate w, this forces
`d'(u0)=0`; b is nonzero in characteristic zero. But the parametrization
of the image c at that point is

```
u -> (w=-d(u), q1(u), q2(u)).
```

All three coordinate derivatives now vanish. Since c maps
isomorphically to the smooth embedded curve C, its differential must
be nonzero. This is the contradiction.

A singular rational plane quartic has either a beta fiber of cardinality
at least two or a unibranch ramified point. Sections 2 and 3 exclude
both. This proves the proposed theorem if the divisor and map reductions
survive the independent audit.

## 4. Scope and continuation

The argument covers every finite normalization by the polarized rational
normal quartic cone, including arbitrarily degenerate singularities
of the rational plane-quartic base. It does not classify normalization
surfaces of other sectional genera or establish that all nonnormal
quartics are polarized cones or smooth scrolls. It does not extend to
positive characteristic, where b may vanish in the derivative test.

There is no use of first-normal data to bound arbitrary higher jets.
The only jet identity is an exact necessary condition for every function
in the explicitly described target ring; the global mate is a power
of a global hyperplane equation. The previously retained local
ramification-scheme counterexample therefore does not imply a gap:
no ramification-subscheme containment is asserted here.

No canonical frontier file was edited, and no unrestricted C0 or
universal STCI conclusion is claimed. The proof was sent to the existing
normal-global adversarial auditor for independent checking.
