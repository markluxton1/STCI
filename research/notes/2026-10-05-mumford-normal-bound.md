# Numerical self-intersection versus the normal sheaf

## Result and precise scope

Let `S` be an integral projective surface over an algebraically closed field
of characteristic zero, and let `C` be a smooth integral projective curve
embedded in `S`. Assume that `S` is regular at the generic point of `C`.
Let `nu:S^nu -> S` be the normalization and let `C^nu` denote the unique
curve above `C` dominating it. It maps isomorphically to `C`.

Write

\[
 N_{C/S}=\mathcal Hom_{O_C}(I_C/I_C^2,O_C).
\]

This is a line bundle: the conormal sheaf has generic rank one, and its
torsion-free quotient is a line bundle on the smooth curve. On a resolution
of `S^nu`, use Mumford's numerical pullback `(C^nu)^#`. Then

\[
 \boxed{((C^\nu)^\#)^2\leq\deg N_{C/S}.}
\]

In particular, the asserted inequality is valid when `S` is normal. It
also applies to an integral hypersurface that is normal only generically
along `C`; normality at every point of `C` is unnecessary. It does **not**
apply as stated if `S` is singular at the generic point of `C`, because the
generic conormal rank and the total-transform coefficient can change.

## Proof through the blowup of the curve ideal

Take a smooth projective surface `Y` with a proper birational morphism
`pi:Y -> S` factoring through both `S^nu` and `Bl_{I_C} S`. Such a surface
exists by resolving the normalization of the blowup. By the defining
property of the blowup, the image ideal `I_C O_Y` is invertible. Write

\[
 I_C O_Y=O_Y(-D),\qquad D=\widetilde C+Z,
 \qquad Z=\sum_i z_iE_i.
\]

Here `E_i` are exceptional curves for `Y -> S^nu`, and `Z` is effective
with integral coefficients. There is no other nonexceptional component of
`D`: the vanishing set of the pulled-back ideal maps into `C`, and `S` is
regular at its generic point. The coefficient of `Ctilde` is one for the
same generic regularity reason.

The strict transform `Ctilde` is isomorphic to `C`. One way to check this
carefully is to extend the rational lift `C -->> Y` across each missing
point using the valuative criterion for the proper map `Y -> S`. Smoothness
of `C` makes each local ring there a DVR. The extension is a section of
`Y x_S C -> C`, hence is a closed immersion, and its image is the strict
transform. The same argument gives `C^nu ~= C`.

Restrict the surjection from the pulled-back ideal onto its image to
`Ctilde`. It gives a surjection

\[
 I_C/I_C^2\longrightarrow O_{\widetilde C}(-D).
\]

The factorization through `I_C/I_C^2` is justified because the image of
`I_C` has a factor cutting out `Ctilde`, so the image of `I_C^2` vanishes
after restricting to `Ctilde`. Torsion maps to zero in the line bundle on
the right. The resulting map

\[
 (I_C/I_C^2)/\mathrm{tors}\longrightarrow
 O_{\widetilde C}(-D)
\]

is a surjection of line bundles of generic rank one, and is consequently
an isomorphism. Thus

\[
 \deg N_{C/S}=D\widetilde C
             =\widetilde C^2+Z\widetilde C.                 \tag{1}
\]

The invertible ideal `O_Y(-D)` is generated relatively over `S` by the
original curve ideal. On each complete exceptional curve this implies

\[
 (-D)E_i\geq0,\qquad DE_i\leq0.                          \tag{2}
\]

Indeed a restriction generated by sections has nonnegative degree.

Let `M=(E_i E_j)` and `c_i=Ctilde E_i`. The exceptional matrix is negative
definite, and its off-diagonal entries are nonnegative. Write the numerical
pullback as

\[
 (C^\nu)^\#=\widetilde C+R,
 \qquad R=\sum_i r_iE_i,
 \qquad c+Mr=0.
\]

Equation (2) says `c+Mz <= 0` coordinatewise. Hence

\[
 M(z-r)\leq0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z-r\geq0.           \tag{3}
\]

For completeness, the matrix implication uses no geometric assertion
beyond those already stated. For `A=-M`, `A` is positive definite with
nonpositive off-diagonal entries. If `Ax=b>=0`, split
`x=x_+-x_-`, where the nonnegative vectors have disjoint support. Then

\[
 0\leq x_-^tb=x_-^tAx_+-x_-^tAx_-
            \leq -x_-^tAx_-.
\]

Positive definiteness forces `x_-=0`. Apply this to `x=z-r` and
`b=-M(z-r)` to get (3).

Finally, numerical orthogonality to all exceptionals gives

\[
 ((C^\nu)^\#)^2=(\widetilde C+R)\widetilde C.
\]

Together with (1) and (3), and `c_i>=0`, this proves the inequality. The
proof gives the stronger exact expression

\[
 \boxed{\deg N_{C/S}-((C^\nu)^\#)^2
       =(Z-R)\widetilde C\geq0.}                          \tag{4}
\]

Neither Cartier index cancellation nor an identification of the pulled-back
adjunction class with a canonical divisor occurs in this proof.

## Ordinary-node contribution

At an ordinary `A_1` point through which `C` is smooth, its strict transform
in the blowup of the node meets the exceptional `(-2)`-curve `E` once.
Locally

\[
 I_C O_Y=O_Y(-\widetilde C-E),\qquad
 C^\#=\widetilde C+\tfrac12E.
\]

Therefore the local contribution to (4) is `1/2`.

For a direct model take

\[
 S:\ xy=z^2,\qquad C:\ x=z=0.
\]

On the `y` blowup chart put `y=t,z=tv,x=tv^2`; then `Ctilde=(v=0)` and
`I_C O_Y=(tv)`. On the `x` chart the same ideal is generated by `x`.
These formulas show coefficient one along `E` and `Ctilde`. Orthogonality
to `E` gives the numerical coefficient `1/2`.

The statement holds for an arbitrary smooth curve through the node, not
only a coordinate branch. Its tangent direction belongs to the smooth
tangent conic and the blowup resolves the node. The strict curve intersects
`E` transversely, since its ambient parameter has order one. Its ideal has
order one along `E` because a linear equation vanishing on the curve does
not vanish identically on the tangent conic. These determine the same two
coefficients.

## Consequence for the two-ramified-root split-sextic family

Use the hypotheses and notation of
`research/notes/2026-10-05-split-sextic.md`: the low branch is a smooth
section, the strict sextic is an integral surface regular generically along
that branch, and both selected ramified-root endpoints are ordinary nodes.
Let `delta` be the zero-divisor length of the nonzero Jacobian map

\[
 N_{\Gamma/B}\longrightarrow O_\Gamma(D)=O_{\mathbf P^1}(12).
\]

From `deg N_{Gamma/B}=2+6=8`, its saturated kernel is

\[
 N_{\Gamma/D}=O_{\mathbf P^1}(\delta-4).
\]

This degree computation uses the normal extension only through its
determinant; it does not split that extension.

P-020/P-023 force `Gamma#^2=13/5` for a mate-compatible type `d=1`.
The general inequality alone would imply `delta>=7`. The two endpoint
contributions in (4) improve it to

\[
 \delta-4-13/5\geq1,
 \qquad\boxed{\delta\geq8.}
\]

Each ordinary endpoint has Jacobian common order one. Thus the interior
Jacobian common divisor must have length at least six. In the finite
chart, away from the endpoints and after the stated trivializations, this
means

\[
 \deg\gcd(R,g)\geq6,
\]

provided `R` and `g` are the exact two Jacobian components there (as in the
split-sextic note). Since `R(0)=a_h u_0 != 0` and `g=zG` with
`deg G <= 6`, **if `G` is nonzero**, this further forces

\[
 \boxed{\deg G=6,\qquad G\mid R.}
\]

The identically-zero case is a separate stratum: the coefficient of
`z^6` in `G` can vanish even though the high factor is content-free. In
that case `gcd(R,0)=R`, so the degree-six argument does not apply. It is
handled separately in `2026-10-06-split22-finite-reduction.md`.

This is a necessary condition, not an STCI exclusion by itself. It turns
the previously unproved normal-sheaf reduction into a rigorous route to
explicit finite common-factor equations.

## Primary-source context and audit status

The negative-definite exceptional intersection matrix and the numerical
pullback construction are classical. A primary reference is David Mumford,
*The topology of normal singularities of an algebraic surface and a
criterion for simplicity*, Publ. Math. IHES **9** (1961), 5–22: the
negative-definiteness proof is on printed page 6, and the numerical
intersection construction is on printed pages 17–18.

- [Mumford's paper, Numdam PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/BF02698717.pdf)
- [Author-hosted copy](https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/mumford/alg_geom/papers/1961a--TopSingSurface-NumDam.pdf)

The inequality and exact defect formula above are proved here from the
blowup universal property and the elementary matrix lemma. No claim is
made that this presentation is novel. They should receive an independent
proof audit before integration into the canonical research record.
