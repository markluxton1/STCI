# A tropical complete intersection, its endpoint normal maps, and characteristic

Date: 2026-10-31. Status: **independently reviewed provisional deductions**;
see the [bounded audit](2026-10-31-tropical-model-characteristic-audit.md).
This responds to the user's tropical-compactification and characteristic
suggestions. It supplies no unrestricted characteristic-zero STCI conclusion.

Let C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4] in P3 over an algebraically closed field.
The toric construction and normal-map calculation below work in every
characteristic. Write p0=[1:0:0:0] and pInfinity=[0:0:0:1].

## 1. An actual tropical compactification with two global equations

On the dense torus of P3, set
\[
 t=x_1/x_0,\qquad u=x_0^2x_2/x_1^3,\qquad
 v=x_0^3x_3/x_1^4.
\]
This is a unimodular change of torus coordinates: its inverse is
\((x_1/x_0,x_2/x_0,x_3/x_0)=(t,ut^3,vt^4)\).
Thus \(C^\circ\) is the subtorus \(u=v=1\). Its tropicalization over
the trivially valued field is the line generated by (1,3,4) in the
original cocharacter coordinates. The fan consisting of its two rays
and the zero cone has toric variety
\[
Y=\mathbf P^1_t\times\mathbf G_m^2{}_{u,v}.
\]
The proper closure is
\[
\overline C=\mathbf P^1\times\{(1,1)\}
=V_Y(u-1,v-1).
\tag{1}
\]
It is a **scheme-theoretic complete intersection of global functions**.

This is tropical in Tevelev's precise sense. The closure is proper and
the multiplication map
\[
\mathbf G_m^3\times\overline C\longrightarrow Y,
\quad (a,b,c;z)\longmapsto(a\cdot z,b,c)
\]
is smooth and surjective: the change of source coordinate z'=a·z makes
it a projection with fiber G_m. Thus the compactification is also schön.
The definition requires properness of the curve, not of Y; Y is not proper.
See [Tevelev, Definitions 1.1 and 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0412329).

## 2. The map back to P3 and its two normal lattices

There is a birational toric morphism \(\phi:Y\to\mathbf P^3\), given
on the two charts, with \(\tau=t^{-1}\), by
\[
(t,u,v)\longmapsto[1:t:ut^3:vt^4],
\qquad
(\tau,u,v)\longmapsto[\tau^4/v:\tau^3/v:(u/v)\tau:1].
\tag{2}
\]
The formulas agree on the overlap. Its restriction to \(\overline C\)
is the original embedding, while the two boundary tori map to p0 and
pInfinity. The morphism is not proper: these fibers are G_m^2.

The induced normal-bundle map is
\[
\mathcal O_C^2=N_{\overline C/Y}\longrightarrow N_{C/\mathbf P^3}.
\]
It is an isomorphism off the endpoints. Its endpoint cokernels have
invariant factors (3,4), in every characteristic.

Here is the actual calculation. Put U=u-1,V=v-1. On x0=1 use the
normal coordinates A=x2−x1^3, B=x3−x1^4. Their pullbacks are exactly
\[
A=t^3U,\qquad B=t^4V.
\tag{3}
\]
At infinity put a=x0/x3, b=x1/x3, c=x2/x3 and use normal coordinates
\(A_\infty=a-c^4,\ B_\infty=b-c^3\). Modulo (U,V)^2, (2) gives
\[
\begin{pmatrix} A_\infty\\B_\infty\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}-4\tau^4&3\tau^4\\-3\tau^3&2\tau^3\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}U\\V\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4}
\]
The determinant is \(\tau^7\). The constant matrix after factoring
the row powers has determinant one over Z; the smaller invariant factor
is \(\tau^3\). Consequently the normal map is injective and its torsion
cokernel has local modules
\[
\bigl(k[t]_{(t)}/(t^3)\oplus k[t]_{(t)}/(t^4)\bigr)_{p_0},
\quad
\bigl(k[\tau]_{(\tau)}/(\tau^3)\oplus
k[\tau]_{(\tau)}/(\tau^4)\bigr)_{p_\infty}.
\tag{5}
\]
Its total length is fourteen, the degree of the projective normal bundle.
This does not determine that bundle's splitting: the already promoted
[R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md) gives
\(N=\mathcal O(7)^2\) when 2 is invertible, and
\(N=\mathcal O(6)\oplus\mathcal O(8)\) in characteristic two.

Geometrically, the tropical ambient retains two independent transverse
coordinates at both endpoints. The projective map collapses the boundary
tori and sends these normal directions to directions vanishing to orders
three and four. Formula (5) measures the differential of this actual
morphism; it is not a residual-curve count or a higher-neighborhood theorem.

The two global equations in (1) do not descend as regular functions on P3.
Clearing their displayed denominators gives
\[
x_0^2x_2-x_1^3=0,\qquad x_0^3x_3-x_1^4=0.
\tag{6}
\]
On x0≠0 these cut out C, but at x0=0 they leave the entire line
x0=x1=0. Thus this particular descent introduces an actual residual
curve. This does not prove that every possible choice of equations must
do so. Conversely the boundary tori in (2) map to points; those exceptional
surfaces are not themselves residual curves downstairs.

## 3. Two different characteristic effects

For any proper pair Z=V(F_a,G_b) with Z_red=C, Z denotes the full
nonreduced intersection, of generic thickness m=ab/4. The projection
\(\pi=[x_0:x_3]\) is finite flat of degree ab. Its **reduction** is
\[
f:C\to\mathbf P^1,\qquad [s:t]\mapsto[s^4:t^4].
\]
In characteristic zero or odd characteristic this is separable, with
ramification index four at each endpoint and no other ramification.
In characteristic two it is purely inseparable of degree four. The full
nonreduced Z is not an ordinary separable branched cover in either case.

There is a separate multiplicity effect. Locally choose a parameter z on
C and lift it to the smooth ambient threefold. The completed algebra of
Z is free of rank m over k[[z]], and
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal O_Z/k[[z]]}(h)=m\bar h.
\tag{7}
\]
This follows over the fraction field by the filtration of a local Artin
algebra by its maximal ideal, and then everywhere by freeness. The trace
element is the relative Jacobian times a duality frame. If the field
characteristic divides m, that Jacobian is zero in the algebra; if m is
invertible, it generates the annihilator of the nilradical. This is the
standard input used in the [Jacobian/contact note](2026-10-31-cyclic-cover-duality.md),
not a characteristic-zero-only definition of trace.

For a concrete **odd-characteristic** comparison, in characteristic seven
take
\[
F=x_1^4-x_0^3x_3,\qquad G=x_2^7-x_0x_1x_3^5.
\tag{8}
\]
On x0=1, eliminate x3=x1^4: G becomes
\(x_2^7-x_1^{21}=(x_2-x_1^3)^7\). On x0=0, F and G force
x1=x2=0. Therefore (8) is an STCI with generic thickness seven, while
the reduced map f is still tamely ramified. The plane-pencil Jacobian
M12 is identically zero, since F does not involve x2 and G_x2=0.
This is a coordinate-reflected instance of existing P-028 in
[RESEARCH_RECORD.md](../RESEARCH_RECORD.md), not a new construction.
The characteristic-two (4,4) example is already
[R-0021](../results/quartic_mu4_degeneration.md).

Thus odd-characteristic existence cannot be explained only by wild
ramification of f. Vanishing of the multiplicity-weighted trace is an
additional characteristic-sensitive mechanism. The same tropical scheme
CI (1) exists in every characteristic; that fact alone does not resolve
descent to P3 in characteristic zero.

## 4. What the general tropical literature establishes

[Luxton–Qu, Theorems 1.4–1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.2009) prove in
characteristic zero that every variety has a schön very affine open, and
every fan supported on the tropicalization of a schön variety gives a
schön compactification. Combined with
[Tevelev, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0412329), a smooth
projective curve therefore admits such a compactification with a regular
embedding in a smooth toric ambient; its smooth proper model is the
original abstract curve.

Regular embedding gives a **local** complete intersection. These cited
statements do not assert that codimension-many global Cartier divisors
cut out the entire closure set-theoretically. The stronger claim after
suitable reembedding is not proved or disproved here. Our explicit C has
the stronger property (1) because its torus part is itself a subtorus.
No general-curve claim should be inferred from that special feature.

The linked independent audit reconstructs the explicit tropical model,
both normal matrices, characteristic qualifications, and the scope of the
general literature. No material correction was required. No canonical
result is promoted.
