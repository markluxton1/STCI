## 2. Detailed Breakdown of Mathematical Errors

### A. Fatal Error in Branch 1 (Normal Carriers)
In `independent_stci/normal_carriers/02_complete_normal_carrier_exclusion.md`, it was claimed that for every isolated singularity on a normal surface:
$$-(M_P^{-1})_{j, j} \le \frac{r_P}{4}$$
where $r_P$ is the local Cartier index of $C_0$ at $P$.
From this, it was claimed that $\sum_P c_{P, j_P} \le 2a - 2$, leading to $C_0^2 \le 12 - 2a$ and $\Gamma_X(C_0) > 0$.

**The Counterexample:**
Consider an $A_3$ rational double point ($n = 4$):
The intersection matrix $M$ is the $3 \times 3$ Cartan matrix of type $A_3$:
$$M = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$$
Its inverse $-M^{-1}$ has diagonal entries:
$$-(M^{-1})_{1, 1} = \frac{1(4 - 1)}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$$
$$-(M^{-1})_{2, 2} = \frac{2(4 - 2)}{4} = 1$$
$$-(M^{-1})_{3, 3} = \frac{3(4 - 3)}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$$

For the middle exceptional component $j = 2$:
The class in the local class group $\operatorname{Cl}(A_3) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ generated by $E_2$ has order:
$$\operatorname{ord}(E_2) = \frac{4}{\gcd(4, 2)} = 2$$
Therefore, a smooth curve $C$ whose strict transform intersects $E_2$ transversally has **local Cartier index $r_P = 2$**.
However:
$$-(M^{-1})_{2, 2} = 1 > \frac{r_P}{4} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}!$$
The bound $c \le r_P / 4$ fails by a factor of 2.
More generally, whenever $j$ and $n$ share a common factor $g = \gcd(n, j) > 1$, the local Cartier index is $r = n / g < n$, and:
$$-(M^{-1})_{j, j} = \frac{j(n - j)}{n} = \frac{j}{g} \frac{g(n - j)}{n} \text{ can be strictly larger than } \frac{r}{4}$$
For instance:
- $n = 6, j = 3 \implies r = 2$, but $c = \frac{3(3)}{6} = 1.5 > \frac{2}{4} = 0.5$ (factor of 3 failure).
- $n = 8, j = 4 \implies r = 2$, but $c = \frac{4(4)}{8} = 2.0 > \frac{2}{4} = 0.5$ (factor of 4 failure).
- $n = 2m, j = m \implies r = 2$, but $c = \frac{m}{2} \to \infty$ as $m \to \infty$, while $r/4 = 1/2$ remains constant!

Thus, the local Cartier index $r_P = 2$ can carry an arbitrarily large self-intersection coefficient $c = m/2$, totally invalidating the bound $\sum c_P \le 2a - 2$ and the bound $C_0^2 \le 12 - 2a$.

Additionally:
- The discrepancy argument incorrectly assumed discrepancies $-k_P \ge 0$ for arbitrary normal surface singularities. For non-canonical surface singularities, discrepancies can be negative, changing the inequality direction.
- The gradient vanishing bound $\operatorname{ord}_P(\nabla F|_{C_0}) \ge r_P - 1$ was asserted without proof for general non-ADE singularities.

### B. Substantial Gaps in Branch 2 (Non-Normal Carriers)
In `independent_stci/nonnormal_carriers/01_cartier_singleton_exclusion.md`:
1. **Multiplicity vs. Distinct Points:** The calculation $\Delta \cdot c = 20$ (or 51) is an intersection multiplicity, not a count of distinct geometric points. A single non-transverse or non-reduced contact can absorb arbitrarily high intersection multiplicity without producing 20 distinct points.
2. **Immersion vs. Disjoint Sheets:** The claim that non-vanishing of the transverse differential $\alpha(P) \neq 0$ implies the existence of a second distinct sheet off $c$ is an unproved leap. A conductor curve may have cuspidal, non-reduced, or higher-order tangential singularities that do not correspond to two distinct transverse sheets.
3. Therefore, $p_1^* c \neq p_2^* c$ was not established in full geometric generality.

### C. Unproved Reductions in Branch 3 (Entirely Thick Carriers)
In `independent_stci/FINAL_STCI_RESOLUTION.md`:
1. The moving pure-power obstruction and the degree contradiction $(d - 1)(d - 2) \ge 4r(r - 1)$ with $d \le r + 2$ applies specifically to movement-two root lifts under specific formal assumptions.
2. It was not proved that an arbitrary entirely thick STCI presentation $(F, G)$ of $C_0$ produces such a movement-two root lift.
3. The parameter $e = 4$ was substituted into formulas without establishing that arbitrary higher-order thick presentations preserve the degree-four balanced conormal bundle structure without degenerations.
