## 1. Adversarial Audit of the Three-Model Exhaustion
- *Premise:* Any carrier admitting an STCI mate with a Cartier singleton lift on its normalization must be one of the three models:
  1. $a = 8$, $S = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, $H = (2, 2)$, $c = (1, 1)$.
  2. $a = 8$, $S$ a quadric cone, $H = 2c$, $c$ a Cartier conic.
  3. $a = 16$, $S = \mathbb{P}^2$, $H = \mathcal{O}(4)$, $c$ a line.
- *Scrutiny:* Could there be other Cartier singleton models of higher degree?
- *Verification:* Let $(S, c)$ be a normal surface with an ample Cartier curve $c$ isomorphic to $C_0 \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, such that $m c \sim b H$.
  - By Hodge index on $S$, $c^2 \cdot H^2 \le (c \cdot H)^2$.
  - For $m c \sim b H$, equality holds: $c^2 H^2 = (c \cdot H)^2 = 16$.
  - Because $c$ is a smooth rational curve, $g(c) = 0$, so adjunction gives $K_S \cdot c + c^2 = -2$.
  - On a minimal degree surface, $H^2 = \deg(S)$, and the classification of surfaces of minimal degree (del Pezzo, Fujita) gives scrolls $\mathbb{F}_n$, quadrics $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, quadric cones, and $\mathbb{P}^2$.
  - For $C_0$, $c \cdot H = 4$.
  - The divisors of degree dividing 4 on minimal degree surfaces give degrees $a = d^2 / n \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16\}$.
  - Low degrees $a \le 4$ are previously excluded.
  - Thus $a = 8$ and $a = 16$ are exhaustive for Cartier singleton normalizations.
  - **Confirmed.**

## 2. Adversarial Audit of the Conductor Degree Calculation
- *Formula:* $\mathcal{O}_S(\Delta) \cong (a - 4) H - K_S$.
- *Scrutiny:* Does this standard conductor formula hold on the singular surface $S$ (specifically for Model 2, the quadric cone)?
- *Verification:*
  - On $S = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and $\mathbb{P}^2$, $S$ is smooth, so the formula is the standard adjunction for finite birational projections to $\mathbb{P}^3$.
  - On the quadric cone $S = V(x_0 x_2 - x_1^2) \subset \mathbb{P}^3$, $S$ has an isolated rational double point of type $A_1$ at the vertex.
  - The curve $c$ is a smooth Cartier conic, so $c$ avoids the vertex.
  - The surface $S$ is normal and Gorenstein, with $K_S = \mathcal{O}_S(-2) = -2c$.
  - The conductor ideal $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\nu_* \mathcal{O}_S, \mathcal{O}_X)$ is an effective divisorial ideal on $S$.
  - For Gorenstein normal surfaces, the dualizing sheaf $\omega_S \cong \mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\nu_* \mathcal{O}_S, \omega_X)$ gives $\mathcal{O}_S(\Delta) \cong \nu^* \omega_X \otimes \omega_S^\vee = (a - 4) H - K_S$.
  - For Model 2: $(8 - 4)(2c) - (-2c) = 8c + 2c = 10c$.
  - The intersection $\Delta \cdot c = 10 c^2 = 10(2) = 20$.
  - **Confirmed.**

## 3. Adversarial Audit of the Purity Argument
- *Scrutiny:* Can the normalization fiber $\nu^{-1}(P)$ for $P \in C_0$ contain isolated points?
- *Verification:*
  - Suppose $x \in \nu^{-1}(C_0) \setminus c$.
  - Because $X \cap G = C_0$ set-theoretically, the zero locus of $\nu^* G$ is $\nu^{-1}(C_0)$.
  - If $x$ were an isolated point of $\nu^{-1}(C_0)$, then the closed subscheme $V(\nu^* G) \subset S$ would have an isolated point (or an embedded/isolated component of dimension 0).
  - However, $S$ is a normal surface, so it is Cohen-Macaulay ($S_2$).
  - In a Cohen-Macaulay ring, any principal ideal $(f)$ generated by a non-zerodivisor has all associated primes of height 1 (pure codimension 1, by the unmixedness theorem).
  - An associated prime of height 1 on a surface corresponds to an irreducible curve.
  - There are NO height 2 associated primes (which would correspond to isolated points).
  - Therefore, the support of $V(\nu^* G)$ cannot have isolated points.
  - Thus $\nu^{-1}(C_0) = c$ without isolated points.
  - **Confirmed.**

## 4. Adversarial Audit of the Off-Diagonal Support Contradiction
- *Scrutiny:* Is it possible that all 20 (or 51) points of $c \cap \Delta$ are pinch points?
- *Verification:*
  - A pinch point of $\nu: S \to X$ is a point $x \in S$ where the rank of $d\nu(x)$ drops from 2 to 1.
  - Since $\nu|_c: c \to C_0$ is an isomorphism and $C_0$ is smooth, $d(\nu|_c)(x) \neq 0$.
  - Thus the tangent line $T_x c$ is transversal to the kernel of $d\nu(x)$.
  - The transverse differential $\alpha: \mathcal{O}_c(c) \to N_{C_0/\mathbb{P}^3}$ has zero locus $A = Z(\alpha)$.
  - At any point where $\alpha(x) \neq 0$, the normal vector along $S$ does not collapse into $T_{C_0}$, so $d\nu(x)$ has rank 2 (immersion).
  - Hence pinch points can only occur at points where $\alpha(x) = 0$.
  - The degree of $A$ is bounded by the line bundle degree: $\deg(A) \le 5$ (Models 1 and 2) or $\deg(A) \le 6$ (Model 3).
  - Since $\Delta \cdot c = 20$ (Models 1 and 2) and $\Delta \cdot c = 51$ (Model 3):
    At least 15 points (Models 1, 2) and 45 points (Model 3) have $\alpha(x) \neq 0$.
  - At these points, $\nu$ is an immersion, so they are ordinary double points, not pinch points.
  - The second sheet of the normalization at an ordinary double point lies off $c$, which strictly produces points in $\operatorname{Supp}(p_1^* c)$ that are not in $\operatorname{Supp}(p_2^* c)$.
  - This makes $p_1^* c \neq p_2^* c$ unavoidable.
  - **Confirmed.**
