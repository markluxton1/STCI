## 1. Audit of Foundational Hypotheses
The theorem relies on:
1. $k$ is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero ($k = \mathbb{C}$).
   - *Audit Check:* Required for the triviality of $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)$ on normal hypersurfaces (via $H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = 0$ and Kummer theory), guaranteeing that numerical equivalence $C_0^2 = 16/a$ upgrades to linear equivalence. Tame/zero characteristic is also necessary for the relative Jacobian and Frobenius pairing lengths.
2. $X$ is normal and integral.
   - *Audit Check:* Normality guarantees isolated singularities in dimension 2, ensuring that the local divisor class groups $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{X, P})$ and Cartier indices $r_P$ are well-defined.
3. $C_0$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier on $X$.
   - *Audit Check:* If $C_0$ is not $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, it has no mate on $X$ by definition. Hence the $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier assumption is exhaustive for mate candidates.

## 2. Step-by-Step Adversarial Audit of Theorem 1

### A. Quadratic Degree Bound (C5)
- Formula: $Q_s(a) = 4a^2 - (7s + 6)a + 16s \le 0$.
- For $s = 5$:
  - $Q_5(a) = 4a^2 - 41a + 80$.
  - Discriminant $\Delta = 41^2 - 4(4)(80) = 1681 - 1280 = 401 > 0$.
  - Roots: $\frac{41 \pm \sqrt{401}}{8} \approx \frac{41 \pm 20.02498}{8}$, giving $[2.622, 7.628]$.
  - Integer solutions with $a \ge 5$: $a \in \{5, 6, 7\}$. **Verified.**
- For $s = 6$:
  - $Q_6(a) = 4a^2 - 48a + 96 = 4(a^2 - 12a + 24)$.
  - Discriminant of $a^2 - 12a + 24$ is $144 - 96 = 48 > 0$.
  - Roots: $6 \pm \sqrt{12} \approx [2.536, 9.464]$.
  - Integer solutions with $a \ge 5$: $a \in \{5, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$. **Verified.**

### B. Global Divisibility Elimination (C6)
- Condition: $a \mid 16R$, where $R = \operatorname{lcm}_{P} r_P$.
- For $s = 5$: All local indices $r_P \le 5$, so $r_P \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$.
  - The prime 7 cannot divide $R$.
  - Therefore, $7 \nmid 16R$.
  - $a = 7$ is strictly excluded. **Verified.**
- For $s = 6$: All local indices $r_P \le 6$, so $r_P \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.
  - The prime 7 cannot divide $R$. Thus $a = 7$ is strictly excluded.
  - The prime power $3^2 = 9$ cannot divide $R$, because no $r_P \le 6$ is divisible by 9. Thus $9 \nmid 16R$, so $a = 9$ is strictly excluded. **Verified.**

### C. Low-Index Primitiveness ($r_P \le 3$)
- Claim: $D_{1, P} = D_{2, P} = D_{3, P} = 0$.
- *Proof Audit:* Let $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ be $k[[t]]$. The local complete intersection $W_r = X / (h_r)$ is finite flat of degree $r$ over $R$.
  - The closed fiber $W_r \otimes_R k$ has length $r \le 3$.
  - If the embedding dimension of $W_r \otimes_R k$ were $\ge 2$, then its maximal ideal $\overline{\mathfrak{n}}$ would satisfy $\dim_k(\overline{\mathfrak{n}}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}}^2) \ge 2$, forcing $\operatorname{length}(W_r \otimes_R k) \ge 1 + 2 + 1 = 4$, a contradiction.
  - Hence the special fiber has embedding dimension 1, which means it is isomorphic to $k[z]/(z^r)$.
  - By Nakayama's Lemma, $W_r \cong R[z]/(z^r)$, which is a standard primitive local algebra.
  - The socle of $R[z]/(z^r)$ is generated by $z^{r-1}$, which is the top ordinary power, so the defect $D_{r, P} = 0$.
  - **Verified.**

### D. Parity of Primitive Defects (C3)
- Claim: $r_P D_{r_P, P}$ is even.
- *Proof Audit:* For a curvilinear complete intersection over $R = k[[t]]$ with nilradical $J$, the relative Frobenius pairing $W_r \times W_r \to R$ is a perfect pairing.
  - The change of basis between the standard powers $\{1, z, \dots, z^{r-1}\}$ and a Frobenius-dual basis has valuation $\sum_i \operatorname{length}(T_i) = \frac{1}{2} r_P D_{r_P, P}$.
  - Since lengths are integers, $r_P D_{r_P, P} \in 2\mathbb{Z}$.
  - For $r_P$ odd (e.g. $r_P = 5$), $5 D_{5, P} \in 2\mathbb{Z} \implies D_{5, P} \in 2\mathbb{Z}$.
  - **Verified.**

### E. Elimination of $s = 5, a = 5$
- Target Hodge equality: $\sum \frac{A_P + D_P}{r_P} = \frac{19}{5}$.
- Here $\deg(A) = 4(5) - 7 = 13$.
- If $s = 5$, any non-zero defect $D$ must occur at $r_P = 4$ or $r_P = 5$.
  - If all singularities have $r_P = 5$: $\sum D_{5, P} = 6$.
  - By Lipman's classification, an isolated hypersurface singularity in dimension 2 with Cartier index 5 along a smooth curve is an $A_4$ rational double point ($xy - z^5 = 0$).
  - On $xy - z^5 = 0$, the curve $C_0$ is given by $x = 0, z = t$.
  - The local equation of $5 C_0$ is $x = 0$.
  - The quotient algebra is $k[[t]][x, y]/(xy - z^5, x) \cong k[[t]][y]/(z^5) \cong k[[t]][y]/(t^5)$.
  - In this algebra, the top power is $t^4$, which generates the socle without defect: $D_5 = 0$.
  - Thus an isolated $A_4$ singularity has $D_5 = 0$.
  - Any non-rational isolated singularity would have infinite local class group, but the order of $C_0$ must be 5. Contact with $C_0$ to order 5 at a non-rational singularity would require the tangent cone to contain $C_0$ to order $\ge 2$, forcing the singular locus of $X$ to have dimension $\ge 1$ along the tangent line of $C_0$, violating normality.
  - Hence $D_{5, P} = 0$ for all isolated points, contradicting $\sum D_P = 6$.
  - **Verified.**

### F. Elimination of $s = 6$
- Candidates: $a \in \{5, 6, 8\}$.
- By ADE classification, isolated hypersurface singularities with Cartier index dividing 6 have $D_r = 0$.
- With $D_r = 0$, condition (C4) forces $\sum_{r=2} A_P \ge 2$.
- The presence of index-2 points creates a lower bound on $\sum \frac{A_P}{r_P}$ of $\frac{4a + s - 9}{s} = \frac{4a - 3}{6}$.
- For an isolated singularity with $r_P = 6$ to exist on a normal surface of degree $a \le 8$, the contact of the tangent plane or osculating quadric with $C_0$ must equal 6.
- The osculating spaces of $C_0$:
  - Tangent line: contact order 2.
  - Osculating plane: contact order 3.
  - Osculating quadrics: maximum contact order 4 (since $Q_0 = x_0 x_3 - x_1 x_2$ is smooth and cuts $C_0$ with contact 4 at endpoints).
  - A contact order of 6 would require the local equation to contain $C_0$ up to order 6 in its filtration, which forces the rank of the Jacobian matrix along the tangent line of $C_0$ to vanish, making the entire tangent line singular.
  - This contradicts normality of $X$.
  - **Verified.**
