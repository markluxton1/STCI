# Foundational Invariants and Baseline Premise Ledger for $C_0$

## 1. Geometric Definition of $C_0$
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero ($k = \mathbb{C}$).
The curve $C_0 \subset \mathbb{P}^3_k$ is the smooth rational curve given by the parametric map $\mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^3$:
$$(s : t) \mapsto [s^4 : s^3 t : s t^3 : t^4] = [x_0 : x_1 : x_2 : x_3]$$

### Invariants:
- **Degree:** $e = 4$.
- **Arithmetic & Geometric Genus:** $g(C_0) = 0$.
- **Conormal Bundle:** 
  The conormal bundle of $C_0$ in $\mathbb{P}^3$ splits as:
  $$N^*_{C_0/\mathbb{P}^3} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-7) \oplus \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-7)$$
  Hence $C_0$ is balanced with conormal slope $\ell = 7$.

### Homogeneous Ideal $I(C_0)$:
The homogeneous ideal $I(C_0) \subset k[x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3]$ is minimally generated by four polynomials:
1. Unique quadric:
   $$Q_0 = x_0 x_3 - x_1 x_2$$
2. Three cubics:
   $$K_1 = x_1^3 - x_0^2 x_2, \quad K_2 = x_0 x_2^2 - x_1^2 x_3, \quad K_3 = x_2^3 - x_1 x_3^2$$

## 2. Established Baseline Exclusions
1. **Quadric Carrier ($a = 2$):**
   The quadric $Q_0$ is a smooth Segre quadric $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$. The curve $C_0$ has bi-degree $(1, 3)$ (or $(3, 1)$). The Picard group is $\operatorname{Pic}(Q_0) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}$, and every ambient hypersurface of degree $b$ cuts out a divisor of bi-degree $(b, b)$. A relation $m [C_0] \sim b H$ would require $(m, 3m) = (b, b)$, forcing $m = 3m$, which has no non-zero solution. Hence $Q_0$ admits no mate.
2. **Cubic and Quartic Carriers ($a = 3, 4$):**
   Accepted established exclusions show that no integral cubic or quartic carrier containing $C_0$ admits an STCI mate in any positive degree $b$.
   **Consequence:** Any carrier $X$ in an STCI pair $(X, G)$ for $C_0$ must satisfy:
   $$\deg(X) = a \ge 5, \quad \deg(G) = b \ge 5$$

## 3. The Normal Carrier Extremal-Ray Theorem (Premise)
Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a normal integral hypersurface of degree $a \ge 5$ containing $C_0$.
Let $H = \mathcal{O}_X(1)$.

**Theorem (Extremal-Ray Sufficiency):**
The following are equivalent:
1. There exists a hypersurface $G$ not containing $X$ such that $\operatorname{Supp}(X \cap G) = C_0$.
2. Some positive Cartier multiple $m C_0$ is linearly equivalent to $b H$ on $X$.
3. $C_0$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier on $X$ and satisfies the Hodge-index equality:
   $$C_0^2 = \frac{e^2}{a} = \frac{16}{a}$$
4. The normal Hodge gap vanishes:
   $$\Gamma_X(C_0) := \frac{16}{a} - C_0^2 = 0$$

### Intrinsic Divisor Formula for $C_0^2$:
For each point $P \in C_0 \cap \operatorname{Sing}(X)$:
- $r_P \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$: the actual local Cartier index of $C_0$ on $X$ at $P$.
- $A_P \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$: the vanishing order of the saturated first normal symbol along $C_0$.
- $D_{r_P, P} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$: the top ordinary-power/socle defect of the local Cartier multiple $r_P C_0$.

Then:
$$C_0^2 = \ell - \sum_{P \in C_0 \cap \operatorname{Sing}(X)} \frac{A_P + D_{r_P, P}}{r_P} = 7 - \sum_P \frac{A_P + D_{r_P, P}}{r_P}$$
Therefore, the normal Hodge gap is:
$$\Gamma_X(C_0) = \sum_{P \in C_0 \cap \operatorname{Sing}(X)} \frac{A_P + D_{r_P, P}}{r_P} - \left( 7 - \frac{16}{a} \right) \ge 0$$
A mate exists on $X$ if and only if $\Gamma_X(C_0) = 0$.
