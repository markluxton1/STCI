# Geometric integration of six synthesis chapters

**Status:** editorial cross-document map and research agenda, not theorem authority. This note compares the six revised synthesis chapters as they stand on main on 2026-11-01. No new theorem or implication is asserted. Result records and the scoped proofs in the linked chapters retain authority.

## One geometric problem, several moduli problems

Fix the embedded rational quartic \(C\subset\mathbf P^3\), with ideal \(I\), and a degree pair \((a,b)\). A supported complete intersection is a homogeneous regular-sequence ideal \(J=(F_a,G_b)\) with \(\sqrt J=I\). Its Hilbert polynomial is fixed by \((a,b)\); its generic length along \(C\) is \(ab/4\).

The natural ambient spaces must not be collapsed:

1. **Supported Hilbert locus:** subschemes with the specified Hilbert polynomial and reduced support \(C\). It remembers the entire ideal, including endpoint and embedded structure, but does not impose two generators.
2. **Marked thickening/Quot locus:** embedded lower multiple structures in a finite neighborhood of \(C\). This is a useful incidence witness during residual absorption, not necessarily the full primary component.
3. **Actual-equation incidence:** pairs of homogeneous sections containing a given thickening, with the relevant source, compatibility, properness, and support conditions. A normal-jet solution or a rank-stratum solution alone does not imply membership.
4. **Torus-fixed boundary:** fixed points of the supported Hilbert locus obtained by flat limits of full ideals. These can acquire additional generators. A fixed supported *complete-intersection* ideal is a strictly stronger condition.

A proposed global obstruction must bridge the third and fourth spaces, not simply classify torus eigenform pairs.

## Shared vocabulary and distinctions to preserve

- **Full torus limit** means the flat limit of the *entire saturated ideal*, not the ideal generated by two initial forms. In particular it can retain support while losing the two-generator property.
- **Normal initial ideal** is the full initial ideal of \(J\) for the \(I\)-filtration, including cancellation-generated elements. It is not generated in general by the two displayed first normal symbols. The comparison defect of the filtered equation module is a separate invariant.
- **Endpoint gluing defect** for a finite orbit-family modification is not automatically the normalization conductor of a reduced curve. Likewise a local conductor descent test is not an abstract ribbon extension or a global hypersurface lift.
- **Residual absorption** means redistribution of the fundamental cycle inside a flat family of total intersections. The full \(C\)-primary component can jump in degree and need not form a flat family; a marked lower witness may remain flat.
- **Rank stratum** is a scheme-theoretic determinantal stratum on which a bundle map locally splits. Constant geometric fiber rank over a nonreduced base does not suffice. A zero locus of a cokernel-section is a low-order incidence, not a supported CI certificate.
- **Characteristic scope** must travel with each theorem. Characteristic-two thickening and Frobenius-descent examples are controls, not characteristic-zero constructions.

## Recommended next geometric experiment

**One useful local experiment: the supported Hilbert boundary for a fixed degree pair.** For a chosen \((a,b)\), first fix the complete-intersection Hilbert polynomial and a bounded neighborhood \(I^N\subset J\subset I\). For a fixed integer N, the bounded supported Hilbert locus defined by I^N ⊂ J ⊂ I and the prescribed Hilbert polynomial is closed in the Hilbert scheme (equivalently realized through the corresponding Quot construction). Because the torus preserves C and its ideal powers, this bounded locus is torus-stable, and full-ideal torus limits remain in it. The unrestricted exact-support locus need not be closed. For a supported complete intersection of degrees (a,b), the finite-length generic structure and its global scheme-theoretic support give some exponent N with I^N ⊂ J; uniformity across a chosen family must be established before applying this bounded argument. Prefer organizing the moving ideals by both opposite full-ideal limits and testing two-generation on their attracting intersections. Studying deformations of a selected fixed ideal remains a useful local method, not the default classification.

A practical staged test is:

1. Identify a *specified* torus-fixed supported ideal of the required Hilbert polynomial, or prove a bounded list of possible fixed ideals. Do not assume eigenform pairs describe these ideals.
2. Compute the embedded Hilbert tangent/obstruction problem and its torus weights at that ideal, retaining the full initial ideal and endpoint structure.
3. Add the **actual homogeneous equation incidence** and check whether a supported deformation can enter the open complete-intersection locus. This comparison is the missing theorem, not a consequence of tangent weights alone.
4. For a conditional sextic source lane, use the proven rank-stratum incidence and all pivot opens as an exact finite subproblem, without identifying that lane with arbitrary sextic pairs.
5. Track genus/Hilbert polynomial and the full equation module throughout; do not replace the flat limit by its pure hull or discard embedded endpoint structure.

**Secondary target:** on the blowup of \(C\), interpret the full I-adic associated graded quotient gr_I(O/J) = gr_I(O)/in_I(J) as a subscheme of the normal cone and compare its deformation theory with the marked Quot incidence. Its projectivization on the exceptional ruled surface records projective normal directions, but can discard zero-section-supported information and does not retain all higher-order relations. An explicit comparison map, including its kernel and cokernel, would be a meaningful bridge between the normal-filtration and Hilbert-boundary programs.

## Editorial findings and remaining checks

The six documents are compatible at the level of their stated geometric distinctions. No contradiction was identified in the reviewed framing passages. This is **not** an independent verification of every mathematical proof in these long chapters.

Three potential sources of future confusion merit ongoing checks:

1. The words *fixed*, *invariant*, and *supported* must always identify whether they refer to the reduced curve, a full ideal, or a chosen pair of equations.
2. Quot, Hilbert and actual-source incidence should be related by explicitly constructed morphisms; no automatic equivalence or smoothness assertion should be inferred from the conceptual diagrams.
3. A constant transverse quotient, a fixed root/jet model, and a global supported complete intersection occupy successively broader or different parameter spaces. Each exclusion must state exactly which space it covers.

A bounded degree-pair study of full-ideal attracting strata and moving-ideal generators is one useful next mathematical test, not a required new research lane. The [attracting-strata synthesis](torus_attracting_strata_and_generator_incidence.md) states the two-direction refinement and its limitations.

## Updated organizing principle: strongly consider invariant-limit strata and two-generation

A particularly useful role for the torus is **stratification**, not a search for individually smoothable fixed ideals. For a fixed degree pair (a,b) and a fixed bound N, consider the supported Hilbert locus with I^N contained in J contained in I and the complete-intersection Hilbert polynomial. Consider organizing its points by the *full flat T-limit* J_0. Each fiber of this limit assignment is an attracting set; call it a stratum only with the relevant scheme-theoretic or locally closed structure specified. On a projective Hilbert scheme these attracting sets can be studied by equivariant methods, but a finite, smooth or affine-cell decomposition is not automatic.

A central question on each attracting set is **which moving ideals J are generated by two homogeneous equations of degrees a and b?** The invariant ideal J_0 need not itself have two generators. At the algebraic level use the graded minimal-generator spaces M_d(J)=J_d/(S_+J)_d, together with the actual multiplication and relation maps. For (6,6), the target is exactly two minimal generators in degree six and none in other degrees; numerical conditions on a few graded pieces alone do not establish this. Equivalently, test whether a sextic pencil generates the full saturated ideal scheme-theoretically, rather than merely vanishing on its subscheme.

For a stratification-based pilot, a useful order is: (1) determine possible invariant full ideals and their supported attracting sets, with boundedness and coverage explicit; (2) stratify those sets further by graded-generator and multiplication-map behavior; (3) locate the actual two-generator locus, using higher-order equations or deformation obstructions only where necessary; (4) compare existing normal-filtration, endpoint and residual invariants as geometric labels for the strata. Tangent weights at a selected fixed ideal are one tool, not the defining question. In particular, excluding eigenform *pairs* does not exclude two-generated moving ideals whose full limits require extra generators.

This is a research standard, not a theorem asserting classification or nonemptiness of any attracting stratum. The older staged experiment above remains a possible local technique but is complementary to this stratification-based program. The stratification is a strong consideration, not a required preliminary classification for independent geometric or constructive work.
