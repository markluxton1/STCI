# Torus attracting strata and two-generator incidence

**Status:** geometric research formulation, not a classification or STCI exclusion. This develops the [Hilbert-boundary framework](geometric_integration_and_hilbert_boundary.md) while retaining the option of independent geometric constructions.

The [surface-pair VGIT and graph construction](vgit_surface_pairs_and_full_limits.md)
adds a complete coarse weight partition on the pencil space and a comparison
with full intersection limits. Its intrinsic primary-power lemma gives
N=ab/4 for every actual sole-supported degree-(a,b) CI, in every characteristic;
thus H_9 covers all actual supported sextic CIs and their full limits. This
does not classify arbitrary supported Hilbert ideals or their attracting loci.
The contribution remains unpromoted, with its proofs, certificate and review
at that mathematical home.

## Invariant limits stratify moving ideals

Fix the rational quartic C in projective three-space with ideal sheaf I, degrees (a,b), and the Hilbert polynomial P of a proper complete intersection of those degrees. For a fixed positive integer N, consider the closed supported Hilbert locus H_N of ideal sheaves J with I^N contained in J contained in I and Hilbert polynomial P. These inclusions force reduced support C. The torus with coordinate weights (0,1,3,4) preserves H_N.

Fix the one-parameter subgroup λ(t) with the indicated weights. Every J in H_N has two potentially different full flat limits J_+(J)=lim_{t→0} λ(t)·J and J_−(J)=lim_{t→∞} λ(t)·J, both in H_N. For each torus-fixed ideal J_0, define A_+(J_0) and A_−(J_0) by the respective limits. These give two direction-dependent attracting decompositions, not a single canonical stratification; the two limits of a moving ideal need not coincide. This is an organizing decomposition in each direction; smooth affine cells, finite fixed-point lists, or a uniform N for all possible supported ideals are not assumed. Fixed components may have positive dimension. A full ideal limit is not the ideal generated by the two initial forms of a selected pair, nor the I-adic normal initial ideal.

## Two-generation on each attracting set

Write S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3], and use J also for the saturated homogeneous ideal of a point. Its graded minimal-generator module is M(J)=J/S_+J. The condition that J be generated by two homogeneous forms of degrees (a,b) is equivalent to M(J) having precisely those two graded generators and no others. Because J has height two, the resulting two generators form a regular sequence. This tests generation of the *entire ideal*, not just the existence of two sections vanishing on its scheme.

For (6,6), the criterion is dim M(J)_6=2 and M(J)_d=0 for every d other than six. The fixed limit J_0 can have more generators even when A_+(J_0) or A_−(J_0) contains such ideals. Therefore eigenform-pair exclusions do not exclude the desired moving ideals.

## Geometric incidence and finite tests

Over a suitable flattening/rank stratum, the graded pieces J_d and multiplication maps S_1 tensor J_{d-1} to J_d define fiberwise generator spaces. A uniform finite cutoff requires a regularity bound on the chosen bounded family. For (6,6), wherever J_6 is a vector bundle, a relative Grassmannian of two-planes in J_6 parametrizes candidate sextic pencils. The required incidence imposes equality between the ideal generated by the universal pencil and J, including higher-degree relations. This construction treats both sextics symmetrically.

The research question is to describe, for each geometrically meaningful direction-specific attracting locus A_±(J_0), the subset where the moving ideal satisfies this two-generator incidence. Normal-filtration layers, endpoint modifications, conormal modules, and quotient geometry may provide intrinsic labels for strata. Tangent weights and deformation obstructions are auxiliary tests, not a substitute for incidence or a classification of fixed eigenform pairs.

## Sextic pilot and scope

For (6,6), the target polynomial is P(n)=36n-144, with degree 36, arithmetic genus 145, and generic length nine along C for a sole-supported proper intersection. The [sextic examples](toric_sextic_residuals_and_limits.md) give eigenform-pair residual bounds and controls on full limits; examples retaining residual boundary curves lie outside H_N even if their total Hilbert polynomial is P.

A substantive next result would exhibit and verify a sole-supported invariant ideal J_0 of this polynomial, describe the attracting families in both directions, and decide the two-sextic incidence on that family. An exclusion on one direction-specific attracting set is not a global exclusion without coverage of all relevant invariant limits. For actual supported sextic CIs the linked primary-power lemma supplies N=9; classifying the relevant full limits and their moving incidence is still open. The comparison with the characteristic-two (4,4) example must retain its characteristic and lifting qualifications.

## Open geometric interfaces

- Describe fixed ideals through transverse quotient geometry together with endpoint modifications, without assuming constant or curvilinear transverse families.
- Relate normal and endpoint invariants to generator jumps along attracting loci.
- Find finite, geometric rank or degeneracy tests that detect the full homogeneous ideal-generation condition.
- Compare both opposite limits of each moving ideal and their endpoint behavior; neither direction may be silently discarded.
- Prove coverage before transferring a stratum-specific conclusion to all supported complete intersections.

No new theorem or canonical status is asserted.

## Opposite-limit pairs as a refined geometric decomposition

The two attracting decompositions admit a common refinement. For fixed
T-invariant ideals J_+, J_- in H_N, define the **opposite-limit
intersection**
\[
A(J_+,J_-)=A_+(J_+)\cap A_-(J_-).
\]
This is the locus of moving ideals with the *specified pair* of full flat
limits. Its two-generator subset is
[
A^{(a,b)}(J_+,J_-)
 = A(J_+,J_-)cap{J:	ext{the saturated homogeneous ideal }J
 	ext{ has exactly two minimal generators in degrees }a,b}.
]
The pair can encode different endpoint degenerations and different
generator jumps in the two directions. It is finer than either
single-limit classification, but does not by itself imply finiteness,
local closedness, smoothness, or nonemptiness. When fixed loci have
positive dimension, one may instead index by fixed components and
specify the resulting incidence geometry.

**Compatibility before calculation.** A candidate pair must be realized
by a single point of H_N, not by two unrelated one-sided families.
Consequently each limit has the same Hilbert polynomial and lies in
the same connected component of H_N as the moving ideal. These are
necessary conditions, not sufficient criteria for realizing the pair.
Both limits retain reduced support C within H_N. Their normal and
endpoint structures can differ while the full Hilbert polynomial
agrees. Comparing these two endpoint profiles may constrain which
generator-jump patterns occur on a common opposite-limit intersection.

**Two-generation is tested in the intersection, not at its endpoints.**
Neither J_+ nor J_- must have two generators. For a degree-(6,6) pilot,
the question is whether A(J_+,J_-) contains a J with
M(J)_6 of dimension two and no other minimal generators. A proposed
pairwise obstruction must rule out that incidence on the entire
specified intersection, not just at the fixed endpoints. Conversely,
a successful pairwise construction still needs proof that its ideal
has sole reduced support C and is generated by two actual sextics.

**Geometric next test.** Begin with a specified bounded family where
both limits can be computed as full ideals. Determine which pairs of
fixed ideals occur, interpret the two endpoint degenerations through
normal and quotient geometry, and compute the generator incidence on
the resulting nonempty intersections. The existing sextic residual
examples are controls for the full-limit calculation but cannot be
mistaken for points of H_N when residual curves survive. Exhaustive
conclusions require full-ideal and incidence coverage; the pair refinement
does not supply it. The linked N=9 bound already supplies neighborhood
coverage for actual supported sextic CIs.

## Comparison with the moving transverse quotient

The opposite-limit labels should be compared with the **entire moving
transverse quotient**, not only the finite algebra obtained from a
constant quotient of the dense torus. For an actual sole-supported
intersection, the finite modification over the parameter curve carries
generic transverse length m=ab/4 and a possibly positive degree e.
Its endpoint image lattices measure how the modification descends to
the projective embedded scheme. The precise construction and
characteristic scope belong to
[the tropical modification theorem](tropical_thickenings_and_endpoint_duality.md)
and [the endpoint-descent account](toric_quotients_and_endpoint_descent.md).

For (6,6), the established necessary numerical relation in that model
is delta_0+delta_infinity=e+153, with e positive in characteristic zero
and in the stated prime-to-degree range. This equation constrains
the *total* endpoint defect of one actual embedded quotient. It is
not a formula for the difference between the two torus limits, nor
does it determine either full limit ideal. In particular, one must
not identify the two geometric endpoints of a single orbit closure
with the t→0 and t→infinity limits in the Hilbert scheme.

A precise comparison problem for a pair (J_+,J_-) is therefore:

1. Start with a single supported moving ideal J and its entire
   finite modification; retain the base-dependent transverse algebra,
   its endpoint image lattices, and the actual homogeneous equation
   module.
2. Compute both flat limits of the **full ideal** J, and ask which
   pieces of the quotient/endpoint data specialize functorially.
   Establish a comparison morphism or a specialization statement
   before equating defects or ranks across these objects.
3. On the common opposite-limit attracting locus, test the graded
   minimal-generator condition. A numerical endpoint budget alone
   cannot certify or exclude two-generation.
4. Only after establishing a comparison may one ask whether paired
   endpoint profiles impose a new compatibility restriction beyond
   the individual Hilbert-polynomial and defect budgets.

This is a concrete **missing bridge**, not a proved identification.
It keeps two different notions of endpoint separate and leaves room
for moving, noncurvilinear and characteristic-dependent quotient
families. A useful first test is a known proper sextic residual family
where both full limits can be calculated, explicitly labeled as a
control outside the sole-supported locus whenever residual support
persists.
