# Torus attracting strata and two-generator incidence

**Status:** geometric research formulation, not a classification or STCI exclusion. This develops the [Hilbert-boundary framework](geometric_integration_and_hilbert_boundary.md) while retaining the option of independent geometric constructions.

## Invariant limits stratify moving ideals

Fix the rational quartic C in projective three-space with ideal sheaf I, degrees (a,b), and the Hilbert polynomial P of a proper complete intersection of those degrees. For a fixed positive integer N, consider the closed supported Hilbert locus H_N of ideal sheaves J with I^N contained in J contained in I and Hilbert polynomial P. These inclusions force reduced support C. The torus with coordinate weights (0,1,3,4) preserves H_N.

Each point has a full flat torus limit J_0 in H_N. The attracting set A(J_0) consists of moving ideals with that limit. This is an organizing decomposition; smooth affine cells, finite fixed-point lists, or a uniform N for all possible supported ideals are not assumed. Fixed components may have positive dimension. A full ideal limit is not the ideal generated by the two initial forms of a selected pair, nor the I-adic normal initial ideal.

## Two-generation on each attracting set

Write S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3], and use J also for the saturated homogeneous ideal of a point. Its graded minimal-generator module is M(J)=J/S_+J. The condition that J be generated by two homogeneous forms of degrees (a,b) is equivalent to M(J) having precisely those two graded generators and no others. Because J has height two, the resulting two generators form a regular sequence. This tests generation of the *entire ideal*, not just the existence of two sections vanishing on its scheme.

For (6,6), the criterion is dim M(J)_6=2 and M(J)_d=0 for every d other than six. The fixed limit J_0 can have more generators even when A(J_0) contains such ideals. Therefore eigenform-pair exclusions do not exclude the desired moving ideals.

## Geometric incidence and finite tests

Over a suitable flattening/rank stratum, the graded pieces J_d and multiplication maps S_1 tensor J_{d-1} to J_d define fiberwise generator spaces. A uniform finite cutoff requires a regularity bound on the chosen bounded family. For (6,6), wherever J_6 is a vector bundle, a relative Grassmannian of two-planes in J_6 parametrizes candidate sextic pencils. The required incidence imposes equality between the ideal generated by the universal pencil and J, including higher-degree relations. This construction treats both sextics symmetrically.

The research question is to describe, for each geometrically meaningful attracting locus A(J_0), the subset where the moving ideal satisfies this two-generator incidence. Normal-filtration layers, endpoint modifications, conormal modules, and quotient geometry may provide intrinsic labels for strata. Tangent weights and deformation obstructions are auxiliary tests, not a substitute for incidence or a classification of fixed eigenform pairs.

## Sextic pilot and scope

For (6,6), the target polynomial is P(n)=36n-144, with degree 36, arithmetic genus 145, and generic length nine along C for a sole-supported proper intersection. The [sextic examples](toric_sextic_residuals_and_limits.md) give eigenform-pair residual bounds and controls on full limits; examples retaining residual boundary curves lie outside H_N even if their total Hilbert polynomial is P.

A substantive next result would exhibit and verify a sole-supported invariant ideal J_0 of this polynomial, describe an attracting family, and decide the two-sextic incidence on that family. An exclusion on one attracting set is not a global exclusion without coverage of all relevant invariant limits and bounds N. The comparison with the characteristic-two (4,4) example must retain its characteristic and lifting qualifications.

## Open geometric interfaces

- Describe fixed ideals through transverse quotient geometry together with endpoint modifications, without assuming constant or curvilinear transverse families.
- Relate normal and endpoint invariants to generator jumps along attracting loci.
- Find finite, geometric rank or degeneracy tests that detect the full homogeneous ideal-generation condition.
- Prove coverage before transferring a stratum-specific conclusion to all supported complete intersections.

No new theorem or canonical status is asserted.
