# What genus and local positivity can measure for a supported complete intersection

A general locally Cohen–Macaulay genus bound has strict room for every
surviving characteristic-zero degree pair on the fixed quartic. Local
threshold and mixed-multiplicity bounds recover the first normal-order
inequality. Neither comparison bounds the later contact needed to force
a genuine residual component. These independently reviewed deductions
are deductions under the stated hypotheses; they change no STCI status.

## The least containing surface belongs to the full scheme

Let \(C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3\) in
characteristic zero, and suppose a proper complete intersection
\(Y=V(F_a,G_b)\), with \(5\le a\le b\), is supported on it.
The degree and arithmetic genus of the full scheme are
\[
D=ab,\qquad p_a(Y)=1+\frac{ab(a+b-4)}2.
\]
Its least containing-surface degree is \(s=a\): the saturated ideal
of this complete intersection is generated by \(F_a,G_b\), so it
has no nonzero piece below degree a. The reduced support lies on a
quadric, but using s=2 for Y would change the object to which the
bound is applied.

For every locally Cohen–Macaulay space curve of degree D not contained
in a surface of degree less than s, with \(D\ge2s+1\),
[Beorchia–Lella–Schlesinger, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.08731)
recalls the bound
\[
p_a(Y)\le B(D,s)=\binom{D-s}{2}-\binom{s-1}{3}.
\tag{1}
\]
It applies to the full nonreduced scheme, without a primitive,
normal-flatness or smooth-carrier hypothesis. In the present range
\(ab\ge2a+1\). Direct expansion of the two binomial coefficients gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
B(ab,a)-p_a(Y)
&=\frac{a(a-1)}6\bigl(3b^2-9b-a+8\bigr)>0.\tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, for \(b\ge a\ge5\), the last factor is at least
\(3a^2-10a+8=(3a-4)(a-2)>0\). Thus (1) excludes no such
pair, even before support is imposed.

The sharpness question left conjectural in the 2018 paper is settled
in characteristic zero by
[Sammartano–Schlesinger, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00809):
the bound is attained throughout \(D\ge2s+1\), and also for D=s.
Their multiple-line and biliaison constructions do not prescribe fixed
C0 support or the arithmetic Gorenstein structure required of a CI.
They therefore supply neither a supported construction here nor a
support-sensitive exclusion. A universal upper bound depending only
on D and s in the full locally CM class cannot remove the strict gap
in (2).

The upper bound (1) itself is valid in arbitrary characteristic; the
sharpness theorem just cited is used only in characteristic zero.
The restriction to the surviving range \(5\le a\le b\) is a scope
choice for this comparison, not a genus-theoretic exclusion of the
other pairs. A test depending only on the usual CI Hilbert series,
resolution or canonical twist also accepts smooth CIs of the same
type. Any further restriction must use the marked support or another
condition distinguishing these schemes.

## Threshold and mixed-multiplicity comparisons

Work now over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero
at the generic point of a smooth curve component of any proper pair.
Its completed transverse ring is a two-dimensional regular local
ring R. Put
\[
I=(F,G)\subset R,\qquad
m=\ell(R/I)=e(I),\qquad
q=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak m_R}I
 =\min\{\operatorname{ord}F,\operatorname{ord}G\},\qquad
c=\operatorname{lct}(I).
\]
The equality of colength and Samuel multiplicity uses that I is a
parameter ideal. The theorem of
[de Fernex–Ein–Mustaţă, Theorem 0.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0205171)
and the first exceptional divisor of the point blowup give
\[
\frac2{\sqrt m}\le c\le\frac2q,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad m\ge q^2.\tag{3}
\]
The upper bound uses log discrepancy 2 and order q on that divisor;
it does not assume it computes c.

The stronger mixed-number estimate of
[Demailly–Hiệp, Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.4086), on the
complex analytic transverse surface, gives
\[
c\ge\frac1q+\frac q m.
\]
Here the first and second multiplicity numbers are q and m. Comparing
with \(c\le2/q\) again gives exactly \(m\ge q^2\), without an
upper bound on later contact.

The reverse Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality of
[Kaveh–Khovanskii, Theorem 9.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.7979)
applies to the two primary ideals I and \(\mathfrak m_R\), giving
\[
e(I)\,e(\mathfrak m_R)\ge e(I,\mathfrak m_R)^2.
\]
In this regular surface ring \(e(\mathfrak m_R)=1\), and intersecting
a general member of I with a general line gives
\(e(I,\mathfrak m_R)=q\); thus it gives (3). The two individual
principal ideals (F) and (G) are not primary for
\(\mathfrak m_R\), so that theorem cannot be applied to them as
stated. The ideal-primary comparison is weaker than the elementary
local intersection inequality
\[
m\ge(\operatorname{ord}F)(\operatorname{ord}G)\ge q^2.
\]

The [normalized-CI-blowup identity](blowup_residual_geometry.md#the-normalized-blowup-of-the-full-complete-intersection)
likewise distributes existing component multiplicities exactly; it
supplies no extra upper capacity. The
[Milnor comparison](pencil_milnor_budget.md#5-cluster-interpretation-and-a-stronger-local-bound)
uses the threshold theorem with an additional, exact discrepancy-weighted
cluster identity to bound a different quantity. Its quartic polar
inequality is already implied locally. None of these comparisons
turns exceptional contact into a curve component downstairs.
