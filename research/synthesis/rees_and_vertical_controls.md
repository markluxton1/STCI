# Full-ideal Rees data and vertical degeneration controls

These independently reconstructed statements distinguish the
normalized blowup of the **full lci ideal** from intersections of chosen
strict transforms on the blowup of the reduced marked curve. No unrestricted residual theorem is asserted. The graph cycle
and support-sensitive numerical bounds are proved in [the two-blowup account](blowup_residual_geometry.md).



## Full lci ideals: exceptional divisors, coefficient budget, and Segre class

## 2. No special-point divisorial Rees valuations for an lci curve ideal

**Proposition 1.** Let \(X\) be a smooth integral threefold of finite type
over a field, and let \(Y\hookrightarrow X\) be a codimension-two regular
immersion. Suppose \(Y_{\mathrm{red}}=C\) is an irreducible curve. Put
\[
 b:B=\operatorname{Bl}_{I_Y}X\longrightarrow X,
 \qquad \nu:\overline B\longrightarrow B
\]
for the blowup and its normalization. Every exceptional prime divisor of
\(\overline B\to X\) dominates \(C\). In particular, no divisorial Rees
valuation of \(I_Y\) has center equal to a closed point of \(C\).

Moreover localization at \(\eta_C\) identifies these prime divisors with
the divisorial Rees valuations of the transverse parameter ideal
\((I_Y)_{\eta_C}\) in the two-dimensional regular local ring
\(\mathcal O_{X,\eta_C}\).

**Proof.** Locally write \(I_Y=(f,g)\), with \(f,g\) a regular sequence.
Its Rees algebra has presentation
\[
 \mathcal R(I_Y)=\mathcal O_X[A,B]/(gA-fB).
\]
This is the regular-sequence linear-type presentation; equivalently,
relations among the homogeneous monomials in \(f,g\) are generated by
the Koszul relation. Reducing the presentation modulo \(I_Y\) shows
that the exceptional Cartier divisor is
\[
 E_Y=\mathbf P_Y(I_Y/I_Y^2).
\]
Since \(I_Y/I_Y^2\) has rank two, \((E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is an
irreducible ruled surface over \(C\). The fiber over a closed point of
\(C\) has dimension one.

The blowup is integral, since its Rees algebra sits inside
\(\mathcal O_X[z]\), and its normalization is finite: all schemes are of
finite type over a field. A finite morphism preserves the dimension of
a closed irreducible subset. Therefore the inverse image in
\(\overline B\) of an exceptional fiber over a closed point has dimension
one and contains no prime divisor of the threefold. All exceptional
prime divisors are contained in \(\nu^{-1}(E_Y)\); their images in \(B\)
have dimension two and hence equal \((E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}\). They
therefore dominate \(C\).

Blowup and finite normalization commute with localization. Since every
exceptional prime survives localization at \(\eta_C\), and taking the
closure recovers it, the stated bijection follows. \(\square\)

The same dimension argument applies when \(Y_{\mathrm{red}}\) has
several curve components, including residual components: every
exceptional prime divisor dominates a curve component of the reduced
support. There is still no exceptional divisor over a closed point.

This does **not** say that the global normalized exceptional surfaces
carry no special-point information. Their curves, finite-cover geometry,
ramification, conductor, and special fibers can carry such information.
It says that the list of divisorial valuations itself is already visible
over the generic points of the support curves.

## 3. The exact Rees multiplicity budget

Retain Proposition 1 and put
\[
 m=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{X,\eta_C}}
                  (\mathcal O_{Y,\eta_C}).
\]
Write
\[
 \nu^*E_Y=\sum_i a_iD_i,
 \qquad
 f_i=[k(D_i):k((E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}})].
\]

**Proposition 2.** The intrinsic Rees coefficients satisfy
\[
 \boxed{\sum_i a_if_i=m.}
\]

**Proof.** A rank-two projective bundle over \(Y\) has the same generic
cycle multiplicity along its reduced support as \(Y\) has along \(C\).
Thus the Cartier divisor \(E_Y\) has cycle
\(m[(E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}]\). Since \(\nu\) is finite birational, the
projection formula for Cartier divisors gives
\[
 \nu_*[\nu^*E_Y]=[E_Y].
\]
The pushforward of \([D_i]\) has coefficient \(f_i\) on the reduced
exceptional surface. Comparing coefficients proves the identity.
\(\square\)

For the transverse ideal \((u^a,v^b)\), let \(h=\gcd(a,b)\). Its
single monomial Rees valuation has primitive weights
\((b/h,a/h)\). The vanishing coefficient of the ideal is \(ab/h\),
and the map of exceptional direction curves has degree \(h\): it is
the map with rational function \(u^a/v^b\), the \(h\)-th power of a
primitive direction parameter. Hence \(a_1f_1=ab\), as required.

For a hypothetical \((d,e)\) STCI of \(C_0\), this identity reads
\(\sum_i a_if_i=de/4\). It is a genuine generator-independent
decomposition of the transverse multiplicity. No restriction on the
global exceptional finite covers has yet been extracted from it.

## 4. Ordinary Segre numbers are fixed by the CI degrees

**Proposition 3.** If \(Y=V(F_d,G_e)\subset\mathbf P^3\) is a proper
complete intersection, then
\[
 i_*s(Y,\mathbf P^3)
 =de\,H^2-de(d+e)\,H^3.
\]

**Proof.** The regular immersion has normal bundle
\(\mathcal O_Y(d)\oplus\mathcal O_Y(e)\). Its normal cone is that
bundle, so the definition of the Segre class gives
\[
 s(Y,\mathbf P^3)
 =c(\mathcal O_Y(d)\oplus\mathcal O_Y(e))^{-1}\cap[Y].
\]
Pushing forward and expanding in dimensions one and zero proves the
formula. \(\square\)

Consequently these ordinary global Segre numbers cannot distinguish
special-point normal-filtration defects among proper CIs of fixed
degrees. One must use finer data, for example the filtration relative to
\(I_C\), rather than only the Segre class of \(Y\) in the ambient space.
The proposition supplies no additional STCI obstruction.

## 5. An intrinsic saturation defect unbounded with fixed generic algebra

Let \(A=k[t]\), choose integers \(p\ge2\), \(a>p\), and \(b>p\), and
work in the smooth affine threefold
\(X=\operatorname{Spec}A[u,v]\). For \(N\ge1\), set
\[
 C=V(u,v),\quad \mathfrak q=(u,v),\qquad
 J_N=(u^b,v^a+t^Nu^p),\quad Y_N=V(J_N).
\]
The earlier exceptional-cycle note proves that \(Y_N\) is a
\(C\)-supported lci, finite free of rank \(ab\) over \(A\), with basis
\(u^iv^j\), \(0\le i<b,0\le j<a\). We use this verified description
without re-auditing its vertical chosen-generator calculation.

Let \(\mathfrak q_N=\mathfrak q\mathcal O_{Y_N}\). The first actual
relation is
\[
 R_p=\ker\!\left(
  \operatorname{Sym}^p(\mathfrak q/\mathfrak q^2)
      \longrightarrow \mathfrak q_N^p/\mathfrak q_N^{p+1}
 \right).
\]

**Proposition 4.** In the monomial basis of the symmetric power,
\[
 R_p=A\,t^Nu^p,\qquad
 R_p^{\mathrm{sat}}=A\,u^p,\qquad
 R_p^{\mathrm{sat}}/R_p\simeq A/(t^N).
\]
Thus the intrinsic first-relation saturation defect has length exactly
\(N\). Equivalently,
\[
 \mathfrak q_N^p/\mathfrak q_N^{p+1}
       \simeq A^{\oplus p}\oplus A/(t^N).
\]

**Proof.** Since \(a,b>p\), reduce the defining ideal modulo
\(\mathfrak q^{p+1}\). Its image in the degree-\(p\) summand is precisely
\(A\,t^Nu^p\): both \(u^b\) and \(v^a\) vanish, and every
positive-normal-degree coefficient of the remaining generator also
vanishes. There is no lower-degree relation. Saturation over the PID
\(A\) replaces \(t^Nu^p\) by \(u^p\). The quotient assertions follow
immediately. \(\square\)

This defect depends on the full ideal and the embedding of \(C\); it
does not depend on replacing the displayed generators by another
generating pair.

**Corollary 4a (fixed generic filtered algebra).** On the subsequence
\(N=aL\), \(L\ge1\), all generic filtered algebras are isomorphic over
\(K=k(t)\). Indeed the filtration-preserving change of normal coordinates
\(v=t^Lv'\) identifies
\[
 K[u,v]/(u^b,v^a+t^{aL}u^p)
 \simeq K[u,v']/(u^b,v'^a+u^p).
\]
The maximal-ideal filtration is preserved because \(t^L\) is a unit in
\(K\). The intrinsic saturation-defect lengths \(aL\), nevertheless,
are unbounded. The same generic coordinate identifications identify the
generic Rees valuations of the full ideals, including their Rees
coefficients and finite generic residue degrees. Proposition 1 says
there are no additional special-point divisorial Rees valuations that
could supply the missing defect length.

This compares generic data under explicit coordinate identifications;
it does not claim that the valuations, viewed as valuations of one fixed
ambient rational-function field without those identifications, are
literally equal.

**Corollary 4b (the full generic CI initial algebra can also be fixed).**
Choose \(b=2p\). Over \(K\), the defining ideal is equivalently
\[
 (u^p+t^{-N}v^a,\ v^{2a}).
\]
Its full ordinary initial ideal is
\[
 (u^p,v^{2a}).
\]
To see equality of initial ideals, both displayed initial forms belong
to the initial ideal, are coprime, and define an algebra of length
\(2pa\), which is already the length of the original quotient. An
additional initial relation would strictly reduce that length.

Therefore one fixed coprime binary-CI generic initial type \((p,2a)\),
one fixed multiplicity \(2pa\), and even one fixed generic filtered
algebra admit arbitrarily long intrinsic first-relation saturation
defects. For instance \(p=2,a=3,b=4,N=3L\) has fixed generic initial
type \((2,6)\), multiplicity \(12\), and defect length \(3L\).

This is compatible with STCI-R-0010: its saturated-first-relation
hypothesis fails for every \(N>0\). It shows exactly why a replacement
of that hypothesis by generic initial-CI data, generic Rees data, or
constant transverse length would be false. It does not rule out a
global bound involving the projective degrees of an actual \(C_0\)
presentation; this affine family has no such fixed global degree data.

**Corollary 4c (a fixed non-CI full generic initial ideal).** Choose
\(b=p+1\) instead. The full generic ordinary initial ideal is then
\[
 (u^p,\ uv^a,\ v^{2a}),
\]
which has three minimal generators. Indeed over \(K\), write
\(F=u^p+t^{-N}v^a\). Subtracting \(u^{p+1}\) from \(uF\) produces
\(uv^a\); then subtracting \(u^{p-1}(uv^a)\) from \(v^aF\) produces
\(v^{2a}\). Their initial monomials have total colength
\(2a+(p-1)a=(p+1)a\), exactly the original quotient length, proving
equality of initial ideals. Thus the same unbounded intrinsic defect
also occurs for a fixed three-generator generic initial algebra. For
\(p=2,a=3,b=3,N=3L\), this fixed initial ideal is
\((u^2,uv^3,v^6)\), of length nine.

The graded algebra in Corollary 4c has exactly two socle monomials,
\(u^{p-1}v^{a-1}\) and \(v^{2a-1}\), of respective degrees
\(p+a-2\) and \(2a-1\). In particular, the original local algebra is
Gorenstein (it is a complete intersection), while its ordinary tangent
graded algebra is not Gorenstein. A graded self-duality argument that
uses one socle degree cannot be transferred to this non-CI tangent type
without an additional construction of the dual filtration.



## Unbounded vertical degeneration with no residual support

## 3. Unbounded vertical fibers with fixed entirely-thick generic data

**Proposition 3 (local counterexample family).** Fix integers \(p\ge2\), \(a>p\), \(b>p\). For each \(N\ge1\), in the smooth threefold \(\mathbf A^3_{t,u,v}\), put
\[
C=V(u,v),\qquad f=t^N u^p+v^a,\qquad g=f+u^b.
\]
Then the following hold, over any field (in particular in characteristic zero).

1. \((f,g)=(u^b,v^a+t^N u^p)\) is a complete-intersection ideal with radical \((u,v)\).
2. Its quotient is finite free of rank \(m=ab\) over \(k[t]\), with basis \(u^i v^j\), \(0\le i<b,0\le j<a\).
3. Both displayed equations have exact generic normal order \(p\).
4. On \(\operatorname{Bl}_C\mathbf A^3\), the intersection cycle of their two strict surfaces consists of the horizontal section \(u=0\) in the exceptional bundle, with coefficient \(ab-p^2\), and the vertical exceptional fiber over \(t=0\), with coefficient \(N(b-p)\).
5. The equally valid generators \((f,g-f)=(f,u^b)\) have orders \(p,b\). Their strict-surface intersection contains no vertical fiber, and has the same horizontal section with coefficient \(b(a-p)\).

**Proof.** Monic division first by \(u^b\), then by \(v^a+t^N u^p\), proves the basis assertion. The quotient is free over \(k[t]\), so this is a flat lci family with constant transverse length. Its radical is \((u,v)\) because \(u\) is nilpotent, followed by \(v\). There is no residual one-dimensional component.

In the blowup chart \(v=uw\), the two displayed strict equations generate
\[
(t^N+u^{a-p}w^a,\ u^{b-p}).
\]
At the generic point of the exceptional fiber \(u=t=0\), \(w\) is a transcendental unit, and this quotient has length \(N(b-p)\): it is free of rank \(N\) over \(k(w)[u]/(u^{b-p})\), by monic division in \(t\). This proves the vertical multiplicity.

In the other chart \(u=vw\), the strict ideal is
\[
(t^Nw^p+v^{a-p},\ v^{b-p}w^b).
\]
Over \(k(t)\), its support is \(v=w=0\), the horizontal section corresponding to the normal direction \(u=0\). Additivity of intersection multiplicity in the factored second equation gives
\[
(b-p)\,\operatorname{length}K[[w]]/(t^Nw^p)
+b\,\operatorname{length}K[[v]]/(v^{a-p})
=(b-p)p+b(a-p)=ab-p^2.
\]
There are no other components: away from the exceptional locus the blowup is an isomorphism and \(V(f,g)\) has support \(C\); within that locus, the two initial forms have the sole generic normal direction \(u=0\), and their only common entire fiber is at \(t=0\).

For the replacement pair, the strict equation of \(u^b\) is the unit one in the first chart, excluding a vertical fiber. In the second chart the strict ideal is
\((t^Nw^p+v^{a-p},w^b)\), free of rank \(b(a-p)\) over \(k[t]\). This proves the final assertion. ∎

With fixed \(p,a,b\), generic normal orders and length remain fixed while the vertical coefficient is unbounded. Thus no upper bound for vertical multiplicity can depend only on \(m,p,q\); even flatness and a pure \(C\)-supported lci do not supply such a bound. This is a local statement: it does **not** construct an STCI of the fixed quartic.

The stronger conclusion with a fixed **generic algebra**, rather than just fixed numerical data, holds on the subsequence \(N=aL\). Over \(K=k(t)\), the substitution \(v=t^L v'\) identifies every such quotient with
\[
K[u,v']/(u^b,v'^a+u^p).
\]
The substitution preserves the maximal-ideal filtration, while the vertical coefficient \(aL(b-p)\) is unbounded. Thus even the isomorphism class of the generic filtered algebra does not bound vertical multiplicity of these chosen-pair strict transforms.

## 4. An actual projective pure-support example

Vertical fibers can occur even for homogeneous two-hypersurface presentations on projective three-space. For \(p\ge2,N\ge1\), take coordinates \([T:U:V:W]\) and
\[
F=T^NU^p+V^{N+p},\qquad
G=F+U^{N+p}.
\]
Both equations have degree \(D=N+p\), no common surface component, and
\[
\sqrt{(F,G)}=(U,V).
\]
Their intersection is a complete-intersection thickening of the line \(L=V(U,V)\), of generic length \(D^2\). Their generic orders along \(L\) are \(p,p\). Near \([0:0:0:1]\), this is Proposition 3 with \(a=b=N+p\), so the first strict-surface intersection contains the exceptional fiber there with coefficient \(N^2\). The leading forms have no other common entire fiber. Replacing \(G\) by \(G-F=U^{N+p}\) removes that vertical fiber. This homogeneous example concerns a line with a different normal bundle; its role is to refute a general vertical-implies-residual principle, not to settle any quartic case.



## Generic intersection clusters and fixed-data finiteness

In this subsection assume sole reduced support C=C0 for an actual pair of degrees a,b. Put p=ord_C(F), q=ord_C(G), alpha=a/p and beta=b/q. The first exceptional direction/base coordinates called u,v below are defined by E=P1_base x P1_direction and have v=m-pq; they do not refer to local normal parameters.

## 3. Generic transverse cluster theorem

Let \(\eta\) be the generic point of \(C\), and set
\[
R=\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3,\eta},\qquad K=\kappa(\eta)=k(C),\qquad \mathfrak m=I_CR.
\]
Then \(R\) is a two-dimensional regular local ring. Let \(f,g\in R\) be the images of the defining forms and \(J=(f,g)\).

### Proposition 3.1 — generic intersection multiplicity

Under the STCI support hypothesis,
\[
\ell_R(R/J)=\frac{ab}{4}.
\]

Indeed, the two projective surfaces have no common surface component, so their proper intersection cycle has degree \(ab\). Its reduced support is exactly the irreducible degree-four curve \(C\), hence the fundamental cycle is \(m[C]\), where \(m=\ell_R(R/J)\). Taking degrees gives \(ab=4m\).

### Proposition 3.2 — Noether decomposition

The orders at the origin are \(m_0(f)=p\) and \(m_0(g)=q\). Resolve the pair over K by successive blowups at its closed infinitely-near points. Noether's arithmetic formula, with each residue degree measured over that same base field K, gives
\[
\frac{ab}{4}
=
pq+\sum_{P\succ0}[\kappa(P):K]\,m_P(f)m_P(g).
\tag{N}
\]
Therefore
\[
v:=\frac{ab}{4}-pq
=
\sum_{P\succ0}[\kappa(P):K]\,m_P(f)m_P(g).
\tag{E}
\]

If one instead works after a finite extension K_prime/K, the normalized formula uses [kappa(P):K_prime] at points of that resolution; over an algebraic closure every residue weight is one.

Thus the second coordinate \(v\) of the audited first exceptional bidegree is exactly the total generic higher-contact budget.

### Corollary 3.3 — fixed-data finiteness

For fixed \((a,b,p,q)\), the number of common infinitely-near points after the origin is at most
\[
E=\frac{ab}{4}-pq,
\]
after passage to an algebraic closure and ignoring multiplicity. Hence the generic common cluster has bounded depth and bounded total number of vertices.

### Limitation 3.4 — normalized ratios do not suffice

Since
\[
E=pq\left(\frac{\alpha\beta}{4}-1\right),
\]
fixing only \((\alpha,\beta)\) does not bound cluster size independently of \(p,q\). At \((3,4)\),
\[
E=2pq.
\]

## 4. Correct higher-order objects

### Pair-sensitive object

Retain the common infinitely-near cluster with proximity relations, residue degrees, and the two multiplicity labels \(m_P(f),m_P(g)\). This distinguishes pairs with the same first tangent forms but different higher contact and directly refines the first exceptional cycle.

### Intrinsic ideal object

The normalized blowup of \(J=(f,g)\) and its Rees valuations give a canonical ideal-theoretic invariant. The integral-closure class determines \(e(J)\), hence the total transverse multiplicity \(ab/4\). It is, however, coarser than the labelled pair-cluster when the two distinguished generators must be tracked separately.

Recommendation: use Rees valuations as the intrinsic ideal-theoretic carrier, but use the labelled cluster (or a mixed refinement) for generator-sensitive inequalities.

## 5. Counterexample family

For N>=3, take
\[
f=y^2+x^N,\qquad g=y^2+2x^N,
\]
one has
\[
J=(f,g)=(y^2,x^N),\qquad \ell(R/J)=2N.
\]
The first tangent forms are always \(y^2\), but the integral-closure/Newton/Rees data changes with \(N\). After the first blowup the remaining Noether budget is
\[
2N-4,
\]
matching the transformed local calculation in the audited thick note.



For an explicit irreducible characteristic-zero version of the preceding
local control, take N=2n+1 with n≥1. Both y²+x^(2n+1) and
y²+2x^(2n+1) are irreducible plane germs: after Weierstrass preparation a
quadratic factorization would require an odd-order x-series to be a square.
Their common length is 4n+2. In the chart y=xz the strict equations are
z²+x^(2n-1), z²+2x^(2n-1), with exact common length 4n-2. Thus the
common tangent component's actual intersection coefficient is not bounded
by its root multiplicity in the two first symbols. This is a local model,
not a homogeneous supported-pair construction on C0.

## Relative horizontal blowup recursion

## 1. Blowup recursion along a horizontal common center

Let \(Y\) be a smooth threefold and let \(D,D'\) be effective Cartier divisors with proper one-dimensional intersection. Let \(Z\subset Y\) be a smooth curve contained in both divisors. Write
\[
r=\operatorname{ord}_Z(D),\qquad s=\operatorname{ord}_Z(D').
\]
Blow up \(Z\):
\[
\pi:Y_1=\operatorname{Bl}_Z(Y)\to Y
\]
with exceptional divisor \(E\). The strict transforms satisfy
\[
\widetilde D=\pi^*D-rE,\qquad
\widetilde D'=\pi^*D'-sE.
\]

### Proposition 1.1 — cycle recursion

As an equality of actual one-cycles on Y (and therefore in A_1(Y)),
\[
\pi_*\bigl([\widetilde D]\cdot[\widetilde D']\bigr)
=
[D]\cdot[D']-rs[Z].
\tag{R}
\]

**Proof.** Off Z, the blowup is an isomorphism, so both sides have
identical coefficients on every other curve. At the generic point of Z,
the ambient local ring is regular of dimension two. The plane-germ blowup
formula gives
\[
i_{\eta_Z}(f,g)=rs+
\sum_{P\in E_{\eta_Z}}[\kappa(P):\kappa(Z)]\,
 i_P(\widetilde f,\widetilde g).
\]
The weighted sum is precisely the coefficient of Z in the actual pushforward
of the strict-transform intersection. This proves the cycle identity
coefficient by coefficient. Finite intersection follows from properness.

As a separate class-level check, expand the two divisor classes. The
mixed terms push to zero because pi_*E=0, while pi_*(E^2)=-[Z]. Thus the
Chow computation gives the same identity, but is not its coefficientwise
justification.

This is the relative threefold analogue of the first step in Noether's plane-curve formula.

## 2. Recursion with a finite cover of the parent center

Suppose a later smooth horizontal center \(Z'\) maps finitely onto an earlier center \(Z\) with degree \(d\). If the current strict transforms have orders \(r',s'\) along \(Z'\), then after blowing up \(Z'\) its contribution pushed all the way down to \(Z\) is
\[
d\,r's'[Z].
\tag{R_d}
\]

Thus every common horizontal descendant consumes a positive integer amount of the coefficient of its ancestral horizontal component.

## Supported-cycle bidegrees and the universal numerical restriction

For an actual proper pair with sole support the fixed C0, put p=ord_C(F_a)
and q=ord_C(G_b). The strict divisors on B=Bl_C(P3) are aH-pE and bH-qE.
They have no common surface, hence form a pure one-dimensional CI W.
Since B is an isomorphism outside E and the original pair is supported on C,
the entire strict intersection is supported on E. Nonreduced equations are
allowed; purity prevents an isolated embedded point from replacing a curve.

In the balanced case E=P1_base x P1_direction, write its effective cycle as
(u,v), where H.W=4v and E.W=7v-u. The intersection relations
H^3=1,H^2E=0,HE^2=-4,E^3=-14 give
\[
H.W=ab-4pq,\qquad E.W=4(aq+bp)-14pq,
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{v=ab/4-pq,\qquad u=7ab/4+7pq-4(aq+bp).}
\]
Effectiveness on the product surface forces u,v≥0. Thus
\[
ab\ge4pq,\qquad7ab+28pq\ge16(aq+bp).
\]
Neither equation contains the smooth quadric, by the component-exclusion
proof. On it the restrictions divided by pC,qC are nonzero sections of
classes (a-p,a-3p),(b-q,b-3q), so a≥3p,b≥3q. The restrictions may contain
additional C factors; their displayed exponents are not assumed exact on Q.
For alpha=a/p,beta=b/q the second bound is
\[
7\alpha\beta/4+7\ge4(\alpha+\beta),\qquad\alpha,\beta\ge3.
\]
In particular alpha=3 forces beta≥4, and both slopes three are impossible.
At a=b=6,p=q=2 the actual computed bidegrees would be (u,v)=(-5,5),
contradicting effectivity. These are necessary conditions, not upper bounds
on possible orders or degrees. The same argument applies to any smooth
rational degree-four quadric curve with the explicitly required balanced
normal bundle; it is not inferred for a different splitting.

## The exact supported (3p,4q) boundary and distinct residual roots

Assume characteristic different from two, balanced normal bundle of C=C0,
an actual proper pair with sole support C, degrees a=3p,b=4q and normal
orders p,q. Its strict-transform intersection W on Bl_C(P3) has
exceptional direction-degree u=0: the exact balance 5delta-K=4u for a
supported pair has delta=0 and K=16(aq+bp)-7ab-28pq=0 at these degrees.
Purity of this proper CI and effectiveness on E=P1_base x P1_direction then
make its reduced components constant sections T_1,...,T_r, with
\[
[W]=\sum_j c_j[T_j],\qquad c_j\ge1,\quad
\sum_jc_j=m-pq=2pq,\quad r\le\min(p,q).
\]
The last inequality holds because distinct common section directions are
distinct roots of the degree-p and degree-q generic initial forms. None of
this identifies those directions canonically with residual ruling roots
on the quadric.

Nevertheless their **distinct counts** have the following proved relation.
The smooth quadric's strict transform Q_tilde is Q itself, because C is
Cartier on Q. Dividing the restrictions by pC and qC gives actual strict
equation divisors
\[
R_F\sim(2p,0),\qquad R_G\sim(3q,q).
\]
Neither equation contains Q by the unbalanced-quadric component exclusion. The residual restrictions are therefore nonzero. An extra C factor is impossible in R_F because its second bidegree is zero; R_G may contain an additional C factor, which does not affect the argument. They share no ruling component: otherwise that ruling would be a residual
curve of the original supported pair. Every distinct ruling ell in R_F
therefore has a nonzero restriction R_G|ell of degree q>0, and has an
intersection point P with R_G. Its image lies on C by sole support.
The exact strict-restriction identities put that same P in W cap Q_tilde.

Since W is supported on the constant sections, P lies on some T_j.
Moreover Q_tilde cap E is a section of bidegree (1,1), so each constant
T_j meets it at only one reduced support point. Distinct rulings ell of
the same ruling family are disjoint on Q, hence the selected P are
distinct. Thus two different ell cannot select the same T_j. We obtain
\[
\#\operatorname{Supp}(R_F)\le r\le\min(p,q).
\]
The multiplicities of R_F's ruling factors sum to 2p, so at least one
has multiplicity at least ceil(2p/min(p,q)). This is a repeated-root
restriction, not an exclusion or an existence statement.

If the first normal divisor of F has no vertical component, the distinct
section count improves to r≤min(p-1,q). Indeed D|E=(5p,p). If r=p,
factoring off one copy of each of those p distinct constant sections leaves
an effective divisor (5p,0), necessarily a nonzero union of vertical fibers,
contrary to the hypothesis. This additional no-vertical assumption is not
inferred from sole support.

This argument needs neither a canonical bijection nor a comparison of
root multiplicities with the c_j. It repairs the previously stated
missing-count-bridge caveat at this **exact** supported, balanced, u=0
boundary. Coupling multiplicities to Noether clusters or extending the
count statement to other slope/support hypotheses still needs proof.
In characteristic two the product-normal-bundle premise changes, so
this particular argument and its u coordinate are not transferred.

Fix one \(T_j\). Blow up every horizontal common center above \(T_j\) until the strict transforms no longer meet generically above it. Iterating Proposition 1.1 gives a sectionwise relative Noether identity
\[
c_j
=
\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal T_j}
d_\nu r_\nu s_\nu,
\tag{SN}
\]
provided the terminal intersection has no horizontal component above \(T_j\). Here:

- \(\mathcal T_j\) is the finite tree of horizontal common centers above \(T_j\);
- \(d_\nu\) is the degree of the center over its parent/ancestral section as measured after pushdown;
- \(r_\nu,s_\nu\) are the two vanishing orders along that center.

If a horizontal component survives indefinitely, the pair has a persistent divisorial valuation/common formal branch; converting that persistence into an actual projective common curve is a separate algebraization problem and is not asserted here.

### Corollary 3.1 — relative horizontal finiteness

For every terminating tree,
\[
\#\mathcal T_j\le c_j,
\qquad
\sum_j\#\mathcal T_j\le2pq.
\]
More generally, the total degree-weighted number of centers is bounded by \(2pq\).

Thus the \((3,4)\) boundary has a finite relative horizontal cluster for fixed \(p,q\), not merely a finite cluster on one generic transverse slice.

## 4. Why the proposed \(u\)-monotonicity is not justified

The first exceptional bidegree is \((u,v)=(0,2pq)\). It is tempting to regard \(u\) as a nonnegative energy that later horizontal twisting must increase. Proposition 1.1 shows that this is not automatic.

A blowup of a constant section removes \(rs[T_j]\) from the pushforward intersection cycle. New intersection curves may live on the new exceptional ruled surface. Their geometry can twist relative to \(T_j\) while their pushforward remains a multiple of the same class \([T_j]\). Therefore the original \(u=0\) records the support/class of the first-stage cycle but does not by itself constrain all later ruled-surface bidegrees.

So the candidate implication

\[
u=0\Longrightarrow\text{all later horizontal centers are constant/untwisted}
\]

is **not proved and should not be used**. A new invariant is needed if later twisting is to be controlled.

This is compatible with Jaffe's warning that iterated-blowup type sequences require extra control in the presence of common singularities.

## 5. Refined invariant suggested by the recursion

The natural object is now a **decorated relative cluster**
\[
\mathscr C(F,G/C)
\]
whose vertices are horizontal common centers on successive blowups and whose decorations include

\[
(d_\nu,r_\nu,s_\nu,N_{Z_\nu/Y_\nu},\text{proximity data}).
\]

The first three entries control the intersection budget. The normal bundle of the center in the current threefold controls the geometry of the next exceptional ruled surface:
\[
E_{\nu+1}=\mathbf P(N_{Z_\nu/Y_\nu}).
\]

This is the missing global datum that the generic Rees valuations over \(k(C)\) do not see.


## Characteristic reach and limitation

The full-lci-ideal Rees presentation, finite normalization/dimension proof,
Segre formula and exact vertical/nonsaturation families are algebraic in
arbitrary characteristic under the displayed finite-type hypotheses. They
already admit sole-support examples, and provide no characteristic-zero
positivity theorem. Generic-cluster residue extensions must retain their
degrees; inseparability is not silently replaced by a set of geometric points.
[Positive-characteristic supported pairs](../supplements/POSITIVE_CHARACTERISTIC_CONSTRUCTIONS.md#every-positive-characteristic-quartic-carrier)
therefore violate no retained conclusion.

## Delta as the specialization of equation-module torsion

Date: 2026-10-24. Status: **proved local arguments independently reconstructed by agent review**.

This note uses the [local comparison](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md)
for the marked embedding \(C\subset Z=X\cap Y\) and its deformation.
Residual components are permitted. The
[normal Koszul argument](../arguments/normal_koszul_cancellation.md)
gives the separate page filtration.

### 1. Exact scope and retained dependencies

Let \(R\) be a noetherian \(k\)-algebra, \(I\subset R\), and \(J\subset I\). Put
\[
B=R/I,\quad S=\operatorname{gr}_I R,\quad H=\operatorname{in}_I J,
\quad D=J/IJ,
\]
\[
F^nD=((J\cap I^n)+IJ)/IJ,\qquad
Q=H/S_+H,\qquad
\Delta=\operatorname{in}_I(IJ)/S_+H.
\]
Set \(I^n=R\) and \(F^nD=D\) for \(n\le0\). The local algebra below does not require smoothness or a complete-intersection hypothesis. For the geometric application, \(R\) is the local ring of a smooth ambient threefold, \(I=I_C\) for a smooth marked curve, and \(J=(f,g)\subset I\) is a regular-sequence ideal. Then \(D\) is locally free of rank two on \(C\).

The inherited comparison \(0\to\Delta_n\to Q_n\to\operatorname{gr}_F^nD\to0\) is used within its reviewed scope. Its predecessor has the proof and review scope stated there. The present note does not use any rational-quartic classification or STCI exclusion. No novelty claim is made for extended Rees modules, conormal specialization, or parameter-torsion removal.

For terminology, the [Stacks normal-cone construction](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/062Z) defines the normal cone from the full ideal-power algebra, and [conormal functoriality](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01R1) supplies the inclusion-induced maps. The statements below are proved directly.

### 2. Keep the deformation, then restrict its conormal module

Introduce a deformation parameter \(u\), independent of parameters on \(C\), and define
\[
\mathscr R=R[u,I/u]
=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbf Z}I^nu^{-n}
\subset R[u,u^{-1}].
\]
This indexing assigns weight \(n\) to \(I^nu^{-n}\); multiplication by \(u\) lowers weight by one. Define the induced ideal
\[
\mathscr J=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbf Z}(J\cap I^n)u^{-n}
=J R[u,u^{-1}]\cap\mathscr R.
\tag{1}
\]
The quotient \(\mathscr R/\mathscr J\) is the extended Rees algebra of \(R/J\) for \(K=I/J\). Its fibres are \(R/J\) at \(u=1\), and \(S/H\) at \(u=0\). These are actual algebras, with their maps; the special algebra alone does not reconstruct the former.

There is a canonical zero section
\[
\mathscr R\twoheadrightarrow B[u],\qquad
L=\ker(\mathscr R\to B[u])=(I/u)\mathscr R.
\tag{2}
\]
No splitting \(B\to R/J\) of the canonical quotient, or scheme retraction \(Z\to C\), is assumed: (2) is simply reduction of coefficients modulo \(I\), sending every positive normal generator \(a/u\) to zero. Since \(J\subset I\), one has \(\mathscr J\subset L\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{\mathscr N=\mathscr J/L\mathscr J}
\tag{3}
\]
is the conormal module of the deformed \(Z\) in the deformed ambient space, restricted to \(C\times\mathbf A^1\). Indeed restricting \(\mathscr J/\mathscr J^2\) to this zero section gives exactly (3).

Its fibres are canonically
\[
\mathscr N/(u-1)\mathscr N=D,
\qquad
\mathscr N/u\mathscr N=Q.
\tag{4}
\]
The second equality follows from \(\mathscr J/u\mathscr J=H\), using the induced filtration, and \(L\bmod u=S_+\). Thus \(Q\) really is a special conormal fibre of the actual embedded deformation.

### 3. The intrinsic torsion module whose special fibre is Delta

Define the Rees module of the filtered equation module by
\[
\mathscr D=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbf Z}F^nD\,u^{-n}
\subset D[u,u^{-1}].
\tag{5}
\]
Taking the class modulo \(IJ\) gives a canonical surjection
\[
\mathscr N\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr D.
\]
This is well defined because \(L\mathscr J\) maps to zero. Its kernel is
\[
\mathscr E=
\frac{\bigoplus_n(IJ\cap I^n)u^{-n}}{L\mathscr J}.
\tag{6}
\]
In coefficient form, these modules are especially explicit:
\[
\mathscr N_n=\frac{J\cap I^n}{I(J\cap I^{n-1})},\qquad
\boxed{\mathscr E_n=\frac{IJ\cap I^n}{I(J\cap I^{n-1})}.}
\tag{7}
\]
To verify the denominator, \(L\) is generated in weight one by \(I/u\). Equivalently, every term \(I^a(J\cap I^{n-a})\), \(a\ge1\), lies in \(I(J\cap I^{n-1})\), and the \(a=1\) term gives equality. Multiplication \(u:\mathscr E_n\to\mathscr E_{n-1}\) is induced by inclusion of the numerators.

**Proposition.** There is a canonical exact sequence of finite graded \(B[u]\)-modules
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathscr E\longrightarrow\mathscr N
\longrightarrow\mathscr D\longrightarrow0,
\tag{8}
\]
where \(\mathscr E\) is exactly the submodule of elements killed by a power of \(u\). Moreover
\[
\boxed{\Delta\simeq\mathscr E/u\mathscr E.}
\tag{9}
\]
In weight \(n\), the denominator in (9) is the image of \(u\mathscr E_{n+1}\).

**Proof.** Surjectivity and the kernel formula follow degree by degree from (5)–(7). The target \(\mathscr D\) embeds in \(D[u,u^{-1}]\), so multiplication by \(u\) is injective. After inverting \(u\), both middle and target modules become \(D[u,u^{-1}]\), and their map is the identity. Thus every kernel element is killed by a power of \(u\), and every such element of \(\mathscr N\) lies in the kernel.

The induced Rees ideal \(\mathscr J\) is a finite ideal of the noetherian ring \(\mathscr R\). Hence \(\mathscr N\), \(\mathscr D\), and \(\mathscr E\) are finite over \(B[u]=\mathscr R/L\). One can also see uniform nilpotence directly: \(\mathscr E_n=0\) for \(n\le1\), while Artin–Rees gives \(J\cap I^n=I(J\cap I^{n-1})\) for large \(n\). Thus only finitely many weights occur in \(\mathscr E\).

Tensor (8) with \(B[u]/(u)=B\). Its possible left obstruction is \(\operatorname{Tor}_1^{B[u]}(\mathscr D,B)\), which is the kernel of multiplication by \(u\) on \(\mathscr D\), hence zero. Since \(\mathscr D/u\mathscr D=\operatorname{gr}_FD\), this gives
\[
0\to\mathscr E/u\mathscr E\to Q\to\operatorname{gr}_FD\to0.
\]
The map sends the symbol of \(j\in J\cap I^n\) to its class in \(F^nD/F^{n+1}D\), exactly the inherited comparison map. Its kernel is \(\Delta\), proving (9). \(\square\)

This identifies the defect intrinsically: it is the special fibre of the parameter-torsion in the restricted conormal module. It is not torsion in the algebra \(\mathscr R/\mathscr J\), which is already parameter-torsionfree.

### 4. What vanishing does and does not say

Uniform \(u\)-nilpotence and (9) imply
\[
\Delta=0\iff\mathscr E=0
\iff\mathscr N\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr D.
\tag{10}
\]
For instance \(\mathscr E=u\mathscr E\) would imply \(\mathscr E=u^N\mathscr E=0\) for a sufficiently large \(N\).

Since \(k\) is a field, this is also equivalent to **flatness over the deformation parameter**:
\[
\boxed{\Delta=0\iff\mathscr N\text{ is flat over }k[u].}
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed (10) makes \(\mathscr N\) a submodule of \(D[u,u^{-1}]\), which is torsionfree over the PID \(k[u]\); torsionfree modules over this PID are flat. Conversely a \(k[u]\)-flat module has no \(u\)-torsion. Equivalently, any polynomial torsion in \(\mathscr N\) must already be \(u\)-power torsion, since its localization away from \(u\) is \(D[u,u^{-1}]\).

**This is not a statement of flatness over \(B[u]\).** Filtration pieces on the curve may fail to be saturated even when \(D\) is locally free and \(\Delta=0\). The [supported quadruple](../results/supported_quadruple_equation_filtration.md) has precisely this behaviour: its associated filtered pieces retain torsion on the curve. Thus there are separate directions of variation, the deformation parameter and the marked-curve parameter; they must not be merged into a single flatness assertion.

Likewise \(\Delta=0\) does not imply that the original embedding is determined by its associated graded algebra, or that its normal cone is a complete intersection, or that the carrier normal cones meet scheme-theoretically in that normal cone.

### 5. Preserve the carrier maps: a distinct saturation defect

Write \(A=I_X\), \(B_Y=I_Y\), so \(J=A+B_Y\). The different notation \(B_Y\) avoids confusing the carrier ideal with the base ring \(B=R/I\). Form their induced Rees ideals \(\mathscr A\), \(\mathscr B_Y\) by (1). All inclusions and quotient maps remain:
\[
C\times\mathbf A^1\subset\mathscr Z\subset
\mathscr X,\mathscr Y\subset\mathscr P.
\]
The two routes through the carriers commute with the direct embeddings. However, the original Cartesian square need not remain Cartesian at the special fibre:
\[
\boxed{\mathscr J=(\mathscr A+\mathscr B_Y):u^{\infty}.}
\tag{12}
\]
The right side is the contraction after inverting \(u\), where the sum is simply \(J R[u,u^{-1}]\). This proves (12). Thus \(\mathscr Z\) is the flat closure of the generic carrier intersection, and maps as a closed subscheme to \(\mathscr X\times_{\mathscr P}\mathscr Y\). Their difference is measured by
\[
\mathscr V=\mathscr J/(\mathscr A+\mathscr B_Y),
\tag{13}
\]
not by \(\Delta\). In particular, \(\mathscr V\) is parameter torsion of the carrier-intersection algebra; \(\mathscr E\) is parameter torsion of a restricted conormal module for the already saturated \(\mathscr Z\).

An elementary distinction is \(J=(x,x+y^m)=(x,y^m)\), \(m\ge2\), with \(I=(x,y)\). Both displayed carriers are smooth. In normal coordinates \(X=x/u,Y=y/u\), their deformed intersection has ideal
\[
(X,X+u^{m-1}Y^m)=(X,u^{m-1}Y^m),
\]
whose saturation is \((X,Y^m)\). The carrier cone intersection at \(u=0\) has ideal \((X)\), while the actual normal cone has ideal \((X,Y^m)\). Nonetheless \(\mathscr N\) is freely generated over \(B[u]\) by the classes of \(X,Y^m\), so \(\Delta=0\). Carrier-intersection saturation and conormal specialization therefore measure different failures.

These vertical features belong to the deformation and do not by themselves establish an extra component of the original \(Z\).

### 6. The torsion duration refines Delta: an exact family

Work locally with \(R=B[x,y]_{(\mathfrak m_B,x,y)}\), where \(B=k[t]_{(t)}\), and set
\[
I=(x,y),\qquad J=(x^a+y^b,xy),\qquad 2\le a<b.
\tag{14}
\]
Its quotient is free over \(B\) with basis
\[
1,x,\ldots,x^{a-1},y,\ldots,y^b,
\]
using \(xy=0\), \(x^a=-y^b\), and \(y^{b+1}=0\). The ideal is a regular-sequence ideal and is supported on the marked curve in this local neighbourhood. The example is used only to examine the invariant; no support condition is imposed in the general theorems.

Put \(r=b-a>0\). In deformation coordinates, the full induced Rees ideal is
\[
\mathscr J=(F,G,T),\quad
F=X^a+u^rY^b,\quad G=XY,\quad T=Y^{b+1}.
\tag{15}
\]
To see fullness, this ideal has the correct localization at \(u\), and its quotient is free over \(B[u]\) on the analogous displayed basis. The reduction rules \(X^a=-u^rY^b,XY=0,Y^{b+1}=0\) are compatible: their only nontrivial overlap is \(YF-X^{a-1}G=u^rT\). Hence the ideal is \(u\)-saturated and is exactly (1).

All syzygies of the three generators are generated by
\[
(Y,-X^{a-1},-u^r),\qquad (0,Y^b,-X).
\tag{16}
\]
Here is a direct completeness check. In a relation \(A_0F+B_0G+C_0T=0\), reduction modulo \(Y\) forces \(A_0\) to be divisible by \(Y\). Subtracting that multiple of the first relation eliminates its \(F\)-coefficient. Dividing the resulting relation by \(Y\) leaves \(X B_1+Y^b C_1=0\), and the regular polynomial pair \(X,Y^b\) gives the second relation. Localization preserves the presentation.

Modulo \(L=(X,Y)\), (16) gives just \(u^r[T]=0\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{\mathscr N\simeq
B[u]\,e_F\oplus B[u]\,e_G\oplus
\bigl(B[u]/(u^{b-a})\bigr)e_T,}
\tag{17}
\]
with weights \(a,2,b+1\), respectively. Thus
\[
\mathscr E\simeq(B[u]/(u^{b-a}))e_T,
\qquad
\boxed{\Delta\simeq B e_T\text{ in weight }b+1.}
\tag{18}
\]
For fixed \(b\), varying \(a\in\{2,\ldots,b-1\}\) leaves this graded defect unchanged but changes the parameter-torsion exponent \(b-a\). The full equation module also records the surviving weights \(a,2\); the claim of unchanged data concerns \(\Delta\), not all filtered invariants.

This duration matches the normal-Koszul cancellation weight gap: the relation \(YF-X^{a-1}G\) has source weight \(a+1\), the killed equation has weight \(b+1\), and their gap is \(b-a\). Here the explicit calculation establishes the match; no general classification of all relative torsion modules by spectral-sequence pages is claimed.

If integral algebraic carriers are desired, the same local ideal is realized by
\[
\widetilde f=x^a+y^b+t y^{b+1},\qquad
\widetilde g=\widetilde f+xy.
\tag{19}
\]
Both are primitive linear polynomials in \(t\), with relatively prime coefficient \(y^{b+1}\) and constant coefficient, hence irreducible in \(k[t,x,y]\). They are distinct. Since
\(y\widetilde f-x^{a-1}xy=y^{b+1}(1+ty)\), and \(1+ty\) is a unit at the origin, their localized ideal contains \(y^{b+1}\), then \(x^a+y^b\). Conversely \(y^{b+1}\) already belongs to the model ideal in (14). Thus the ideals agree locally. This is a local marked-line realization, not a new global projective pair for the fixed rational quartic.

### 7. Consequences, limits, and next questions

The geometric meaning is now precise: \(\Delta\) detects the failure of the conormal module, restricted to the fixed marked curve, to vary flatly in deformation to the normal cone. The flat algebra of the embedded deformation and all carrier maps remain necessary context. The module \(\mathscr E\), not merely its special fibre \(\Delta\), additionally records how long a spurious special conormal equation persists under the deformation parameter.

Promising questions are therefore intrinsic and separate:

1. Determine the \(u\)-power filtration of \(\mathscr E\) as sheaves on \(C\), and its precise relation to the incoming normal-Koszul differential filtration already identified in the predecessor. Over a transverse field one has elementary-divisor blocks; over \(\mathcal O_C[u]\) there need not be a compatible splitting.
2. Study curve-parameter base change of the exact sequence (8). Formation of \(\Delta\) can fail to commute with specialization on \(C\); this should be expressed using explicit Tor terms rather than inferred from deformation-parameter flatness.
3. Compare the distinct modules \(\mathscr V\) and \(\mathscr E\) while preserving the carrier source lines and all conormal maps. The smooth-carrier example proves that one cannot replace either invariant by the other.
4. Investigate local freeness of \(\mathscr N\) over \(\mathcal O_C[u]\), which is stronger than \(\Delta=0\), and identify the additional saturation conditions on the filtered equation bundle that it requires.

No assertion here reconstructs \(Z\) from \(\Delta\), determines unmarked components, or resolves unrestricted STCI. The core advance is an exact intrinsic interpretation of the existing invariant and a finer module that retains deformation duration.

## Delta, normal-cone regularity, and the labelled carrier square

Date: 2026-10-24. Status: **research with an independent agent reconstruction**.

### Scope and retained inputs

Let \(p\) be a closed point of a smooth integral curve \(C\) in a smooth threefold \(P\), over a field of arbitrary characteristic. Put \(R=\mathcal O_{P,p}\), \(B=\mathcal O_{C,p}\), \(I=I_C\), and \(J=(f,g)\subset I\), where \(f,g\) form a regular sequence. The labelled principal ideals \(A=(f), B_Y=(g)\) are retained; the letter \(B\) denotes the curve ring, not the second carrier ideal. There is **no assumption** that \(\sqrt J=I\). All statements concern the marked embedding, not classification of any additional components. The examples are local algebraic examples, not constructions for the fixed projective rational quartic.

Use the reviewed comparison from [the merged investigation, section 4](IDEAL_FILTRATIONS_AND_44_FILTERED_EXCLUSION.md#2026-10-23-ideal-filtrations-independent-investigationmd):
\[
S=\operatorname{gr}_I R=\operatorname{Sym}_B(I/I^2),\quad
H=\operatorname{in}_I J,\quad Q=H/S_+H,\quad D=J/IJ\simeq B^2,
\]
\[
F^nD=((J\cap I^n)+IJ)/IJ,\quad
0\longrightarrow\Delta_n\longrightarrow Q_n\longrightarrow G_n\longrightarrow0,
\qquad G_n=F^nD/F^{n+1}D. \tag{1}
\]
The generic generator count and supported quadruple used below are in
[R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md) and
[R-0069](../results/supported_quadruple_equation_filtration.md).
For the standard definition of regular immersion, see
[Stacks, regular immersions](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0638).

### 1. Two separate obstructions to regularity of the normal-cone embedding

Write \(\mathfrak n=(\mathfrak m_B,S_+)\). The following conditions are equivalent:

1. \(C_{C/Z}=\operatorname{Spec}(S/H)\hookrightarrow N_{C/P}=\operatorname{Spec}S\) is a regular immersion on a neighborhood of the zero section over \(p\).
2. \(Q\) is a free \(B\)-module of rank two.
3. \(\Delta=0\), and every \(G_n\) is \(B\)-torsion-free.
4. \(\Delta=0\), and every \(F^nD\) is saturated in \(D\), meaning that \(D/F^nD\) is torsion-free.

This is a criterion for the **embedding of the normal cone in the ambient normal bundle**. It is neither a flatness criterion for the cone over \(C\) nor a regular-immersion criterion for \(C\hookrightarrow Z\).

**Proof.** The associated graded dimension theorem for a proper ideal in a noetherian local ring gives
\[
\dim\operatorname{gr}_{I/J}(R/J)=\dim(R/J)=1.
\]
Consequently \(H_{\mathfrak n}\) has height two in the regular local ring \(S_{\mathfrak n}\) of dimension three. This dimension input is essential: the abstract homogeneous ideal \((tX,tY)\subset B[X,Y]\) has \(Q\simeq B^2\) but height one, and cannot be substituted for the full initial ideal in this argument.

Moreover
\[
Q/\mathfrak m_BQ\simeq H_{\mathfrak n}/\mathfrak nH_{\mathfrak n}.
\]
If the immersion is regular near the zero section, this vector space has dimension two. Choose two homogeneous lifts \(h_1,h_2\in H\) of a minimal generating set. Nakayama over \(B\), followed by graded Nakayama over \(S\), shows \(H=(h_1,h_2)\). A height-two ideal generated by two elements in a regular ring is a regular sequence. Its syzygies are generated by \((-h_2,h_1)\). Since \(h_i\in S_+\), reducing the presentation modulo \(S_+\) gives
\[
Q\simeq B(-a)\oplus B(-b),\qquad a=\deg h_1,\ b=\deg h_2.
\]
Conversely, a homogeneous basis of a free rank-two \(Q\) lifts by graded Nakayama to two generators of \(H\), which form a regular sequence by the same height argument. This proves 1 \(\Longleftrightarrow\) 2.

At the generic point of \(C\), \(J\) becomes primary to the transverse maximal ideal: a prime contained in the height-two prime \(I\) and containing the height-two regular-sequence ideal \(J\) must be \(I\) itself. The finite filtration of \(D_\eta\simeq k(C)^2\) therefore has total graded dimension two. If \(Q\) is free of rank two, summing (1) gives \(\Delta_\eta=0\). Hence \(\Delta\) is torsion; its inclusion in the torsion-free module \(Q\) forces \(\Delta=0\). Now (1) identifies each \(G_n\) with \(Q_n\), so all are free. Conversely, condition 3 identifies \(Q\) with a direct sum of free graded pieces; their total generic rank is two. Finally, for a finite filtration of a finite free module over a DVR, all successive quotients are torsion-free if and only if every filtration stage is saturated. For the forward direction build the quotients \(D/F^nD\) successively by extensions of free modules. For the reverse direction \(G_n\) is a submodule of the free module \(D/F^{n+1}D\). This proves all equivalences. \(\square\)

The local statement sheafifies on a smooth curve. The filtration and its quotients are intrinsic; no preferred splitting of \(D\), or of its filtration, is asserted. In particular, \(\Delta=0\) leaves a separate possible failure: initial equation classes can be retained in \(G_n\) as torsion arising from nonsaturated filtration stages.

### 2. The labelled carrier square has a stronger condition

Let \(f^*,g^*\in S\) denote the first nonzero normal symbols of the **original labelled equations**. Since \(S\) is a domain, normal order is additive on nonzero products, so
\[
\operatorname{in}_I(A)=(f^*),\qquad
\operatorname{in}_I(B_Y)=(g^*).
\]
There is a natural diagram
\[
C_{C/Z}\longrightarrow C_{C/X},\ C_{C/Y}\longrightarrow N_{C/P}.
\]
Its scheme-theoretic fibre product has coordinate algebra \(S/(f^*,g^*)\), and the actual normal cone has algebra \(S/H\). Thus the natural inclusion is measured by the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow H/(f^*,g^*)\longrightarrow S/(f^*,g^*)
\longrightarrow S/H\longrightarrow0. \tag{2}
\]
The normal-cone square is Cartesian if and only if \(H=(f^*,g^*)\), which holds if and only if \(f^*,g^*\) form a regular sequence in \(S\).

For sufficiency, take \(j=uf+vg\). If the lowest weighted terms of the two summands cancel, their coefficients form a homogeneous syzygy of \(f^*,g^*\). Regularity makes it a Koszul syzygy. Lift its multiplier and modify \((u,v)\) by an actual multiple of \((g,-f)\); this leaves \(j\) unchanged and strictly raises the canceled weighted order. Only finitely many such steps are needed to reach the fixed normal order of nonzero \(j\). Its symbol is therefore in \((f^*,g^*)\). Necessity follows from height two. This is the elementary two-generator standard-basis argument; it does not require a choice of formal splitting of the ambient ring.

The condition in (2) retains the maps through both carriers and is stronger than regularity of \(C_{C/Z}\hookrightarrow N_{C/P}\). It differs from \(\Delta\): (2) compares the **two labelled principal carrier ideals with their sum**, whereas \(\Delta\) compares **the full initial ideal with the intrinsic filtered equation module**. Changing \((f,g)\) by a generator transformation preserves \(J,D,F,\Delta\), but can change the labelled carrier ideals and the quotient in (2).

For example, for \(m\ge2\),
\[
f=x,\qquad g=x+y^m,\qquad I=(x,y),\qquad J=(x,y^m),
\]
both labelled carriers are smooth and integral. The full initial ideal is \(H=(X,Y^m)\), with
\[
Q=B(-1)\oplus B(-m),\quad \Delta=0,\quad
F^2D=\cdots=F^mD=B(e_g-e_f),\quad F^{m+1}D=0.
\]
But \((f^*,g^*)=(X)\), and the carrier normal cones intersect in a line bundle, larger than the actual normal cone. Replacing \(g\) by \(g-f=y^m\) adapts the generators but changes the labelled carrier; it must not be used to declare the original normal-cone square Cartesian.

### 3. Two geometric distinctions that Delta alone cannot settle

First, take two smooth integral algebraic carriers
\[
f=y,\qquad g=y+tx,\qquad I=(x,y),\qquad J=(y,tx).
\]
Here
\[
H=(Y,tX),\quad Q=B(-1)^2,\quad F^2D=0,\quad\Delta=0.
\]
The first conormal map has columns \(Y\) and \(Y+tX\); it is injective with cokernel \(B/(t)\). The cone embedding is a regular immersion, while
\[
S/H\simeq B[X]/(tX)
\]
has \(t\)-torsion and is not flat over \(B\). This example is permitted by the marked-embedding scope. No component-classification objective is attached to it.

Second, the [supported quadruple](../results/supported_quadruple_equation_filtration.md)
\[
J=(x^2,y^2+tx),\quad
H=(tX,X^2,XY^2,Y^4)
\]
has \(\Delta=0\), but \(Q_2\simeq Q_3\simeq B/(t)\). These torsion classes survive in \(G_2,G_3\). The normal-cone embedding is therefore not a regular immersion. This is exactly the additional filtration-saturation obstruction in section 1, and the example does not require residual support.

Finally, regularity of \(C\hookrightarrow Z\) itself is much stronger: it is equivalent to \(C=Z\) locally at \(p\). Indeed, both local rings have dimension one and \(R/J\) is Cohen–Macaulay; a regular sequence cutting out \(C\) in \(Z\) must have length zero. Equivalently, the central conormal map \(D\to I/I^2\) is surjective; Nakayama applied to \(I/J\) then gives \(I=J\). A regular normal-cone embedding can coexist with an arbitrarily thick marked curve.

### 4. Next directions and evidence boundary

The full embedding framework now separates three questions: whether degeneration preserves the labelled carrier fibre product; whether the actual normal cone is regularly embedded in the ambient normal bundle; and whether its structural map to the marked curve is flat. Formula (2), the two conditions in section 1, and \(B\)-torsion of \(S/H\) address these respectively. Their distinctions should be retained in future examples and global constructions.

The most useful next local question is how the nonsaturated stages \(F^nD\) interact with the algebra filtration of \(\mathcal O_Z\), especially when \(\Delta\) vanishes. A second question is to identify global bundle/extension data obstructing locally adapted normal equations from coming from the original global carriers. Neither question is resolved by the local criteria here.

## Repair of the fixed-ruling STCI implication

The earlier naive inference "a positive-degree mate meets a fixed line, hence
creates support outside C" is false: a finite zero divisor on the line could
be supported on its intersection with C.

The correct argument uses the quartic surface. Let F be a quartic whose first
normal direction along C is basepoint-free. Then F is smooth along C. If
\[
V(F,G_b)_{\mathrm{red}}=C,
\]

divisor supported on C. Its generic multiplicity is b, because the generic
transverse complete intersection has length b. Since C is irreducible, this
coefficient is constant:
\[
\operatorname{div}_F(G_b)=bC
\]
in a neighborhood of C.

Consequently, if a curve L is contained in F and meets C properly on F, then
unless G_b contains L,
\[
\deg(G_b|_L)\ge b(L\cdot_F C).
\]
For a line L, the left side is b. Hence any fixed line with
\(L\cdot_F C>1\) must also lie in G_b, producing an extra common curve.

## H. Type D separation arithmetic: a rigid multiplicity sequence

Ishii--Nakayama define separation by repeatedly blowing up a point of the current intersection of the transforms of B and G. At each step the intersection number B.G drops by one. Type D starts with a smooth plane quartic B and an effective plane cubic G, so B.G=12. Hence the separation consists of exactly twelve blowup steps, allowing infinitely-near centers.

Use the total-transform basis L,e_1,...,e_12 for Pic(M). Since B is smooth and every separation center lies on its current strict transform, the hyperplane pullback is

    H=D=4L-sum e_i.

The anticanonical exceptional divisor is

    E=3L-sum e_i=-K_M.

Let p be the Type D irrational point on the smooth rational quartic C. Projection from p has degree three, so its plane image R is a cubic. If n_i is the multiplicity of the successive strict transform of R at the i-th separation center, then

    c=3L-sum n_i e_i.

Because H.c=deg C=4,

    12-sum n_i=4,

hence

    sum n_i=8.                                      (D1)

Also

    c.E=9-sum n_i=1.                               (D2)

This already proves:

> **PROVED (Type D anticanonical passage).** Any smooth rational quartic of degree four through the Type D irrational point has strict transform meeting the exceptional anticanonical divisor with total intersection c.E=1.

Adjunction gives

    -2=c.(c+K_M)=c^2-c.E,

so c^2=-1. On the other hand

    c^2=9-sum n_i^2.

Therefore

    sum n_i^2=10.                                  (D3)

Subtracting (D1) from (D3),

    sum n_i(n_i-1)=2.

All n_i are nonnegative integers. Thus exactly one n_i equals 2, no n_i exceeds 2, and the remaining positive n_i are six 1's. Therefore:

> **PROVED (Type D multiplicity theorem).** In the twelve-step separation cluster, the projected cubic R meets exactly seven centers (counting infinitely-near centers): one with multiplicity two and six with multiplicity one. Equivalently the nonzero multiplicity sequence is
>
>     (2,1,1,1,1,1,1)
>
> up to order.

For the endpoint p=[1:0:0:0] of C0, the projected cubic is

    R: y^3=x z^2,

parametrized by [s:t] -> [s^3:s t^2:t^3]. Its unique double point is the image of t=0, i.e. the point corresponding to p. Thus in this endpoint model the multiplicity-two separation center must be that cusp if the separation cluster contains it. The containment identity

    s^4 Phi_3(s^3,s t^2,t^3)
      = t Phi_4(s^3,s t^2,t^3)

indeed forces Phi_3 restricted to R to vanish at t=0, so the cusp belongs to the B/G separation support. Hence the double center is the cusp.

Since E is effective and c.E=1, if E=sum a_j E_j in prime components, then c can meet only coefficient-one components, and the weighted total intersection is one. In particular c meets exactly one coefficient-one component transversely provided c is not itself contained in E (it is not, since H.c=4 while H.E=0).

### Consequence for the numerical correction

The universal intrinsic equation specializes in Type D to

    -Z^2=6-c.E=5,

or, for m_j=c.E_j and A=(-E_i.E_j),

    m^T A^(-1)m=5.

Write the exceptional locus as the disjoint union of the block over the irrational monoid point and the blocks over any rational double points met by c. The equality c.E=1 constrains only the irrational block. It implies that inside Supp(E), c meets exactly one coefficient-one component once. It does NOT imply that the full exceptional intersection vector m is supported there: c may also meet coefficient-zero exceptional curves over Du Val singularities. Accordingly the total correction 5 splits as

    delta_p + delta_ADE = 5,

where delta_p is the correction at the monoid point. The earlier draft's stronger assertion (A^(-1))_{jj}=5 for the irrational block alone was false and is superseded by this block decomposition.

### Scope

The calculation uses only the Type D separation construction, degree of the projected curve, H.c=4, K_M=-E, and adjunction. It does not assume B and G meet transversely, does not assume twelve distinct proper points, and does not yet determine the proximity graph of the twelve centers. That proximity graph is precisely what is needed to compute A^(-1)e_j and finish the Cartier-index test.


## I. Type D: literature compression and first-normal split

### I.1 External classification of quartic monoids

Johansen--Løberg--Piene, *Monoid hypersurfaces*, classify the complex tangent cubic of a quartic monoid into nine types: nodal cubic, cuspidal cubic, conic plus chord, conic plus tangent line, three general lines, three concurrent lines, double line plus line, triple line, and smooth cubic. Their Theorem 9 also gives the complete possibilities for singularities away from the monoid point. In every case those additional singularities are of type A_n, with n determined by residual intersection multiplicities of the tangent cubic and quartic.

Thus for Type D the rational exceptional blocks relevant to c are A-type only. This is stronger than the generic ADE allowance used in the earlier budget.

### I.2 Local expansion at the endpoint of C0

Take p=[1:0:0:0] and q=t/s. Near p,

    C0(q)=[1:q:q^3:q^4].

Put plane coordinates [x:y:z]=[X1:X2:X3]. Projection from p is

    [x:y:z]=[1:q^2:q^3]

after dividing by q. Let

    g(q)=Phi_3(1,q^2,q^3),
    b(q)=Phi_4(1,q^2,q^3).

The monoid containment equation X0 Phi_3=Phi_4 becomes

    g(q)=q b(q).                                    (D4)

Since g has no q^1 term, (D4) forces b(0)=0. Hence the smooth plane quartic B passes through the cusp point [1:0:0] of the projected cubic.

Because B is smooth there, its restriction b(q) has order either 2 or 3 according as its linear term contains y or only z. Consequently g(q) has order respectively 3 or at least 4.

In affine coordinates (u,v,w)=(X1/X0,X2/X0,X3/X0)=(q,q^3,q^4), the carrier equation is

    f(u,v,w)=Phi_3(u,v,w)-Phi_4(u,v,w).

If ord b=2, the identity permits an x^2 z term in Phi_3; then partial f/partial w can have order 2 along C0. If ord b>=3, all first partials have order at least 3 along C0. Therefore:

> **PROVED (endpoint first-normal split).** The Type D monoid point consumes first-normal order at least 2. It consumes at least 3 unless the smooth quartic B has order exactly 2 on the projected cusp.

The global quartic first-normal budget is at most 9 (or 9-e in the refined order-one notation). Thus the A-type singularities away from p have total first-normal budget at most 7 in the order-2 subcase and at most 6 in the order-at-least-3 subcase.

### I.3 Correction at the irrational block

Let A_p be the negative intersection matrix of the exceptional block over p and write E=sum a_i E_i there. Since c.E=1, c meets a unique component E_j with a_j=1, once, and no other component of Supp(E). Thus

    delta_p=(A_p^(-1))_{jj}.

The component met by c is the component reached by the tangent direction of the projected cubic after separation. At the initial cusp center it comes from a reduced local branch of the tangent cubic. For a coefficient-one strict-transform component R of plane degree d=1,2,3, adjunction/separation gives -E.R=K_M.R>=1. Since

    A_p a = (-E.E_i)_i >=0

and its j-th entry is at least one, positivity of A_p^(-1) gives

    A_p^(-1)e_j <= a

componentwise. In particular

    delta_p <= a_j=1.

This comparison is valid when the component met by c is such a reduced strict-transform component. The remaining possibility that the tangent direction terminates on a coefficient-one (-2)-component created by a nonreduced/infinitely-near separation needs a separate check; it is not silently included.

### I.4 Harmonic consequence

Let N be the total A-root rank away from p and P their total first-normal order. Since rho(M)=13 and the irrational block has positive rank,

    N<=11.

The existing harmonic estimate gives

    delta_A <= N P/(N+P).

In the order-at-least-3 subcase, P<=6, hence

    delta_A <= 66/17 < 4.

If delta_p<=1 as in I.3, the mate relation requires

    delta_A=5-delta_p >=4,

a contradiction, with exact gap at least 2/17.

Therefore:

> **CONDITIONAL EXCLUSION.** Type D is excluded whenever (i) the endpoint monoid passage has first-normal order at least 3 and (ii) c meets a reduced coefficient-one strict-transform component of the tangent cubic.

The only endpoint configurations still requiring analysis are:
- the first-normal-order-2 case (ord b=2), where the coarse harmonic bound with P<=7 is not by itself contradictory; and
- any nonreduced/infinitely-near configuration in which the component met by c is a coefficient-one (-2)-curve, for which delta_p<=1 has not yet been justified.

This is now a small local problem. The monoid classification suggests treating it case-by-case through the nine tangent-cubic types rather than enumerating arbitrary negative-definite graphs.


## J. Type D: sharp Bezout correction bound

For a normalized quartic monoid, every isolated singularity away from the monoid point is A-type. If the tangent cubic G and quartic B have local intersection multiplicity m at the corresponding base point, the singularity is A_{m-1}. Hence for the off-vertex singularities met by c,

    sum (n_i+1) <= B.G = 12.

For Jaffe's A_n^k pair,

    delta(n,k)=k(n+1-k)/(n+1) <= (n+1)/4.

Therefore

    delta_off <= 3.                                (D5)

Since the total correction is 5,

    delta_p = 5-delta_off >= 2.                    (D6)

Thus every Type D STCI survivor must have correction at least two at the irrational monoid point.

### Endpoint order-two passage

In the endpoint notation, the first-normal-order-two case has ord b=2 and ord g=3 on the projected cusp (y,z)=(q^2,q^3). Smoothness of B makes its linear term a nonzero y-term, while the order-three identity makes G have a nonzero z-linear term. Thus B and G are smooth and transverse at the cusp, so their separation has exactly one blowup there.

The projected cusp is tangent to G. After the blowup, its strict transform still meets the reduced coefficient-one strict transform of G once. Hence the anticanonical comparison of I.3 applies and gives delta_p<=1, contradicting (D6).

> **PROVED (endpoint order-two exclusion).** No Type D STCI candidate with the irrational point at the endpoint [1:0:0:0] can lie in the first-normal-order-two branch.

Every endpoint survivor must therefore have first-normal order at least three and, by (D6), local irrational correction at least two. The remaining finite question is whether the cusp-following separation for one of the nine tangent-cubic types can land c on a coefficient-one exceptional component with inverse-matrix diagonal at least two. If such a component exists, its inverse column also gives the exact Cartier denominator for the compression test.


## K. Type D endpoint exclusion completed

This section finishes the Type D analysis when the irrational triple point is the endpoint p=[1:0:0:0] of C0.

### K.1 Exact local shape in the remaining branch

In the order-at-least-three branch of I.2, B is smooth at the projected cusp a and its restriction to R has order exactly three. Indeed the y-linear coefficient must vanish, while the z-linear coefficient is nonzero by smoothness. Thus

    ord_R(B)=3,
    ord_R(G)=4

by the containment identity g=q b.

In local cusp coordinates (y,z)=(q^2,q^3), order four for a cubic G means that its first nonzero term is a nonzero multiple of y^2. Hence G has multiplicity exactly two at a and has double tangent y=0. In particular the multiplicity-three tangent-cubic configurations (triple line, or the intersection point of a double line with another component) cannot occur at a.

The possible local forms are therefore:
1. an irreducible cuspidal branch;
2. two reduced tangent branches (the conic-plus-tangent-line type);
3. a smooth point of a double line.

### K.2 The separation over a

Ishii--Nakayama separation uses

    B_{j+1}=rho^* B_j - Gamma,
    G_{j+1}=rho^* G_j - Gamma,

not merely the strict transform of G. Consequently B_j.G_j drops by exactly one at each blowup. Since B is transverse to the double tangent direction of G, I_a(B,G)=2. Exactly two separation blowups occur over a.

After the first blowup,

    G_1 = G_strict + F_1

in the reduced multiplicity-two cases. B_1 meets F_1 at the B tangent direction, while the strict transform of the projected cusp meets F_1 at the distinct G tangent direction. The second blowup is therefore at B_1 cap F_1 and misses c. After it, the component met by c is the coefficient-one curve F with

    F^2=-2.

Thus the exceptional (-2)-curve found in the previous section is real, but its correction can be computed explicitly from the adjacent strict transform(s) of G.

### K.3 Local correction in the three cases

**Irreducible cusp.** The strict transform Q of the cubic loses 2^2 at a and one square for each of the remaining ten units of B.G, hence

    Q^2 = 9-4-10 = -5.

Moreover Q.F=2. The relevant block is

    A = [[5,-2],[-2,2]],

so

    delta_p = (A^{-1})_{FF}=5/6.

**Two reduced tangent branches.** The branches are a line and a conic. The previous draft incorrectly asserted that adding negative-definite attachments could only decrease the diagonal Green value at F. That monotonicity statement is false and is superseded here.

Let r and s be the remaining separation lengths assigned to the line and conic branches after the two cusp blowups. Since the residual B.G intersection is ten, r+s=10. In the total-transform calculation the relevant three-vertex Schur block has diagonal entries r, s-3, 2 and F meets each branch once. Negative definiteness leaves r=1,...,6. Direct inversion gives respectively

    delta_p = 6/5, 10/13, 12/17, 12/17, 10/13, 6/5.

Thus in every admissible allocation

    delta_p <= 6/5 < 2.

This finite calculation replaces the false monotonicity argument. Infinitely-near distribution along a fixed branch does not change the Schur-complement value: successive separation curves contract back to the same effective self-intersection contribution determined by the total separation length on that branch.

**Double line.** At a smooth point of the doubled line, F meets the line once. The line strict transform has square at most -3. The two-vertex comparison gives

    delta_p <= ([[3,-1],[-1,2]]^{-1})_{FF}=3/5.

The other line does not pass through a (otherwise mult_a G=3); including its attachment to the doubled line does not approach the required value two.

Thus every admissible local multiplicity-two form satisfies

    delta_p <= 6/5 < 2.

But J.1 gives the necessary inequality delta_p>=2. Therefore:

> **PROVED (Type D endpoint exclusion).** No Type D normal quartic STCI carrier can have its irrational triple point at the endpoint p=[1:0:0:0] of C0.

The remaining Type D question is whether an arbitrary point p in C0 reduces to the same local pattern after projection, or whether interior points produce a nodal rather than cuspidal projected cubic and require a separate calculation.


## L. Type D at a general point of C0: nodal projection and contact tradeoff

Let p=C0(a) with a nonzero finite parameter, so

    C0(u)=[1:u:u^3:u^4].

Projection from p can be represented by the three linear forms

    X1-a X0,  X2-a^3 X0,  X3-a^4 X0.

After cancelling u-a, the projected cubic R has parametrization

    [1 : u^2+a u+a^2 : (u+a)(u^2+a^2)].

The two distinct parameters

    u=a omega,  u=a omega^2,   omega^3=1, omega!=1,

have the same image. Thus for a!=0,infinity the projected cubic is nodal. The marked image r of p itself is smooth: its parameter value u=a has no second preimage.

### L.1 Local monoid identity at the marked smooth point

Put q=u-a. In projection coordinates the normalization near r is

    [1 : 3a^2+3a q+q^2 : 4a^3+6a^2 q+4a q^2+q^3].

The coordinate along the line through p has a simple pole in q. Hence the Type D equation again restricts to

    g(q)=q b(q),                                   (D7)

where b=B|_R and g=G|_R.

Unlike the endpoint cusp, the local ring of R at r is regular, so there is no semigroup gap forcing b(0)=0.

If b(0)!=0, then g has a simple zero. Thus G is smooth and transverse to R at r, while B misses r. No B/G separation blowup occurs over r. The strict transform c meets a reduced coefficient-one component of the anticanonical divisor directly, so the anticanonical comparison gives

    delta_p<=1.

This contradicts the universal Type D necessity delta_p>=2 from (D6). Therefore every general-point survivor must satisfy

    b(0)=0.

### L.2 Local normal form and contact accounting

Because R is smooth at r, choose local coordinates with R=(y=0), x=q. Since B is smooth, after a local coordinate normalization write

    B = y + beta(x),

where h=ord_x beta>=1 is the contact order I_r(B,R).

Equation (D7) says G(x,0)=x beta(x). Therefore locally

    G = x B + y A

for some local function A (after absorbing units/signs). In particular

    I_r(R,G)=h+1.                                  (D8)

Since deg R=deg G=3,

    R.G=9,

so

    1<=h<=8.                                       (D9)

This is the correct finite parameter for the general-point Type D problem.

There is also a compensation with the off-vertex correction. Let mu=I_r(B,G), i.e. the number of Ishii--Nakayama separation steps over r. Then only 12-mu units of the global B.G=12 intersection remain available away from r. The monoid A-type estimate sharpens from (D5) to

    delta_off <= (12-mu)/4.                        (D10)

Hence the mate relation forces

    delta_p >= 5-(12-mu)/4 = 2+mu/4.              (D11)

So increasing the local B/G contact makes the required irrational correction grow linearly. The remaining task is to bound the actual local Green value delta_p from above in terms of the same separation length mu (or the contact h). If one proves

    delta_p < 2+mu/4

for every 1<=h<=8 and every cubic local form compatible with G=xB+yA, Type D is excluded for every point of C0.

This is now a finite local inequality rather than a global quartic-carrier classification problem.

## M. General-point Type D: local separation algebra

### M.1 Reduced one-branch case

A general-point Type D survivor must therefore make the anticanonical separation tree branch at r. For a plane cubic this means that, locally at r, G is one of:

## N. General-point branching bound

This section closes the local branching alternatives left in M.2, subject to the matrix interpretation stated there.

Because R is not a component of G, I_r(R,G) is finite. If mult_r(G)=3, then a plane cubic has no terms of degree below three at r; its local equation is its cubic tangent cone. Restriction to the smooth branch R=(y=0) therefore has order exactly three unless y divides G, which would make R a component. Hence

    h+1=3, so h=2.                                 (D14)

Thus multiplicity-three local forms do not generate arbitrarily long contact sequences.

### N.1 Reduced branching

Suppose the coefficient-one (-2)-curve F met by c is adjacent to k reduced branches of G. A reduced cubic branch, after the full B/G separation, contributes an effective negative diagonal at least 3 at the attachment. The Green value at F is therefore bounded by the corresponding star Schur complement.

For two branches,

    delta_p <= 1/(2-1/3-1/3)=3/4.

For three branches,

    delta_p <= 1/(2-1/3-1/3-1/3)=1.

The endpoint finite calculation shows how further separation along a branch is absorbed into its effective diagonal; with fixed cubic self-intersection and the B.G budget it does not make a reduced branch contribution exceed the extremal 1/3 used above.

Hence every reduced branching configuration has

    delta_p<=1.                                    (D15)

### N.2 Nonreduced adjacency

If F is adjacent to a doubled component, the corresponding intersection entry can have magnitude two. The worst negative-definite two-vertex block is

    A=[[3,-2],[-2,2]],

whose inverse has

    (A^{-1})_{FF}=3/2.

Larger branch diagonal only decreases this value. Thus

    delta_p<=3/2                                   (D16)

for doubled adjacency before any further constraints are imposed.

A triple adjacency would require an off-diagonal magnitude three. With F^2=-2 and a cubic-component diagonal in the available range, the corresponding principal minor fails negative definiteness; it cannot occur as such an exceptional block. Multiplicity-three cubic configurations are in any case constrained by (D14).

### N.3 Consequence

Combining the unbranched reduced estimate (D13), reduced branching (D15), and nonreduced estimate (D16), every general-point local configuration satisfies

    delta_p<=3/2.

But the global B/G Bezout tradeoff requires

    delta_p>=2+mu/4>2.

Therefore:

> **PROVED, subject to independent audit of the branch-effective-diagonal lemma.** No Type D normal quartic STCI carrier can have its irrational point at an interior point p=C0(a), a!=0,infinity.

Together with the endpoint exclusion, this would remove Type D completely for C0. The branch-effective-diagonal assertion used in N.1 should be checked directly from the Ishii--Nakayama proximity basis (or by a small exhaustive proximity-matrix certificate). That check is preferable to relying on electrical-network intuition.

## O. Audit of the general-point bound: blowup coefficients and a corrected target

The proposed proximity enumeration in N.4 can be constrained much more strongly by tracking the anticanonical divisor itself.

    -K_{M'} = rho^*(-K_M) - F.

Writing E=-K_M, if the total multiplicity of E at q is s, the coefficient of F in the new anticanonical divisor is

    a_F=s-1.                                       (D17)

Thus:
- a smooth point of a coefficient-one component (s=1) creates a coefficient-zero exceptional;
- an anticanonical multiplicity-two center creates a coefficient-one exceptional;
- a multiplicity-three center creates a coefficient-two exceptional.

This corrects the informal picture used in parts of M--N. In particular, because c.E=1, the component met by c must have coefficient one. ## P. Intrinsic reduction of the remaining general-point certificate

The blowup recursion can be compressed further using the anticanonical equations themselves.

Let F=E_j be the component met by c. Since a_j=1,

    1 = a_j = sum_i (A^{-1})_{ji} b_i,

where b=Aa and b_i=-E.E_i>=0. The inverse of the connected negative intersection M-matrix is entrywise nonnegative. Therefore, if b_j>=1,

    delta_p=(A^{-1})_{jj} <= 1.

Such a component cannot survive (D11). Hence every survivor must satisfy

    b_j=-E.F=0.

Adjunction then identifies F as a rational (-2)-curve.

### P.1 Weighted-adjacency dichotomy

Because F has coefficient one and F^2=-2,

    0=E.F=-2 + sum_{D adjacent F} a_D (F.D).

Thus the weighted adjacency sum is exactly two. There are only two local boundary patterns:

(A) F meets one coefficient-two component transversely once; or

(B) F meets two coefficient-one components transversely once each,

with the equivalent possibility of total intersection multiplicity two with one coefficient-one component.

This is intrinsic and replaces the arbitrary star enumeration in N.

### P.2 Pattern B

For the minimal three-vertex block with coefficient vector (1,1,1), F central, E^2=-3 and Aa>=0 force the neighboring negative self-intersections to be (2,3) up to order. Exact inversion gives

    delta_p=6/7.

Further anticanonical attachments must preserve Aa>=0 and total energy a^T A a=3; they cannot be inserted freely. A complete proof that they do not raise delta_p above the threshold should follow by pruning coefficient-one leaves using the same adjunction equations. This is now a much smaller lattice lemma.

### P.3 Pattern A

The minimal two-vertex block with coefficient vector (1,2) cannot satisfy E^2=-3 and Aa>=0 for any integral neighbor self-intersection. Hence pattern A necessarily contains additional anticanonical structure.

Geometrically the coefficient-two neighbor can only be produced by a blowup at anticanonical multiplicity three. Since c.E=1, the marked direction cannot terminate there. To reach F it must subsequently pass through a multiplicity-two center, which creates the coefficient-one (-2)-curve F. Thus any pattern-A survivor contains a forced two-stage proximity motif:

    multiplicity 3 center -> coefficient 2 component
    followed along c by
    multiplicity 2 center -> coefficient 1 (-2) component F.

The remaining certificate has therefore collapsed to two tiny tasks:
1. prove the pruning lemma for pattern B;
2. enumerate the degree-three multiplicity-three centers compatible with the forced pattern-A motif.

No enumeration of all twelve-step proximity matrices is required.


## P. Potential-theoretic reduction and an adjunction caveat

Let F=E_j be the coefficient-one component met by c and put

    b=Aa=(-E.E_i)_i.

Since a=A^{-1}b and a_j=1,

    1 = sum_i (A^{-1})_{ji} b_i.                  (D21)

If b_j>=1, positivity of A^{-1} immediately gives

    delta_p=(A^{-1})_{jj}<=1.

Hence every possible survivor has b_j=0. For a blowup-created smooth rational component F, adjunction then gives F^2=-2. The anticanonical equation E.F=0 and a_F=1 forces the weighted neighboring coefficient sum to equal two. Thus only the local motifs

    1+1   or   2

can occur at F.

If z=A^{-1}e_j, then on any chain of blowup-created coefficient-one (-2)-curves with b=0,

    2 z_i = z_{i-1}+z_{i+1}.

Thus z is affine along such zero-charge chains. Equation (D21) says that the boundary values are normalized by the total anticanonical charge:

    sum_i z_i b_i=1.

This is the correct discrete-potential formulation of the remaining Type D problem.

### P.1 Important adjunction caveat

One must not replace b_i by (-E_i^2-2) on every vertex of the irrational block. The strict transforms of components of the plane cubic G can themselves be singular (for example nodal or cuspidal), and G can be nonreduced. The smooth-rational adjunction formula applies without qualification only to the exceptional curves created by blowups (and to strict-transform components separately known to be smooth rational).

A brute-force search that imposes b_i=d_i-2 on the entire block therefore searches the wrong class and can even return an empty state space. Such a failed search is not evidence for exclusion.

### P.2 Correct proof architecture

Separate the irrational block into:
1. the blowup-created rational tree T, on which the zero-charge (-2) potential recurrence is exact; and
2. the strict transforms of the original components of G, treated as boundary vertices with coefficients and intersection data inherited from the plane cubic.

The marked vertex F lies in T in the only unresolved cases. The desired bound delta_p<2+mu/4 can therefore be proved by eliminating T via continued fractions/Schur complements and checking the finite list of possible cubic boundary types. This is smaller and safer than enumerating arbitrary proximity matrices.

The next calculation should compute the effective conductance from F through T to each original G component for the local multiplicity-three configurations and the 2-to-1 coefficient transition. The boundary calculation remains necessary for this argument.


## Q. Exact elimination of blowup-created (-2)-chains

The conditional Type D audit can be compressed further by eliminating every zero-charge rational (-2)-chain exactly.

Let F be the coefficient-one (-2)-component met by c. Suppose a chain of r blowup-created (-2)-curves lies between F and a boundary component D. The A_r Cartan block has inverse

    (A_r^{-1})_{ij}=min(i,j)(r+1-max(i,j))/(r+1).

Schur elimination therefore subtracts

    r/(r+1)

from each endpoint diagonal and replaces the path by an effective off-diagonal

    -1/(r+1).                                      (D21)

This corrects another unsafe monotonicity heuristic: a longer (-2)-tail can lower the effective diagonal at F and hence increase its Green value. Extra separation blowups cannot simply be discarded.

### Q.1 Anticanonical coefficients restrict the chain lengths

On a zero-charge (-2)-chain, Aa=b with b_i=0 gives

    2a_i=a_{i-1}+a_{i+1}.                          (D22)

Thus the positive integral anticanonical coefficients are affine along the chain.

Starting at a_F=1 and ending at a boundary coefficient m, a chain with r internal vertices has

    a_k=1+k(m-1)/(r+1).

Consequently integrality gives:

- m=1: arbitrary r is arithmetically possible, with every internal coefficient equal to 1;
- m=2: no nonempty zero-charge (-2)-chain is possible;
- m=3: the only nonempty possibility has r=1 and coefficient sequence 1--2--3.

So a coefficient-two original/boundary component must be directly adjacent to F. A coefficient-three boundary can be separated from F by at most one zero-charge (-2)-vertex. The only potentially long tails are constant coefficient-one chains.

### Q.2 Remaining obstruction is coefficient-one tails

This isolates the genuine unresolved case. A long coefficient-one (-2)-chain can connect F to a coefficient-one boundary component without violating (D22). Its Schur contribution approaches one as its length grows, so an abstract lattice argument alone does not yet give delta_p<2.

The plane-cubic geometry must now be used precisely once: determine whether such a constant coefficient-one chain can occur along the marked tangent direction in the Ishii--Nakayama B/G separation process, and if so bound its length/contact by I_r(R,G)=h+1 and the local separation multiplicity mu.

Thus the certificate no longer needs arbitrary proximity trees. It needs only:
1. direct coefficient-2 attachments;
2. the unique 1--2--3 short chain;
3. constant coefficient-one (-2)-chains, whose lengths are controlled by the local plane-curve contact data.

This is the smallest audited formulation reached so far.


## Q. Two-arm coefficient-one tails: exact comparison

The remaining dangerous lattice motif is a coefficient-one (-2)-curve F met by c with two coefficient-one zero-charge arms. Pure lattice theory alone does not bound its Green value below 2: two sufficiently long arms can make (A^{-1})_{FF}>2. Thus the B/G separation budget is essential.

Collapse an arm of r coefficient-one (-2)-curves terminating at a boundary with effective diagonal d>=3. In the extremal case d=3 its Schur load at F is

    lambda_r=(2r+1)/(2r+3).

Hence for two arms of lengths r,s the worst Green value is

    delta_F
      =1/(2-lambda_r-lambda_s)
      =((2r+3)(2s+3))/(4(r+s+3)).                 (D21)

Put t=r+s. By AM-GM,

    (2r+3)(2s+3) <= (r+s+3)^2=(t+3)^2,

so

    delta_F <= (t+3)/4.                            (D22)

Each zero-charge arm vertex arises from a B/G separation blowup, so the local separation length satisfies

    mu >= r+s=t.                                   (D23)

The global Bezout tradeoff requires

    delta_p >= 2+mu/4 >= (t+8)/4.                  (D24)

Comparing (D22) and (D24) leaves a uniform gap 5/4. Therefore no two-arm coefficient-one tail can survive, provided the terminal effective boundary on each arm has diagonal at least 3.

This is stronger than a finite mu<=12 check: it holds for arbitrary arm lengths.

### Q.1 Exact remaining audit point

The only unproved input in this reduction is the terminal-response assertion

    d_eff >= 3.                                    (D25)

It must be verified for the finite list of original degree-three G boundary germs allowed by the Ishii--Nakayama Type D separation process. It should not be assumed from rational-component adjunction, because an original cubic component can be singular or nonreduced.

Thus the general-point Type D exclusion is now conditional on one finite boundary calculation: prove (D25) for each original cubic boundary type reached by a coefficient-one arm. Once (D25) is established, (D21)--(D24) eliminate the last 1+1 motif, while the direct coefficient-two motifs are handled separately by the coefficient recursion.
