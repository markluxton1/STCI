# Full-ideal Rees data and vertical degeneration controls

These independently reconstructed statements distinguish the
normalized blowup of the **full lci ideal** from intersections of chosen
strict transforms on the blowup of the reduced marked curve. No unrestricted residual theorem is asserted. The graph cycle
and support-sensitive numerical bounds are proved in [the two-blowup account](blowup_residual_geometry.md).



## Full lci ideals: exceptional divisors, coefficient budget, and Segre class

## 2. No special-point divisorial Rees valuations for an lci curve ideal

**Proposition 1.** Let \(X\) be a smooth integral threefold of finite type
over a field, and let \(Y\hookrightarrow X\) be a codimension-two regular
immersion. Suppose \(Y_{\mathrm{red}}=C\) is an irreducible curve. Put
\[
 b:B=\operatorname{Bl}_{I_Y}X\longrightarrow X,
 \qquad \nu:\overline B\longrightarrow B
\]
for the blowup and its normalization. Every exceptional prime divisor of
\(\overline B\to X\) dominates \(C\). In particular, no divisorial Rees
valuation of \(I_Y\) has center equal to a closed point of \(C\).

Moreover localization at \(\eta_C\) identifies these prime divisors with
the divisorial Rees valuations of the transverse parameter ideal
\((I_Y)_{\eta_C}\) in the two-dimensional regular local ring
\(\mathcal O_{X,\eta_C}\).

**Proof.** Locally write \(I_Y=(f,g)\), with \(f,g\) a regular sequence.
Its Rees algebra has presentation
\[
 \mathcal R(I_Y)=\mathcal O_X[A,B]/(gA-fB).
\]
This is the regular-sequence linear-type presentation; equivalently,
relations among the homogeneous monomials in \(f,g\) are generated by
the Koszul relation. Reducing the presentation modulo \(I_Y\) shows
that the exceptional Cartier divisor is
\[
 E_Y=\mathbf P_Y(I_Y/I_Y^2).
\]
Since \(I_Y/I_Y^2\) has rank two, \((E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is an
irreducible ruled surface over \(C\). The fiber over a closed point of
\(C\) has dimension one.

The blowup is integral, since its Rees algebra sits inside
\(\mathcal O_X[z]\), and its normalization is finite: all schemes are of
finite type over a field. A finite morphism preserves the dimension of
a closed irreducible subset. Therefore the inverse image in
\(\overline B\) of an exceptional fiber over a closed point has dimension
one and contains no prime divisor of the threefold. All exceptional
prime divisors are contained in \(\nu^{-1}(E_Y)\); their images in \(B\)
have dimension two and hence equal \((E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}\). They
therefore dominate \(C\).

Blowup and finite normalization commute with localization. Since every
exceptional prime survives localization at \(\eta_C\), and taking the
closure recovers it, the stated bijection follows. \(\square\)

The same dimension argument applies when \(Y_{\mathrm{red}}\) has
several curve components, including residual components: every
exceptional prime divisor dominates a curve component of the reduced
support. There is still no exceptional divisor over a closed point.

This does **not** say that the global normalized exceptional surfaces
carry no special-point information. Their curves, finite-cover geometry,
ramification, conductor, and special fibers can carry such information.
It says that the list of divisorial valuations itself is already visible
over the generic points of the support curves.

## 3. The exact Rees multiplicity budget

Retain Proposition 1 and put
\[
 m=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{X,\eta_C}}
                  (\mathcal O_{Y,\eta_C}).
\]
Write
\[
 \nu^*E_Y=\sum_i a_iD_i,
 \qquad
 f_i=[k(D_i):k((E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}})].
\]

**Proposition 2.** The intrinsic Rees coefficients satisfy
\[
 \boxed{\sum_i a_if_i=m.}
\]

**Proof.** A rank-two projective bundle over \(Y\) has the same generic
cycle multiplicity along its reduced support as \(Y\) has along \(C\).
Thus the Cartier divisor \(E_Y\) has cycle
\(m[(E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}]\). Since \(\nu\) is finite birational, the
projection formula for Cartier divisors gives
\[
 \nu_*[\nu^*E_Y]=[E_Y].
\]
The pushforward of \([D_i]\) has coefficient \(f_i\) on the reduced
exceptional surface. Comparing coefficients proves the identity.
\(\square\)

For the transverse ideal \((u^a,v^b)\), let \(h=\gcd(a,b)\). Its
single monomial Rees valuation has primitive weights
\((b/h,a/h)\). The vanishing coefficient of the ideal is \(ab/h\),
and the map of exceptional direction curves has degree \(h\): it is
the map with rational function \(u^a/v^b\), the \(h\)-th power of a
primitive direction parameter. Hence \(a_1f_1=ab\), as required.

For a hypothetical \((d,e)\) STCI of \(C_0\), this identity reads
\(\sum_i a_if_i=de/4\). It is a genuine generator-independent
decomposition of the transverse multiplicity. No restriction on the
global exceptional finite covers has yet been extracted from it.

## 4. Ordinary Segre numbers are fixed by the CI degrees

**Proposition 3.** If \(Y=V(F_d,G_e)\subset\mathbf P^3\) is a proper
complete intersection, then
\[
 i_*s(Y,\mathbf P^3)
 =de\,H^2-de(d+e)\,H^3.
\]

**Proof.** The regular immersion has normal bundle
\(\mathcal O_Y(d)\oplus\mathcal O_Y(e)\). Its normal cone is that
bundle, so the definition of the Segre class gives
\[
 s(Y,\mathbf P^3)
 =c(\mathcal O_Y(d)\oplus\mathcal O_Y(e))^{-1}\cap[Y].
\]
Pushing forward and expanding in dimensions one and zero proves the
formula. \(\square\)

Consequently these ordinary global Segre numbers cannot distinguish
special-point normal-filtration defects among proper CIs of fixed
degrees. One must use finer data, for example the filtration relative to
\(I_C\), rather than only the Segre class of \(Y\) in the ambient space.
The proposition supplies no additional STCI obstruction.

## 5. An intrinsic saturation defect unbounded with fixed generic algebra

Let \(A=k[t]\), choose integers \(p\ge2\), \(a>p\), and \(b>p\), and
work in the smooth affine threefold
\(X=\operatorname{Spec}A[u,v]\). For \(N\ge1\), set
\[
 C=V(u,v),\quad \mathfrak q=(u,v),\qquad
 J_N=(u^b,v^a+t^Nu^p),\quad Y_N=V(J_N).
\]
The earlier exceptional-cycle note proves that \(Y_N\) is a
\(C\)-supported lci, finite free of rank \(ab\) over \(A\), with basis
\(u^iv^j\), \(0\le i<b,0\le j<a\). We use this verified description
without re-auditing its vertical chosen-generator calculation.

Let \(\mathfrak q_N=\mathfrak q\mathcal O_{Y_N}\). The first actual
relation is
\[
 R_p=\ker\!\left(
  \operatorname{Sym}^p(\mathfrak q/\mathfrak q^2)
      \longrightarrow \mathfrak q_N^p/\mathfrak q_N^{p+1}
 \right).
\]

**Proposition 4.** In the monomial basis of the symmetric power,
\[
 R_p=A\,t^Nu^p,\qquad
 R_p^{\mathrm{sat}}=A\,u^p,\qquad
 R_p^{\mathrm{sat}}/R_p\simeq A/(t^N).
\]
Thus the intrinsic first-relation saturation defect has length exactly
\(N\). Equivalently,
\[
 \mathfrak q_N^p/\mathfrak q_N^{p+1}
       \simeq A^{\oplus p}\oplus A/(t^N).
\]

**Proof.** Since \(a,b>p\), reduce the defining ideal modulo
\(\mathfrak q^{p+1}\). Its image in the degree-\(p\) summand is precisely
\(A\,t^Nu^p\): both \(u^b\) and \(v^a\) vanish, and every
positive-normal-degree coefficient of the remaining generator also
vanishes. There is no lower-degree relation. Saturation over the PID
\(A\) replaces \(t^Nu^p\) by \(u^p\). The quotient assertions follow
immediately. \(\square\)

This defect depends on the full ideal and the embedding of \(C\); it
does not depend on replacing the displayed generators by another
generating pair.

**Corollary 4a (fixed generic filtered algebra).** On the subsequence
\(N=aL\), \(L\ge1\), all generic filtered algebras are isomorphic over
\(K=k(t)\). Indeed the filtration-preserving change of normal coordinates
\(v=t^Lv'\) identifies
\[
 K[u,v]/(u^b,v^a+t^{aL}u^p)
 \simeq K[u,v']/(u^b,v'^a+u^p).
\]
The maximal-ideal filtration is preserved because \(t^L\) is a unit in
\(K\). The intrinsic saturation-defect lengths \(aL\), nevertheless,
are unbounded. The same generic coordinate identifications identify the
generic Rees valuations of the full ideals, including their Rees
coefficients and finite generic residue degrees. Proposition 1 says
there are no additional special-point divisorial Rees valuations that
could supply the missing defect length.

This compares generic data under explicit coordinate identifications;
it does not claim that the valuations, viewed as valuations of one fixed
ambient rational-function field without those identifications, are
literally equal.

**Corollary 4b (the full generic CI initial algebra can also be fixed).**
Choose \(b=2p\). Over \(K\), the defining ideal is equivalently
\[
 (u^p+t^{-N}v^a,\ v^{2a}).
\]
Its full ordinary initial ideal is
\[
 (u^p,v^{2a}).
\]
To see equality of initial ideals, both displayed initial forms belong
to the initial ideal, are coprime, and define an algebra of length
\(2pa\), which is already the length of the original quotient. An
additional initial relation would strictly reduce that length.

Therefore one fixed coprime binary-CI generic initial type \((p,2a)\),
one fixed multiplicity \(2pa\), and even one fixed generic filtered
algebra admit arbitrarily long intrinsic first-relation saturation
defects. For instance \(p=2,a=3,b=4,N=3L\) has fixed generic initial
type \((2,6)\), multiplicity \(12\), and defect length \(3L\).

This is compatible with STCI-R-0010: its saturated-first-relation
hypothesis fails for every \(N>0\). It shows exactly why a replacement
of that hypothesis by generic initial-CI data, generic Rees data, or
constant transverse length would be false. It does not rule out a
global bound involving the projective degrees of an actual \(C_0\)
presentation; this affine family has no such fixed global degree data.

**Corollary 4c (a fixed non-CI full generic initial ideal).** Choose
\(b=p+1\) instead. The full generic ordinary initial ideal is then
\[
 (u^p,\ uv^a,\ v^{2a}),
\]
which has three minimal generators. Indeed over \(K\), write
\(F=u^p+t^{-N}v^a\). Subtracting \(u^{p+1}\) from \(uF\) produces
\(uv^a\); then subtracting \(u^{p-1}(uv^a)\) from \(v^aF\) produces
\(v^{2a}\). Their initial monomials have total colength
\(2a+(p-1)a=(p+1)a\), exactly the original quotient length, proving
equality of initial ideals. Thus the same unbounded intrinsic defect
also occurs for a fixed three-generator generic initial algebra. For
\(p=2,a=3,b=3,N=3L\), this fixed initial ideal is
\((u^2,uv^3,v^6)\), of length nine.

The graded algebra in Corollary 4c has exactly two socle monomials,
\(u^{p-1}v^{a-1}\) and \(v^{2a-1}\), of respective degrees
\(p+a-2\) and \(2a-1\). In particular, the original local algebra is
Gorenstein (it is a complete intersection), while its ordinary tangent
graded algebra is not Gorenstein. A graded self-duality argument that
uses one socle degree cannot be transferred to this non-CI tangent type
without an additional construction of the dual filtration.



## Unbounded vertical degeneration with no residual support

## 3. Unbounded vertical fibers with fixed entirely-thick generic data

**Proposition 3 (local counterexample family).** Fix integers \(p\ge2\), \(a>p\), \(b>p\). For each \(N\ge1\), in the smooth threefold \(\mathbf A^3_{t,u,v}\), put
\[
C=V(u,v),\qquad f=t^N u^p+v^a,\qquad g=f+u^b.
\]
Then the following hold, over any field (in particular in characteristic zero).

1. \((f,g)=(u^b,v^a+t^N u^p)\) is a complete-intersection ideal with radical \((u,v)\).
2. Its quotient is finite free of rank \(m=ab\) over \(k[t]\), with basis \(u^i v^j\), \(0\le i<b,0\le j<a\).
3. Both displayed equations have exact generic normal order \(p\).
4. On \(\operatorname{Bl}_C\mathbf A^3\), the intersection cycle of their two strict surfaces consists of the horizontal section \(u=0\) in the exceptional bundle, with coefficient \(ab-p^2\), and the vertical exceptional fiber over \(t=0\), with coefficient \(N(b-p)\).
5. The equally valid generators \((f,g-f)=(f,u^b)\) have orders \(p,b\). Their strict-surface intersection contains no vertical fiber, and has the same horizontal section with coefficient \(b(a-p)\).

**Proof.** Monic division first by \(u^b\), then by \(v^a+t^N u^p\), proves the basis assertion. The quotient is free over \(k[t]\), so this is a flat lci family with constant transverse length. Its radical is \((u,v)\) because \(u\) is nilpotent, followed by \(v\). There is no residual one-dimensional component.

In the blowup chart \(v=uw\), the two displayed strict equations generate
\[
(t^N+u^{a-p}w^a,\ u^{b-p}).
\]
At the generic point of the exceptional fiber \(u=t=0\), \(w\) is a transcendental unit, and this quotient has length \(N(b-p)\): it is free of rank \(N\) over \(k(w)[u]/(u^{b-p})\), by monic division in \(t\). This proves the vertical multiplicity.

In the other chart \(u=vw\), the strict ideal is
\[
(t^Nw^p+v^{a-p},\ v^{b-p}w^b).
\]
Over \(k(t)\), its support is \(v=w=0\), the horizontal section corresponding to the normal direction \(u=0\). Additivity of intersection multiplicity in the factored second equation gives
\[
(b-p)\,\operatorname{length}K[[w]]/(t^Nw^p)
+b\,\operatorname{length}K[[v]]/(v^{a-p})
=(b-p)p+b(a-p)=ab-p^2.
\]
There are no other components: away from the exceptional locus the blowup is an isomorphism and \(V(f,g)\) has support \(C\); within that locus, the two initial forms have the sole generic normal direction \(u=0\), and their only common entire fiber is at \(t=0\).

For the replacement pair, the strict equation of \(u^b\) is the unit one in the first chart, excluding a vertical fiber. In the second chart the strict ideal is
\((t^Nw^p+v^{a-p},w^b)\), free of rank \(b(a-p)\) over \(k[t]\). This proves the final assertion. ∎

With fixed \(p,a,b\), generic normal orders and length remain fixed while the vertical coefficient is unbounded. Thus no upper bound for vertical multiplicity can depend only on \(m,p,q\); even flatness and a pure \(C\)-supported lci do not supply such a bound. This is a local statement: it does **not** construct an STCI of the fixed quartic.

The stronger conclusion with a fixed **generic algebra**, rather than just fixed numerical data, holds on the subsequence \(N=aL\). Over \(K=k(t)\), the substitution \(v=t^L v'\) identifies every such quotient with
\[
K[u,v']/(u^b,v'^a+u^p).
\]
The substitution preserves the maximal-ideal filtration, while the vertical coefficient \(aL(b-p)\) is unbounded. Thus even the isomorphism class of the generic filtered algebra does not bound vertical multiplicity of these chosen-pair strict transforms.

## 4. An actual projective pure-support example

Vertical fibers can occur even for homogeneous two-hypersurface presentations on projective three-space. For \(p\ge2,N\ge1\), take coordinates \([T:U:V:W]\) and
\[
F=T^NU^p+V^{N+p},\qquad
G=F+U^{N+p}.
\]
Both equations have degree \(D=N+p\), no common surface component, and
\[
\sqrt{(F,G)}=(U,V).
\]
Their intersection is a complete-intersection thickening of the line \(L=V(U,V)\), of generic length \(D^2\). Their generic orders along \(L\) are \(p,p\). Near \([0:0:0:1]\), this is Proposition 3 with \(a=b=N+p\), so the first strict-surface intersection contains the exceptional fiber there with coefficient \(N^2\). The leading forms have no other common entire fiber. Replacing \(G\) by \(G-F=U^{N+p}\) removes that vertical fiber. This homogeneous example concerns a line with a different normal bundle; its role is to refute a general vertical-implies-residual principle, not to settle any quartic case.



## Generic intersection clusters and fixed-data finiteness

In this subsection assume sole reduced support C=C0 for an actual pair of degrees a,b. Put p=ord_C(F), q=ord_C(G), alpha=a/p and beta=b/q. The first exceptional direction/base coordinates called u,v below are defined by E=P1_base x P1_direction and have v=m-pq; they do not refer to local normal parameters.

## 3. Generic transverse cluster theorem

Let \(\eta\) be the generic point of \(C\), and set
\[
R=\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3,\eta},\qquad K=\kappa(\eta)=k(C),\qquad \mathfrak m=I_CR.
\]
Then \(R\) is a two-dimensional regular local ring. Let \(f,g\in R\) be the images of the defining forms and \(J=(f,g)\).

### Proposition 3.1 — generic intersection multiplicity

Under the STCI support hypothesis,
\[
\ell_R(R/J)=\frac{ab}{4}.
\]

Indeed, the two projective surfaces have no common surface component, so their proper intersection cycle has degree \(ab\). Its reduced support is exactly the irreducible degree-four curve \(C\), hence the fundamental cycle is \(m[C]\), where \(m=\ell_R(R/J)\). Taking degrees gives \(ab=4m\).

### Proposition 3.2 — Noether decomposition

The orders at the origin are \(m_0(f)=p\) and \(m_0(g)=q\). Resolve the pair over K by successive blowups at its closed infinitely-near points. Noether's arithmetic formula, with each residue degree measured over that same base field K, gives
\[
\frac{ab}{4}
=
pq+\sum_{P\succ0}[\kappa(P):K]\,m_P(f)m_P(g).
\tag{N}
\]
Therefore
\[
v:=\frac{ab}{4}-pq
=
\sum_{P\succ0}[\kappa(P):K]\,m_P(f)m_P(g).
\tag{E}
\]

If one instead works after a finite extension K_prime/K, the normalized formula uses [kappa(P):K_prime] at points of that resolution; over an algebraic closure every residue weight is one.

Thus the second coordinate \(v\) of the audited first exceptional bidegree is exactly the total generic higher-contact budget.

### Corollary 3.3 — fixed-data finiteness

For fixed \((a,b,p,q)\), the number of common infinitely-near points after the origin is at most
\[
E=\frac{ab}{4}-pq,
\]
after passage to an algebraic closure and ignoring multiplicity. Hence the generic common cluster has bounded depth and bounded total number of vertices.

### Limitation 3.4 — normalized ratios do not suffice

Since
\[
E=pq\left(\frac{\alpha\beta}{4}-1\right),
\]
fixing only \((\alpha,\beta)\) does not bound cluster size independently of \(p,q\). At \((3,4)\),
\[
E=2pq.
\]

## 4. Correct higher-order objects

### Pair-sensitive object

Retain the common infinitely-near cluster with proximity relations, residue degrees, and the two multiplicity labels \(m_P(f),m_P(g)\). This distinguishes pairs with the same first tangent forms but different higher contact and directly refines the first exceptional cycle.

### Intrinsic ideal object

The normalized blowup of \(J=(f,g)\) and its Rees valuations give a canonical ideal-theoretic invariant. The integral-closure class determines \(e(J)\), hence the total transverse multiplicity \(ab/4\). It is, however, coarser than the labelled pair-cluster when the two distinguished generators must be tracked separately.

Recommendation: use Rees valuations as the intrinsic ideal-theoretic carrier, but use the labelled cluster (or a mixed refinement) for generator-sensitive inequalities.

## 5. Counterexample family

For N>=3, take
\[
f=y^2+x^N,\qquad g=y^2+2x^N,
\]
one has
\[
J=(f,g)=(y^2,x^N),\qquad \ell(R/J)=2N.
\]
The first tangent forms are always \(y^2\), but the integral-closure/Newton/Rees data changes with \(N\). After the first blowup the remaining Noether budget is
\[
2N-4,
\]
matching the transformed local calculation in the audited thick note.



For an explicit irreducible characteristic-zero version of the preceding
local control, take N=2n+1 with n≥1. Both y²+x^(2n+1) and
y²+2x^(2n+1) are irreducible plane germs: after Weierstrass preparation a
quadratic factorization would require an odd-order x-series to be a square.
Their common length is 4n+2. In the chart y=xz the strict equations are
z²+x^(2n-1), z²+2x^(2n-1), with exact common length 4n-2. Thus the
common tangent component's actual intersection coefficient is not bounded
by its root multiplicity in the two first symbols. This is a local model,
not a homogeneous supported-pair construction on C0.

## Relative horizontal blowup recursion

## 1. Blowup recursion along a horizontal common center

Let \(Y\) be a smooth threefold and let \(D,D'\) be effective Cartier divisors with proper one-dimensional intersection. Let \(Z\subset Y\) be a smooth curve contained in both divisors. Write
\[
r=\operatorname{ord}_Z(D),\qquad s=\operatorname{ord}_Z(D').
\]
Blow up \(Z\):
\[
\pi:Y_1=\operatorname{Bl}_Z(Y)\to Y
\]
with exceptional divisor \(E\). The strict transforms satisfy
\[
\widetilde D=\pi^*D-rE,\qquad
\widetilde D'=\pi^*D'-sE.
\]

### Proposition 1.1 — cycle recursion

As an equality of actual one-cycles on Y (and therefore in A_1(Y)),
\[
\pi_*\bigl([\widetilde D]\cdot[\widetilde D']\bigr)
=
[D]\cdot[D']-rs[Z].
\tag{R}
\]

**Proof.** Off Z, the blowup is an isomorphism, so both sides have
identical coefficients on every other curve. At the generic point of Z,
the ambient local ring is regular of dimension two. The plane-germ blowup
formula gives
\[
i_{\eta_Z}(f,g)=rs+
\sum_{P\in E_{\eta_Z}}[\kappa(P):\kappa(Z)]\,
 i_P(\widetilde f,\widetilde g).
\]
The weighted sum is precisely the coefficient of Z in the actual pushforward
of the strict-transform intersection. This proves the cycle identity
coefficient by coefficient. Finite intersection follows from properness.

As a separate class-level check, expand the two divisor classes. The
mixed terms push to zero because pi_*E=0, while pi_*(E^2)=-[Z]. Thus the
Chow computation gives the same identity, but is not its coefficientwise
justification.

This is the relative threefold analogue of the first step in Noether's plane-curve formula.

## 2. Recursion with a finite cover of the parent center

Suppose a later smooth horizontal center \(Z'\) maps finitely onto an earlier center \(Z\) with degree \(d\). If the current strict transforms have orders \(r',s'\) along \(Z'\), then after blowing up \(Z'\) its contribution pushed all the way down to \(Z\) is
\[
d\,r's'[Z].
\tag{R_d}
\]

Thus every common horizontal descendant consumes a positive integer amount of the coefficient of its ancestral horizontal component.

## Supported-cycle bidegrees and the universal numerical restriction

For an actual proper pair with sole support the fixed C0, put p=ord_C(F_a)
and q=ord_C(G_b). The strict divisors on B=Bl_C(P3) are aH-pE and bH-qE.
They have no common surface, hence form a pure one-dimensional CI W.
Since B is an isomorphism outside E and the original pair is supported on C,
the entire strict intersection is supported on E. Nonreduced equations are
allowed; purity prevents an isolated embedded point from replacing a curve.

In the balanced case E=P1_base x P1_direction, write its effective cycle as
(u,v), where H.W=4v and E.W=7v-u. The intersection relations
H^3=1,H^2E=0,HE^2=-4,E^3=-14 give
\[
H.W=ab-4pq,\qquad E.W=4(aq+bp)-14pq,
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{v=ab/4-pq,\qquad u=7ab/4+7pq-4(aq+bp).}
\]
Effectiveness on the product surface forces u,v≥0. Thus
\[
ab\ge4pq,\qquad7ab+28pq\ge16(aq+bp).
\]
Neither equation contains the smooth quadric, by the component-exclusion
proof. On it the restrictions divided by pC,qC are nonzero sections of
classes (a-p,a-3p),(b-q,b-3q), so a≥3p,b≥3q. The restrictions may contain
additional C factors; their displayed exponents are not assumed exact on Q.
For alpha=a/p,beta=b/q the second bound is
\[
7\alpha\beta/4+7\ge4(\alpha+\beta),\qquad\alpha,\beta\ge3.
\]
In particular alpha=3 forces beta≥4, and both slopes three are impossible.
At a=b=6,p=q=2 the actual computed bidegrees would be (u,v)=(-5,5),
contradicting effectivity. These are necessary conditions, not upper bounds
on possible orders or degrees. The same argument applies to any smooth
rational degree-four quadric curve with the explicitly required balanced
normal bundle; it is not inferred for a different splitting.

## The exact supported (3p,4q) boundary and distinct residual roots

Assume characteristic different from two, balanced normal bundle of C=C0,
an actual proper pair with sole support C, degrees a=3p,b=4q and normal
orders p,q. Its strict-transform intersection W on Bl_C(P3) has
exceptional direction-degree u=0: the exact balance 5delta-K=4u for a
supported pair has delta=0 and K=16(aq+bp)-7ab-28pq=0 at these degrees.
Purity of this proper CI and effectiveness on E=P1_base x P1_direction then
make its reduced components constant sections T_1,...,T_r, with
\[
[W]=\sum_j c_j[T_j],\qquad c_j\ge1,\quad
\sum_jc_j=m-pq=2pq,\quad r\le\min(p,q).
\]
The last inequality holds because distinct common section directions are
distinct roots of the degree-p and degree-q generic initial forms. None of
this identifies those directions canonically with residual ruling roots
on the quadric.

Nevertheless their **distinct counts** have the following proved relation.
The smooth quadric's strict transform Q_tilde is Q itself, because C is
Cartier on Q. Dividing the restrictions by pC and qC gives actual strict
equation divisors
\[
R_F\sim(2p,0),\qquad R_G\sim(3q,q).
\]
Neither equation contains Q by the unbalanced-quadric component exclusion. The residual restrictions are therefore nonzero. An extra C factor is impossible in R_F because its second bidegree is zero; R_G may contain an additional C factor, which does not affect the argument. They share no ruling component: otherwise that ruling would be a residual
curve of the original supported pair. Every distinct ruling ell in R_F
therefore has a nonzero restriction R_G|ell of degree q>0, and has an
intersection point P with R_G. Its image lies on C by sole support.
The exact strict-restriction identities put that same P in W cap Q_tilde.

Since W is supported on the constant sections, P lies on some T_j.
Moreover Q_tilde cap E is a section of bidegree (1,1), so each constant
T_j meets it at only one reduced support point. Distinct rulings ell of
the same ruling family are disjoint on Q, hence the selected P are
distinct. Thus two different ell cannot select the same T_j. We obtain
\[
\#\operatorname{Supp}(R_F)\le r\le\min(p,q).
\]
The multiplicities of R_F's ruling factors sum to 2p, so at least one
has multiplicity at least ceil(2p/min(p,q)). This is a repeated-root
restriction, not an exclusion or an existence statement.

If the first normal divisor of F has no vertical component, the distinct
section count improves to r≤min(p-1,q). Indeed D|E=(5p,p). If r=p,
factoring off one copy of each of those p distinct constant sections leaves
an effective divisor (5p,0), necessarily a nonzero union of vertical fibers,
contrary to the hypothesis. This additional no-vertical assumption is not
inferred from sole support.

This argument needs neither a canonical bijection nor a comparison of
root multiplicities with the c_j. It repairs the previously stated
missing-count-bridge caveat at this **exact** supported, balanced, u=0
boundary. Coupling multiplicities to Noether clusters or extending the
count statement to other slope/support hypotheses still needs proof.
In characteristic two the product-normal-bundle premise changes, so
this particular argument and its u coordinate are not transferred.

Fix one \(T_j\). Blow up every horizontal common center above \(T_j\) until the strict transforms no longer meet generically above it. Iterating Proposition 1.1 gives a sectionwise relative Noether identity
\[
c_j
=
\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal T_j}
d_\nu r_\nu s_\nu,
\tag{SN}
\]
provided the terminal intersection has no horizontal component above \(T_j\). Here:

- \(\mathcal T_j\) is the finite tree of horizontal common centers above \(T_j\);
- \(d_\nu\) is the degree of the center over its parent/ancestral section as measured after pushdown;
- \(r_\nu,s_\nu\) are the two vanishing orders along that center.

If a horizontal component survives indefinitely, the pair has a persistent divisorial valuation/common formal branch; converting that persistence into an actual projective common curve is a separate algebraization problem and is not asserted here.

### Corollary 3.1 — relative horizontal finiteness

For every terminating tree,
\[
\#\mathcal T_j\le c_j,
\qquad
\sum_j\#\mathcal T_j\le2pq.
\]
More generally, the total degree-weighted number of centers is bounded by \(2pq\).

Thus the \((3,4)\) boundary has a finite relative horizontal cluster for fixed \(p,q\), not merely a finite cluster on one generic transverse slice.

## 4. Why the proposed \(u\)-monotonicity is not justified

The first exceptional bidegree is \((u,v)=(0,2pq)\). It is tempting to regard \(u\) as a nonnegative energy that later horizontal twisting must increase. Proposition 1.1 shows that this is not automatic.

A blowup of a constant section removes \(rs[T_j]\) from the pushforward intersection cycle. New intersection curves may live on the new exceptional ruled surface. Their geometry can twist relative to \(T_j\) while their pushforward remains a multiple of the same class \([T_j]\). Therefore the original \(u=0\) records the support/class of the first-stage cycle but does not by itself constrain all later ruled-surface bidegrees.

So the candidate implication

\[
u=0\Longrightarrow\text{all later horizontal centers are constant/untwisted}
\]

is **not proved and should not be used**. A new invariant is needed if later twisting is to be controlled.

This is compatible with Jaffe's warning that iterated-blowup type sequences require extra control in the presence of common singularities.

## 5. Refined invariant suggested by the recursion

The natural object is now a **decorated relative cluster**
\[
\mathscr C(F,G/C)
\]
whose vertices are horizontal common centers on successive blowups and whose decorations include

\[
(d_\nu,r_\nu,s_\nu,N_{Z_\nu/Y_\nu},\text{proximity data}).
\]

The first three entries control the intersection budget. The normal bundle of the center in the current threefold controls the geometry of the next exceptional ruled surface:
\[
E_{\nu+1}=\mathbf P(N_{Z_\nu/Y_\nu}).
\]

This is the missing global datum that the generic Rees valuations over \(k(C)\) do not see.


## Characteristic reach and limitation

The full-lci-ideal Rees presentation, finite normalization/dimension proof,
Segre formula and exact vertical/nonsaturation families are algebraic in
arbitrary characteristic under the displayed finite-type hypotheses. They
already admit sole-support examples, and provide no characteristic-zero
positivity theorem. Generic-cluster residue extensions must retain their
degrees; inseparability is not silently replaced by a set of geometric points.
[Positive-characteristic supported pairs](../supplements/POSITIVE_CHARACTERISTIC_CONSTRUCTIONS.md#every-positive-characteristic-quartic-carrier)
therefore violate no retained conclusion.
