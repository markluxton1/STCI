# Principal e=1, second-defect-one boundary: source preservation

Scope: characteristic zero, fixed rational quartic, accepted canonical-triple ancestor reduction and P-037. Historical sources: `2026-10-08-session-mf6-principal-boundary-audit.md` and `2026-10-08-session-principal-e1-d1-independent-completeness-audit.md`.

The principal direction curve has equation H(p,r)=0 and a complete rank calculation on its declared open chart. The first-normal matrix has rank 11 in the 18-dimensional space of quartics through the curve; the quadratic contact operator has rank six on its seven-dimensional kernel. Thus the complete quartic space is one-dimensional on that open, including the S=0 point that cannot be discarded as a generic unit assumption.

The complementary frames were recomputed from all 35 ambient quartic monomials. The fibers at (8,1/2), (2/3,-1/6), two quadratic conjugates with an infinity defect, and three b0=0 cubic conjugates each have a one-dimensional quartic space. At (2,1/2), the space instead has dimension four and is the fixed cubic Cstar multiplied by all linear forms. The alternative killing section delta=1 at the two infinity-defect fibers is essential: those fibers are genuinely flat and cannot be deleted by an affine-chart calculation.

Consequently no two independent quartics can contain a principal genuine defect-one triple. At the exceptional four-dimensional fiber, division by Cstar reduces the common-ancestor equations to degree-one multipliers, excluded by P-037. This is a **common quartic ancestor exclusion**, not an exclusion of every higher-degree mate for every unique quartic carrier.

The historical boundary analysis separately excludes the three b0=0 carriers using the normal-quartic theorem, the rational and infinity-defect carriers using the twisted-cubic singular-locus theorem, and the reducible cubic-factor node in every mate degree. Its integral nonnormal carrier at (8,1/2) was left open at that checkpoint. That carrier status requires later-source reconciliation before being described as current.

## Subsequent resolution of the (8,1/2) carrier

The October 8 boundary note's open carrier was resolved later the same day in `research/notes/2026-10-08-session-mf6-rhalf-scroll-conductor.md`, with separate independent audit. An explicit linear coordinate change identifies the integral quartic by -64F=(xL-8yz)^2-x^3(x-8y). Its finite birational normalization is the smooth scroll P1 x P1 polarized by O(2,1). The full downstairs conductor is the ACM cubic ideal J=(x²,xy,xL-8yz), and its upstairs conductor is the Cartier divisor 2E+2T. The downstairs conductor is locally Gorenstein; the conductor cover is finite flat of degree two and admits the characteristic-zero involution.

If a mate of degree n existed, its normalization pullback would be a scalar multiple of f^n for a section f cutting out the unique smooth conic lift of C. Descent of f^n forces its involution ratio to be an n-th root of unity on each generic conductor component. On the reduced double-cover component the ratio is ±1; on the generically doubled component it is 1. Reducedness of the generic root-of-unity condition in characteristic zero and absence of embedded primes imply that f² descends along the entire conductor, including nilpotent jets. It glues to a section of O_X(2), which lifts to an ambient quadric. The unique quadric through C has restriction incompatible with support solely on the (1,3) curve. Therefore this **specific (8,1/2) carrier has no mate of any positive degree**. This does not extend to every principal carrier. The earlier open status is superseded.

The October 9 principal weighted-candidate work (`research/notes/2026-10-09-session-mf6-principal-weighted-candidate.md`) is a distinct reduction for remaining principal integral nonnormal carriers: on its contact open, a hypothetical mate forces a multiple root of the actual first-normal binary octic, hence vanishing of an explicit weighted-degree-210 Sylvester determinant on the weighted sextic direction curve. The stated intersection bound is at most 630 distinct principal candidate parameters. This is a **necessary candidate locus**, not a list of surviving mates or an exclusion theorem. Its geometric implication has a separate audit in `research/notes/2026-10-09-session-mf6-weighted-candidate-geometric-audit.md`.

## Independent checks and degree-factor scope

The weighted candidate's algebra and Bezout calculation have a separate October 9 audit (`research/notes/2026-10-09-session-mf6-weighted-candidate-algebra-independent-audit.md`). It reconstructs the first-normal octic from all eighteen ambient quartic forms, checks the binary-gradient Sylvester matrix and weighted degree 210, and uses a nonzero determinant at (p,r,b)=(4,2,1) modulo 101 to show the determinant is not identically zero on the integral sextic. Pullback to P2 gives Bezout degree 6 times 210; two-to-one coverage on the principal locus yields the bound 630. This is a bound on **necessary** candidates, with zero-generator fibers retained; it does not settle the candidate points.

The October 11 degree-factor audit (`research/notes/2026-10-11-session-degree-factor-and-mf6-scope-audit.md`) is logically distinct: if V(F,G)=C, then every irreducible factor f of F and g of G also satisfies V(f,g)=C. Its elementary argument uses coprimality and purity of the projective intersection. Combined with degree-one and quadric obstructions and the literature cubic exclusion, it eliminates irreducible carrier factors of degree at most three. A separately established all-integral-quartic carrier theorem would then force both defining degrees at least five, but the degree-factor lemma by itself does **not** establish the all-quartic theorem or eliminate abstract quartic ancestors. In particular the principal ancestor and carrier lanes must remain separate.

## October 11 all-carrier assembly: subsequent global closure

The accepted result `research/results/quartic_carrier_exclusion.md` (R-0001), supported by `research/notes/2026-10-11-session-all-quartic-dependency-assembly-independent-audit.md`, excludes **every integral quartic carrier** of the fixed C0 in characteristic zero, against mates of any positive degree. Its case split is normal carriers and nonnormal normalizations of sectional genus 0, 1, or 2. The genus-two lane separates bisections from nonendpoint and endpoint trisecants, and the audit checks generic regularity along C0 and full inverse-image support without assuming smooth normalization. Therefore the October 9 finite necessary candidate locus is a useful independent reduction but **not a remaining STCI carrier frontier**. The specific (8,1/2) conductor proof remains a distinct explicit example, not a required exception to R-0001.

The October 11 factor reduction shows that if V(F,G)=C0, every irreducible factor of F and every irreducible factor of G form a pair with the same intersection support. Together with the degree-one, quadric, cubic, and R-0001 quartic exclusions, this yields degrees of both defining forms at least five, even when F and G are reducible or nonreduced. The unrestricted degrees-at-least-five problem remains open. Crucially, **none of this resolves the separate local-cohomology common-quartic-ancestor incidence**, which does not itself assert that the two quartic multipliers form an STCI pair.
