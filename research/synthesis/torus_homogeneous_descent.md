# Torus degeneration, minimal resolutions, and homogeneous descent

**Status:** independently reviewed provisional research report, November 1, 2026.
Independent adversarial reconstruction is recorded in §11. No canonical
result is promoted. The characteristic-zero STCI problem for
\(C=C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3\) remains open.

Throughout the quartic discussion, k is algebraically closed of
characteristic zero, S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3], P=I_C, and T=G_m has weights
(0,1,3,4). An **eigenform** is ordinary homogeneous and T-semi-invariant.
Other examples explicitly change the curve, characteristic, or torus.
All new proofs below are intrinsic algebraic reductions or explicit
counterexamples; the exact calculations verify their displayed controls.
The colon sequence, radical-power lemma, equivariant resolution and
endpoint arguments are intrinsic proofs (policy category i). The explicit
degenerations use geometric support and excess modules together with
necessary standard-basis calculations (category ii). There is no
numerical-only result proposed for promotion (category iii).

## 1. Outcome, scope, and existing authority

The complete-intersection origin preserves the full graded Hilbert series
of the weight initial ideal, the projective Hilbert polynomial, and, under
sole support, the radical and fundamental cycle. It does **not** preserve
two-generation, ACMness, absence of embedded points, or the existence of
a proper pair in the original degrees. The supported line example in §4
proves these failures, while remaining a generic local complete intersection.

For the quartic, the proposed implication

\[
 \sqrt{(F,G)}=P\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sqrt{(f,g)}=P\text{ for eigenforms }f,g                         \tag{1}
\]

is still unresolved. Since Thoma excludes its conclusion, (1) for this
curve is logically equivalent to unrestricted non-STCI. Neither the clean
cancellation lemma nor a Betti table supplies the omitted exceptional-case
argument. A higher-rank analogue of (1) is false, by §5.3; that example
does not decide (1).

The distinction between the following assertions is essential:

| Assertion about a full invariant limit K with radical P | Status |
| --- | --- |
| K is generated by two homogeneous forms | False in general; impossible for the characteristic-zero quartic |
| K contains a proper pair in the original degrees (a,b) | False in general; at the full CI polynomial this would recover the CI itself |
| K contains some ordinary homogeneous pair with radical P | True conditional on the original STCI: sufficiently large powers of its equations belong to K |
| K contains some eigenform pair with radical P | Equivalent to the same assertion for P; excluded for the quartic by Thoma |

### 1.1 Exact literature boundary

Thoma, *On set theoretic complete intersections in P_k^3*, Manuscripta
Math. 70 (1991), 261–266, Theorem 2.5, applies to the smooth monomial
curve [s^d:s^(d-1)t:st^(d-1):t^d], d>3, over an algebraically closed
characteristic-zero field. It excludes an STCI presentation with even
one bihomogeneous carrier and an arbitrary homogeneous mate. The supplied
six-page primary PDF was read in full; the grading and the one-carrier
reduction are on printed p.262, and the theorem is on p.266.
[Article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02568376).

For the quartic, a monomial of ordinary degree e and weight j has Thoma
bidegree (4e-j,j). Thus his bihomogeneity is exactly ordinary homogeneity
plus our T-weight homogeneity, without a characteristic-dependent
identification of integer characters.

The requested 1989 paper is Thoma, *Monomial Space Curves in P_k^3 as
Binomial Set Theoretic Complete Intersections*, Proc. AMS 107, 55–61,
**not** the different 1989 paper *Ideal theoretic complete intersections*,
341–345. Lemma 2.2 starts with one already bihomogeneous equation and
chooses a bihomogeneous mate; Theorem 3.1 excludes one binomial carrier
for smooth monomial curves other than the twisted cubic. “Binomial
STCI” means one binomial and an arbitrary mate. These proofs in the
[author-uploaded article text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/303334542_Monomial_space_curves_in_as_binomial_set_theoretic_complete_intersections)
were checked. Publisher/institutional PDF access failed.
[Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1989-0976361-7).

The limitation also has an intrinsic explanation. Modulo an irreducible
eigenform carrier, an extreme component of g has a power divisible by g.
Additivity of weight spread in this graded domain forces g to have
spread zero. This requires the already invariant carrier.

## 2. The full degeneration and its four different outputs

Let J=(F,G), deg(F)=a, deg(G)=b, gcd(F,G)=1. For this subsection allow
an arbitrary proper containing intersection, with cycle

\[
 [V(J)]=m[C]+R,\qquad ab=4m+\deg R.                              \tag{2}
\]

Sole support is the additional assumption R=0. It is not imposed on the
other examples or on general residual-intersection arguments.

Use minimum-weight initials. If nu_w(F) is its smallest weight, set

\[
 F_t=t^{-\nu_w(F)}F(x_0,tx_1,t^3x_2,t^4x_3),
 \quad G_t=t^{-\nu_w(G)}G(x_0,tx_1,t^3x_2,t^4x_3).
\]

The full family ideal is the contraction from the punctured parameter line:

\[
 \mathcal J=(F_t,G_t):t^\infty
 =\bigl(J(t^wx)S[t,t^{-1}]\bigr)\cap S[t].                       \tag{3}
\]

Equivalently this is the ideal of all weight homogenizations of elements
of J, or the Rees construction for the induced weight filtration on S/J.
Its quotient is torsion-free over k[t], hence flat; each ordinary graded
piece is finite free. Specialization gives

\[
 J_0=\operatorname{in}_w(J),\qquad
 H_{S/J_0}(z)=\frac{(1-z^a)(1-z^b)}{(1-z)^4}.                    \tag{4}
\]

This is the standard construction in Eisenbud, Theorem 15.17; the
[primary textbook theorem](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/Eisenbud.pdf)
uses maximum-weight initials, so reverse the sign to compare conventions.
For the opposite limit replace w by -w, or by (4,3,1,0), since adding a
constant to all weights has no effect on homogeneous initials.

The four outputs are:

1. The individual limiting equations F_0,G_0 generate
   L=(in_w(F),in_w(G)). Always L⊂J_0; equality is not automatic.
2. The initial ideal J_0 includes initials of **every** relation and
   combination of F,G. It retains the full ordinary Hilbert function.
3. The Hilbert-scheme limit is Proj(S/J_0), represented by the saturated
   ideal \(\widehat J_0=J_0:S_+^\infty\). J_0 need not be saturated.
   Saturation removes only irrelevant torsion, preserves the projective
   scheme and polynomial, and need not preserve its small-degree Hilbert
   function or its affine graded Betti numbers.
4. The reduction and fundamental one-cycle forget nilpotent and embedded
   data. Even knowing both does not recover the Hilbert-scheme point.

Precisely, L=J_0 if and only if F_0,G_0 are coprime. In the coprime case
L is a CI of degrees (a,b), so L⊂J_0 and equality of Hilbert series force
equality of ideals. Conversely, if the two initial forms share a factor,
L has height one, whereas J_0 has height two. Saturation cannot remove
the common projective hypersurface. Thus agreement at the projective
ideal level also requires coprimeness. Other generating pairs of J can
of course give a different displayed pair of initial equations.

### 2.1 What sole support really preserves

Now assume sqrt(J)=P. A height-two CI is unmixed, so J is P-primary.
Put m=ab/4. The Artin ring S_P/J_P has length m, hence its maximal ideal
has mth power zero. Primary contraction then gives

\[
 P^m\subset J\subset P.
\]

Since P and P^m are invariant, taking initials preserves these inclusions:

\[
 P^m\subset J_0\subset\widehat J_0\subset P.                    \tag{5}
\]

Consequently both opposite full limits have radical P, and

\[
 [\operatorname{Proj}(S/J_0)]=m[C],\qquad
 P_{a,b}(n)=ab\,n-\frac{ab(a+b-4)}2.                            \tag{6}
\]

The generic local length is still m, because the support is C and the
degree is ab. Embedded points can occur on C. There are no new residual
curves downstairs in a sole-supported full limit. A common surface of
the two separately limiting equations is an excess component of their
intersection, not a component of the full flat limit.

For a pair with genuine residual support in (2), the argument using a
power of P is unavailable. Residual curves can move or be absorbed into
the marked multiplicity. The existing sextic examples exhibit this
distinction. A vertical exceptional curve on a blowup likewise cannot
be declared a residual curve in projective three-space without its
pushforward and multiplicity calculation.

## 3. What minimal resolutions retain and lose

For the **raw graded** initial ideal the ordinary Betti inequalities are

\[
 \beta_{i,j}(S/J)\le\beta_{i,j}(S/J_0).                          \tag{7}
\]

The Betti table of S/J is obtained from that of S/J_0 by consecutive
cancellations in equal ordinary degree. Take a minimal graded free
resolution of the flat Rees quotient over k[t]_(t)[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3].
Since t is regular on the quotient, specialization at zero remains exact
and gives the special-fiber minimal resolution. Passing instead to k(t)
permits cancellation of unit entries between neighboring summands in
equal ordinary degree, giving (7) and the generic Koszul resolution.
The associated graded of the naive two-equation Koszul complex need not
be exact. For a checked primary account of the numerical statements, see
[Murai–Peeva, Lemma 4.6.2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/7843F9C098A8425C10B52739B87CED88/S0010437X1100741Xa.pdf/hilbert-schemes-and-betti-numbers-over-clementslindstrom-rings.pdf),
which states both inequalities and cancellations for initial ideals.
The weight-filtration construction has the same filtered-complex argument.
Do not apply (7) without qualification to the different module
S/\(\widehat J_0\) after projective saturation.

The numerical constraint is the entire alternating numerator

\[
 \sum_{i,j}(-1)^i\beta_{i,j}(S/J_0)z^j
       =1-z^a-z^b+z^{a+b}.                                      \tag{8}
\]

It permits extra generators accompanied by cancelling syzygies, and even
increased projective dimension. The polynomial alone is weaker than (8).
The existing saturated ACM (6,6) limit has generator degrees 6,6,10 and
syzygy degrees 10,12, so its extra degree-ten terms cancel in (8).
The example below instead has projective dimension three.

For a T-stable ideal, J_0/S_+J_0 is a finite-dimensional representation
in each ordinary degree. Since a torus is diagonalizable, its character
basis lifts equivariantly, and graded Nakayama gives eigenform minimal
generators. Repeating for kernels gives an equivariant minimal resolution.
This is a choice of eigenbases for all required generators, not a reduction
in their number. In particular, an invariant height-two ideal generated by
two forms would be generated by two eigenforms.

For a hypothetical quartic STCI, both J_0 and \(\widehat J_0\) must
therefore need at least three generators. If the saturated limit is ACM,
its CM type must exceed one: a height-two arithmetically Gorenstein ideal
in S is a complete intersection. Neither conclusion excludes generic
local complete intersection structure. Betti numbers do not determine the
radical, embedded attachment, or the eigenform set-theoretic rank.

### 3.1 An intrinsic excess module for the extra generators

For the specified two-equation family put A_t=S[t]/(F_t,G_t) and
B_t=S[t]/\(\mathcal J\). Its vertical parameter-torsion module is

\[
 \mathcal D=H^0_{(t)}(A_t)
 =\mathcal J/(F_t,G_t).
\]

There is an exact sequence 0→\(\mathcal D\)→A_t→B_t→0.
Flatness of B_t makes specialization exact on the left, giving

\[
 \mathcal D/t\mathcal D\ \cong\ J_0/(F_0,G_0).
\]

Thus the additional initial equations are precisely the specialization
of the parameter-torsion removed from the raw intersection family.
The quotient J_0/(L+S_+J_0) records those minimal generators not supplied
by the two extreme forms. Successive divisions by t in a standard-basis
calculation construct this module. This supplies a geometric interpretation
as vertical excess data of the raw family. It does not label these classes
as residual curves of the full supported limit. The module depends on the
chosen two-equation presentation; the contracted flat family does not.

## 4. Worked supported example: four generators and one embedded point

Retain **exactly the requested weights** and rename

\[
 v=x_0,\quad x=x_1,\quad y=x_3,\quad z=x_2,
 \qquad w(v,x,y,z)=(0,1,4,3).
\]

Take J=(x^2,xy+z^2). It is a proper CI of degrees (2,2) with radical
(x,z), the invariant line L. The normalized minimum-weight family is
(x^2,xy+t z^2). Its full parameter saturation is

\[
 \mathcal K=(x^2,xy+t z^2,xz^2,z^4),\qquad
 K=\mathcal K|_{t=0}=(x^2,xy,xz^2,z^4).                          \tag{9}
\]

Indeed

\[
 yx^2-x(xy+t z^2)=-t xz^2,\qquad
 z^2(xy+t z^2)-y(xz^2)=t z^4.
\]

These relations require two divisions by the parameter. A monomial order
with x>z>y makes the four displayed polynomials a monic Gröbner basis
over k[t], as its S-polynomials reduce to zero. Its standard monomials
give a free k[t]-module. Thus it is t-saturated; its localization away
from t is the original pair, proving (9) is the full family. This proof
works in every characteristic.

The four special generators are minimal: none of these monomials divides
another. The ideal is saturated since the spare variable v acts freely.
The opposite full limit is (x^2,z^2), because the opposite extreme forms
are coprime. Thus opposite limits can behave differently.

At the generic point of L, y is a unit and K_P=(x,z^4), a local complete
intersection of length four. Globally there is the exact primary
decomposition

\[
 K=(x,z^4)\cap(x^2,y,z^2).                                      \tag{10}
\]

The second component is embedded at [v:x:y:z]=[1:0:0:0]. Equivalently,
(x,z^4)/K has basis x,xz over k[v], with the shifts shown by these
monomials; it has projective length two. This is an intrinsic excess
module supported at that point, not a residual curve.

As a graded k[v,y]-module,

\[
 S/K\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^3 k[v,y](-i)
        \ \oplus\ k[v](-1)\ \oplus\ k[v](-2).                  \tag{11}
\]

The pure hull (x,z^4) has polynomial 4n-2; the embedded correction adds
two, giving 4n, exactly the CI(2,2) polynomial. The cycle is 4[L].
Removing the embedded point would destroy the flat-limit polynomial.

The minimal resolution of S/K has free modules

\[
\begin{split}
 F_0&=S,\\
 F_1&=S(-2)^2\oplus S(-3)\oplus S(-4),\\
 F_2&=S(-3)\oplus S(-4)^2\oplus S(-5),\\
 F_3&=S(-5).
\end{split}                                                     \tag{12}
\]

It has Betti totals (1,4,4,1), compared with (1,2,1) for S/J.
Cancellation in degrees 3, 4, 5 recovers the Koszul table; (8) still holds.
Depth is one, so the special projective curve is not ACM, even though its
generic transverse algebra is Gorenstein.

Extra generators can be essential **for the radical**: omitting z^4
from (9) leaves radical (x), a plane. Yet (x^2,z^4)⊂K is an eigenform
pair with radical (x,z). This example does not refute weak descent.
It does refute preservation of the degree product: every form in K of
degree below four is divisible by x, and K has no linear form. The
minimum product for any homogeneous proper pair in K is therefore eight,
attained by (x^2,z^4), whereas the original product was four.

## 5. The radical question is a different invariant

### 5.1 A power lemma removes dependence on the chosen thickening

For a homogeneous ideal K with sqrt(K)=P, choose N with P^N⊂K⊂P.
Then

\[
 \begin{split}
 &\exists\text{ eigenforms }f,g\in K,\ \sqrt{(f,g)}=P\\
 &\hspace{12mm}\Longleftrightarrow
 \exists\text{ eigenforms }f,g\in P,\ \sqrt{(f,g)}=P.             \tag{13}
 \end{split}
\]

The forward implication is inclusion. For the reverse implication use
f^N,g^N; powers remain eigenforms and preserve the radical. The same
proof works for any finite number of equations, for ordinary homogeneous
equations, and for an invariant radical that is not prime. It imposes no
CI-origin hypothesis and supplies no degree bound.

Thus, under the hypothetical quartic STCI, every same-radical ideal K
contains the ordinary pair F^N,G^N, while **no** such K contains an
eigenform pair, by Thoma. It would be incorrect to say that every pair of
ordinary homogeneous elements in the limit must acquire residual curves.
The correct assertion concerns eigenforms: any proper eigenform pair in
P has an effective nonzero residual curve; a nonproper pair has a common
surface instead. An invariant nilpotent algebra need not encode its
set-theoretic presentation by eigenforms.

### 5.2 The equivariant set-theoretic rank is exactly three here

In the repository notation put

\[
 q=x_0x_3-x_1x_2,\quad A=x_1^3-x_0^2x_2,\quad
 D=x_2^3-x_1x_3^2.
\]

These are eigenforms of weights 4,3,9, respectively, and

\[
 \sqrt{(q,A,D)}=P.                                              \tag{14}
\]

For an intrinsic verification use the smooth quadric Q=V(q) with Segre
coordinates (x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)=(ac,ad,bc,bd). Its curve C is
ad^3-bc^3=0; the two cubic restrictions are
a^2(ad^3-bc^3) and -b^2(ad^3-bc^3). Since [a:b] is a projective
coordinate, they vanish simultaneously precisely on C. This works in
every characteristic. Combining (14), Thoma, and the power lemma, the
minimum number of eigenforms defining the radical of **any** homogeneous
ideal with radical P is exactly three in characteristic zero.

The original conjecture is therefore precisely whether ordinary
homogeneous set-theoretic rank can be two while this equivariant rank is
three for this integral curve. The full-limit Hilbert polynomial does
not by itself compare these two ranks.

### 5.3 Genuine failure for a higher-rank torus

This counterexample changes both the curve and the torus. In k[v,x,y,z]
let B be the union of the three independent concurrent coordinate lines:

\[
 I_B=(xy,xz,yz),\qquad \sqrt{(xy,z(x+y))}=I_B.                    \tag{15}
\]

To verify the radical, xy=0 forces either x=0 or y=0; the other equation
then forces the appropriate second coordinate to vanish. The pair is a
homogeneous CI of degrees (2,2).

Let T'=(G_m)^2 act by (v,x,y,z)↦(v,t x,u y,z). On the invariant plane
v=0, each ordinary homogeneous T'-eigenform restricts to a monomial or
zero: its degree and two characters fix the exponents of x,y,z.
If either restriction is zero, their common zero set on that plane
contains a curve. Otherwise two monomials cannot cut out exactly the
three coordinate points: their radical is generated by at most two
squarefree monomials, whereas the ideal (xy,xz,yz) in k[x,y,z] needs
three. Thus no eigenform pair cuts out B in any degrees.

There is even a CI-origin invariant full limit. For the one-parameter
subgroup λ(t)=(t,t^2)⊂T', the pair in (15) gives

\[
 (xy,xz+t yz):t^\infty\big|_{t=0}
        =K'=(xy,xz,y^2z).                                      \tag{16}
\]

The clean cancellation theorem below proves (16): H=x, u=y, v=z,
and D_0=-y^2z are coprime to H. K' is saturated and ACM, has generator
degrees 2,2,3 and syzygy degrees 3,4, and retains the CI(2,2) series.
Its radical is I_B, so the power lemma forbids an eigenform pair inside
K' for the full T' as well. The cycle is [L_x]+[L_y]+2[L_z], where L_x
means the line with x free, and similarly for the others.

This is a counterexample to general descent for tori, even with a
complete-intersection origin and an ACM invariant boundary. Integrality
and the rank of the torus matter. For λ alone the pair
xy, z(x^2+yz) *is* an eigenform STCI of B, of degrees (2,3) and
λ-weights (3,2). Thus (15)–(16) are explicitly **not** a counterexample
to one-parameter descent. No such counterexample for the prescribed
quartic action has been obtained in this investigation.

## 6. Residual-cycle descent: exact clean theorem and its boundary

To avoid using J both for the original ideal and the residual ideal,
write U=(u,v), F_0=Hu, G_0=Hv, with H the full gcd. Set h=deg H>0.
Then gcd(u,v)=1 and their degrees are a-h,b-h. In the normalized
families form

\[
 vF_t-uG_t=t^rD_0+\text{higher parameter powers},\qquad r>0.       \tag{17}
\]

For the first nonzero coefficient, always

\[
 D_0\in(u,v),\qquad \deg D_0=a+b-h.                             \tag{18}
\]

Indeed D_0=v(F_t)_r-u(G_t)_r. It is also an eigenform: the parameter
normalization gives both products the same character at each parameter
exponent. The gcd H and its quotients u,v are eigenforms up to scalar,
since a connected torus preserves the finite set of factors of an
eigenform. Dividing (17) by t^r puts D_0 in the full initial ideal.

### 6.1 Local length identity

Let R be a two-dimensional regular local ring, U=(u,v) an ideal of
finite colength or the unit ideal, and suppose (H,D_0) is a parameter
ideal. If D_0∈U, multiplication by H gives

\[
 0\longrightarrow R/U\xrightarrow{\ H\ }
 R/(HU,D_0)\longrightarrow R/(H,D_0)\longrightarrow0.            \tag{19}
\]

For injectivity, (H,D_0) regular implies that H is a nonzerodivisor
modulo D_0. If Hr=Ha+cD_0 with a∈U, then c is divisible by H, and
r∈U+(D_0)=U. The last quotient is immediate. Hence

\[
 \ell R/(HU,D_0)=\ell R/U+\ell R/(H,D_0).                        \tag{20}
\]

Without D_0∈U the correct first term is R/(U+(D_0)). For example in
k[[r,s]], take H=r, U=(r,s^2), D_0=s. The left length is two,
but the proposed uncorrected right side is 2+1=3. This is a finite-length
counterexample to dropping the membership hypothesis. Such a D_0 cannot
be the first cancellation (17), because (18) would fail.

At the generic point of C, R=O_(P3,C) has dimension two as required.
This computes that component's cycle coefficient only if the localization
of the **full** flat limit is (HU,D_0). An arbitrary finite-length
three-generator approximation is insufficient.

### 6.2 The clean global theorem proves full equality

**Proposition.** In the homogeneous setup (17)–(18), assume
gcd(H,D_0)=1. Then

\[
 J_0=(Hu,Hv,D_0),\qquad
 [Z_0]=[V(u,v)]+[V(H,D_0)],                                    \tag{21}
\]

with the first term empty if U=S. The ideal in (21) is saturated and
ACM, and has no embedded components.

**Proof.** Put K_3=(HU,D_0). Coprimeness gives
K_3:H=U and K_3+(H)=(H,D_0), by the proof of (19), now in S. Thus

\[
 0\longrightarrow S/U(-h)\xrightarrow{\ H\ }
 S/K_3\longrightarrow S/(H,D_0)\longrightarrow0.                \tag{22}
\]

When U is proper its two coprime generators form a regular sequence.
Both nonzero outer modules are CM of dimension two, so the depth lemma
proves the same for S/K_3. If U=S only the last CI remains. Formula (18)
then gives the Hilbert numerator

\[
 z^h(1-z^{a-h})(1-z^{b-h})+(1-z^h)(1-z^{a+b-h})
       =1-z^a-z^b+z^{a+b}.                                    \tag{23}
\]

As K_3⊂J_0, equality with (4) forces K_3=J_0 in every degree.
There are no missing higher generators in this clean case. Taking generic
lengths in (22) proves the cycle formula. This proves a cycle equality,
not a scheme-theoretic disjoint union. ∎

Thus Claude's proposed formula is rigorous under these hypotheses, with
a proof that it computes the saturated full limit. Proving only (20) at
C would not establish the global cycle identity. Coprimeness **globally**
is stronger than a local test at C; the latter does not exclude components
elsewhere. The global Hilbert-series argument is what closes that gap here.

### 6.3 Precisely when ab decreases

If sqrt(J_0)=I_C for an **integral** projective curve C and
0<h<min(a,b), both terms in (21) are nonempty proper complete
intersections with support C. Thus each supplies an eigenform STCI pair,
and their products satisfy

\[
 (a-h)(b-h)+h(a+b-h)=ab,\qquad
 0<(a-h)(b-h),\ h(a+b-h)<ab.                                   \tag{24}
\]

Each product is strictly below ab; no assertion about a+b is used.
Both cycles have positive degree and support contained in the integral
curve C, hence their reduced support is all of C. For reducible support
they can divide its components, as (16) demonstrates.

If h=min(a,b), one of u,v is a unit. Then only V(H,D_0) remains and
its degree product is **ab**, not a smaller number. It still supplies an
eigenform pair, but does not advance an induction on ab.

For our quartic, Thoma rules out (21) under sole support, including the
unit case. Therefore in **both** directions of any hypothetical STCI,
the first nonzero cancellation necessarily obeys

\[
 \gcd(H,D_0)\ne1.                                              \tag{25}
\]

This is a derived structural refinement of the common-factor constraint,
not a nonexistence theorem. With the
[Hellus–Hübl extreme-component theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.7661),
the initial gcd H is already nonunit, belongs to P, and the two extreme
equations have only height-one minimal primes, for each direction. Their
Theorem 1.2 was checked in the primary article. It concerns the separate
equations and their unsaturated parameter family; it does not identify
that family with the full t-saturated Hilbert limit.

### 6.4 Exceptional cases and failed induction steps

| Situation | What follows, and what does not |
| --- | --- |
| A selected coefficient in (17) is zero | Move to the first nonzero coefficient; it defines no residual hypersurface yet |
| The entire polynomial vF_t-uG_t is zero | Since u,v are coprime, F_t=uL_t and G_t=vL_t; deg L_t=h>0. A nonzero-parameter fiber would have a common factor. This is impossible for the original proper pair |
| H and the actual D_0 share a factor | K_3 has a common surface factor; local finite lengths along it are unavailable. Further parameter divisions can be essential, as in §4 |
| u,v share a factor | H was not the full gcd. Replace it before using the formula; in a local model with nonprimary U the displayed finite-length sum is not defined |
| More generators arise after cancellation | Compute (3), rather than assuming (21). Their new ordinary degrees need not yield any decreasing product |
| Embedded components occur in the full limit | They contribute to the polynomial, not to the one-cycle; the pure hull may have a different polynomial, as in (10)–(11) |
| A local three-generator identity holds only at C | It computes at most that local coefficient. Properness, the full ideal, and other component lengths still need proof |
| One residual factor is a unit | The clean eigenpair has product ab. Strict descent fails even though the cycle formula is valid |

There is no proved repair in all these exceptional cases that constructs
a global supported pair of strictly smaller product. In §4 the minimum
product in the limit actually increases. That example preserves weak
descent but blocks this numerical induction strategy.

Fulton's residual-intersection theorem is compatible with this distinction.
Theorem 9.2, *Intersection Theory*, second edition, pp.161–163, assumes
a regular embedding and a Cartier divisor in the indicated ambient variety;
the residual term is a Chern/Segre **class**, becoming the fundamental
cycle under additional expected-codimension regularity. Example 9.2.8
explicitly warns against automatically equating a general colon residual
scheme with that class. Proposition 10.2 conserves degree under a proper
map and specialization at regular closed base points. These statements
were checked in the
[digitized primary book, §§9.2 and 10.1–10.2](https://djvu.online/file/8KbyCnh9POwWS).
They do not turn an improper intersection of F_0,G_0 into (21) without
the hypotheses proved above. The exact sequence supplies the needed
stronger algebraic statement in the clean case.

## 7. Equivariant normal data and the global presentation obstruction

Let K=\(\widehat J_0\), Z=Proj(S/K), I=I_C, and E=I/I^2.
The intrinsic normal algebra is

\[
 \operatorname{gr}_{I\mathcal O_Z}\mathcal O_Z
 =\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal O_C}E/\operatorname{in}_I(\mathcal I_Z).
                                                                    \tag{26}
\]

It is T-equivariant because both I and K are invariant. The full weight
initial ideal in S and this I-adic initial ideal live in different rings
and use different filtrations. They cannot be identified, nor can their
iterations be interchanged without a compatible double-Rees argument.
Even though the total family is flat, the I-adic associated graded need
not commute with specialization. Comparing (26) with the original normal
algebra requires strictness and flatness of the filtered layers, or
explicit correction terms; normal orders and wedge maps are not thereby
constant.
Taking the associated graded of the two displayed equations alone is
weaker still.

For an actual proper pair, coprime generic leading normal forms of orders
r,s give transverse length rs; common leading roots require subsequent
relations. The normal filtration retains these relations, multiplication,
and special-point torsion. Its equivariant structure puts characters on
the maps; it does not say the full ideal is generated by the selected
first symbols or that a generic lift is a global equation.

For comparison with accepted work, [R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md)
applies to the **actual two-generator ideal** J and its I-adic filtration.
Writing A=Sym(E), H=in_I(J), B=in_I(IJ), it gives

\[
 0\longrightarrow\Delta_n
 \longrightarrow H_n/(A_+H)_n
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}^n(J/IJ)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad\Delta_n=B_n/(A_+H)_n.                                  \tag{27}
\]

Extra generators of a normal initial ideal can disappear in the filtered
actual equation module. Equation (27) is not a count of extra generators
in a different weight degeneration. Its generic excess formula with
“minus two” cannot be applied unchanged to a boundary K that no longer
has two generators.

At generic local CI points, the full equation module
M=(\(\mathcal I_Z/I\mathcal I_Z\))/torsion has rank two. A selected
pair defines a map from the two prescribed twisting line bundles to M.
Its wedge detects generic independence; its determinant divisor detects
the cokernel on the torsion-free quotient. Torsion in the full equation
module and scheme-theoretic generation remain separate conditions. For
a genuine residual intersection, the existing
[full-primary attachment account](residual_intersections_and_jets.md)
retains both terms. A determinant alone does not supply sole support or
two global homogeneous generators.

There is a useful intrinsic endpoint restriction. T acts transitively
and freely on C minus {p_0,p_infinity}. A coherent T-equivariant torsion
sheaf on C has invariant finite support, hence is supported at these two
points. Consequently the Fitting divisor of a normal-layer saturation
quotient H_n^sat/H_n, or any other generically vanishing equivariant
coherent defect on C, is α_n p_0+β_n p_infinity. This locates such
defects; it neither proves that they are nonzero nor assigns them a
residual curve or identifies them with Δ_n. Each layer and its full
multiplication still need to be retained.

For example, writing E_n=I^nO_Z/I^(n+1)O_Z with generic ranks r_n,
the finite nilpotent filtration gives

\[
 P_Z(d)=4d\sum_n r_n+\sum_n\chi(E_n),\qquad
 \sum_n r_n=m.                                                 \tag{28}
\]

On P1, χ(E_n)=r_n+deg(E_n), where the degree of a coherent sheaf
includes torsion length. Thus endpoint torsion is part of the same
polynomial budget, not an extra genus penalty to count twice. The line
example gives a visible instance of this bookkeeping. Formula (28)
constrains totals, while neither forcing each E_n to be locally free
nor reconstructing the ideal from its ranks and degrees.

Finally [R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md)
gives E=O(-7)^2 in characteristic zero and, more generally, when two is
invertible; in characteristic two it is O(-8)⊕O(-6). This is an ordinary
bundle splitting; endpoint character linearizations carry additional
data. One must not transplant a characteristic-zero normal-order
argument to characteristic two just because the torus weights are the
same integer list. Conversely this splitting alone does not explain all
Frobenius STCI constructions or supply a descent theorem.

## 8. Positive controls and characteristic checks

### 8.1 Twisted cubic and invariant plane curves

For C_3=[s^3:s^2t:st^2:t^3], use weights (0,1,2,3). The pair

\[
 f=x_0x_2-x_1^2,\qquad
 g=x_2^3-2x_1x_2x_3+x_0x_3^2                                  \tag{29}
\]

has degrees (2,3), weights (2,6), and radical I_(C_3). On x_0=1,
f gives x_2=x_1^2 and g becomes (x_3-x_1^3)^2. On x_0=0, f forces
x_1=0 and then g forces x_2=0, leaving the missing endpoint. This also
works in characteristic two. It is an explicit positive control for
eigenform descent and does not use the quartic's weights on a different
curve. The CI cycle is 2[C_3].

A T-invariant plane curve in an invariant plane is another positive
control: its plane equation is an eigenform, and its principal ideal
inside the plane is stable, so its defining polynomial can be chosen an
eigenform too. These two equations already give a scheme-theoretic CI
for the plane hypersurface. This assertion includes the hypothesis that
the curve is a hypersurface in that plane.

### 8.2 The repository's fixed-quartic Frobenius equations are eigenforms

Use the actual equations in
[positive-characteristic constructions](../supplements/POSITIVE_CHARACTERISTIC_CONSTRUCTIONS.md),
not just their abstract existence claim.

| Recorded family | Ordinary degrees | T-weights and verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| x_1^4-x_0^3x_3, x_2^N-x_0^b x_1^a x_3^(2a+3b), N=p^e=3a+4b | (4,N) | 4 and 3N; the second mixed monomial has weight a+4(2a+3b)=3N |
| A=x_1^3-x_0^2x_2, uniform G_e, e=3 for p=3 and e=3p otherwise | (3,4e/3) | 3 and 4e; every surviving term satisfies b_j+3c_j+4j=4e |
| x_0x_3^3-x_2^4, x_0^a x_2^b x_3^c-x_1^p, 3b+4c=p≥7 | (4,p) | 12 and p |

For the uniform construction, 3c_j+b_j=4(e-j), proving the weight
identity term by term. The omitted j=1 term has coefficient zero in
characteristic p; no negative-exponent monomial is being included.
For the first row, ordinary degree of the mixed monomial is

They do not claim that every equation from a different projected curve's
family is homogeneous for this fixed action.

Since these ideals are already invariant, their full torus limits equal
themselves. They are genuine positive controls, and prohibit a
characteristic-free assertion that invariant sole-supported CIs cannot
occur. Their support proof uses Frobenius, not a characteristic-zero
descent of a mixed-weight pair. The repository's stronger trace bounds
and minima remain in their existing

this report does not reprove or promote them.

## 10. Short next mathematical steps

1. **Study the noncoprime cancellation locus, rather than repeat the clean
   calculation.** For a fixed allowed degree pair and both extreme
   directions, impose (25), compute the subsequent colon/saturation
   relations with their ordinary degrees and characters, and retain the
   full ideal. A useful outcome is a covered subfamily with a proved
   termination/decrease, or an explicit obstruction to decrease. No
   automatic induction on ab has been established.
2. **Use full invariant-limit strata and actual source incidence.** At the
   exact CI polynomial, study the moving ideal's degree-a and degree-b
   spaces and the absence of a common fixed surface, as in the existing
   [generator-incidence criterion](residual_cycles_and_chow_incidence.md#3-exact-generator-incidence-at-the-complete-intersection-polynomial).
   A boundary ideal need not have two generators. Refine by its normal
   algebra and the two endpoint torsion modules only when these refinements
   retain all supported limits in the declared bounded locus.
3. **Seek a rank-one counterexample outside the quartic.** The rank-two
   arrangement is a completed control; its exhibited one-parameter pair
   shows exactly why it does not suffice. Singular integral monomial
   curves with known ordinary STCI presentations are a sharper test than
   reusing smooth degree>3 examples whose STCI status is open. Compare
   ordinary homogeneous and (degree,weight)-homogeneous arithmetical ranks
   using full theorems rather than binomial-only lower bounds.
4. **Compare the endpoint equation module with lifting, with an actual
   map.** In a specified generic-lci invariant family, compute the full
   module I_Z/I I_Z and its torsion, then the homogeneous source maps
   needed to deform it to a global pair. A wedge, a tangent vector, or a
   constant transverse algebra is not yet such a deformation.

The multigraded language already exists in the literature:
[Katsabekis–Thoma, *Specializations of multigradings and the arithmetical rank of lattice ideals*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.3830),
§1 and Proposition 3.3, distinguishes ordinary arithmetical rank from
the rank constrained by a specified grading. Its equality theorem permits
**coarsening** the grading; it does not preserve the given (degree,weight)
grading. The definitions, Proposition 3.3 with proof, and the example's
scope were checked in the primary text. This
is a relevant framework and warning, not a checked one-parameter
counterexample or a theorem proving (1). No homogeneous-descent theorem
for the present quartic was located in this bounded primary-source review.
