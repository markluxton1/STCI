# Full tropical thickenings and endpoint duality

The fixed curve is \(C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3\), with
marked endpoints \(p_0,p_\infty\). The constructions in the first two
sections work over any algebraically closed field. Strict movement holds
in characteristic zero and in characteristic \(p>0\) when \(p\nmid ab\).
All deductions here retain their independently reviewed
**provisional** status; they do not settle the STCI problem.

## A reduced tropical model and the exact normal map

On the projective dense torus use the unimodular coordinates

\[
t=x_1/x_0,\quad u=x_0^2x_2/x_1^3,\quad v=x_0^3x_3/x_1^4.
\]

Then \(C^\circ=(u=v=1)\) is a subtorus. Its tropical line has the two
rays generated by \(\pm(1,3,4)\). The associated toric ambient is
\(Y=\mathbf P^1_t\times\mathbf G_m^2_{u,v}\), with
\(\bar C=\mathbf P^1\times\{(1,1)\}=V(u-1,v-1)\).
It is an actual scheme CI of global functions. It is a tropical
compactification in [Tevelev's Definitions 1.1, 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0412329):
its closure is proper and the multiplication map is smooth and surjective,
since changing the source coordinate \(z\) to \(a\cdot z\) makes
\((a,b,c;z)\mapsto(a\cdot z,b,c)\) a projection with fiber \(\mathbf G_m\).
The ambient \(Y\) itself is not proper.

There is a birational, nonproper toric morphism

\[
\phi:Y\to\mathbf P^3,\quad
[1:t:ut^3:vt^4]=[\tau^4/v:\tau^3/v:(u/v)\tau:1],\quad\tau=t^{-1}.
\tag{1}
\]

The boundary tori contract to the marked points. Set \(U=u-1,V=v-1\).
At zero the normal coordinates \(A=x_2-x_1^3,B=x_3-x_1^4\) pull back to
\(t^3U,t^4V\). At infinity, with \(a=x_0/x_3,b=x_1/x_3,c=x_2/x_3\),

\[
\binom{a-c^4}{b-c^3}=
\begin{pmatrix}-4\tau^4&3\tau^4\\-3\tau^3&2\tau^3\end{pmatrix}
\binom UV\pmod{(U,V)^2}. \tag{2}
\]

The determinant is \(\tau^7\); its constant row matrix has determinant
one over \(\mathbf Z\). Thus the normal map
\(\mathcal O_C^2\to N_{C/\mathbf P^3}\) has invariant factors \((3,4)\)
at each endpoint in every characteristic. Its cokernel length is fourteen.
This computes the differential of (1), not a higher-neighborhood or residual
support invariant. It does not determine the splitting of the normal bundle;
[R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md) distinguishes
\(\mathcal O(7)^2\) from \(\mathcal O(6)\oplus\mathcal O(8)\) in characteristic two.

Clearing the denominators of the two reduced equations gives
\(x_0^2x_2-x_1^3\) and \(x_0^3x_3-x_1^4\). At \(x_0=0\) these leave the
line \(x_0=x_1=0\), an actual residual curve. This is a failure of this
particular descent; exceptional boundary tori, which map to points, are
not themselves residual curves. General schön compactification and regular
embedding results ([Tevelev](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0412329),
[Luxton–Qu, Theorems 1.4–1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.2009)) do not assert
preservation of two global equations for nonreduced thickenings under (1).

## The entire finite modification

Suppose \(Z=V(F_a,G_b)\) is a proper homogeneous CI with sole reduced
support \(C\), and put \(m=ab/4\). Define \(W\subset Y\) as the
**scheme-theoretic closure of the entire torus restriction** of \(Z\).
Thus its ideal is contracted from the punctured chart at each endpoint,
retaining the saturation of every relation. Dividing two individual
pullback equations does not define this object in general.

Let \(\pi:W\to\mathbf P^1\), \(\mathcal B=\pi_*\mathcal O_W\), and
\(\rho:W\to Z\) be induced by (1). Then:

\[
\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}\otimes k[U,V]/(U,V)^m\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal B,
\qquad
0\to\mathcal O_Z\to\rho_*\mathcal O_W\to Q_0\oplus Q_\infty\to0.
\tag{3}
\]

Here \(\mathcal B\) is a globally generated rank-\(m\) vector bundle,
\(\rho\) is finite and is an isomorphism off the endpoints, and \(Q_i\)
has finite length \(\delta_i\). To prove these facts, the generic length-\(m\)
Artin algebra has nilradical power \(m\) zero. Its vanishing extends over
the torus restriction by CM purity. Closure therefore lies in
\(\mathbf P^1\times\operatorname{Spec}k[U,V]/(U,V)^m\), hence is finite.
The interior is torsion free over the base; contraction removes parameter
torsion at the endpoints. Thus it is flat and has rank \(m\), proving
the first surjection. It is proper over \(k\). Its fibers over \(Z\) have
one underlying point, so \(\rho\) is finite. The kernel in (3) is supported
at the endpoints in the pure sheaf \(\mathcal O_Z\), hence is zero.

Write \(e=\deg\mathcal B\ge0\). Riemann–Roch and CI adjunction give

\[
\chi(\mathcal O_W)=m+e,\quad
\chi(\mathcal O_Z)=-ab(a+b-4)/2,\quad
\delta_0+\delta_\infty=e+m(2a+2b-7). \tag{4}
\]

This is a modification of a nonreduced curve, not a normalization.
The quotient in (3) measures additional endpoint functions, not residual
components. The quotient bundle in (3) gives a map to the finite-flat
length-\(m\) Hilbert scheme of the fixed punctual ambient
\(\operatorname{Spec}k[U,V]/(U,V)^m\); \(e\) is its Plücker degree.
Movement means variation of this **embedded quotient**, not necessarily
variation of the abstract Artin algebra.

## Positive movement and the global presentation barrier

In characteristic zero, or in characteristic \(p>0\) with \(p\nmid ab\),

\[
e>0,\qquad W\text{ is not the intersection of any two global effective
Cartier divisors of }Y. \tag{5}
\]

If \(e=0\), global generation forces \(\mathcal B\simeq\mathcal O^m\).
Its multiplication, unit, and coordinate sections \(U,V\) are then constant,
so (3) has constant kernel and \(W\) is a constant embedded family.
Scaling \(t\) while fixing \(u,v\) preserves \(W\), and equivariance of (1)
preserves its scheme image \(Z\). The saturated graded ideal \((F,G)\)
is therefore torus invariant. Diagonalizing its two-dimensional graded
minimal-generator representation, then lifting weight components, gives
two weight-homogeneous generators by graded Nakayama. This diagonalization
works in every characteristic, and the generators retain the ordinary
degrees \(\{a,b\}\). The [trace theorem](../supplements/EQUIVARIANT_DEGREE_BOUNDS.md#trace-proof-for-two-weight-homogeneous-equations)
excludes such a supported pair in characteristic zero and forces \(p\mid ab\)
in positive characteristic. This proves strict movement in the asserted
range. In characteristic zero it also follows from
[Hellus–Hübl, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.7661).

For the second assertion a useful general lemma works in every
characteristic. Suppose a finite flat \(W\subset\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf G_m^2\)
is supported on the constant section and is cut out by two global effective
Cartier divisors. The nilradical of such a finite flat family is uniformly nilpotent, so it too is a quotient of a fixed constant normal neighborhood; consequently its algebra is globally generated. The Picard group is \(\mathbf Z\): the two affine
factorial charts have overlap units \(ct^ru^iv^j\), whose \(u,v\) parts
are removed by chart units. Write the divisor classes as
\(\pi^*\mathcal O(d_1),\pi^*\mathcal O(d_2)\). Each \(d_i\ge0\), since a
nonzero global section has Laurent coefficients in
\(H^0(\mathbf P^1,\mathcal O(d_i))\). Their codimension-two zero scheme is
locally a regular sequence. Adjunction and finite-flat duality give

\[
\omega_W=\pi^*\mathcal O(d_1+d_2-2),\quad
\mathcal B^\vee\otimes\mathcal O(-2)
 =\mathcal B\otimes\mathcal O(d_1+d_2-2).
\]

Taking degrees yields \(2e=-m(d_1+d_2)\). Global generation gives \(e\ge0\),
so \(d_1=d_2=e=0\), and the preceding constant-family proof applies.
This quantifies over all global two-divisor presentations of \(W\).
It does not rule out local lci equations or sections of nonsplit bundles,
and is compatible with two homogeneous equations for its image downstairs.

The characteristic boundary is concrete. In characteristic seven,
\(F=x_1^4-x_0^3x_3\), \(G=x_2^7-x_0x_1x_3^5\) have sole support \(C\):
on \(x_0=1\), elimination gives \((x_2-x_1^3)^7\); on \(x_0=0\) only
\(p_\infty\) remains. Their full tropical closure is
\((v-1,u^7-1)=(V,U^7)\), constant of length seven. In characteristic zero
it has seven distinct quartic sections and fails sole support.

This multiplicity effect is distinct from inseparability of the reduced
projection \([s:t]\mapsto[s^4:t^4]\) in characteristic two. Locally over
a lifted support parameter, the full algebra's trace is \(h\mapsto m\bar h\),
by the nilradical filtration over the fraction field and freeness over the
DVR. The relative Jacobian vanishes when the characteristic divides \(m\).
The characteristic-seven example therefore has zero Jacobian trace while
its reduced fourfold cover is tame. Invertible \(m\) instead gives the
socle-generator mechanism of the [Jacobian chapter](contact_jacobians_and_hamiltonian_actions.md).

### Ambient Hilbert-scheme nef classes add no inequality here

For this full finite-flat family, view the same classifying map as
\(f:\mathbf P^1\to\operatorname{Hilb}^m(\mathbf P^2)\), supported at one
fixed point of the affine chart. Over characteristic zero,
[Arcara–Bertram–Coskun–Huizenga, Theorem 2.4, Proposition 3.1 and
Corollary 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.0316) identify the divisor classes
\(H,\Delta/2\), the tautological determinant class
\(D_k=kH-\Delta/2\), and the ambient nef rays \(H,D_{m-1}\).
The pullback of \(H\) is zero because the support is fixed. On this affine
chart \(\mathcal O(k)\) is trivial, so its pulled-back tautological
bundle is exactly \(\mathcal B\). Thus

\[
\deg f^*D_k=+e,\qquad\deg f^*(\Delta/2)=-e.
\]

Every ambient nef inequality is therefore only \(\beta e\ge0\), already
known. The nonreduced-boundary section vanishes identically on this
family; its negative pullback degree is not an effective-divisor
contradiction. For small \(k\), use the determinant line-bundle class,
not an assumed everywhere-defined Grassmannian map. The characteristic-zero
numerical assertion transfers from the complex theorem by finite-data
descent and field extension. No positive-characteristic extension of that
cone theorem, and no statement about all intrinsic punctual-Hilbert or
marked equation-incidence inequalities, is being asserted. The sign and
the use of the entire family, including its nilpotents, are essential.

## Actual endpoint lattices and the full conductor boundary

At zero, with \(R=k[[t]]\), the image algebra is precisely

\[
A_0=R[t^3U,t^4V]\subset B_0.
\tag{6}
\]

At infinity one must use the actual projective parameter and full
transverse coordinates

\[
z=x_2/x_3=\tau u/v,\quad U'=v^2/u^3-1,\quad V'=v^3/u^4-1.
\]

Their exponent matrix has determinant \(-1\), and
\(x_1/x_3=z^3(1+U')\), \(x_0/x_3=z^4(1+V')\). Hence
\(A_\infty=k[[z]][z^3U',z^4V']\subset B_\infty\).
Both upstairs algebras are free of rank \(m\) over their indicated DVRs.
Choose standard monomials \(U^iV^j\) of the special fiber; their exponent
set \(E\) is an order ideal of size \(m\) and their lifts form a basis.
The corresponding weighted monomials belong to \(A\), so

\[
\delta_i\le\sum_{(i,j)\in E}(3i+4j)\le2m(m-1),\qquad
0<e\le m(4m-2a-2b+3) \quad(\operatorname{char}k=0\text{ or }p\nmid ab). \tag{7}
\]

For the second bound, the monomial at position \(r\ge0\) in degree order
has total degree at most \(r\), since its divisor chain lies in \(E\).
The local upper bound is attained by \(B=R[V]/(V^m),U=0\); it is not
an asserted simultaneous projective realization. Equation (7) leaves
room in the surviving degrees and is not a second socle exclusion.

Let \(\mathfrak c\) be the conductor of \(\mathcal O_Z\subset\rho_*\mathcal O_W\).
At an endpoint write \(A\subset B\), \(Q=A[1/t]=B[1/t]\),
\(\delta=\ell(B/A)\), \(D=B/\mathfrak c\), and \(E=A/\mathfrak c\subset D\).
Then

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_A(B,A)=\mathfrak c,\quad
\omega_W=\mathfrak c\otimes\rho^*\omega_Z,\quad
\pi_*\mathfrak c=\mathcal B^\vee\otimes\mathcal O(14-4a-4b). \tag{8}
\]

For the first identity, an \(A\)-linear map becomes multiplication by
\(q\in Q\) after inversion. Its value at \(1\) puts \(q\in A\), and it
maps \(B\) to \(A\) exactly when \(qB\subset A\). The duality in
[Stacks 49.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BUK) then applies without
reducedness or a Gorenstein hypothesis on \(B\). Finally (1) uses four
base-point-free sections of \(\pi^*\mathcal O(4)\), so
\(\rho^*\mathcal O_Z(1)=\pi^*\mathcal O(4)\), giving the last twist.

There is a full finite-algebra statement behind the degrees:

\[
\ell(D)=2\delta,\quad\ell(E)=\delta,\quad
D\text{ is Artinian Gorenstein},\quad E=E^\perp,\quad A=B\times_D E.
\tag{9}
\]

The orthogonal complement uses some Frobenius product pairing on \(D\),
and \(E\) is a unital **subalgebra**, not an ideal. Here is a complete
local proof. Since \(A\) is finite free Gorenstein over \(R\), choose a
Frobenius functional \(\lambda:A\to R\), extended to \(Q\). Its lattice
dual \(A^\#=\{q:\lambda(qA)\subset R\}\) equals \(A\). Then
\(B^\#=\mathfrak c\): if \(\lambda(qB)\subset R\), first \(q\in A\),
and testing \(ba\in B\) for \(b\in B,a\in A\) puts \(qb\in A\).
Dualizing the inclusion of free DVR lattices preserves its index, so
\(\ell(A/\mathfrak c)=\ell(B/A)=\delta\). Define \(\epsilon:D\to k\)
by the coefficient of \(t^{-1}\) in \(\lambda(b)\). It kills \(E\).
If \(b\) is orthogonal to every \(b'\in B\), also test \(t^jb'\) for
all \(j\ge0\); every negative Laurent coefficient vanishes. Hence
\(b\in B^\#=\mathfrak c\), proving the pairing perfect and \(D\)
Gorenstein. Multiplication closes \(E\), so it is isotropic; half the
length makes it self-orthogonal. The inverse image of \(E\) is literally
\(A\), proving the fiber product. For \(\delta=0\) the boundary is empty.
This is the relevant conductor pinching datum, consistent with
[Ferrand's fiber-product framework](https://www.numdam.org/article/BSMF_2003__131_4_553_0.pdf).
The functional here is not the often-degenerate algebra trace.

Taking degrees in (8) gives only twice (4):
\(2(\delta_0+\delta_\infty)=2e+m(4a+4b-14)\).
The duality supplies the whole boundary algebra and compatible subalgebra,
but not an independent numerical contradiction.

### A conductor that cannot be replaced by a scalar

In every characteristic take

\[
A=R[x,y]/(x^2+y^2,xy),\quad x=t^3U,\ y=t^4V,
\quad B=R[U,V]/(U^2+t^2V^2,UV,V^3).
\]

The basis \(1,U,V,V^2\) makes \(B\) free and identifies it with the full
contraction from the punctured chart. The \(A\)-lattice exponents are
\((0,3,4,8)\), giving \(\delta=15\). Multiplication by \(U,V,V^2\) imposes
exactly the conductor bounds \((8,6,8,8)\); thus

\[
\mathfrak c=Rt^8\oplus Rt^6U\oplus Rt^8V\oplus Rt^8V^2=(t^8,t^6U)B.
\]

The special fiber of \(B\) has two-dimensional socle spanned by \(U,V^2\),
so \(B\) is not Gorenstein and its canonical conductor module is not
invertible. Nevertheless \(D\) has length thirty and one-dimensional
socle spanned by \(t^7V^2\); its coefficient functional kills the
fifteen-dimensional \(E\) and gives the perfect pairing in (9).
This local exact calculation prevents replacing the conductor by a
principal scalar or assuming that \(W\) has a dualizing line bundle.
No homogeneous projective realization is asserted.

## A moving primitive family with both contractions locally CI

The stronger controls work for every \(m\ge2\) in characteristic zero.
Let

\[
S=V(F),\qquad F=x_0^2x_2-x_1^3+x_2^3-x_1x_3^2,\qquad Z_m=mC\subset S.
\tag{10}
\]

The partials are \((2x_0x_2,-3x_1^2-x_3^2,x_0^2+3x_2^2,-2x_1x_3)\).
Their simultaneous vanishing forces all four coordinates zero, so \(S\)
is smooth. Thus \(C\) is Cartier on \(S\), and locally \(Z_m\) has
regular-sequence equations \((f,y^m)\), equivalently the ideal sheaf
\((F)+\mathcal I_C^m\). Adjunction gives \(C^2=2\), so its nilpotent layers
are \(\mathcal O_C(-2i)\), \(0\le i<m\), and

\[
\deg Z_m=4m,\quad \chi(\mathcal O_{Z_m})=2m-m^2,\quad p_a(Z_m)=(m-1)^2.
\tag{11}
\]

The strict surface transform in \(Y\) has equations

\[
f_0=U+t^6(u^3-v^2),\qquad f_\infty=\tau^6U+u^3-v^2.
\tag{12}
\]

Its class is \(\mathcal O(6)\). The relative derivatives along the section
are \((1+3t^6,-2t^6)\), and \((3,-2)\) at infinity; these never vanish
together. The transform \(T\to\mathbf P^1\) is smooth near the section.
Let \(W_m=m\bar C\subset T\), locally \((f_0,(U,V)^m)\). It is finite
flat, locally CI, and every fiber is curvilinear of length \(m\). It
agrees with the full torus pullback and has no parameter torsion, so it
is the **entire** closure. Its section conormal \(M\) fits

\[
0\to\mathcal O(-6)\xrightarrow{(s^6+3t^6,-2t^6)}\mathcal O^2\to M\to0.
\]

Therefore \(M=\mathcal O(6)\) and its nilpotent layers give
\(e=6\binom m2=3m(m-1)>0\). Its first direction already moves by the
degree-six map \([s:t]\mapsto[2t^6:s^6+3t^6]\), while every abstract
fiber remains \(k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^m)\).

Both actual endpoint contractions remain primitive. At zero put
\(A=x_2/x_0-t^3,B=x_3/x_0-t^4\). Then

\[
F/x_0^3=(1+3t^6)A-2t^5B+3t^3A^2+A^3-tB^2.
\]

Solve for \(A\), whose coefficient is a unit. Upstairs solve (12) for
\(U\). The exact inclusion is
\(k[[t]][t^4V]\subset k[[t]][V]/(V^m)\), with index \(4\binom m2\).
At infinity use the full coordinates after (6), put
\(X=x_1/x_3-z^3,Y'=x_0/x_3-z^4\), and compute

\[
F/x_3^3=-(1+3z^6)X+2z^5Y'-3z^3X^2-X^3+z(Y')^2.
\]

Solving for \(X\) gives the exact inclusion
\(k[[z]][z^4V']\subset k[[z]][V']/(V'^m)\), with the same index.
Thus both contractions are lci/Gorenstein, the finite scheme image is
precisely \(Z_m\), and \(\delta_0=\delta_\infty=2m(m-1)\).
The conormal comparison \(\mathcal O(-2)\to\mathcal O(6)\) has divisor
\(4p_0+4p_\infty\), explaining every layer's index. The primitive local
model and conormal layers are the classical geometry of
[Bănică–Forster, §§2.1–2.3](https://www.mathematik.uni-muenchen.de/~forster/eprints/multip_struct.pdf).

Yet \(Z_m\) is never a homogeneous scheme CI. There are no linear
equations, and the sole quadric containing \(C\) is
\(q=x_0x_3-x_1x_2\), since its ten quadratic monomials have nine distinct
images. Its normal linear term is \(B-tA\); its determinant with the
linear term of \(F\) is \(1+t^6\ne0\) generically. Thus \(q\) has order
one on \(C\subset S\) and vanishes on none of the \(Z_m\), \(m\ge2\).
Their least equation degree is three. A hypothetical homogeneous CI
presentation would have degrees \(3,b\), with \(3b=4m\). Its genus
\(1+3b(b-1)/2\), compared with (11), gives \(m=2b\), incompatible with
\(3b=4m\). Positive movement, curvilinear fibers, and both Gorenstein
contractions therefore coexist but do not supply two homogeneous equations.

There is an even stronger affine control. Any closed lci curve
\(X\subset\mathbf A^3_k\) with \(X_{\rm red}\simeq\mathbf A^1_k\) is a
scheme CI. Indeed coherent cohomology on the affine nilpotent thickening
and the unit sequence give \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)=0\). Its determinant
normal module is trivial, so choose a generator of
\(\operatorname{Ext}^2_R(R/I,R)=\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(I,R)\).
The corresponding extension \(0\to R\to P\to I\to0\) has locally free
rank-two middle term: the local Koszul calculation makes a unit extension
class free at \(X\), and away from \(X\) it splits. Quillen–Suslin makes
\(P\) free, so two generators form a regular sequence. The local extension
criterion is the affine Serre construction; see
[Schnell, Lemma 1 and §3](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~cschnell/pdf/notes/serre.pdf)
and [Forster, §IV, Theorem 4.3 proof](https://www.math.lmu.de/~forster/eprints/Compl_inter.pdf).
No replacement of the original thickening is made.

Apply this to either entire affine endpoint chart of \(Z_m\). Every pair
generating that affine ideal, ordinarily homogenized, has no common
factor: a nonhyperplane factor would dehomogenize, and ordinary
homogenizations have no hyperplane factor. Its projective CI is pure.
If it had only support \(C\), the omitted hyperplane coordinate would be
regular, and saturation would identify it with the scheme closure \(Z_m\),
a contradiction. Thus every such presentation introduces a residual
curve in the omitted hyperplane. Projective CI connectedness forces the
residual union to meet \(C\), necessarily at the omitted endpoint.
This quantifies over presentations of these **fixed affine thick schemes**,
not all affine set-theoretic presentations of reduced \(C\).

## Characteristic scope of the finite-modification mechanism

The toric morphism and its two normal invariant factors, full-ideal
closure, finite flatness, exact endpoint image algebras, Euler-characteristic
identity, coarse lattice bound, and conductor module/pairing (8)–(9)
work in every characteristic for an actual supported CI. These arguments
use purity, DVR lattices and finite duality, not logarithms, separability
of the cyclic fourfold projection, or an invertible generic multiplicity.
The proof of the all-two-divisor lemma is also characteristic free: it
says such a presentation forces embedded constancy. To deduce its
impossibility for \(W\), however, one needs \(e>0\). The trace theorem
establishes that strict inequality in characteristic zero and in
characteristic \(p>0\) when \(p\nmid ab\). Independent reconstruction on
October 31, 2026 checked the characteristic-free constancy argument,
diagonalization with preservation of ordinary degrees, and the resulting
extensions of (5) and (7). The characteristic-seven constant family above
has \(7\mid ab\), and witnesses failure of strict movement outside the
asserted range. The fixed quartic
in fact has an STCI with a quartic carrier in **every** positive
characteristic; the [characteristic chapter](../supplements/POSITIVE_CHARACTERISTIC_CONSTRUCTIONS.md#every-positive-characteristic-quartic-carrier) owns the full construction.
This broader existence is consistent with characteristic-free conductor
self-duality and the constancy of permitted tropical models.

The particular smooth-cubic control (10)–(12) and all its displayed
invariants extend for \(p\ne2,3\). Its partial-derivative smoothness
argument uses invertibility of two and three, as do its two unit relative
derivative checks; the determinant \(1+t^6\) is not identically zero in
these characteristics. The formal implicit eliminations only require
the indicated unit coefficients, so the weighted contraction indices
remain exact even if \(p\mid m\). Its degree/genus contradiction uses
integer Hilbert polynomials, not division inside the field. For \(p=2\)
or \(p=3\), this chosen cubic is singular and this construction is not
asserted to extend. The general affine scheme-CI lemma, by contrast,
works over any field when its reduced scheme is the affine line over
that field. The ambient Hilbert nef-cone calculation is only claimed
in characteristic zero, as specified at its application.

## The remaining global interface

For an actual supported CI, (3)–(9) give a finite punctual family (moving
in characteristic zero or when \(p\nmid ab\)),
two exact weighted contractions, and compatible conductor pinching algebras.
The moving primitive construction proves these conditions are not
inconsistent. The global two-generator ideal and its twists are additional
data. Pulling back a candidate dualizing line bundle to \(W\) can erase
endpoint descent information, just as replacing the full conductor by its
length does. A construction must recover two compatible homogeneous
equations; an obstruction must prove that no allowed moving family has
such an image, or must force an actual residual curve downstairs.
None of these implications is established here.
