# 2. The simultaneous geometry of F and G

Let \(C=C_0\subset\mathbf P^3\) be our fixed smooth rational quartic, and suppose
\[
V(F,G)_{\mathrm{red}}=C,\qquad \deg F=d,\quad\deg G=e.
\]
Write \(X=V(F)\), \(Y=V(G)\), and denote their **generic** orders along \(C\) by \(r,s\). The discussion is symmetric in \(F,G\); no minimum-degree choice is needed for its basic local geometry.

## 2.1 Generic intersection multiplicity and normal forms

At the generic point \(\eta\) of \(C\), the regular local ring \(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3,\eta}\) has dimension two, with maximal ideal generated by two transverse parameters \(u,v\) and residue field \(k(C)\). Write
\[
F=F_r+F_{r+1}+\cdots,\qquad G=G_s+G_{s+1}+\cdots
\]
in the completed local ring, with \(F_r,G_s\) homogeneous binary forms over \(k(C)\). The length
\[
m=\operatorname{length}\bigl(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3,\eta}/(F,G)\bigr)=\frac{de}{4}
\]
follows from Bézout and the assumption that the entire projective intersection is supported on \(C\).

The local intersection inequality is
\[
m\ge rs,
\]
with equality **if and only if** \(F_r,G_s\) have no common factor over \(\overline{k(C)}\) (equivalently, no common projective zero). If \(m>rs\), the initial forms necessarily share a factor. This is a statement about the *normal cone*, not about a common irreducible factor of \(F,G\) in \(k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\).

**Scope warning.** The converse “shared initial factor implies \(m>rs\)” is valid for this two-dimensional regular local complete intersection, but neither \(m\) nor the full higher-order intersection structure can be recovered from the first symbols alone.

## 2.2 First blowup and the exceptional common-direction scheme

Let \(\pi:B=\operatorname{Bl}_C\mathbf P^3\to\mathbf P^3\), let \(H=\pi^*\mathcal O(1)\), and let \(E\) be the exceptional divisor. Since \(N_{C/\mathbf P^3}\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(7)^{\oplus2}\), we have \(E\cong\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) abstractly. The strict transforms have classes
\[
\widetilde X\sim dH-rE,\qquad\widetilde Y\sim eH-sE.
\]
Their restrictions to \(E\) are the projectivized initial normal equations. On the generic fiber \(E_\eta\cong\mathbf P^1_{k(C)}\), their common zeros are exactly the common projective factors of \(F_r,G_s\).

The closure of a generic common zero in \(E\) gives a curve dominating \(C\) (possibly after passing to a finite extension of \(k(C)\)); multiple factors and special-fiber degeneration require scheme-theoretic care. The **horizontal common-direction curve** is a natural object to study, but it is not automatically a smooth section, and the scheme-theoretic intersection of the two exceptional restrictions may have vertical or embedded features.

This is the geometric interface with the root-scheme approach of [Chapter 3](03-infinitesimal-geometry.md). The existence of a common initial factor alone does **not** supply the saturation, smoothness, or global second-relation hypotheses required by [STCI-R-0006](../results/actual_ci_smooth_root_bridge.md).

## 2.3 Excess contact and successive transforms

The strict transforms of \(X\) and \(Y\) can continue to meet over \(C\). The higher-order terms of \(F,G\), or equivalently appropriate successive blowups and valuations, determine how much contact remains after removing the first exceptional orders.

A local illustration is
\[
F=u,\qquad G=u+v^N\quad (N\ge2).
\]
The initial forms share the factor \(u\), while \((F,G)=(u,v^N)\) has length \(N\). Thus identical first-order normal data can coexist with arbitrarily different intersection multiplicity. This is only a transverse local model, not a proposed global STCI pair.

The global question is therefore not merely whether common directions exist, but what **geometric and line-bundle constraints** prevent their excess contact from being absorbed indefinitely without producing an additional component of \(V(F,G)\).

## 2.4 Global numerical constraints

The audited entirely-thick analysis records, for generic orders \(r,s\ge2\), the necessary inequalities
\[
de\ge4rs,\qquad 7de+28rs\ge16(ds+er),
\]
together with \(d\ge3r\) and \(e\ge3s\) from restriction to the unique quadric. See [entirely-thick restrictions](../results/entirely_thick.md) and its linked audit. These conditions constrain possibilities but do not exclude the entire branch.

A separate common-normal-factor/resultant argument appears as P-006 in the [chronological research record](../RESEARCH_RECORD.md). Its hypotheses and precise scope should be rechecked before promoting it as a new canonical theorem or combining it with the numerical inequalities above.

## 2.5 The blowup of the actual intersection

Put \(Z=V(F,G)\) with its complete-intersection scheme structure. Since \(C=Z_{\mathrm{red}}\), the ideals satisfy \(I_Z\subset I_C\). The universal property of blowups **does not by itself** provide a morphism \(\operatorname{Bl}_C\mathbf P^3\to\operatorname{Bl}_Z\mathbf P^3\): one must first check that \(I_Z\mathcal O_B\) is invertible. When the initial forms share a projective zero, their residual ideal on \(E\) can fail to be invertible, which is precisely why a further modification or common resolution is useful.

Nor should the exceptional divisor of \(\operatorname{Bl}_Z\mathbf P^3\) be identified automatically with \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\). Its structure depends on the nonreduced complete-intersection center \(Z\), not merely on its reduced support.

**Provisional research interface:** compare the transforms of \(C\), \(Z\), \(X\), and \(Y\) on a common birational model, and identify the horizontal exceptional cycle and its degeneration divisor. The recent chat-based investigations need an independent source audit before any resulting assertions are promoted.


## 2.6 Proper residual cycles and global multiplicity bounds

The formula m=de/4 at the beginning of this chapter assumes *sole* projective support. For a general proper intersection of two surfaces containing C, write the fundamental cycle

    [V(F,G)] = m_C[C] + R,

where R is an effective residual one-cycle, possibly zero. Then

    deg(R)=de-4*m_C.

This is the bridge between local transverse algebra and global residual geometry. In particular, any rigorous upper bound m_C < de/4 for all actual mates in a specified family forces residual support in that family. Conversely, a vertical component on a blowup is not by itself a positive-degree component of R.

Three established examples delimit what can be inferred. [R-0023](../results/ci_quartic_explicit_residual_geometry.md) constructs a degree-12 residual for explicit quartic pairs, both smooth and in a line-plus-degree-11 degeneration. [R-0024](../results/ci_quartic_fixed_boundary_smoothing.md) shows that the five residual nodes of one such pencil can be smoothed independently with the C-attachment boundary fixed; it does not remove the residual degree. [R-0025](../results/ci_fixed_sextic_jet_rigidity.md) uses a specific sextic's degree-sixteen jet kernel to bound m_C at 22 and force deg(R)>=8. This last implication is a genuine global-source calculation, but only for the selected sextic and mate degree.

The [residual-intersection supplement](residual_intersections_and_jets.md) compares these mechanisms with [R-0019](../results/quasiprimitive_residual_cycles.md), whose proper residual *zero-cycle* formula requires a finite-flat curvilinear saturated flag and a suitable auxiliary surface. A zero-cycle on that auxiliary surface should not be substituted for the residual one-cycle R without an additional argument.

## 2.7 Valuations and global source spaces

The blowup has intrinsic horizontal direction schemes. Weighted valuations in rational normal coordinates depend on the frame, and scalar values alone do not recover transverse intersection length. Full initial ideals, integral models at exceptional points, and residue data are required; see [formal-normal geometry](formal-normal-intersection.md).

For the fixed quartic, the historical ideal-power theorem identifies global sections of the m-th ideal-sheaf power in degree d at least 3m with the degree-d ordinary homogeneous ideal power and proves H1 vanishing. This is a complete description of an ambient equation space in that range, not a theorem about its singularity strata or possible mates. Global properness and residual support remain separate tests.

## 2.8 Questions generated by this picture

- Can the common-direction curve be characterized intrinsically using the first-relation sheaf and its saturation defects?
- Which special-fiber degenerations are forced by global degree and conormal-bundle data?
- Can excess intersection be expressed by a finite valuation-theoretic invariant compatible with the conductor constraints on a fixed carrier?
- Under exactly which hypotheses does the blowup of \(Z\) admit a map from the blowup of \(C\), or from a controlled further modification?

These are **open research questions**, not consequences established by the preceding numerical formulas.
