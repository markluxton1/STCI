# 3. From common normal factors to actual equations

This chapter explains **four different levels of structure**: generic normal forms, global graded relations, embedded infinitesimal lifts, and actual complete intersections. Moving from one level to the next requires additional hypotheses. See [Chapter 2](02-two-surfaces.md) for the symmetric geometry of the original equations.

## 3.1 Generic normal forms versus global relations

At the generic point of C, first normal forms may share a factor without the original equations sharing a global factor. Cancellation can produce additional generators of the full initial ideal: for example, the ideal generated by u squared and u squared plus v cubed also contains v cubed, despite both displayed generators having order two.

The generic intersection and blowup geometry are developed in [Chapter 2](02-two-surfaces.md). For the separate local equation-module comparison, punctual defects and global source problem, use [Normal-filtration defects](normal_filtration_boundary_obstructions.md). Here the question is narrower: when does the generic common-direction data extend to a saturated global first relation?

## 3.2 Saturating the first relation

The conormal bundle \(N_C^*\) records the transverse linear directions. The associated graded algebra of the ideal of \(C\) has pieces \(\operatorname{Sym}^j N_C^*\). A first global relation may determine a line subsheaf in one symmetric power, but it need not be **saturated** or fiberwise generated. At exceptional points, a generic relation may acquire base points, lose rank, or have extra torsion in its quotient.

When the first degree-\(r\) relation is a saturated line subbundle, its binary form defines a relative degree-\(r\) divisor
\[
\pi:\mathcal R\longrightarrow C
\]
on the exceptional ruled surface. This is the **full root scheme**: repeated roots and nilpotent structure are retained. It should not be replaced silently by its reduced support. In the balanced split setting \(N_C^*=H^{-1}\otimes V\), the exceptional surface is \(C\times\mathbf P(V^*)\) and the root direction bundle is \(\mathcal O_{\mathcal R}(1)\).

The root scheme here is the divisor of a **single saturated first relation**, not automatically the whole common-direction scheme of two arbitrary equations.

## 3.3 The second relation lives in a quotient

A second independent graded relation is naturally defined **modulo multiples of the first**. It may fail to lift to a global polynomial line, even when it exists perfectly well as a line in the quotient. This is not merely a technicality: requiring such a global lift would exclude legitimate abstract graded configurations.

The established [spectral Picard criterion, STCI-R-0008](../results/spectral_root_picard_criterion.md) applies when the conormal is balanced split, the first relation is saturated, and the second relation is required to be coprime to it in **every** fiber. For degree \(s>r\), it asserts that a second quotient relation exists exactly when
\[
\mathcal O_{\mathcal R}(s)\cong\pi^*M
\]
for some line bundle \(M\) on \(C\). Equivalently, the class of \(\mathcal O_{\mathcal R}(1)\) in \(\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal R)/\pi^*\operatorname{Pic}(C)\) is killed by \(s\).

The criterion uses the Picard group of the **full** finite flat scheme, including nonreduced fibers. It constructs an abstract graded regular-sequence family, **not** a lift to the embedded formal neighborhood of \(C\), and not a pair of projective defining equations.

## 3.4 Why the Picard criterion is not enough

The conormal graded algebra is only the first approximation to the actual infinitesimal neighborhoods. Lifting a root relation requires correcting its equation successively so that it remains compatible with the nonlinear transition functions of the ambient blowup.

[STCI-R-0005](../results/smooth_root_formal_obstruction.md) proves that, under its balanced-normal-bundle assumptions, a **smooth movement-two root** of class \((2,r)\), \(r\ge2\), has no compatible embedded Cartier lift through *all* exceptional neighborhoods. This does not rule out finite lifts, singular roots, nonreduced roots, or other root classes.

[STCI-R-0007](../results/quadratic_root_obstruction_model.md) makes the first two ambient stages explicit in a quadratic movement-two family for the fixed quartic: a first rank-incidence test and a second test expressed by 25 scalar pairings. An explicit smooth spectral-torsion seed passes first compatibility but fails the second ambient test. Thus **spectral torsion does not imply embedded liftability**. The full second zero locus remains uncomputed.

The distinction is:
\[
\text{graded second relation}\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{embedded formal lift}\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{global STCI pair}.
\]
These arrows express missing implications, not new nonexistence theorems.

## 3.5 The actual complete-intersection bridge

[STCI-R-0006](../results/actual_ci_smooth_root_bridge.md) provides a precise, restrictive passage **from an actual complete intersection back to a controlled root thickening**. Its hypotheses include:

1. An actual complete intersection with reduced support \(C\).
2. A saturated degree-\(r\) first relation, generated **everywhere** by the degree-\(r\) symbols of the two actual equations; both have generic order \(r\).
3. Full generic initial ideal \((f_r,g_s)\), with coprime generators and \(s>r\), gap \(\ell=s-r\ge2\).
4. A **smooth full Cartier root**, not merely a smooth reduction.

Then the intersection of the strict transforms is locally a primitive \(\ell\)-fold structure \((h,u^\ell)\) on the full root and yields compatible finite embedded Cartier lifts through orders below \(\ell\). Combining this bridge with STCI-R-0005 gives a **conditional finite-gap obstruction**, not an explicit universal degree bound.

Notice the indexing subtlety: the two original equations in the bridge have order \(r\), while the independent second generator of the **full initial ideal** has degree \(s\). One must not confuse this \(s\) with the original order of the second equation in Chapter 2.

## 3.6 Moving pure powers and degeneration

[STCI-R-0009](../results/moving_purepower_boundary.md) treats a saturated first relation that is the \(r\)-th power of a moving normal direction, \(r\ge2\), in characteristic zero. Finite first-relation lifting can persist, but an everywhere-coprime next graded relation cannot: its evaluation divisor has degree at least \((r-1)\varepsilon\), where \(\varepsilon>0\) is the direction-map degree.

This produces a **necessary degeneration divisor**, not a contradiction to every actual complete intersection. A genuine STCI presentation could lie outside the theorem through special-point degeneration, noncoprime relations, nonsaturation, or a different full initial ideal.

## 3.7 The other multiple-structure calculations

The older [order-one](../results/order_one.md), [jet geometry](../results/jet_geometry.md), and [entirely-thick](../results/entirely_thick.md) analyses ask how ambient sections lift through successive thickenings and how the relevant restriction maps fail to have expected rank. These provide complementary tools for studying the same local-to-global problem. Their historical quartic-carrier cases are not reopened after [STCI-R-0001](../results/quartic_carrier_exclusion.md).

## 3.8 The unresolved interface

The missing theorem would give checkable hypotheses under which an actual pair's common-direction scheme determines a saturated global root relation and a controlled second quotient relation, including special-point defects. The distinctions between generic factors, global saturation, full initial ideals, fiberwise coprimality and embedded lifting remain essential.

The valuation and specialization program is maintained in [Formal normal geometry](formal-normal-intersection.md); residual detection in [Residual intersections and jets](residual_intersections_and_jets.md); and punctual comparison defects and boundary lifting in [Normal-filtration defects](normal_filtration_boundary_obstructions.md). None of these interfaces is a proved general implication.

## 3.9 A practical hierarchy for thickening investigations

For an actual pair \(J=(F,G)\subset I_C\), distinguish the generic length of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3}/J\) along \(C\), the graded conormal layers of its supported Cohen–Macaulay structure, the full \(I_C\)-initial ideal, and the images of **global** degree-bounded homogeneous sections. These four objects can have different generators and different exceptional-point behavior. The cancellation example in §3.1 already shows why the first two displayed symbols need not generate the initial ideal.

The balanced characteristic-zero normal bundle of the fixed quartic and its characteristic-two jump are recorded precisely in [R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md). This is a useful test of globalization: a locally chosen normal direction over \(k(C)\) need not extend as a line summand on \(C\). Do not infer that the characteristic-two split model lifts to characteristic zero.

For **saturated, full generic binary complete-intersection** initial data, [R-0010](../results/saturated_first_relation_normal_flatness.md) supplies a conditional normal-flatness result; [R-0013](../results/saturated_actual_generator_movement.md) and [R-0014](../results/movement_two_six_rows.md) further restrict actual generators and movement-two numerical possibilities under their own hypotheses. The six rows are necessary cases within that class, not a classification of arbitrary supported intersections. [R-0015](../results/fixed_small_movement_finiteness.md) is a fixed-range arithmetic reduction, not a completed all-degree obstruction. Reduced singular roots with a regular lifted formal surface are treated separately in [R-0018](../results/regular_singular_root_formal_obstruction.md); dropping regularity or saturation is not justified.

When saturation fails, [R-0016](../results/nonsaturated_first_relation_resultant.md) measures a conditional resultant/defect budget, while [R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md) identifies a *different* comparison between the filtered actual equation module and its initial generators. The latter leaves open whether a generically vanishing defect can be punctual under sole reduced support. Neither defect is automatically the jet-map rank shortfall from the historical order-one calculations. [R-0032](../results/full_conormal_socle_budget.md) imposes a separate socle budget on supported complete intersections; [R-0033](../results/constant_first_relation_lifting.md) obstructs a specified constant-relation lifting model, not every moving relation.

### Structural lessons from unsuccessful shortcuts

- **First symbols are insufficient.** Higher initial generators may arise through cancellation; the prescribed characteristic-two example has an essential fourth equation [R-0031](../results/quartic_char2_fourth_equation_kernel.md).
- **A graded model is not an embedded thickening.** The full-root Picard criterion [R-0008](../results/spectral_root_picard_criterion.md) must be combined with ambient compatibility, not treated as a global construction.
- **Numerical budgets do not realize equations.** Conormal degrees, socle identities, and finite jet ranks constrain candidates but do not establish that a pair of homogeneous forms realizes them.
- **Special-point defects are not residual curves by definition.** Residual support requires a separate actual intersection argument; see [R-0019](../results/quasiprimitive_residual_cycles.md).

### Concrete remaining tests

1. For a candidate actual pair with \(\sqrt J=I_C\), compute the full local initial ideal and the R-0030 comparison at every exceptional point; decide whether a nonzero punctual defect is compatible with sole support.
2. In a surviving saturated-root family, check full fiberwise coprimality, the actual generator hypotheses, and successive embedded lifting separately; calculate the remaining zero locus rather than extrapolating from one obstructed seed.
3. For a fixed lower thickening, compute the extension class and permitted repair divisor described in [the provisional one-step note](../notes/2026-10-27-one-step-boundary-obstruction.md), then impose the actual global degree-\(d\) source image. A vanishing extension class alone does not supply two defining equations.

These tests are complementary rather than a mandated sequence. For detailed boundary-repair and punctual-defect discussion, continue to [the filtration supplement](normal_filtration_boundary_obstructions.md).

### Jet-condition budgets: a precise historical model

In the order-one quartic calculations, the successive restriction map from degree-a ambient sections through a primitive layer to the next conormal layer has two different shortfalls: failure to hit the full target and failure to attain the maximal rank allowed by source and target dimensions. A quasiprimitive defect divisor modifies the target line bundle; it is not the same as either rank shortfall. The [order-one result](../results/order_one.md) now records the definitions and the concrete quadric-comparison example using R and 2R. This is a model for higher-degree global-source problems, not a universal implication from expected jet counts to residual support. Historical quartic-mate open cases are superseded by R-0001.
