# 3. From common normal factors to actual equations

This chapter explains **four different levels of structure**: generic normal forms, global graded relations, embedded infinitesimal lifts, and actual complete intersections. Moving from one level to the next requires additional hypotheses. See [Chapter 2](02-two-surfaces.md) for the symmetric geometry of the original equations.

**Authority boundary.** The conormal/socle budget [R-0032](../results/full_conormal_socle_budget.md) is necessary for actual supported complete intersections, not sufficient to produce an embedding or equations. The local comparison [R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md) does not decide punctual defects under sole support. The October 27 embedded one-step criterion is reviewed source-level work, not an independently promoted general theorem.

**Varying-lift qualification.** A compatible quadratic symbol determines a sextic only modulo \(\eta q^3\). Since \(q^3\) has normal order three, the correct existence test for some sextic lift is \(h+\eta(q^3)_3=f(\ell u+mv)\). The correction map alone has rank sixteen; the augmented map including \(\eta\) has rank sixteen or seventeen depending on whether \((q^3)_3\) lies in its image. Consequently a fixed-lift cubic failure does not, without checking this extra direction, exclude every lift of the same symbol. The reported \(-384\) pairing must be checked against \((q^3)_3\) before it is used as a varying-lift obstruction. See [the precise correction](normalization_conductor_phase2_addendum.md#varying-the-sextic-lift-the-missing-q3-direction).


## 3.1 Generic normal forms versus global relations

At the generic point of C, first normal forms may share a factor without the original equations sharing a global factor. Cancellation can produce additional generators of the full initial ideal: for example, the ideal generated by u squared and u squared plus v cubed also contains v cubed, despite both displayed generators having order two.

The generic intersection and blowup geometry are developed in [Chapter 2](02-two-surfaces.md). For the separate local equation-module comparison, punctual defects and global source problem, use [Normal-filtration defects](normal_filtration_boundary_obstructions.md). Here the question is narrower: when does the generic common-direction data extend to a saturated global first relation?

## 3.2 Saturating the first relation

The conormal bundle \(N_C^*\) records the transverse linear directions. The associated graded algebra of the ideal of \(C\) has pieces \(\operatorname{Sym}^j N_C^*\). A first global relation may determine a line subsheaf in one symmetric power, but it need not be **saturated** or fiberwise generated. At exceptional points, a generic relation may acquire base points, lose rank, or have extra torsion in its quotient.

When the first degree-\(r\) relation is a saturated line subbundle, its binary form defines a relative degree-\(r\) divisor
\[
\pi:\mathcal R\longrightarrow C
\]
on the exceptional ruled surface. This is the **full root scheme**: repeated roots and nilpotent structure are retained. It should not be replaced silently by its reduced support. In the balanced split setting \(N_C^*=H^{-1}\otimes V\), the exceptional surface is \(C\times\mathbf P(V^*)\) and the root direction bundle is \(\mathcal O_{\mathcal R}(1)\).

The root scheme here is the divisor of a **single saturated first relation**, not automatically the whole common-direction scheme of two arbitrary equations.

## 3.3 The second relation lives in a quotient

A second independent graded relation is naturally defined **modulo multiples of the first**. It may fail to lift to a global polynomial line, even when it exists perfectly well as a line in the quotient. This is not merely a technicality: requiring such a global lift would exclude legitimate abstract graded configurations.

The established [spectral Picard criterion, STCI-R-0008](../results/spectral_root_picard_criterion.md) applies when the conormal is balanced split, the first relation is saturated, and the second relation is required to be coprime to it in **every** fiber. For degree \(s>r\), it asserts that a second quotient relation exists exactly when
\[
\mathcal O_{\mathcal R}(s)\cong\pi^*M
\]
for some line bundle \(M\) on \(C\). Equivalently, the class of \(\mathcal O_{\mathcal R}(1)\) in \(\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal R)/\pi^*\operatorname{Pic}(C)\) is killed by \(s\).

The criterion uses the Picard group of the **full** finite flat scheme, including nonreduced fibers. It constructs an abstract graded regular-sequence family, **not** a lift to the embedded formal neighborhood of \(C\), and not a pair of projective defining equations.

## 3.4 Why the Picard criterion is not enough

The conormal graded algebra is only the first approximation to the actual infinitesimal neighborhoods. Lifting a root relation requires correcting its equation successively so that it remains compatible with the nonlinear transition functions of the ambient blowup.

[STCI-R-0005](../results/smooth_root_formal_obstruction.md) proves that, under its balanced-normal-bundle assumptions, a **smooth movement-two root** of class \((2,r)\), \(r\ge2\), has no compatible embedded Cartier lift through *all* exceptional neighborhoods. This does not rule out finite lifts, singular roots, nonreduced roots, or other root classes.

[STCI-R-0007](../results/quadratic_root_obstruction_model.md) makes the first two ambient stages explicit in a quadratic movement-two family for the fixed quartic: a first rank-incidence test and a second test expressed by 25 scalar pairings. An explicit smooth spectral-torsion seed passes first compatibility but fails the second ambient test. Thus **spectral torsion does not imply embedded liftability**. The full second zero locus remains uncomputed.

The distinction is:
\[
\text{graded second relation}\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{embedded formal lift}\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{global STCI pair}.
\]
These arrows express missing implications, not new nonexistence theorems.

### Initial generators versus the actual equation module

The full initial ideal of an actual pair may have cancellation-generated generators beyond the two first normal forms. Its minimal graded generator module can also differ from the associated graded of the actual equation module J/IJ. The canonical comparison and its kernel are established in [R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md), with a concrete local example in [the filtration synthesis](normal_filtration_boundary_obstructions.md#full-initial-ideals-and-the-filtered-equation-module).

The comparison defect is supported where both carrier surfaces are singular along C. Whether it can be nonzero for a proper pair with sole reduced support C is unresolved. This distinction does not remove the actual-generator hypotheses of the complete-intersection bridge below.

## 3.5 The actual complete-intersection bridge

[STCI-R-0006](../results/actual_ci_smooth_root_bridge.md) provides a precise, restrictive passage **from an actual complete intersection back to a controlled root thickening**. Its hypotheses include:

1. An actual complete intersection with reduced support \(C\).
2. A saturated degree-\(r\) first relation, generated **everywhere** by the degree-\(r\) symbols of the two actual equations; both have generic order \(r\).
3. Full generic initial ideal \((f_r,g_s)\), with coprime generators and \(s>r\), gap \(\ell=s-r\ge2\).
4. A **smooth full Cartier root**, not merely a smooth reduction.

Then the intersection of the strict transforms is locally a primitive \(\ell\)-fold structure \((h,u^\ell)\) on the full root and yields compatible finite embedded Cartier lifts through orders below \(\ell\). Combining this bridge with STCI-R-0005 gives a **conditional finite-gap obstruction**, not an explicit universal degree bound.

Notice the indexing subtlety: the two original equations in the bridge have order \(r\), while the independent second generator of the **full initial ideal** has degree \(s\). One must not confuse this \(s\) with the original order of the second equation in Chapter 2.

## 3.6 Moving pure powers and degeneration

[STCI-R-0009](../results/moving_purepower_boundary.md) treats a saturated first relation that is the \(r\)-th power of a moving normal direction, \(r\ge2\), in characteristic zero. Finite first-relation lifting can persist, but an everywhere-coprime next graded relation cannot: its evaluation divisor has degree at least \((r-1)\varepsilon\), where \(\varepsilon>0\) is the direction-map degree.

This produces a **necessary degeneration divisor**, not a contradiction to every actual complete intersection. A genuine STCI presentation could lie outside the theorem through special-point degeneration, noncoprime relations, nonsaturation, or a different full initial ideal.

## 3.7 The other multiple-structure calculations

The older [order-one](../results/order_one.md), [jet geometry](../results/jet_geometry.md), and [entirely-thick](../results/entirely_thick.md) analyses ask how ambient sections lift through successive thickenings and how the relevant restriction maps fail to have expected rank. These provide complementary tools for studying the same local-to-global problem. Their historical quartic-carrier cases are not reopened after [STCI-R-0001](../results/quartic_carrier_exclusion.md).

## 3.8 The unresolved interface

The missing theorem would give checkable hypotheses under which an actual pair's common-direction scheme determines a saturated global root relation and a controlled second quotient relation, including special-point defects. The distinctions between generic factors, global saturation, full initial ideals, fiberwise coprimality and embedded lifting remain essential.

The valuation and specialization program is maintained in [Formal normal geometry](formal-normal-intersection.md); residual detection in [Residual intersections and jets](residual_intersections_and_jets.md); and punctual comparison defects and boundary lifting in [Normal-filtration defects](normal_filtration_boundary_obstructions.md). None of these interfaces is a proved general implication.

## 3.9 A practical hierarchy for thickening investigations

For an actual pair \(J=(F,G)\subset I_C\), distinguish the generic length of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3}/J\) along \(C\), the graded conormal layers of its supported Cohen–Macaulay structure, the full \(I_C\)-initial ideal, and the images of **global** degree-bounded homogeneous sections. These four objects can have different generators and different exceptional-point behavior. The cancellation example in §3.1 already shows why the first two displayed symbols need not generate the initial ideal.

The balanced characteristic-zero normal bundle of the fixed quartic and its characteristic-two jump are recorded precisely in [R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md). This is a useful test of globalization: a locally chosen normal direction over \(k(C)\) need not extend as a line summand on \(C\). Do not infer that the characteristic-two split model lifts to characteristic zero.

For **saturated, full generic binary complete-intersection** initial data, [R-0010](../results/saturated_first_relation_normal_flatness.md) supplies a conditional normal-flatness result; [R-0013](../results/saturated_actual_generator_movement.md) and [R-0014](../results/movement_two_six_rows.md) further restrict actual generators and movement-two numerical possibilities under their own hypotheses. The six rows are necessary cases within that class, not a classification of arbitrary supported intersections. [R-0015](../results/fixed_small_movement_finiteness.md) is a fixed-range arithmetic reduction, not a completed all-degree obstruction. Reduced singular roots with a regular lifted formal surface are treated separately in [R-0018](../results/regular_singular_root_formal_obstruction.md); dropping regularity or saturation is not justified.

When saturation fails, [R-0016](../results/nonsaturated_first_relation_resultant.md) measures a conditional resultant/defect budget, while [R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md) identifies a *different* comparison between the filtered actual equation module and its initial generators. The latter leaves open whether a generically vanishing defect can be punctual under sole reduced support. Neither defect is automatically the jet-map rank shortfall from the historical order-one calculations. [R-0032](../results/full_conormal_socle_budget.md) imposes a separate socle budget on supported complete intersections; [R-0033](../results/constant_first_relation_lifting.md) obstructs a specified constant-relation lifting model, not every moving relation.

### Structural lessons from unsuccessful shortcuts

- **First symbols are insufficient.** Higher initial generators may arise through cancellation; the prescribed characteristic-two example has an essential fourth equation [R-0031](../results/quartic_char2_fourth_equation_kernel.md).
- **A graded model is not an embedded thickening.** The full-root Picard criterion [R-0008](../results/spectral_root_picard_criterion.md) must be combined with ambient compatibility, not treated as a global construction.
- **Numerical budgets do not realize equations.** Conormal degrees, socle identities, and finite jet ranks constrain candidates but do not establish that a pair of homogeneous forms realizes them.
- **Special-point defects are not residual curves by definition.** Residual support requires a separate actual intersection argument; see [R-0019](../results/quasiprimitive_residual_cycles.md).

### Concrete remaining tests

1. For a candidate actual pair with \(\sqrt J=I_C\), compute the full local initial ideal and the R-0030 comparison at every exceptional point; decide whether a nonzero punctual defect is compatible with sole support.
2. In a surviving saturated-root family, check full fiberwise coprimality, the actual generator hypotheses, and successive embedded lifting separately; calculate the remaining zero locus rather than extrapolating from one obstructed seed.
3. For a fixed lower thickening, compute the extension class and permitted repair divisor described in [the provisional one-step note](../supplements/BOUNDARY_ONE_STEP_AND_ENDPOINT_SCOPE.md), then impose the actual global degree-\(d\) source image. A vanishing extension class alone does not supply two defining equations.

These tests are complementary rather than a mandated sequence. For detailed boundary-repair and punctual-defect discussion, continue to [the filtration supplement](normal_filtration_boundary_obstructions.md).


### Jet-condition budgets: a precise historical model

In the order-one quartic calculations, the successive restriction map from degree-a ambient sections through a primitive layer to the next conormal layer has two different shortfalls: failure to hit the full target and failure to attain the maximal rank allowed by source and target dimensions. A quasiprimitive defect divisor modifies the target line bundle; it is not the same as either rank shortfall. The [order-one result](../results/order_one.md) now records the definitions and the concrete quadric-comparison example using R and 2R. This is a model for higher-degree global-source problems, not a universal implication from expected jet counts to residual support. Historical quartic-mate open cases are superseded by R-0001.

### Movement and socle: exact scope

In R-0013 the saturated first-relation line has degree -7r-epsilon. Its coefficient map to P^r has degree epsilon. Saturation prevents coefficient base points, not root collisions or failure of fiberwise coprimality. Under the actual supported-CI and full generic binary-CI assumptions, the necessary equations are ab=4rs, 4(a+b)=7(r+s)-epsilon(s-r)/r, and r divides epsilon*s. Movement epsilon=1 is excluded. R-0014 restricts epsilon=2 to six necessary tuples (r,s;a,b): (2,8;8,8), (2,12;6,16), (6,21;18,28), (14,42;48,49), (15,45;50,54), (20,60;64,75). These are not constructions, and they do not cover nonsaturated relations.

The socle identity in R-0032 is n(lambda*d-L)=lambda*(n*d-2*M)+2*Delta, with n the generic length, d the Loewy index, M the weighted sum of layer ranks, L the canonical degree parameter, lambda a valid conormal degree bound, and Delta the corrected layer defect. Equality gives graded self-duality; a constant algebra requires additional balanced-rational hypotheses. R-0033 then excludes only specified constant-first-relation equality models, assuming the next relation space is exactly fV. Moving relations, extra relations and positive-defect models remain outside its scope.


## 3.10 The characteristic-two arithmetic test case

For the fixed quartic the conormal sequence is

    0 -> O_C(-8) -> N_C^* -> O_C(-6) -> 0.

Its extension class is proportional to 2 in H1(P1,O(-2)). Accordingly [R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md) gives N_C^*=O(-7)^2 in characteristic different from two and O(-8)+O(-6) in characteristic two. This changes the available symmetric-power directions; it does not itself exclude or construct defining equations.

The prescribed integral quartics F=x1^4-x0^3*x3 and G=x2^4-x0*x3^3 give a complementary example. By [R-0021](../results/quartic_mu4_degeneration.md), their intersection is flat over Z and, away from the endpoints, equals G_m times mu_4. In characteristic zero it is the reduced union of four distinct quartic curves, while in characteristic two it has sole reduced support C and generic algebra K[Y]/(Y^4). This is coalescence of *actual projective components*, not merely exceptional directions.

The full initial ideal explains the jump. In characteristic zero the two generic first normal symbols are independent and the transverse multiplicity along C is one. In characteristic two they become dependent, and cancellation using the actual equations supplies the order-four generator. The special ideal is (X,Y^4), not merely the ideal of the displayed first symbols. [R-0031](../results/quartic_char2_fourth_equation_kernel.md) identifies the fourth minimal initial-equation bundle and the filtered equation-module kernel with O(-24), with zero comparison defect, for this prescribed pair. It does not give a general theorem for arbitrary characteristic-two pairs.

Finally [R-0012](../results/prescribed_z4_arithmetic_nonlifting.md) excludes a *flat embedded lift of this particular special complete intersection* over Z_(2) or Z_2 whose generic reduced support is the fixed relative quartic. The obstruction is a forced primitive-triple class in H1(P1,O(-4)) with a middle Laurent coefficient congruent to 2 modulo 4. The theorem neither covers arbitrary ramified mixed-characteristic DVRs nor excludes an unrelated characteristic-zero STCI. Flatness of the displayed integral family is consistent with nonlifting under the extra requirement of sole generic reduced support: its characteristic-zero generic fiber has four components.

These results separate four questions: conormal splitting, full initial equations, global reduced support, and arithmetic deformation of a prescribed embedded scheme. None may be substituted for another.


## 3.10A Characteristic-two cancellation versus embedded lifting

The prescribed pair \(F=x_1^4-x_0^3x_3\), \(G=x_2^4-x_0x_3^3\) gives an exact arithmetic comparison, not a deformation-invariant STCI certificate. In characteristic two the generic initial equations first share a normal factor; cancellation supplies a fourth-order initial equation and generic transverse length four. The local filtered comparison defect nevertheless vanishes. In characteristic zero the same integral pair has additional reduced components, so the special-fiber support cannot be transported by flatness alone. See [R-0021](../results/quartic_mu4_degeneration.md) and [R-0031](../results/quartic_char2_fourth_equation_kernel.md).

The conormal splitting change R-0020 (see [canonical results](../results/INDEX.md)) and the prescribed embedded nonlifting result R-0012 (see [canonical results](../results/INDEX.md)) address different objects. The latter excludes the specified supported embedded lift over its stated two-adic bases, **not** the displayed integral family (whose generic support is larger), nor every characteristic-zero pair. This example tests three separate interfaces: full initial ideals, integral embedded liftability, and generic support.

## 3.10B An explicit sextic normal-symbol image

The conditional movement-two (6,16) investigation computes the first nontrivial global-source map rather than assuming normal-symbol surjectivity. For the fixed characteristic-zero quartic, the map from degree-six forms vanishing to order two to quadratic normal symbols has a 23-dimensional domain, a 22-dimensional image and kernel spanned by the cube of the containing quadric. The target has dimension 33, so its image has codimension eleven. The [conditional incidence calculation](normalization_conductor_phase2_addendum.md) gives an explicit source basis and all eleven independent linear equations.

For a moving quadratic root f with symbol multiplier alpha, these equations become eleven bilinear conditions on f and alpha. They are necessary before considering the next cubic jet; in particular alpha must not be inverted at its zeros. The subsequent cubic-jet compatibility is a separate finite 40-coefficient equation, and its solvability does not construct a proper mate of degree sixteen. This is a concrete instance where complete knowledge of the ambient ideal-power space does **not** make its leading-symbol map surjective.

## 3.10C A failed seed and an exact cubic correction

The conditional (6,16) calculation has a smooth torsion-two root \(f_0=(z^2+4)u^2+(z^2-8)v^2\) with a unique projective multiplier \(\alpha_0=z^8-8z^6+96z^4+256z^2+1024\) satisfying all eleven leading-symbol image equations. For a fixed sextic lift, the cubic comparison has nonzero pairing \(-384\); this does not test the additional \(q^3\) lift direction. The seed independently fails the second obstruction under its stated hypotheses, excluding that seed but not the family.

The cubic correction is \(f(\ell u+mv)\), where \(f=au^2+buv+cv^2\) and \(\deg\ell,\deg m\le7\). For a prescribed cubic \(h=h_0u^3+h_1u^2v+h_2uv^2+h_3v^3\), compatibility means \(h_0=a\ell\), \(h_1=b\ell+am\), \(h_2=c\ell+bm\), \(h_3=cm\). Multiplication by nonzero \(f\) is injective, giving a rank-sixteen correction map into forty cubic coefficients and a twenty-four-dimensional quotient obstruction. A correction, if it exists, is unique. These undivided identities remain valid for singular roots and coefficient zeros.

The twenty-five second-obstruction pairings, full-root torsion and existence of a proper degree-sixteen mate remain separate tests. See [the full calculation](normalization_conductor_phase2_addendum.md#coefficient-level-cubic-obstruction-without-elimination).

## 3.10E A full-lift certificate beyond the failed seed

The historical [free cube-orbit calculation](normalization_conductor_phase2_addendum.md#existing-full-lift-exclusion-on-the-free-cube-orbit-chart) includes the free \(\eta q^3\) sextic lift. Its restricted chart reduces to \(p(h)=648h^4+45h^2-4=0\), while two full cubic equations satisfy \(E_4-6E_8=18h-324h^3\) and a polynomial unit certificate. Thus that entire chart is empty even after varying the lift. The endpoint and orbit-collision strata remain unexcluded; application to supported pairs depends on the separately proposed norm necessity bridge.

## 3.10F Two-node reduction and the surviving sextic pencil

The [forced-norm two-node continuation](normalization_conductor_phase2_addendum.md#two-node-root-strata-exact-exclusions-and-retained-pencils) excludes the generic nonzero-defect line component and its \(B=0,C=0\) boundaries by exact scoped computations. The generic conic component remains unresolved. On the distinct zero-defect stratum, the global sextic has the exact form \(H_1H_2+\eta q^3\), and an actual supported pair must have \(\eta\ne0\) by cubic factor reduction. This is a necessary pencil description, not a mate construction.

## 3.10G Actual-pair geometric obstruction

The reviewed geometric continuation of the conditional (6,16) investigation forces a nonreduced full-root multiplier divisor for an actual supported pair with the stated saturated movement-two bridge. The normal strict-sextic singularity budget gives 144 versus at most 50; ampleness of the strict mate extends the restriction to nodal roots. The resulting degree-52 exceptional locus is necessary, not sufficient, and is not a condition on arbitrary abstract root incidence points.

## 3.11 Embedded one-step lifting and the actual homogeneous source

The [October 27 one-step investigation](../supplements/BOUNDARY_ONE_STEP_AND_ENDPOINT_SCOPE.md) supplies a source-level theorem for a *fixed embedded lower thickening* Y with generic algebra K[y]/(y^n), n at least two. Write I=I_C, J=I_Y, and let M_Y=(J/IJ)/torsion. The first-symbol quotient is V_Y=R(-B_Y), and the saturated kernel of the first-symbol map is Ubar_Y=L^n(A_Y), where L is the torsion-free first normal quotient. The actual equation module sits in

    0 -> Ubar_Y -> M_Y -> V_Y -> 0,

with extension class e_Y in Ext^1(V_Y,Ubar_Y). These objects depend on the embedded lower scheme, not only on its graded Hilbert function.

To add a saturated next layer T=L^n(D), a necessary condition is A_Y <= D. Under that condition, extension of the prescribed power map from Ubar_Y to all of M_Y is equivalent to vanishing of the pushed-forward class in H^1(Ubar_Y tensor V_Y^vee(D-A_Y)). Maps satisfying the extension condition form a torsor; *surjectivity* is a further requirement for an actual pure embedded extension, automatic when D=A_Y. Thus an abstract cohomological repair is not itself an embedded thickening.

At the final step of a generically curvilinear Gorenstein multiple curve of length at least three, the source proves the stronger necessary conditions e_Y=0 and A_Y=D_soc. The final socle divisor has no unused repair allowance. For a fixed lower Y the source also gives a completion criterion: splitting of the equation-module extension together with all required perfect-pairing divisor equalities. This is a criterion for an embedded Gorenstein completion, not for two homogeneous carrier equations.

There is a separate exact test for the actual global equations. Suppose C has degree c, ab=(n+1)c, and F,G are actual sections of J(a),J(b) forming a proper pair. Their classes in M_Y give a determinant section theta_Y(F,G). Its vanishing is equivalent to sole reduced support C: if nonzero, the generic length is n and the residual cycle has degree c; if zero, Bezout and purity force generic length n+1 and exhaust the projective degree. For equal degrees the zero-pencils form a Grassmannian linear section of the kernel of the determinant map, restricted to proper pairs; for unequal degrees use the corresponding Segre locus. A generic-rank computation alone does not settle its exceptional decomposable kernel points.

These statements are retained as reviewed source-level deductions, not independently promoted canonical theorems. Their practical contribution is to distinguish the lower embedded extension class, its boundary repair, the final Gorenstein pairing, and the actual global source map. Passing any one of these tests does not imply the next.

## 3.12 Boundary repair versus embedded lifting

For a **specified** obstruction \(o\in H^1(C,V)\) in a vector bundle \(V\) on a smooth curve, and an effective divisor \(D\), the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow V(D)\longrightarrow V(D)/V\longrightarrow0
\]
shows that \(o\) dies in \(H^1(C,V(D))\) exactly when it lies in the image of the connecting map \(H^0(C,V(D)/V)\to H^1(C,V)\). By Serre duality this is equivalent to \(o\) annihilating \(H^0(C,V^\vee\otimes\omega_C(-D))\). This is a **principal-parts repair test for the specified class**, not a construction of an arbitrary embedded thickening.

On \(C=\mathbf P^1\), take \(V=\mathcal O(-r)\), \(r\ge2\), and \(n=r-2\). A divisor \(D\) of degree \(B\) corresponds to a nonzero \(h\in H^0(\mathcal O(B))\) up to scalar. The class \(o\) is reparable by **some** such divisor exactly when the catalecticant map
\[
H^0(\mathcal O(B))\longrightarrow H^0(\mathcal O(n-B))^\vee,\qquad
h\longmapsto (q\mapsto\langle o,hq\rangle)
\]
has a nonzero kernel. If \(B\ge\lfloor r/2\rfloor\), a kernel exists by dimension for every class, but this only allows an *arbitrarily chosen* divisor. For a direct sum of obstruction lines the **same** \(h\) must repair all components, so their catalecticant maps must be stacked. The [boundary supplement](../supplements/NORMAL_BOUNDARY_WEIGHTED_INITIALS_AND_LIFTING.md) gives the complete calculation and the unproved comparison with the ambient lifting complex.

The [one-step package](../supplements/BOUNDARY_ONE_STEP_AND_ENDPOINT_SCOPE.md) is more restrictive: it fixes an embedded lower thickening, its equation module and permissible socle boundary. One must establish that the geometric boundary actually induces the proposed modification of the **correct obstruction bundle**, rather than applying an arbitrary twist to an unrelated cohomology class. Vanishing, the final Gorenstein pairing and the existence of two **proper decomposable** elements in actual homogeneous source spaces are distinct tests. A nonzero linear kernel need not meet the decomposable locus.

The [Delta-embedding package](../supplements/DELTA_EMBEDDING_NORMAL_AND_REES_GEOMETRY.md) addresses a different local comparison defect. It is not interchangeable with the repair divisor, with the saturated first-relation defect or with a residual projective curve. The supported punctual-defect question remains open under [R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md).
