# Minimal-generator criterion for moving height-two ideals

## Scope

Let \(k\) be any field and \(S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\) with its
standard grading. Let \(J\subset S\) be a homogeneous ideal of height
two and \(a,b\) positive integers. In the Hilbert-locus application,
\(J\) is the saturated homogeneous ideal of the moving embedded
scheme, not the ideal of a chosen torus-fixed endpoint. Define
\[
M(J)_d=J_d/(S_1J_{d-1}),\qquad J_{-1}=0.
\]
For the optional finite test, additionally declare an integer
\(r\ge\max(a,b)\) and assume the actual bound
\(\operatorname{reg}_S J\le r\). For a family this bound is required
for every fiber to which that finite test is applied. No such numerical
bound for a sextic family is asserted here.

## Statement

The following are equivalent:

1. \(J\) is generated by two ordinary homogeneous forms \(F,G\) of
   degrees \((a,b)\).
2. For every integer \(d\ge0\),
   \[
   \dim_k M(J)_d=\mathbf 1_{d=a}+\mathbf 1_{d=b}.
   \]

In this case the two generators form a regular sequence. Thus they are
actual complete-intersection equations for the entire ideal, and the
projective scheme has Hilbert polynomial
\(abn+ab(4-a-b)/2\).

Under the additional regularity bound in Scope, condition 2 is
equivalent to the same equalities only for \(0\le d\le r\). Each
dimension in this finite test is computed as
\[
\dim J_d-\operatorname{rank}
 \bigl(S_1\otimes_k J_{d-1}\longrightarrow J_d\bigr).
\]
On a family stratum where the displayed source and target pieces are
vector bundles with the correct fibers, these are fiberwise ranks of
the corresponding multiplication maps.

In particular, for \((a,b)=(6,6)\), the criterion is
\(\dim M(J)_6=2\) and \(M(J)_d=0\) for every other degree. A finite
version requires a declared valid \(r\ge6\) as above.

## Limitations

Two independent sections of \(J_a\) and \(J_b\), or a pencil in
\(J_6\), do not certify generation. Every other minimal-generator
degree must be excluded, using a justified cutoff if only finitely many
degrees are tested. Fiberwise use of matrices requires the stated base
change and vector-bundle hypotheses.

The criterion concerns the moving saturated ideal. Its torus-fixed
limit can have additional generators. This result neither classifies
attracting sets nor proves that any such set contains a supported
complete intersection, and it establishes no universal STCI obstruction.

## Review

Independent reconstruction review accepted the exact height-two
generator criterion, regular-sequence argument, and finite cutoff
conditional on \(\operatorname{reg}_S J\le r\), including the required
base-change and fiber qualifications for matrix tests. The reviewer
reconstructed the proof, compared the declared source, and found no
blocking mathematical correction or lost contribution. No mathematical
computation was needed. No numerical cutoff, nonempty incidence,
attracting-locus classification, or coverage theorem was accepted.
The review covers only the stated criterion and matrix tests.

The source frames this as a geometric research formulation. It asserts no classification. This framing is distinct
from the independent acceptance of this algebraic route; the surrounding
attracting-locus questions remain open. This acceptance does not cover
the composite trace corollary STCI-R-0040, whose separate reconstruction
decision is recorded in its own Result.
