# Conormal entry annihilation and punctual comparison defects

## Scope

The annihilator theorem applies to any coherent \(J\subset I\) in a
regular codimension-two embedding of locally Noetherian schemes.
The cubic sharpness family and the supported quadruple work over every
field. The family realizing all punctual degrees and lengths has
characteristic zero. Its polynomial carriers contain a marked line, and
the calculation of the actual finite flat algebra is over the completed
DVR at the origin of that line.

Use the full initial ideals \(H=\operatorname{in}_I(J)\) and
\(T=\operatorname{in}_I(IJ)\), with \(S=\operatorname{gr}_I R\) and
\(S_+\) its positive-degree ideal. The [comparison of R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md)
identifies \(\Delta=T/S_+H\) as the kernel of
\(Q=H/S_+H\to G=\operatorname{gr}_F(J/IJ)\).
Here \(T\) is the product-ideal initial denoted \(B\) in R-0030.
In the examples over \(B=k[[t]]\), write \(\Delta\otimes_B k\) for the
tensor of the relative defect, and \(\Delta_0^{\rm act}\) for the defect
computed by taking initials of the actual equations and their product
ideal in \(k[[x,y]]\). Coefficient specialization of an initial ideal
means its image in \(k[X,Y]\).

## The conormal entry ideal annihilates the defect

Work locally with \(I=(x,y)\) generated by a regular sequence. Put
\(B=R/I\), \(E=I/I^2\), \(D=J/IJ\), and \(S=\operatorname{gr}_I R=B[X,Y]\),
where \(X,Y\) are the symbols of \(x,y\). Write \(H=\operatorname{in}_I(J)\)
and \(T=\operatorname{in}_I(IJ)\). The
[R-0030 comparison](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md)
identifies \(\Delta=T/S_+H\) with a submodule of \(Q=H/S_+H\).

### Entrywise annihilation

Since \(IJ\subset I^2\), degree one of \(\Delta\) vanishes. For \(n\ge2\)
choose a representative \(h\in IJ\cap I^n\) of a degree-\(n\) class.
Since \(I^n=xI^{n-1}+yI^{n-1}\) and \(IJ=xJ+yJ\), write
\[
h=xc+yd=xu+yv,\qquad c,d\in I^{n-1},\quad u,v\in J.
\]
Exactness of the Koszul complex for the regular sequence \(x,y\) gives
\(c-u=-yw\) and \(d-v=xw\) for some \(w\in R\).
For any \(f\in J\) write \(f=xa'+yb'\), with \(a,b\) the reductions
of \(a',b'\) modulo \(I\). Its degree-one symbol is
\(f_1=aX+bY\in H_1\). In \(R\) we have
\[
a'd-b'c=a'v-b'u+fw\in J\cap I^{n-1}.
\]
Write \(c^*,d^*\in S_{n-1}\) for the classes modulo \(I^n\). The displayed
identity yields \(\lambda=ad^*-bc^*\in H_{n-1}\). For the degree-\(n\) class
\(h^*=Xc^*+Yd^*\) the identities are
\[
ah^*=c^*f_1+Y\lambda,\qquad
bh^*=d^*f_1-X\lambda.
\]
Both right sides belong to \((S_+H)_n\) because \(n-1\ge1\). This uses
the full ideal \(H\) to retain the symbol of the actual equation
\(a'd-b'c\). Varying \(f\) proves that all entries of the conormal map
\(\phi:D\to E\) annihilate \(\Delta\).

Intrinsically, the entries generate the image of evaluation
\(E^\vee\otimes_B D\to B\) via \(\phi\). A finite free module surjecting
onto \(D\) gives a presentation of \(\operatorname{coker}\phi\) with
target \(E\) of rank two. The size-one minors generate
\(\operatorname{Fitt}_1(\operatorname{coker}\phi)\), with
\(\operatorname{coker}\phi=I/(I^2+J)\). This computation only uses a
generating set of \(D\). The evaluation description identifies the ideal
in overlapping frames, so the action on \(\Delta\) factors canonically
through the structure sheaf of the subscheme cut out by that ideal.

For \(J=I_X+I_Y\), the two labelled Cartier source lines surject jointly
onto \(D\). Their entries therefore generate the sum ideal in \(B\).
When the ambient threefold and marked curve are smooth, the conormal
fiber of the curve injects into the ambient cotangent fiber. The
hypersurface smoothness criterion makes the zero image of a local
carrier equation equivalent to singularity there. Thus the zero set of
the sum ideal is the intersection
of the two singular loci with \(C\). At a DVR stalk where the entry
ideal is \((t^r)\ne0\), the finite module \(\Delta_n\) is killed by
\(t^r\). Each cyclic summand in its elementary divisor decomposition
then has length at most \(r\), giving the stated length bound.

### Koszul interpretation of the entry identities

In the normal Koszul complex over \(R/J\), the image of \((c,d)\) is a
boundary and that of \((a',b')\) is a cycle. Their wedge is zero because it
is the differential of a product in homological degree three, which
vanishes in rank two. The coefficient of that wedge over \(R\) is the
determinant \(a'd-b'c\) above. Under the graded Tor identification of
\(Q\) in [R-0065](../results/normal_koszul_comparison_defect.md), the
two identities express this vanishing entry by entry in the minimal
initial equations. The direct identities apply without any convergence
or completeness hypothesis.

### Sharpness over a DVR

Let \(B=k[[t]]\), \(a=t^r\) for \(r\ge1\), \(R=B[[x,y]]\), and
\[
f=ax+y^2,\qquad g=ay+x^2,\qquad h=xg-yf=x^3-y^3 .
\]
The polynomial \(f\) is primitive and linear in \(x\) over \(k[t,y]\),
since \(t^r\) and \(y^2\) are coprime. It is irreducible by Gauss's lemma.
The same argument in \(y\) applies to \(g\). The two irreducibles are
distinct up to associates and thus form a regular sequence at the
origin of \(\mathbf A^3_k\). Completion preserves this regular sequence
in \(R\).

Let \(q=X^3-Y^3\), and give \(f,g,h\) weights \(1,1,3\) from their
respective symbols \(aX,aY,q\). To compute all symbol syzygies, consider
a relation \(aXU+aYV+qW=0\) in \(B[X,Y]\). Reducing modulo \(a\) gives
\(q\bar W=0\). The polynomial \(q\) is monic in \(X\) over
\((B/(a))[Y]\), so multiplication by \(q\) is injective by the leading
coefficient calculation, including for nonreduced \(B/(a)\). Write
\(W=aW'\) and cancel \(a\). The remaining relation is
\[
X(U+X^2W')+Y(V-Y^2W')=0.
\]
The Koszul syzygy on \(X,Y\) now expresses \((U,V,W)\) as a linear
combination of \((Y,-X,0)\) and \((-X^2,Y^2,a)\). These two homogeneous
syzygies have exact lifts
\[
yf-xg+h=0,\qquad -x^2f+y^2g+ah=0.
\]

For any \(z\in J\cap I^n\), start with a representation by \(f,g,h\).
The minimum of the orders of the coefficients plus \(1,1,3\) is its
lowest possible degree. If this degree \(m\) is less than \(n\), the
coefficient symbols of weight \(m\) give a homogeneous syzygy among
\(aX,aY,q\). Express it using the two displayed generators with
homogeneous coefficients and lift those coefficients to \(R\).
Subtracting the resulting combination of the exact lifts raises the
minimum weight by at least one and leaves \(z\) unchanged. After
finitely many steps the minimum weight is at least \(n\). Taking the
degree-\(n\) symbol proves \(H\subseteq(aX,aY,q)\). The opposite
inclusion follows from \(f,g,h\in J\).

Apply this same filtered representation to \(z\in IJ\cap I^n\), say
\(z=uf+vg+wh\). The degree-one conormal symbol of \(z\) vanishes since
\(IJ\subset I^2\). The symbol is \(a(\bar u X+\bar v Y)\), so
\(\bar u=\bar v=0\) in \(B\). Thus \(u,v\in I\), and taking degree \(n\)
in this representation puts the symbol of \(z\) in
\((aX^2,aXY,aY^2,q)\). Conversely, \(xf,yf,xg,yg,h\) are all in \(IJ\),
and their symbols generate that ideal. We obtain the two full ideals
\[
H=(aX,aY,q),\qquad T=(aX^2,aXY,aY^2,q).
\]
Here \(S_+H\) is generated by \(aX^2,aXY,aY^2,Xq,Yq\). The quotient
\(T/S_+H\) is generated by the class of \(q\) in degree three and
vanishes in all other degrees. In degree three,
\((S_+H)_3=aS_3\) and the coefficients of \(q\) include a unit, so the
class has annihilator \((a)\). This gives \(\Delta_3\simeq B/(t^r)\)
and \(\operatorname{Ann}_B\Delta=(t^r)\). In the \(f,g\) generator
frames of \(D\) the conormal map is diagonal with entries \(a,a\), giving
\(\mathfrak a=(t^r)\). This map is injective because \(a\) is a
nonzerodivisor, so \(F^2D=0\) by R-0030. All identities and the monicity
argument hold over every field \(k\).

## Arbitrary punctual degree and length, with integral carriers

This proves [R-0068](../results/punctual_comparison_defect_family.md).
Work over a field \(k\) of characteristic zero with \(r\ge1\), \(m\ge2\),
\(a=t^r\), \(B=k[[t]]\) and \(R=B[[x,y]]\). Use \(I=(x,y)\) for the
marked \(t\)-axis. The actual equations and the first cancellation are
\[
\begin{aligned}
f&=ax+x^2+y^m, & g&=ay+xy+y^m+x^{m+1},\\
h&=xg-yf=xy^m-y^{m+1}+x^{m+2}, & q&=XY^m-Y^{m+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

### Integral carriers and the full initial ideal

As a monic quadratic in \(x\), \(f\) has discriminant
\(a^2-4y^m\). This polynomial is squarefree in \(k(t)[y]\) since it is
coprime to its derivative \(-4my^{m-1}\). Its irreducible factors occur
with multiplicity one and it has positive degree, so it cannot be a square
in \(k(t,y)\). Hence \(f\) is irreducible over that field. The polynomial
\(g\) is monic in \(x\) and Eisenstein at \(y\) over \(k(t)[y]\).
Its nonleading coefficients are divisible by \(y\), and the constant
coefficient \(ay+y^m\) has order one in \(y\). Gauss's lemma over
\(k[t,y]\) gives irreducibility of both in \(k[t,x,y]\).
Their degrees in \(x\) differ. The ideal \((f)\) is prime and the class
of \(g\) is nonzero in its quotient. This gives the regular sequence in
the polynomial local ring at the origin and flat completion preserves it.

Use \(S=B[X,Y]\) and write the first symbols of \(f,g,h\) as
\(aX,aY,q\), of degrees \(1,1,m+1\), respectively. Every polynomial
syzygy of these symbols is generated by
\[
(Y,-X,0),\qquad (-Y^m,Y^m,a).
\]
Indeed, reduce a relation \(aXU+aYV+qW=0\) modulo \(a\).
Multiplication by \(Y\) is injective in \((B/(a))[X,Y]\), and so is
multiplication by \(X-Y\) by monicity in \(X\). Thus \(q=Y^m(X-Y)\)
is a nonzerodivisor even over this possibly nonreduced coefficient ring.
It follows that \(W=aW'\) for some \(W'\in S\). Subtract
\(W'(-Y^m,Y^m,a)\) from the relation and cancel \(a\). What remains
is a syzygy of \(X,Y\), generated by \((Y,-X)\) since they form a
regular sequence in \(S\). This reasoning also generates each
homogeneous syzygy by homogeneous multiples in the indicated weights.

The exact lifted syzygies are
\[
yf-xg+h=0,\qquad
(-y^m-x^{m+1})f+y^m g+(a+x)h=0 .
\]
The second identity expands to \(-gf+fg=0\) after substituting for \(h\).
Assign weights \(1,1,m+1\) to the three presentation generators.
The \(h\)-term in the first lift has weight \(m+1>2\).
The two additional terms in the second lift both have weight
\(m+2>m+1\). Their lowest weighted forms are therefore exactly the
two syzygies of the symbols.

For an actual nonzero equation \(u\in J\) of order \(n\), start with a
presentation \(u=A_f f+A_g g+A_h h\) and let \(w\) be the minimum of
the weighted orders of its three entries. If \(w<n\), the weight-\(w\) terms form
a homogeneous syzygy. Express it using the two displayed symbol
syzygies and lift the homogeneous multipliers to the same normal
degrees in \(R\). Subtract the resulting combination of the actual
lifted syzygies from the presentation. This preserves \(u\) and
increases \(w\) by at least one. Each such correction has a finite
target, namely \(n\). At weight \(n\) the symbol of \(u\) lies in
\((aX,aY,q)\). The three generating symbols themselves come from
equations in \(J\), proving
\[
H=(aX,aY,q).
\]
The argument applies directly in the polynomial local ring with
\(B_0=k[t]_{(t)}\), since the same graded syzygies exist over
\(B_0[X,Y]\) and their homogeneous coefficients lift in that ring.

### Filtered equation module, exact defect, and degree bound

The sole relation generator of the regular-sequence presentation
of \(J\) has coefficients \((-g,f)\), both in \(I\). Therefore
\(D=J/IJ\) is free over \(B\) with basis the classes of \(f,g\).
The conormal map is \(\operatorname{diag}(a,a)\) in this basis and the
basis \(x,y\) of \(I/I^2\). As \(a\) is a nonzerodivisor on \(B\),
its kernel \(F^2D\) is zero by
[R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md).
Consequently \(G=\operatorname{gr}_F D\) is concentrated in degree one.
The identity \(G_n=H_n/T_n\) of R-0030 gives \(T_n=H_n\) for \(n\ge2\)
and \(T_1=0\). Since \(q\) has degree at least three, this is precisely
\[
T=(aX^2,aXY,aY^2,q).
\]
In particular \(h\in IJ\) explicitly lifts \(q\) to an actual
product-ideal equation.

In \(Q=H/S_+H\) the symbols \(aX,aY\) freely generate degree one.
In degree \(m+1\) the class of \(q\) generates, and its annihilator is
exactly \((a)\). In that degree the submodule \((S_+H)_{m+1}\) is
\(aS_{m+1}\). Therefore \(bq\in(S_+H)_{m+1}\) for \(b\in B\) precisely
when \(b\in(a)\), by the coefficient of \(Y^{m+1}\).
The displayed generators of \(H\) exhaust the degrees of \(Q\).
By the comparison exact sequence the class of \(q\) generates the
entire defect, a module \(B/(t^r)\) in degree \(m+1\). The argument
for \(D,T,Q,\Delta\) holds over \(B_0\) as well.
The degree bound \(\Delta_1=\Delta_2=0\) in
[R-0065](../results/normal_koszul_comparison_defect.md) applies to the
complete Noetherian \(I\)-adic setting with regular normal parameters.
Thus taking \(m=2\) attains the smallest possible degree in this
relative setting. The bound for a transverse parameter ideal over a
field in R-0065 is four.

### Actual closed algebra and specialization

Let \(J_0=(x^2+y^m,xy+y^m+x^{m+1})\) be the actual closed ideal in
\(k[[x,y]]\). In its quotient, \(x^2=-y^m\).
If \(m+1=2s\), then \(s\ge2\) and the second equation becomes
\(xy=-y^m(1+(-1)^s y^{m(s-1)})\).
If \(m+1=2s+1\), then \(s\ge1\) and it becomes
\[
xy(1+(-1)^s y^{ms-1})=-y^m .
\]
The parenthesized coefficient in this last formula is a unit.
In both cases \(xy=-y^m v(y)\) with \(v(0)=1\).
Associativity gives \(y(x^2)=-y^{m+1}\) and
\(x(xy)=y^{2m-1}v(y)^2\), so
\[
y^{m+1}(1+y^{m-2}v(y)^2)=0 .
\]
The second factor has constant term one for \(m>2\) and two for \(m=2\).
It is a unit in either case. We obtain \(y^{m+1}=0\), \(xy=-y^m\), and
\(x^3=-xy^m=0\). This gives the spanning set \(1,x,y,\ldots,y^m\).

For independence take a vector space with basis \(e_0,e_x,e_1,\ldots,e_m\).
Define multiplication operators \(L_x,L_y\) by
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & L_x & L_y \\ \hline
e_0 & e_x & e_1 \\
e_x & -e_m & -e_m \\
e_1 & -e_m & e_2 \\
e_j\ (2\le j<m) & 0 & e_{j+1} \\
e_m & 0 & 0 .
\end{array}
\]
Both compositions give \(-e_m\) on \(e_0\) and zero on all other
basis vectors, so the operators commute. They are nilpotent with a
cyclic vector \(e_0\), giving a \(k[[x,y]]\)-action. The two equations
of \(J_0\) annihilate \(e_0\) since \(L_x^2 e_0=L_xL_y e_0=-e_m\),
\(L_y^m e_0=e_m\), and \(L_x^{m+1}=0\). Hence evaluation at \(e_0\)
factors through the closed quotient and takes the proposed spanning
vectors to the chosen basis. The actual closed algebra has length
\(m+2\), and the two equations are a regular sequence because the
ideal is primary to the maximal ideal in the regular local surface.

For \(m\ge3\), the full actual closed initial ideal contains
\((X^2,XY,Y^{m+1})\) by the closed equations and the vanishing of
\(y^{m+1}\). The candidate quotient has length \(m+2\), equal to the
length of the actual associated graded since the actual closed
normal filtration is finite. Thus this inclusion is equality.
For \(m=2\) the quadratic symbols are \(X^2+Y^2,XY+Y^2\).
The second quadratic is \(Y(X+Y)\). Evaluating the first at \(Y=0\)
and at \(X=-Y\) gives \(X^2\) and \(2Y^2\), so the quadratics are coprime. They form a
regular sequence, and the quotient by them has length four by its
Hilbert series \((1+z)^2\). This again equals the length of the
actual associated graded and proves fullness.

In each case the two quadratic symbols are linearly independent, so the
closed actual equation module has both classes in degree two and
\(F^3=0\). Indeed a linear combination of the two closed equations
has quadratic symbol given by the constant terms of its multipliers,
and vanishing of the symbol forces both constants to be zero.
R-0030 then gives one defect class in degree \(m+1\) for \(m\ge3\),
represented by \(Y^{m+1}\), and zero defect for \(m=2\).
Equivalently, the full actual initial ideal of \((x,y)J_0\) is
\((X^3,X^2Y,XY^2,Y^{m+1})\) for \(m\ge3\) and
\((X,Y)(X^2+Y^2,XY+Y^2)\) for \(m=2\).

Coefficient specialization sends each of \(H,T\) to the image
\((q)\subset k[X,Y]\). The quotient by this image surjects onto
\(k[X,Y]/(Y)\) and has infinite length. Independently
\(\Delta\otimes_B k=k\) in degree \(m+1\). The specialization images of
\(H,T\) lie in the corresponding actual closed initial ideals because a
degree-\(n\) equation specializing from \(I^n\) gives the specialized
degree-\(n\) symbol, which can be zero. This induces the map from the
tensor of the relative defect to the actual closed defect. For \(m\ge3\)
the map sends the class of \(q\) to the class of \(-Y^{m+1}\),
since \(XY^m\) is a positive-degree multiple of \(XY\).
For \(m=2\) the actual closed defect is zero, while the tensor
of the relative defect is one-dimensional in degree three.

### Finite flat algebra and genuine residual support

Put \(M=R/J\). The finite closed algebra makes \(t\) a parameter
on the one-dimensional complete intersection \(M\).
The maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m_M\) has a power contained in \(tM\),
so the maximal-ideal and \(t\)-adic topologies on \(M\) are
equivalent. The ring \(M\) is complete for them. To express an element of \(M\) in the lifts of the closed basis,
subtract a \(k\)-linear combination of them modulo \(t\). The remainder
has the form \(t u_1\) for some \(u_1\in M\). Apply the same step to
\(u_1\) and iterate. The convergent sums of
coefficients belong to \(B\). This proves finiteness. As \(M\) is
Cohen–Macaulay, the parameter \(t\) is a nonzerodivisor. Finiteness
and torsion-freeness over the DVR give freeness of rank \(m+2\),
the dimension of the actual closed algebra.

Write \(K=k((t))\) and \(M_K=M\otimes_B K\). In the full finite algebra
\(M_K\) the augmentation to \(K\) has kernel \(\mathfrak p=(x,y)M_K\).
In the local factor \((M_K)_{\mathfrak p}\) the two equations give
\(ax,ay\in\mathfrak p^2(M_K)_{\mathfrak p}\). Since \(a\) is a unit
over \(K\), Nakayama's lemma gives \(\mathfrak p(M_K)_{\mathfrak p}=0\).
The marked factor is therefore \(K\). By the product decomposition of
finite Artinian algebras, the remaining factors have total
\(K\)-dimension \(m+1\). Their primes contract to primes of \(M\)
avoiding \(t\) and distinct from the marked prime \(I/J\). Finiteness
over the DVR makes these the generic points of residual components
dominating the base. This computes their total generic contribution
directly from the actual intersection.

Completing the ambient local ring at the marked generic point gives
\(K[[x,y]]\), since the quotients modulo \((x,y)^n\) are the truncated
polynomial rings over \(K\) for every \(n\). In this ring the two equations generate \((x,y)\) by the
same linear-term argument, hence the completed marked transverse
quotient is \(K\). The full generic base change \(M_K\) also contains
the residual factors. The relative normal algebra after inverting
\(t\) is \(K[X,Y]/(X,Y)\), as the full relative initial ideal gives
\(H\otimes_B K=(X,Y)\). It records the length of the marked transverse
quotient. The dimension of the residual algebra comes from the finite
free actual algebra and its decomposition.

## A supported quadruple: torsion in Q is not enough

This proves [R-0069](../results/supported_quadruple_equation_filtration.md)
using R-0030. Take \(B=k[[t]]\) over any field, \(R=B[[x,y]]\), \(I=(x,y)\),
\(f=x^2\), \(g=y^2+tx\), and \(J=(f,g)\).
First divide by the monic relation \(x^2=0\) and then by the monic relation
\(y^2+tx=0\). The polynomial quotient over \(B\) is free on \(1,x,y,xy\).
Every monomial of normal degree at least four vanishes there, since
\(x^2=0\), \(y^4=t^2x^2=0\), and \(xy^3=-tx^2y=0\). Thus the polynomial
quotient already equals its normal completion \(M=R/J\). It follows that
\(\sqrt J=I\), and height two in the regular local ring gives the
regular-sequence assertion. The polynomials \(g,g+f\) are distinct and
primitive of degree one in \(t\) over \(k[x,y]\), since \(x\) is coprime
to each constant coefficient \(y^2,x^2+y^2\). Hence both carriers are
integral. Their terms all have ordinary degree two, so both are singular
at the origin. Their first normal symbols agree and equal \(tX\).

### The normal algebra of the quadruple

With \(K=I/J\), the powers in the free actual algebra are
\[
K=Bx\oplus By\oplus Bxy,\qquad
K^2=Btx\oplus Bxy,\qquad K^3=Btxy,\qquad K^4=0 .
\]
The lifts \(g,f,xg-tf,(y^2-tx)g+t^2f\) have respective initial symbols
\(tX,X^2,XY^2,Y^4\). In degree one the quotient by these symbols has
pieces \((B/(t))X\) and \(BY\), and in degree two it has pieces
\((B/(t))XY\) and \(BY^2\). The image of \(Y^2\) in \(K^2/K^3\) is
\(-tx\), which freely generates its summand. The degree-three quotient
is freely generated by \(Y^3\), whose image is \(-txy\) in \(K^3\).
Degree zero is \(B\), and degrees at least four vanish. The power
calculation therefore identifies the candidate quotient with
\(\operatorname{gr}_K M\) in every degree, proving fullness of \(H\).

### The equation filtration of the quadruple

Use the actual algebra \(N=R/IJ\). The sequence
\(0\to D\to N\to M\to0\) has \(D=J/IJ\) free on \(e_f,e_g\), by the
regular-sequence syzygy. Thus \(N\) is free on \(1,x,y,xy,e_f,e_g\).
Replacing \(e_f,e_g\) by \(x^2,y^2\) still gives a basis because
\(e_g=y^2+tx\). In \(N\),
\[
x^3=x^2y=0,\qquad xy^2=-t x^2,\qquad y^3=-txy,\qquad y^4=t^2x^2.
\]
These relations give all the powers of the image of \(I\) from degree two
onward:
\[
I^2N=Bx^2\oplus Bxy\oplus By^2,\qquad
I^3N=tBx^2\oplus tBxy,\qquad I^4N=t^2Bx^2,\qquad I^5N=0.
\]
Here \(F^nD=D\cap I^nN\). For \(n\ge2\), a vector \(u e_f+v e_g\) in
the intersection has zero \(x\)-coefficient, so \(tv=0\) and \(v=0\).
The displayed powers therefore give \(F^2D=Be_f\), \(F^3D=tBe_f\),
\(F^4D=t^2Be_f\) and \(F^5D=0\).

In \(Q\), \(tX\) freely generates degree one. In degree two the class of
\(X^2\) is killed exactly by \((t)\), from multiples of \(tX\). In degree
three the class of \(XY^2\) is also killed exactly by \((t)\), since the
remaining lower generator \(X^2\) has no monomial multiple proportional to
\(XY^2\). In degree four \(Y^4\) is free because the lower symbols are all
divisible by \(X\). The four lifts above map to classes represented by
\(e_g,e_f,-t e_f,t^2e_f\) in the corresponding filtered quotients. These
are isomorphisms in every degree. In particular the torsion in degrees
two and three survives in \(G\). The R-0030 exact comparison gives the
full \(T=S_+H\) and \(\Delta=0\). Its DVR formula subtracts length one
from length one in each of degrees two and three.

### Actual fibers of the quadruple

Over \(k((t))\), the equation \(y^2+tx=0\) eliminates \(x\) and \(x^2=0\)
becomes \(y^4=0\). At \(t=0\), the actual pair becomes the homogeneous
regular sequence \(x^2,y^2\). Both \(J_0\) and \((x,y)J_0\) are generated
by homogeneous normal forms, so their full initial ideals are
\((X^2,Y^2)\) and \((X,Y)(X^2,Y^2)\). The actual closed defect and the
specialization of the relative defect are both zero. The coefficient image
of \(H\) at \(t=0\) is \((X^2,XY^2,Y^4)\), and that of \(T\) is its product
with \((X,Y)\). The quotient by the image of \(H\) has basis
\(1,X,Y,XY,Y^2,Y^3\), while the actual closed normal quotient has basis
\(1,X,Y,XY\).

## Conclusion

The conormal entry ideal controls the coefficient exponents of
\(\Delta\), with equality of ideals in the cubic sharpness family.
The characteristic-zero punctual family realizes arbitrary normal
degree at least three and arbitrary positive DVR length. Its actual
finite generic algebra contains the marked factor and a residual
algebra of dimension \(m+1\). In the supported quadruple the full
comparison retains both torsion pieces, and the normal algebra
still changes under transverse specialization.
