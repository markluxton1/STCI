# Endpoint delta invariants of transverse quartic arrangements

**Status:** self-contained calculation; new bridge between Chow-arrangement parameters, the Hilbert polynomial, and the two marked endpoints. This is not an STCI existence or exclusion theorem. Field \(k\) is algebraically closed, of arbitrary characteristic; all configurations in the statements below have **distinct geometric points**.

## Coordinates and scope

Put \(T=(\mathbf G_m)^2\). For \(z=(u,v)\in T\), let
\[
 C_z=\{[s^4:s^3t:u st^3:v t^4]\}\subset\mathbf P^3.
\]
For a finite *reduced* configuration \(Z=\{z_1,\dots,z_m\}\subset T\), let \(A_Z=\bigcup_i C_{z_i}\) have the reduced union scheme structure. Distinct curves meet only at \(p_0=[1:0:0:0]\) and \(p_\infty=[0:0:0:1]\). (The parameter \(t/s\) is \(r\) near \(p_0\).)

The following calculation concerns the **reduced union**, not a complete intersection with multiplicities, a Hilbert-flat collision, or the full Chow cycle. It complements the existing [coefficient evaluation for arbitrary containing surfaces](fixed_surface_restriction_lifts.md), the [cycle classification R-0074](../../results/chow_polytope_rigidity.md), and the [fixed-carrier realization locus](fixed_carrier_chow_arrangements.md).

## The exact two-endpoint rank formula

For \(n\ge0\) define a subspace of \(k^m\)
\[
 W_n(Z)=
 \operatorname{span}_k\big\{(u_i^a v_i^b)_{i=1}^m:
              a,b\ge0,\ 3a+4b\le n\big\}.
\]
Define the **opposite-endpoint torus parameters**
\[
 \alpha_i=\frac{v_i^2}{u_i^3},\qquad
 \beta_i=\frac{v_i^3}{u_i^4},\qquad
 Z^\vee=\{(\alpha_i,\beta_i)\}_{i=1}^m
\]
and likewise \(W_n(Z^\vee)\). The matrix
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-3&2\\-4&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
has determinant \(-1\), so \(Z\mapsto Z^\vee\) is a torus automorphism and preserves distinctness.

**Proposition (exact endpoint defect and Hilbert polynomial).** Set
\[
 \delta_0(Z)=\sum_{n\ge0}(m-\dim W_n(Z)),\qquad
 \delta_\infty(Z)=\sum_{n\ge0}(m-\dim W_n(Z^\vee)).
 \tag{1}
\]
These sums are finite. They equal the lengths of the normalization cokernels of \(A_Z\) at the corresponding endpoints:
\[
 \delta_p(Z)=
 \operatorname{length}\bigl(
 (\nu_*\mathcal O_{\coprod_i C_{z_i}}/\mathcal O_{A_Z})_p\bigr).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{P_{A_Z}(d)=4m\,d+m-\delta_0(Z)-\delta_\infty(Z).}
 \tag{2}
\]
The endpoint-rank filtration is a concrete stratification of the reduced-configuration space by Hilbert polynomial. It is **not** the same as a stratification by the Chow cycle.

**Proof.** In the chart \(x_0=1\), with \(r=x_1\), branch \(i\) is
\[
 (x_1,x_2,x_3)=(r,u_i r^3,v_i r^4).
\]
The completed normalization of the reduced local ring at \(p_0\) is
\(\prod_i k[[r]]\). The image of \(k[[x_1,x_2,x_3]]\) under the restriction map to these branches is the \(k[[r]]\)-submodule generated by the vectors
\[
 (u_i^a v_i^b)_i\,r^{3a+4b}.
\]
At coefficient order \(n\), its image is precisely \(W_n(Z)\): multiplication by \(r^{n-3a-4b}\) supplies every allowed monomial, and collecting coefficients cannot supply any others. Equivalently, the image is \(\prod_{n\ge0}r^n W_n(Z)\) inside the product of power-series rings. Thus the finite normalization-cokernel length at \(p_0\) is the first sum in (1).

In the chart \(x_3=1\), take the common uniformizer \(w=x_2/x_3\) on every branch. Writing \(s/t=z\) gives
\[
 (x_2/x_3,x_1/x_3,x_0/x_3)
     =(w,\alpha_i w^3,\beta_i w^4).
\]
The identical argument gives the second sum in (1). Finiteness follows because evaluation of all Laurent monomials (equivalently polynomial monomials, since every \(u_i,v_i\ne0\)) separates a finite set of distinct points, so \(W_n=k^m\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). The normalization is the disjoint union of \(m\) copies of \(\mathbf P^1\). Hence
\(\chi(\mathcal O_{A_Z})=m-\delta_0-\delta_\infty\), and
\(\deg A_Z=4m\), proving (2). \(\square\)

### Immediate consequences

1. For **two** distinct translates, the intersection length at \(p_0\) is \(3\) unless \(u_1=u_2\), when it is \(4\). At \(p_\infty\) it is \(3\) unless \(\alpha_1=\alpha_2\), when it is \(4\). This follows directly by restricting the two defining normal equations to the other smooth branch. It is also the \(m=2\) instance of (1).
2. Thus **distinct-translate configurations need not have a constant Hilbert polynomial**, even without collisions in \(T\). For general two points, \((\delta_0,\delta_\infty)=(3,3)\) and \(P(d)=8d-4\). If, for example, \(u_1=u_2\) but \(v_1\ne v_2\), the first defect rises to \(4\). The ordinary symmetric power of \(T\), even restricted to distinct points, therefore does **not** universally parametrize a flat family of reduced arrangement schemes. One must stratify or flatten (for example by the appropriate Hilbert functor).
3. For **four general** points, the evaluation vectors
\((1),(u_i),(v_i),(u_i^2)\) are independent, at each endpoint (using the automorphism \(Z\mapsto Z^\vee\)). The sequence of ranks at orders \(0,1,2,3,4,5,6\) is \(1,1,1,2,3,3,4\). Thus \(\delta_0=\delta_\infty=13\), giving
\[
 \boxed{P_{A_Z}(d)=16d-22}
\]
on a dense open subset of \(\operatorname{Sym}^4 T\). This differs from the \((4,4)\)-complete-intersection polynomial \(16d-32\).

## Why realized reduced quartic pairs have larger endpoint defects

Consider a proper pair of eigenquartics in the standard weight-\(4\) and weight-\(12\) eigenspaces:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F&=a x_1^4+b x_0^2x_1x_2+c x_0^3x_3,\\
 G&=d x_2^4+e x_1x_2x_3^2+f x_0x_3^3,
 \end{aligned}
 \qquad acdf\ne0.
\]
On the quotient \(T\), their equations are
\[
 a+bu+cv=0,\qquad d u^4+euv^2+fv^3=0.
\]
Suppose this quotient intersection consists of **four distinct points**. The first equation gives \(v=A+Bu\), with \(A=-a/c\ne0\); distinct points therefore have distinct \(u\)-coordinates. The second, after dividing by \(u^4\), gives
\[
 d+e\alpha+f\beta=0,\qquad
 \alpha=v^2/u^3,\quad \beta=v^3/u^4.
\]
Because \(f\ne0\) and \(Z^\vee\) is distinct, the four \(\alpha\)-coordinates are also distinct.

At \(p_0\), \(v\) is affine-linear in \(u\), so the rank filtration increases by exactly one at weights \(0,3,6,9\), reaching dimension four at \(n=9\). There are three successive blocks of three orders with defects \(3,2,1\):
\[
 \delta_0=3(3+2+1)=18.
\]
At \(p_\infty\), \(\beta\) is affine-linear in \(\alpha\); the same argument gives \(\delta_\infty=18\). Therefore
\[
 P_{A_Z}(d)=16d+4-18-18=16d-32.
 \tag{3}
\]
This recovers the **correct Hilbert polynomial** of a reduced \((4,4)\) complete intersection directly from the two endpoint rank filtrations. The existing [realization theorem](../equations/chow_polytope_rigidity.md#quartics-including-invariant-collision-lifts) supplies the properness and absence-of-boundary assertions: equation (3) by itself is only a necessary numerical check.

In particular the root-of-unity example
\(F=x_1^4-x_0^3x_3\),
\(G=x_2^4-x_0x_3^3\)
in characteristic prime to four has four distinct quotient points \((u,1)\) with \(u^4=1\), and both defects equal 18. In characteristic two the quotient is nonreduced, so the reduced-arrangement formula does **not** give the \((4,4)\) CI scheme's Hilbert polynomial.

## Reduced-complete-intersection numerical gate in all bidegrees

If the reduced union \(A_Z\) is itself a proper scheme-theoretic complete intersection of degrees \((a,b)\), then \(ab=4m\), and comparison of (2) with the standard complete-intersection polynomial
\[
 P_{\mathrm{CI}}(n)=ab\,n+\frac{ab(4-a-b)}2
\]
forces
\[
 \boxed{\delta_0(Z)+\delta_\infty(Z)=m\bigl(2(a+b)-7\bigr).}\tag{4}
\]
It is **necessary**, not sufficient: the two actual projective equations must still exist and intersect properly. For a merely set-theoretic complete intersection with thick generic fibers, (4) is not a valid constraint on the reduced union.

As a second check, in characteristic different from three consider the known proper \((3,4)\) pair
\[
 G=x_1^3-cx_0^2x_2,\qquad A=x_2^4-x_0x_3^3,\quad c\in k^\times.
\]
It realizes the three translates \(C_{u,v}\) with \(u=c^{-1}\) and \(v^3=u^4\). At \(p_0\), all three \(u\)-coordinates agree, and the distinct \(v\)'s give rank jumps only at orders \(0,4,8\), so \(\delta_0=12\). At the opposite endpoint the parameters \((\alpha,\beta)=(v^2/u^3,v^3/u^4)\) have constant \(\beta=1\) and three distinct \(\alpha\)'s, so the jumps occur at \(0,3,6\), giving \(\delta_\infty=9\). The sum \(21=3(2(3+4)-7)\) agrees with (4), and the resulting polynomial \(12n-18\) is exactly the \((3,4)\) CI polynomial. This exhibits how the two endpoints can have different defects even for a completely realized eigenform pair.

## Implications for the proposed quotient/blow-up program

- The numbers \(3,4\) in the endpoint evaluation weights are exactly the intrinsic endpoint exponents occurring in the [normal-frame comparison](../../frameworks/torus-supported-boundaries/torus_actions_and_endpoint_frames.md); the rank formula turns them into Hilbert-polynomial constraints.
- On each constant-rank stratum of \(\operatorname{Sym}^mT\), the formula provides a necessary test for equality to the CI polynomial \(ab\,d+ab(4-a-b)/2\), **only** when the desired CI scheme is the reduced union with \(ab=4m\). For set-theoretic realization or nonreduced cycles the embedded ideal and generic lengths remain indispensable.
- Endpoint ranks do not follow from the Chow polytope alone. They depend on the actual quotient points \(Z\), including the opposite-endpoint monomial transform \(Z^\vee\). They supply information absent from the cycle-level invariant.
- The exact filtration suggests a controlled **flattening stratification** before forming relative blow-ups. A universal reduced-union blow-up over the full symmetric power must not be assumed flat.

## Existing theory / boundaries

The normalization sequence, arithmetic-genus formula and flatness/Hilbert-polynomial principle are standard. The explicit rank-filtration evaluation is an elementary consequence of the chosen monomial parametrizations, not a new general algebraic-geometric theorem. This calculation does **not** establish a new degree-independent obstruction, nor does it extend the fixed-carrier mate theorem to arbitrary equations. For proof coverage and actual-pair criteria see [R-0074](../../results/chow_polytope_rigidity.md), [actual translate restriction](fixed_surface_restriction_lifts.md), and [the \(V_5\) section dictionary](../residuals/blowup_residual_geometry.md).
