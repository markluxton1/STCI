# Chow-matched pairs, canonical equation sources, and hidden parameter layers

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero unless a
statement explicitly needs less. Fix
\(C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3\),
\(S=k[x,y,z,w]\), intrinsic weights \((0,1,3,4)\),
\(P=(x,w)\), and the disjoint line \(L=V_+(P)\).
A pair is **proper** if its two homogeneous equations form a regular sequence.
A **Chow match** means literal full-polytope equality
\(P_{[Y]}=mP_C\), not sole support on C. The
[cycle-level rigidity theorem](../../results/chow_polytope_rigidity.md)
allows several torus translates of C, with their actual multiplicities.

The main conclusions are source-sensitive. The first missed-line length of
an arbitrary displayed pair is not an invariant of its ideal. For unequal
minimal generator degrees there is, however, a canonical end-adapted
first-layer comparison; nonadaptation shifts all line-supported layers by
an exact weight defect. These statements do not supply a bound excluding
all Chow-matched pairs or recover raw equation relations from endpoint
conductor data. The remaining comparison must retain the actual filtered
homogeneous equation spaces at **both** weight ends.

## Chow support and the two different pairs of ends

Every reduced translate in a Chow-matched proper intersection is invariant
under the intrinsic torus and disjoint from L. A complete intersection is
pure, so the entire scheme has this support. Over the punctured degeneration
base its reduced support is a fixed union Z of translates. If I is its
ideal, a power of the fixed ideal of Z is contained in I; contraction to
the full flat closure preserves this inclusion. Conversely contraction is
contained in the fixed ideal of Z. Thus the closure has support exactly
Z times the base, and **both full flat limits miss L**, including embedded
points. This does not say their embedded structures are unchanged.

The two weight orientations are separate degenerations. Within **each**
degeneration, the two endpoint charts are \(x\ne0\) and \(w\ne0\).
Do not identify these two distinctions or add the opposite residual lower
bounds. Each full flat limit preserves the complete-intersection polynomial
\[
 P_{a,b}(n)=abn-\frac{ab(a+b-4)}2,
\]
not merely its leading degree or Chow cycle.

## Canonical extreme equation sources

Let \(J=(F,G)\subset S\) be a proper homogeneous complete-intersection ideal
of minimal generator degrees \(a<b\). Then \(J_a=kF\), and
\(J_b/(F S_{b-a})\) is one-dimensional. Fix one weight orientation. Write
\(\operatorname{in}\) for its lowest-weight initial form (reverse the
weights for the other orientation).

**End-adaptation.** Replace G by \(G+FH\), with \(H\in S_{b-a}\), until
its initial form is not divisible by \(\operatorname{in}F\). Whenever
it is divisible, its quotient is an ordinary-homogeneous, weight-homogeneous
polynomial and subtracting its product with F strictly raises the minimum
weight. There are finitely many weights in degree b, so this process stops.
It cannot end in zero, since G is a minimal generator.

The resulting extremal weight \(\beta\) and initial class in
\(S_b/(\operatorname{in}F)S_{b-a}\) are canonical up to nonzero scalar.
Indeed
\[
 \operatorname{in}(F S_{b-a})=(\operatorname{in}F)S_{b-a}
\]
as associated graded subspaces, by multiplicativity of initial forms.
If two adapted representatives had different minimum weights, their
subtraction would express the smaller initial form as a multiple of
\(\operatorname{in}F\), a contradiction. If their weights agree, their
initial forms differ by such a multiple. Consequently the ideal
\[
 J_{\rm ext}=(\operatorname{in}F,\operatorname{in}G_{\rm ad})
\]
is canonical, as are its gcd and the ideal generated by the two forms
with that gcd removed. Its generic L-length e is therefore canonical.
Adding a multiple of the first form does not change that stripped ideal.

For equal degrees use the whole two-dimensional filtered space
\(W=J_a\): the ideal generated by \(\operatorname{gr}W\) is canonical,
and a weight-adapted basis realizes it. For two adapted bases, their normalized transition matrix is polynomial in the parameter and has nonzero constant determinant, since both bases have the same multiset of weights. Thus the optimized raw ideal is canonical here too. Both endpoint filtrations of a two-dimensional space admit one common adapted basis: each has at most one nontrivial flag line. The unequal-degree no-common-representative example below concerns polynomial, not constant, row operations.

The full initial ideal \(\operatorname{in}J\) is different from
\(J_{\rm ext}\): cancellations among polynomial multiples can produce
further initial equations. For an end-adapted presentation,
[R-0073](../../results/endpoint_equation_torsion.md#scope) gives the canonical
first layer
\[
 M_{1,\rm ad}\simeq\operatorname{in}J/J_{\rm ext}.
\]
This is the ordinary graded module **before irrelevant saturation**.
Projectivize or saturate when interpreting geometric support; possible
irrelevant torsion is not a projective component. The line part is
\(H^0_P(M_{1,\rm ad})\). On the Chow-matched locus its generic length
is e, since the full flat limit misses L. The optimized raw ideal, and hence all its parameter layers, are also canonical: the normalized row-operation argument below proves this before parameter saturation. Arbitrary displayed presentations need not have this same raw ideal.

### Opposite adaptation need not have a common representative

Put \(U=y^4-x^3w\) and \(V=z^4-xw^3\), of weights 4 and 12.
For any \(k\ge2\), the pair \(F=U+V,G=U^k\) is proper, of degrees
\((4,4k)\), with radical \(\sqrt{(U,V)}\). It is Chow-matched: the
reduced binomial intersection is the four translated quartics.
Modulo F, G can also be represented by \((-V)^k\).
At the low end this representative has minimum weight \(12k\), and its
leader is not divisible by \(\operatorname{in}_{\rm low}F=U\), so it is
adapted. At the high end \(U^k\) has maximum weight \(4k\), with leader
not divisible by \(\operatorname{in}_{\rm high}F=V\), so it is adapted.
Canonicity proves these are the optimal two weights. No nonzero polynomial
has minimum weight \(12k\) and maximum weight \(4k\) simultaneously.
Thus use the **same ideal and its filtered source spaces** at both ends,
not a hypothetical single representative adapted to both.

## Exact shift of the line-supported layers

Fix the unequal-degree situation and one orientation. Let G be any
representative of the second generator class, with minimum weight
\(\gamma\), and let \(G_{\rm ad}=G+FH\) have adapted minimum weight
\(\beta\). Put \(d=\beta-\gamma\ge0\). If f, g and
\(g_{\rm ad}\) are the separately parameter-normalized orbit equations,
then modulo f their relation is
\[
 g=r^d g_{\rm ad}.
\]
When d=0 this gives the same raw ideal for every adapted representative, so the optimized raw ideal and its filtered parameter torsion are canonical. The multiplier removed with f has no negative parameter powers: when
\(d>0\) its minimum weight is exactly \(\gamma\); when \(d=0\) it is
at least \(\gamma\). Work in \(R'=S[r]/(f)\), which is r-torsion-free
because \(f\bmod r\ne0\). Set
\[
 N=(g_{\rm ad})\subset R',\qquad N^{\rm sat}=N:r^\infty.
\]
The raw torsion for the nonadapted and adapted presentations is respectively
\[
 T_d=N^{\rm sat}/r^dN,\qquad T_0=N^{\rm sat}/N.
\]
For the layer convention \(M_j(T)=r^{j-1}T/r^jT\), cancellation of
powers of r in R' gives
\[
 M_j(T_d)\simeq
 \begin{cases}
 N^{\rm sat}/rN^{\rm sat},&1\le j\le d,\\
 M_{j-d}(T_0),&j>d.
 \end{cases}
\]
Since \(R'/N^{\rm sat}\) is r-torsion-free, the early layer injects
into \(R'/rR'=S/(\operatorname{in}F)\). A hypersurface in S has only
height-one associated primes, none containing the height-two ideal P.
Hence its \(H^0_P\), and that of the early layer, is zero. Therefore
\[
 H^0_P(M_j(T_d))\simeq
 \begin{cases}0,&j\le d,\\
 H^0_P(M_{j-d}(T_0)),&j>d.
 \end{cases}
\]
This is a module identity, not just equality of generic lengths. It is
valid over any field for the stated proper homogeneous pair and weight
filtration, before irrelevant saturation. Nonadaptation delays the hidden
line layers; it does not erase them. In particular the sum of generic line-layer lengths is presentation-independent in this unequal-degree setting; only their parameter indices are shifted. The canonical optimized raw ideal is still distinct from its parameter saturation and its full initial ideal.

## A same-ideal change from first missed length eight to zero

Continue in characteristic zero and set
\[
 D=V^2+x^4U,\quad A=wy^3,\quad
 F=U^3+AD,\quad G=D^3,\quad G'=G+x^{12}F.
\]
Both pairs have the same ideal and degrees \((12,24)\). Their radical is
\(\sqrt{(U,V)}\): G forces D, then F forces U, and D forces V.
Thus they are proper and Chow-matched, with cycle degree 288 and
\(P_{[Y]}=72P_C\). No equality of the separate translate multiplicities
is needed.

At the low end, put \(D_r=x^4U+r^{20}V^2\). The first raw pair is
\[
 f=AD_r+rU^3,\qquad g=D_r^3.
\]
The relation \((f-AD_r)^3=r^3U^9\) vanishes modulo (f,g). At the generic
line U is a unit, so the parameter-saturated local family is empty there.
Its extreme forms have gcd \(x^4U\); the stripped ideal at L is
\((w,x^8)\), of length eight. The second leader is not divisible by
\(f_0=Ax^4U\), so this is already an adapted presentation. R-0073 gives
\(e_+=\kappa_+=8\).

For the sheared mate the raw equation is
\[
 g'=x^{12}f+rD_r^3.
\]
Its minimum weight is 11 rather than 12. Its leader is a multiple of
\(f_0\), so after gcd stripping one form is a unit and
\(e'_+=\kappa'_+=0\). The adaptation defect is \(d=1\): the preceding
theorem moves the old length-eight first line layer to **layer two**.
A local replay sees the next layer as well. Complete at the generic line over \(k(y,z)\). The equation f has unit r-derivative and solves \(r=wx^4\cdot\text{unit}\); then \(D_r=x^4\cdot\text{unit}\). In the resulting regular ring \(D_0=k(y,z)[[x,w]]\), the adapted raw quotient is \(D_0/(x^{12})\). Its successive layers are \(D_0/(x^{12},wx^4)\), \(D_0/(x^8,wx^4)\), and \(D_0/(x^4)\), with line-torsion lengths \((8,4,0)\). The sheared quotient is \(D_0/(wx^{16})\), giving \((0,8,4,0)\) by the shift theorem. At the high end the maxima are \(AV^2,V^6\), of weights 31 and 72.
The shear has weight at most 31. The gcd is \(V^2\), and \(V^4\) is a
unit at L; both high-end first missed lengths are zero.

Consequently the numerical pair of raw first missed lengths changes from
\((8,0)\) to \((0,0)\), while the ideal, equation degrees, whole moving
embedded family and both flat limits are unchanged. This refutes a formula
for **raw** first missed length using only those intrinsic data. It does
not refute a relation involving canonical adapted sources or all layers.

## The global Cech kernel and the scope of Kummer comparisons

In either orientation use the R-0073 notation
\(E=S[r]/(f,g)\), \(T=E[r^\infty]\), \(B=E/T\).
The exact sequence \(0\to rT\to T\to T/rT\to0\), and its proved
\(H^0_P(T)=0\), give
\[
 H^0_P(T/rT)\simeq
 \ker\bigl(H^1_P(rT)\longrightarrow H^1_P(T)\bigr).
\]
Concretely a global torsion class can be r-divisible on each of the x and
w charts without being an element of rT globally. This is a Cech extension condition
for the actual raw equation module, not merely a local defect of the
parameter-saturated family.

For the natural endpoint comparison, adjoin the chosen half-parameter
\(w_2\) with \(w_2^2=xw\), a degree-one section of torus weight two,
on the finite double cover of projective space. On each reduced translated
quartic choose the sheet prescribed by its parameter; off its endpoints
it lifts uniquely over nilpotents by etaleness. Take the selected finite
algebra on the generic family and its r-flat closure, with pushforward
\(\widetilde B\). The map \(B\to\widetilde B\) is injective, since it
is generically injective and B is r-flat. Its quotient Q is supported on
the two geometric endpoint sections, disjoint from L. This assertion uses
the fixed reduced support of the Chow-matched family, not an arbitrary
moving family.

Since both algebras are r-flat, reduction gives
\(Q[r]\hookrightarrow B/rB\) and
\[
 0\longrightarrow T/rT\longrightarrow
 \ker(E/rE\longrightarrow\widetilde B/r\widetilde B)
 \longrightarrow Q[r]\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here the exact sequence and support comparison are interpreted as projective sheaves, or localized at the generic point of L; no claim about possible irrelevant cone torsion is inferred from sheaf support. The line-supported sheaf is unchanged: Q[r] has no support at L.
Thus this direct endpoint-local or saturated Kummer comparison does not
recover the global line cancellation. It does not exclude a theorem which
uses the same global filtered equation sources in addition to the endpoint
algebra. The canonical source/layer refinement above is precisely such
extra information.

In the explicit shear example the selected missing weight-two class is
itself nonzero, and unchanged. At the zero endpoint reduce to the four
branches of (U,V), parameterized by \(y=\zeta t,z=t^3,x=1,w=t^4\).
Over \(k[[u]]\), \(u=t^4\), the reduced algebra has basis
\(y^iz^j=\zeta^i t^{i+3j}\), \(0\le i,j<4\).
In the trivial Fourier character the exponents are 0,3,6,9; in residue
class two modulo four the first is 6, not 2. Thus \(t^2\) is absent,
although \(ut^2=t^6\) is present. Failure to descend after reduction
implies failure before reduction. Both presentations have the identical
nonzero endpoint defect, despite their different raw first line lengths.

## A canonical leading relation, and why its generic line class is blind

Use the ordinary Poisson biderivation
\[
 \{F,G\}=F_yG_z-F_zG_y-2(F_xG_w-F_wG_x),
\]
of ordinary degree -2 and intrinsic weight -4. The
[contact-Jacobian condition](contact_jacobians_and_hamiltonian_actions.md)
for a sole-supported pair gives \(\{F,G\}\in J\). The same condition
is unchanged by homogeneous generator row operations. At an adapted weight
end, its extreme coefficient is \(\{f_0,g_0\}\), possibly zero; hence
its class modulo \(J_{\rm ext}\) belongs to the canonical module
\(\operatorname{in}J/J_{\rm ext}\). Adapted shear changes this class
only by a multiple of the first extreme equation, and scalar generator
changes rescale it. It is a forced cancellation class, not a contradiction.

But this first class cannot count hidden generic-line cancellation. Write
\(f_0=HU,g_0=HV\); modulo their ideal the bracket is
\(H^2\{U,V\}\). If the stripped intersection at L has positive finite
length, its transverse Artin-CI Jacobian is a socle generator in
\(S_P/(U,V)\) in characteristic zero. Tangential derivative terms vanish
modulo (U,V), leaving the nonzero transverse coefficient -2. If \(H\in P\),
the socle is annihilated by H, so \(H\{U,V\}\in(U,V)S_P\), and
\(H^2\{U,V\}\in(HU,HV)S_P\): the leading class is zero at the generic
line. If H is a unit there and e is positive, R-0073 already forces a
residual line, since the two raw special equations form a regular sequence at L, making r a nonzerodivisor in the raw quotient there. Thus extracting only this
leading Poisson coefficient cannot bound the allowed hidden line length;
a higher global filtered relation is needed.

## A fixed-depth center-jet limitation

The center restriction
\[
 H^0(\mathcal I_C(d))\longrightarrow (S/P^N)_d
\]
is onto for every \(N\ge1\) and \(d\ge N+2\). Indeed the standard
forms \(q=xw-yz\), \(c_0=y^3-x^2z\), \(c_2=z^3-yw^2\) belong to
\(I_C\). Their binary reductions generate every degree at least three.
For each P-order \(j<N\), its binary coefficient degree \(d-j\) is
at least three; multiply these generators by the relevant normal monomial
to lift it. Their corrections raise P-order by two, so successive lifting
terminates modulo \(P^N\). This prescribes arbitrary finite center jets,
not Chow matching, prescribed opposite extrema, or a global contact identity.
It rules out an obstruction obtained solely by counting these fixed-depth
center coefficients in sufficiently large degree.

## Opposite full limits can retain a large embedded Hilbert correction

A separate proper Chow-matched control has degrees (16,16). Set
\[
 H=x^{12}+w^{12},\qquad G=V^4+HU,\qquad F=U^4+G.
\]
The radical is \(\sqrt{(U,V)}\); its cycle has multiplicity 16 along
each of the four translates. At the low end the raw leading pair is
\((x^{12}U,x^{12}U)\); at the high end it is
\((w^{12}U,w^{12}U)\). Gcd stripping gives a unit at each end, so this
is not a positive stripped-line example.

The eigenrelations \(U^{4-j}V^{4j}\), \(0\le j\le4\), belong to the
moving saturated ideal: modulo it, \(U^4=0\) and \(V^4=-HU\).
In particular both limits contain \(U^4,V^{16}\), and hence miss L.
At every generic translate their ideals also contain U (the coordinate
factor in the raw leader is a unit) and \(V^{16}\). Each generic length
is at most 16. Preservation of degree 256 forces equality at all four
translates. The pure hull of each limit is consequently the complete
intersection \((U,V^{16})\): the two pure ideals agree at their only
associated curve primes.

For the low family, after dividing parameter powers, the second equation
is \((x^{12}+r^{48}w^{12})U+r^{44}V^4\), together with \(U^4\).
Near its special fiber on \(x\ne0\), the first coefficient is a unit.
After r-saturation these equations give
\(U+r^{44}(x^{12}+r^{48}w^{12})^{-1}V^4\) and \(V^{16}\).
Their specializations form a regular sequence, so this is the flat
closure there, with special ideal \((U,V^{16})\). The analogous reverse
calculation works on \(w\ne0\). Thus the embedded correction is supported
only at the opposite endpoint: \(p_\infty\) in the low limit and
\(p_0\) in the high limit.

The CI polynomial of the moving pair is \(256n-3584\); that of its pure
hull of type (4,64) is \(256n-8192\). The kernel of the quotient to the
pure hull therefore has length **4608** at that endpoint in each limit.
The Chow cycle and Hilbert polynomial are both preserved, with substantial
endpoint embedded structure making up the difference. Replacing F by F-G
changes the raw leading picture, not these full limits.

## Remaining equation-source comparison

For a specified degree pair, retain the weight-filtered spaces J_a and J_b,
the multiplication inclusion \(F S_{b-a}\subset J_b\), and their relation
to the full initial ideal at each orientation. Compare canonical adapted
line layers and their higher relations with global contact or endpoint
constraints on **that same ideal**. The exact shift theorem prevents a
change of generators from masquerading as disappearance of the hidden
relation. It supplies no strict upper bound on canonical missed length.
The proper-pencil/higher-contact and unrestricted STCI questions remain open.
