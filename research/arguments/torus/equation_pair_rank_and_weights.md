# Common factors, equation-pair spaces and torus characters

This chapter retains the usable equation-space and common-factor calculations
from the November 1 VGIT branches. The rank-drop statements apply to two
homogeneous equations in a polynomial ring over any field, including unequal
degrees. The weight specializations fix
\(C=C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3_k\),
\(S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\), \(I=I_C\), and the centered ring weights
\((-2,-1,1,2)\). The rank and character formulas are
characteristic-free; the eigenform residual corollary and the
primitive-triple calculations linked at the end use characteristic zero.

**Review state:** independent reconstruction on 2026-11-19 accepted the written
rank, flag-character and parameter-space proofs, reviewed both new support
implementations and their retained exact-source dependency, and replayed both
successfully. The first-jet replay covers degrees five through eight over
\(\mathbb Q,\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3\); the all-degree and
all-characteristic coverage comes from the written proofs. These statements describe
necessary boundary data and parameter spaces, not an STCI exclusion or a
nonempty supported-pair locus. Existing supported-Hilbert arguments and their
review scope remain in the [owning chapter](../torus_hilbert_boundary.md).

## Common-factor rank drop in arbitrary degrees

Let \(a\le b\), let nonzero \(F\in S_a,G\in S_b\) have gcd
\(D\in S_e\), and write \(F=DA,G=DB\), with \(\gcd(A,B)=1\).
Put \(N_j=\binom{j+3}{3}\) for \(j\ge0\), and \(N_j=0\) otherwise.
The full module of relations on \(F,G\) is generated by
\((B,-A)\), of total degree \(a+b-e\). Indeed
\(uA+vB=0\) in the unique factorization domain \(S\) forces
\(u=HB,v=-HA\). Thus
\[
0\longrightarrow S(-a-b+e)\longrightarrow
 S(-a)\oplus S(-b)\longrightarrow(F,G)\longrightarrow0,
\]
and the **ideal-piece** dimension is
\[
 \dim(F,G)_n=N_{n-a}+N_{n-b}-N_{n-a-b+e}.                 \tag{1}
\]
For a proper type-\((a,b)\) pair, the last index is \(n-a-b\).
Consequently the degreewise multiplication-rank loss at this boundary is
\[
 \Delta_n(e)=N_{n-a-b+e}-N_{n-a-b}.                       \tag{2}
\]
For \(e>0\) it first becomes one in degree \(a+b-e\).
This is a statement about the multiplication image, not the quotient-ring
Hilbert function. In \(k[x_0,\ldots,x_r]\) the same proof works after
replacing \(N_j\) by \(\binom{j+r}{r}\).

Here is precisely what (2) says about a flat limit. Suppose a DVR family
\((F_t,G_t)\) has proper generic pair of degrees \((a,b)\) and special
forms \((F_0,G_0)\) of gcd degree \(e>0\). Contract the generic ideal
inside the polynomial ring over the DVR; its quotient has no parameter
torsion and is flat in each degree. Write \(L_n\) for its special
degree-\(n\) subspace and \(J=L^{\mathrm{sat}}\) for the projective
Hilbert-limit ideal. Then
\[
 (F_0,G_0)_n\subset L_n\subset J_n,\qquad
 \dim L_n-\dim(F_0,G_0)_n=\Delta_n(e).                   \tag{3}
\]
Thus the **graded** limit has no new classes below \(a+b-e\) and a
single projective cancellation line in that degree. If the first
nonzero deformation terms have the common order \(k\), its usual
representative is
\[
 B\widetilde F-A\widetilde G\pmod{(F_0,G_0)}
\]
provided that class is nonzero; it comes from dividing
\(BF_t-AG_t\) by \(t^k\). Without this nonvanishing hypothesis a later
parameter order must be used. Saturation can add further low-degree
classes to \(J\). Formula (3) gives a lower bound for
\(\dim J_n-\dim(F_0,G_0)_n\), with equality for sufficiently large
\(n\); it does not by itself identify the first extra minimal generator
of the saturated ideal.

For sextics (2) gives the first graded cancellation degrees
\(11,10,9,8,7\) for \(e=1,2,3,4,5\). The known
[degree-ten residual generator](../torus_sextic_residuals.md) is the
\(e=2\) case. Neither its success nor a degreewise rank drop certifies
sole support or a full fixed-ideal classification.

## The prime-ideal dichotomy

For any prime homogeneous ideal \(P\), the condition \(DA,DB\in P\)
has the exact alternatives
\[
 D\in P,\qquad\text{or}\qquad D\notin P\text{ and }A,B\in P.       \tag{4}
\]
This follows directly from primality, and applies to unequal degrees.
For \(P=I_C\) and equal sextic forms, with \(1\le e\le5\):

| Boundary type | Possible \(e\) | Remaining pair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carrier factor \(D\in I\) | \(2,3,4,5\) | Coprime forms of degree \(6-e\), not required to contain \(C\) |
| \(D\notin I\) | \(1,2,3\) | A proper containing pencil in \(I_{6-e}\) |

Here \(I_1=0\) and \(\dim I_2=1\), excluding a lower-degree
containing pencil for \(e=4,5\). If the pencil is torus-fixed, its
gcd is an eigenform: the gcd line is intrinsic, so the torus preserves
it. The residual coprime pencil is also fixed. In characteristic zero
the [eigenform obstruction](equivariant_degree_bounds.md) then forces
the second row's proper lower-degree containing pair to have residual
support. A supported Hilbert coordinate above it must remove that
residual support with its additional equations. Equation (4) alone
does not say that such removal exists or is impossible.

The carrier-factor profiles are useful when distinguishing this boundary
from the double-surface subsystem. Listed in consecutive centered weights:

| \(e\) | \(\dim I_e\) | Weights | Multiplicities |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 2 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 3 | 7 | \(-3,\ldots,3\) | \(1,1,1,1,1,1,1\) |
| 4 | 18 | \(-5,\ldots,5\) | \(1,2,1,2,2,2,2,2,1,2,1\) |
| 5 | 35 | \(-7,\ldots,7\) | \(1,2,2,2,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,2,2,2,1\) |

For \(e=2\), \(D=q=x_0x_3-x_1x_2\) has weight zero.
The carrier-factor stratum is
\(q\operatorname{Gr}(2,S_4)_{\mathrm{prop}}\), of dimension 66,
with exterior-square weights \(-15,\ldots,15\). The two extreme
weight spaces of \(S_4\), at \(\pm8\), are one-dimensional, so
\(\pm16\) are absent. This 66-dimensional stratum is not contained
in the 42-dimensional double-sextic Grassmannian below: \(qH\)
vanishes to second order along \(C\) precisely when \(H\in I\).

## Characters of all double-surface equation spaces

Write \(V_d=H^0(\mathcal I_C(d))\) and
\(V_{d,2}=H^0(\mathcal I_C^2(d))\); the latter uses the sheaf
power, equivalently its saturated homogeneous degree piece. Let
\(n_d(w)\) count degree-\(d\) monomials of centered weight \(w\).
For \(d\ge2\), every binary monomial of degree \(4d\) occurs as
a restriction from \(S_d\). A weight block restricts onto its single
binary monomial, so
\[
 \dim V_d[w]=n_d(w)-1\quad(-2d\le w\le2d).              \tag{5}
\]
Outside that interval all spaces are zero. Surjectivity follows already
in degree two (the sums of two of \(0,1,3,4\) cover \(0,\ldots,8\))
and induction on degree. In particular \(H^1(\mathcal I_C(j))=0\)
for \(j\ge2\).

On the unique smooth quadric \(Q=V(q)\simeq\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\),
the curve has class \((1,3)\). The regular-sequence identity
\((q)\cap\mathcal I_C^2=q\mathcal I_C\) gives
\[
0\to\mathcal I_C(d-2)\xrightarrow{q}\mathcal I_C^2(d)
 \to\mathcal O_Q(d-2,d-6)\to0.                         \tag{6}
\]
This is the order-two case of the existing
[all-order flag](../equations/global_equation_jets.md#exact-global-freedom).
For \(d\ge4\) the left-hand \(H^1\) vanishes, so (6) is exact on
global sections. For \(d\ge5\), Künneth also kills the right-hand
\(H^1\), giving \(H^1(\mathcal I_C^2(d))=0\). All these assertions
hold over every characteristic; they do not use a balanced splitting
of the conormal bundle.

For clarity about linearizations, write
\((x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)=(ac,ad,bc,bd)\). In the uncentered action give
\(a,c\) weight zero, \(b\) weight three and \(d\) weight one;
then center degree \(j\) by subtracting \(2j\). In (6), a quotient
section is represented by \(f^2\) times a section of
\(\mathcal O_Q(d-2,d-6)\), where \(f=bc^3-ad^3\) has uncentered
weight three. Thus that quotient's character is
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\sum_{j=0}^{d-6}T^{3i+j+6-2d},        \tag{7}
\]
interpreted as zero for \(d<6\). Multiplication by the centered
weight-zero \(q\) leaves the character of \(V_{d-2}\) unchanged.
Equations (5)–(7) are an all-degree, characteristic-free character
formula for \(V_{d,2}\).

There is a useful equivalent formula for \(d\ge5\). The conormal
flag has line terms of degrees \(-8,-6\) on \(\mathbf P^1\).
With the induced centered linearizations their twisted section
characters are respectively
\(\sum_{|w|\le2d-4}T^w\) and
\(\sum_{|w|\le2d-3}T^w\). This statement concerns the character
of the flag, and remains true when its underlying extension changes
in characteristic two. The vanishing after (6) makes conormal
evaluation surjective. Subtracting these characters from (5) yields
\[
 \dim V_{d,2}[w]=n_d(w)-\mathbf1_{|w|\le2d}
 -\mathbf1_{|w|\le2d-4}-\mathbf1_{|w|\le2d-3}
 =\max(0,n_d(w)-3).                                    \tag{8}
\]
For the last equality, the monomial counts at distance zero, one,
two and three from either extreme weight are \(1,1,1,2\).
Every interior weight \(|w|\le2d-4\) has at least three monomials:
check degree five and multiply by \(x_0\) or \(x_3\) to induct.
This proves (8) independently of a characteristic-zero jet-rank test.

In particular \(V_{4,2}=kq^2\), \(V_{5,2}=qV_3\), and
\(\dim V_{6,2}=23\), with profile
\[
 (1,1,2,2,2,2,3,2,2,2,2,1,1)\quad(w=-6,\ldots,6).
\]
Consequently \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_{6,2})\) has dimension 42,
and \(\bigwedge^2V_{6,2}\) has dimension 253 and all weights
\(-11,\ldots,11\). These are 23 candidate torus walls, with
22 intervening chambers and at most \(23\cdot24/2=276\)
extremal-weight blocks. As in the
[ambient sextic partition](../torus_hilbert_boundary.md#reconciled-chow-filtration-and-finite-sextic-weight-partition),
these count possible numerical blocks, not nonempty supported strata.

## Unequal degrees: quotient by every ambient multiple

For any homogeneous ideal \(I\subset S\) and \(a<b\), multiplication
by a nonzero \(F_a\in I_a\) gives a vector subbundle on
\(\mathbf P(I_a)\):
\[
0\to\mathcal O(-1)\otimes S_{b-a}\to I_b\otimes\mathcal O
 \to E\to0.                                             \tag{9}
\]
Fiberwise injectivity uses only that \(S\) is a domain. Lines in
\(E\) parameterize higher equations modulo \(F_aS_{b-a}\), not
just modulo multiples by elements of \(I\). The proper-pair locus
is open in \(\mathbf P(E)\). This removes exactly the row operation
\(G_b\mapsto cG_b+HF_a\), \(H\in S_{b-a}\), together with
the scalings; it is an equation-space construction, not a calculation
of a GIT quotient or its ample cone. A higher equation divisible by
\(F_a\) represents the zero fiber vector and is absent from this
projective bundle. The more general ordered-form rank formula (1)
also allows that boundary case \(e=a\).

Over a torus-eigenform \(F_a\in I_a[w_a]\), the fiber is graded and
\[
 \dim E_{[F_a]}[w]=\dim I_b[w]-\dim S_{b-a}[w-w_a].        \tag{10}
\]
A general nonfixed base point has no torus representation on its
single fiber. For \(C_0\), the useful frontier specializations are:

| Degrees | Equation space | Dimensions | Centered source weights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \((5,8)\) | \(\mathbf P(E)\to\mathbf P(V_5)\) | Base 34; rank \(E=132-20=112\); total 145 | \(V_5:[-7,7]\), \(V_8:[-13,13]\) |
| \((6,16)\) | \(\mathbf P(E)\to\mathbf P(V_6)\) | Base 58; rank \(E=904-286=618\); total 675 | \(V_6:[-9,9]\), \(V_{16}:[-29,29]\) |
| \((8,8)\) | \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_8)\) | \(\dim V_8=132\); Grassmannian 260 | Exterior-square weights \([-25,25]\), all 51 present |
| \((8,8)\), order two | \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_{8,2})\) | \(\dim V_{8,2}=80\); Grassmannian 156 | Exterior-square weights \([-19,19]\), all 39 present |

The actual supported CI subsets and their supported flat limits lie in
\(H_{10},H_{24},H_{16}\), respectively, by the
[primary-power argument](../torus_hilbert_boundary.md#equationhilbertchow-graph-and-its-top-fiber).
Those containments do not cover the entire equation spaces in this table.
The table organizes the existing degree rows; it neither selects a
linearization on (9) nor solves their supported incidence.

## Support, source disposition and implications

The new standard-library [weight check](equation_pair_rank_and_weights.support/weight_profiles.py)
enumerates monomial characters, exterior-square weights, and the
quotient-fiber dimensions. It also constructs the actual first-jet
matrices for \(V_{d,2}\) over \(\mathbb Q,\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3\)
in degrees five through eight, rather than treating (8) as its own
test. The proof of (1)–(10) supplies arbitrary-degree coverage;
the finite check supplies reproducibility of the tables.
