# The normalized orbit Chow quotient for weights (0,1,3,4)

**Status:** explicit calculation using the established toric Chow-quotient/fiber-polytope theorem; includes a concrete obstruction to identifying the quotient graph with the \(V_5\) blow-up. It describes orbit **cycles**, not Hilbert-flat embedded arrangements or STCI equations. No novelty is claimed for the general Chow-quotient theorem.

## Established external input

Kapranov–Sturmfels–Zelevinsky, *Quotients of toric varieties*, Math. Ann. **290** (1991), 643–655, identify the normalization of a toric Chow quotient with the toric variety of the corresponding fiber-polytope fan. See [KSZ publication](https://www.mis.mpg.de/people/bernd-sturmfels/publications). Billera–Sturmfels' fiber-polytope construction and the GKZ secondary-fan description apply to projections of simplices. Chen–Satriano, [*Chow Quotients of Toric Varieties as Moduli of Stable Log Maps*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.3406), supplies a characteristic-zero moduli interpretation for rank-one subtori. The fan and degree-weighted boundary cycles below are explicit specializations for this particular four-weight configuration.

The [repository's R-0074](../../results/chow_polytope_rigidity.md) is a **different** polytope statement: it concerns the full column-weight Newton polytope of the Chow form of an effective curve *in* \(\mathbf P^3\). The toric quotient fan below concerns the Chow **orbit closure of the one-parameter subgroup action on \(\mathbf P^3\)**. These constructions are related but are not identical polytopes.

## 1. Quotient coordinates

On the dense projective three-torus, let
\[
 r=x_1/x_0,\qquad
 U=\frac{x_0^2x_2}{x_1^3},\qquad
 V=\frac{x_0^3x_3}{x_1^4}.
\]
The one-parameter subgroup \(H\) acts with weights \((0,1,3,4)\);
it changes \(r\) and fixes \(U,V\). Its general orbit closure is
\[
 C_{u,v}=[s^4:s^3t:u st^3:v t^4].
\]
The torus quotient is \(T=(\mathbf G_m)^2\), with section on the dense torus \(r=1\). The rational quotient
\(q:\mathbf P^3\dashrightarrow T\) has indeterminacy at the coordinate boundary; it is **not** a morphism on the whole projective space.

Write \(S=(\mathbf P^3/\!/_{\rm Chow}H)^\nu\), the **normalized** Chow orbit closure. By KSZ, \(S\) is the projective toric surface with the fiber (equivalently, secondary) fan of the ordered configuration \(0,1,3,4\). The following gives this fan explicitly, in the cocharacter lattice of \(T\).

## 2. Explicit complete fan of the Chow quotient

Let \(h=(h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3)\) be a height vector for the four columns with weights \(0,1,3,4\). Modulo adding an affine-linear function of the weight, define
\[
 A=4h_1-3h_0-h_3,\qquad
 B=4h_2-h_0-3h_3.
\]
A generic lower regular subdivision of \([0,4]\) is specified by which of the two interior points belong to the lower hull. There are four possibilities. The *closed* cones in \((A,B)\) coordinates, with their walls included, are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{lower-hull vertices}&\text{inequalities}\\ \hline
0,4&A\ge0,\ B\ge0\\
0,1,4&A\le0,\ B\ge A/3\\
0,3,4&B\le0,\ A\ge B/3\\
0,1,3,4&B\ge3A,\ A\ge3B.
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
For example, \(0,1,4\) occurs precisely when the height at weight \(1\) is below the endpoints' chord and the height at \(3\) is above the line segment from \(1\) to \(4\). This proves (1) directly; the other cases follow by reversing the four weights or comparing consecutive lower-hull slopes.

The integral cocharacter coordinates dual to the quotient characters \(U,V\) are
\[
 (p,q)=(h_2-3h_1+2h_0,\ h_3-4h_1+3h_0).
\]
They give an integral basis on the quotient lattice
\(\mathbf Z^4/(\mathbf Z(1,1,1,1)+\mathbf Z(0,1,3,4))\).
In particular
\[
 p=(B-3A)/4,\qquad q=-A.
\]
The *primitive rays* of the complete two-dimensional fan are, in cyclic order,
\[
 \boxed{(1,0),\quad(2,3),\quad(0,1),\quad(-3,-4).} \tag{2}
\]
The four maximal cones are generated by consecutive rays. Under (1), respectively:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{lower-hull vertices}&\text{maximal cone in }(p,q)\\ \hline
0,4&\langle(-3,-4),(1,0)\rangle\\
0,1,4&\langle(1,0),(2,3)\rangle\\
0,1,3,4&\langle(2,3),(0,1)\rangle\\
0,3,4&\langle(0,1),(-3,-4)\rangle .
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
The absolute adjacent lattice determinants are \(3,2,3,4\), respectively in cyclic order starting with \(\langle(1,0),(2,3)\rangle\). In particular this toric Chow quotient is **singular**: it has four torus fixed points, with cyclic quotient indices \(3,2,3,4\). A smooth toric resolution requires further ray subdivisions. The fan calculation is characteristic-independent at the combinatorial level.

**Check of the lattice.** The characters \(U=x_2x_0^2/x_1^3\), \(V=x_3x_0^3/x_1^4\) form a basis of the character lattice of the quotient torus: their exponents are a unimodular basis of the kernel of the weight map on the projective three-torus. Therefore (2) is stated in the correct integral lattice, not merely in a real vector space after nonprimitive rescaling.

## 3. Four torus-fixed Chow limit cycles

For a one-parameter coordinate scaling with generic height in a maximal cone, the lower hull of \((0,h_0),(1,h_1),(3,h_2),(4,h_3)\) determines the limit of the degree-four orbit cycle. Each lower-hull segment joining columns \(i,j\) contributes \((a_j-a_i)[L_{ij}]\), where \(L_{ij}\) is the corresponding coordinate line. Hence the four fixed Chow cycles are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
0,4 & 4[L_{03}]\\
0,1,4 & [L_{01}]+3[L_{13}]\\
0,3,4 &3[L_{02}]+[L_{23}]\\
0,1,3,4 &[L_{01}]+2[L_{12}]+[L_{23}].
\end{array}\tag{4}
\]
Every cycle has degree four. These are statements about fundamental **cycles** and include multiplicity; they do not identify the (possibly nonreduced) flat limit ideal. They are consistent with the existing distinction between the full toric limit and its pure cycle in the [torus-boundary framework](../../frameworks/torus-supported-boundaries/index.md).

The normalized Chow quotient contains one two-dimensional open orbit \(T\), four one-dimensional boundary orbits and the four fixed points of (4). Thus (2) gives a finite, explicit boundary poset for degenerating **one** translate; symmetric powers of this compactification retain sums of single-translate Chow limits but do not automatically retain Hilbert lifts.

### Direct verification from the Chow resultant

The fan and the four cycles can be checked independently through the **actual Chow form**, without assuming that a boundary cycle is scheme-theoretically reduced. Let
\(\Delta_{ij}=a_i b_j-a_j b_i\) be the Plücker minors for the two hyperplanes. The Chow form of \(C_{u,v}\), with the hyperplane rows held fixed, is
\[
 R_{u,v}=\operatorname{Res}_r
 (a_0+a_1r+a_2ur^3+a_3vr^4,\
  b_0+b_1r+b_2ur^3+b_3vr^4).
\]
Expanding the \(8\times8\) Sylvester determinant and collecting powers of \(u,v\) gives precisely the six possible exponent pairs
\[
 \mathcal E=\{(0,3),(0,4),(1,3),(2,2),(3,1),(4,1)\}.
 \tag{4a}
\]
Each coefficient is a nonzero integer polynomial with content \(1\): for the two nonvertex exponents \((1,3)\) and \((2,2)\), the corresponding coefficients contain the monomials \(-a_0^3a_1b_2b_3^3\) and \(-a_0^2a_1a_2b_1b_2b_3^2\), respectively, each with coefficient \(-1\). The four vertex coefficients are the nonzero products of Plücker minors displayed in (4b). Thus no exponent disappears identically after reduction to any field characteristic. The convex hull is the quadrilateral with vertices
\[
 (0,3),\ (0,4),\ (4,1),\ (3,1).
\]
Its inward normal fan in the valuation lattice of \(U,V\) is **exactly** the four-ray fan (2), providing an independent verification of the fiber-polytope calculation.

The four vertex coefficients (up to a common overall scalar and the sign convention for resultants) factor as
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 (i,j)&[u^iv^j]R_{u,v}\\ \hline
 (0,4)&\Delta_{03}^4\\
 (0,3)&-\Delta_{01}\Delta_{13}^3\\
 (4,1)&-\Delta_{02}^3\Delta_{23}\\
 (3,1)&-\Delta_{01}\Delta_{12}^2\Delta_{23}.
\end{array}
\tag{4b}
\]
These are literally the Chow forms of the four boundary cycles (4): each \(\Delta_{ij}\) is the Chow form of the coordinate line \(L_{ij}\), and the exponents are their cycle multiplicities. This also shows exactly how the secondary/fiber-polytope fan is **dual to the parameter-exponent Newton polygon** of the Chow orbit map. It should not be confused with the full column-weight Chow polytope of R-0074, which classifies a different fiber of the cycle invariant.

## 4. Graph of the rational quotient versus the \(V_5\) contraction

The closure of the graph of
\[
 q:\mathbf P^3\dashrightarrow S
\]
and its normalization are toric for the ambient three-torus action. A toric resolution \(W\) has proper maps
\[
  \mathbf P^3\ \longleftarrow\ W\ \longrightarrow\ S
\]
and generic fiber \(C_{u,v}\simeq\mathbf P^1\). Over \(T\), the family of curves is explicitly \(\mathbf P^1\times T\) under
\[
 ([s:t],u,v)\longmapsto([s^4:s^3t:u st^3:v t^4],(u,v)).
\]
The graph-resolution map may require fan subdivisions to become smooth or flat over boundary strata. There is no assertion that every Chow-family fiber has this simple Hilbert structure.

Compare the *different* blow-up \(B=\operatorname{Bl}_C\mathbf P^3\), where \(C=C_{1,1}\). In characteristic zero its cubics give the established Sarkisov link
\[
  \mathbf P^3\longleftarrow B
    =\operatorname{Bl}_{\Gamma}V_5\longrightarrow V_5,
 \qquad L=3H-E,
\]
with the actual section dictionary in [two-blowup geometry](../residuals/blowup_residual_geometry.md). Over the **open parameter torus** this link is a globally trivial family: diagonal automorphisms \(\mathrm{diag}(1,1,u,v)\) identify each pair \((\mathbf P^3,C)\) with \((\mathbf P^3,C_{u,v})\), and identify their blow-ups and \(V_5\) targets.

**Obstruction to identifying the two blow-ups.** Near \(p_0\), put \(x_0=1\), \(r=x_1\), and let
\[
 f=x_2-x_1^3,\qquad g=x_3-x_1x_2
\]
generate the ideal of \(C\). The arcs on other orbit closures have
\[
 (f,g)=((u-1)r^3,(v-u)r^4).
\]
For every \(u\ne1\), their strict transforms in \(B\) approach the same exceptional direction \([f:g]=[1:0]\) over \(p_0\), although their quotient parameters \((u,v)\) differ. Therefore \(q\) **cannot** extend to a morphism \(B\to S\) agreeing with the quotient on its original domain. The graph resolution must distinguish data lost by the ordinary blow-up of \(C\).

A common refinement exists by resolving the quotient graph and then principalizing the pullback of \(I_C\), so that the universal property of the blow-up gives a map to \(B\). **The further principalization need not be toric**: the center \(C\) is preserved by the distinguished one-dimensional torus but not by the full ambient torus. Thus a “completely toric \(V_5\) bridge” is not justified.

## 5. The normalized graph fan and its two global endpoint sections

The preceding toric graph can also be calculated **explicitly**, using standard toric graph normalization. Work in the cocharacter lattice \(N_{\mathbf P^3}=\mathbf Z^3\) of the projective three-torus, with coordinates \(z_i=x_i/x_0\), \(i=1,2,3\). The fan of \(\mathbf P^3\) has rays
\[
 n_0=(-1,-1,-1),\quad n_1=(1,0,0),\quad
 n_2=(0,1,0),\quad n_3=(0,0,1).
\]
The quotient-lattice map is
\[
 Q:\mathbf Z^3\longrightarrow\mathbf Z^2,\qquad
 (b_1,b_2,b_3)\longmapsto(-3b_1+b_2,-4b_1+b_3),
 \quad \ker Q=\mathbf Z h,\quad h=(1,3,4).
 \tag{5}
\]
Its images of \(n_0,n_1,n_2,n_3\) are, respectively,
\((2,3),(-3,-4),(1,0),(0,1)\), the four quotient rays in (2).

By the normalized-graph construction for toric rational maps, the graph fan is the common refinement
\[
 \Sigma_W=\{\sigma\cap Q^{-1}(\tau):
      \sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathbf P^3},\ \tau\in\Sigma_S\}
\]
and all their faces. Its **eight primitive rays** are
\[
 \boxed{n_0,n_1,n_2,n_3,\quad
 e_{23}=(0,2,3),\quad e_{01}=(-1,-3,-3),\quad
 h=(1,3,4),\quad -h=(-1,-3,-4).}\tag{6}
\]
The **twelve maximal cones** are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
\text{group}&\text{generating triples}\\ \hline
\text{around }h&
 (n_2,e_{23},h),\ (n_3,e_{23},h),\
 (n_3,n_1,h),\ (n_2,n_1,h)\\
\text{middle}&
 (n_2,e_{23},n_0),\ (n_3,e_{23},n_0),\
 (n_3,n_0,e_{01}),\ (n_3,n_1,e_{01})\\
\text{around }-h&
 (n_2,n_0,-h),\ (n_0,e_{01},-h),\
 (n_1,e_{01},-h),\ (n_2,n_1,-h).
\end{array}\tag{7}
\]
**Verification:** intersect each of the four 3-cones generated by three \(n_i\)'s with inverse images of the four 2-cones in (3). Each intersection is cut out by the nonnegative source-ray coefficients and the two target-cone inequalities; the nonempty full-dimensional intersections have exactly the generator triples in (7). The rays \(e_{23}=2n_2+3n_3\) and \(e_{01}=3n_0+2n_1\) come from splitting the source two-faces whose quotient images cross a target ray; \(h\) and \(-h\) lie in the interiors of the source 3-cones at \(p_0\) and \(p_\infty\). The absolute determinants of the twelve cones are, in the displayed order,
\[
 (3,2,3,4;\quad 3,2,2,3;\quad 3,2,3,4).
\]
Thus the normalized graph is still singular; (7) is its precise fan, **not** a claim of a smooth resolution.

**Proposition (two global sections).** The toric divisors \(D_h,D_{-h}\subset W\) map *isomorphically* to \(S\) under \(W\to S\), and are disjoint. Under \(W\to\mathbf P^3\), they contract to \(p_0\) and \(p_\infty\), respectively.

**Proof.** The star of \(h\) in (7), in the quotient lattice \(N_{\mathbf P^3}/\mathbf Zh\simeq N_T\), has the four rays represented by \(n_2,e_{23},n_3,n_1\); their images are the four primitive rays of \(\Sigma_S\), and their adjacent cones are precisely its four maximal cones. The induced toric map \(D_h\to S\) is therefore an isomorphism. Similarly the star of \(-h\) has rays \(n_2,n_0,e_{01},n_1\), mapping to the same complete fan. No cone contains both opposite rays \(h,-h\), so the divisors are disjoint. Finally \(h\) is interior to \(\langle n_1,n_2,n_3\rangle\), whose orbit closure in \(\mathbf P^3\) is \(p_0\), while \(-h\) is interior to \(\langle n_0,n_1,n_2\rangle\), whose orbit closure is \(p_\infty\). \(\square\)

These are **endpoint sections**, not the arbitrary section \(r=1\) of the *open* quotient. In particular the compactified graph separates the two endpoint sections globally even though \(\mathbf P^3\) contracts each entire section to one point. The exceptional rays \(e_{23}\) and \(e_{01}\) map to coordinate lines \(L_{01}\) and \(L_{23}\), respectively.

### The two extreme weight coefficients live on these sections

Let \(F\in H^0(\mathbf P^3,O(d))\) and expand on the dense torus
\[
 F(1,r,r^3U,r^4V)=\sum_{w=0}^{4d}r^w f_w(U,V).
\]
If \(w_-\) and \(w_+\) are the least and greatest weights with nonzero coefficients, then the orders of the pullback section along the two endpoint divisors are exactly
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{D_h}(\psi^*F)=w_-,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_{D_{-h}}(\psi^*F)=4d-w_+,
 \quad \psi:W\to\mathbf P^3.
 \tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(h\) is the weighted valuation \((1,3,4)\) in the chart \(x_0=1\); at \(p_\infty\), \(-h\) assigns weights \((4,3,1)\) to \(x_0/x_3,x_1/x_3,x_2/x_3\), producing \(4d-w\). After stripping the corresponding fixed exceptional multiplicity, the restriction of the leading divisor to \(D_h\simeq S\) has dense-torus equation \(f_{w_-}(U,V)\), up to a unit; at \(D_{-h}\simeq S\) it has dense-torus equation \(f_{w_+}(U,V)\), again up to a unit/trivialization. One must retain the correct toric line bundles to interpret their zeros along the boundary.

Thus the **smallest and largest intrinsic weight coefficients of a global surface equation are geometric leading sections on the two canonical endpoint copies of the quotient surface**. For \(F\) containing a configuration \(Z\), these leading sections vanish on \(Z\). This connects the existing [actual coefficient evaluation](fixed_surface_restriction_lifts.md) to a common divisor geometry on \(W\). It does **not** imply that checking only these two extremes suffices for containment: every intermediate coefficient \(f_w\) must also vanish.

## 6. What this does and does not settle

**Solved by established theory plus (1)–(8):** the normalized Chow quotient is an explicit toric surface; its four fixed cycles and the eight-ray/twelve-cone normalized graph fan are known; the graph has two canonical global endpoint sections carrying extreme weight data; the open family of one-orbit blow-ups is trivial; the \(V_5\) blow-up does not itself resolve the quotient.

**Still open in the current program:** the full fiber ideals and Hilbert-flatness at each boundary stratum of the now-explicit normalized graph; a smooth toric resolution if required; a controlled principalization of \(I_C\) over that refinement and behavior of the \(V_5\) contraction; relative blow-ups for *multiple* orbit fibers across nonflat endpoint-contact strata; descent/realization of two actual projective equations. The [endpoint delta formula](arrangement_endpoint_delta.md) shows why ordinary symmetric powers cannot be used indiscriminately as universal Hilbert families.

**Tropical distinction.** The fan above records regular subdivisions of the orbit **weight configuration**, not the full Newton polytope of the Chow form used in R-0074. Tropical intersection of weight-component Laurent equations on \(T\) is a useful constraint on quotient configurations, but set-theoretic intersections of tropical hypersurfaces of chosen generators need not equal the tropicalization of their ideal. Neither the normal fan nor a tropical support condition alone supplies homogeneous equations or correct endpoint embedded schemes.
