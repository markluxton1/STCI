# Torus limits and the selected quadratic conductor

Independent review checked the graph construction, purity comparison,
length identity and both controls in characteristic different from two.

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two, with
\(C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\). Fix a smooth auxiliary cover
\[
X_c=V(z^2-r_c),\qquad
r_c=(c-1)x_0x_3+(2-c)x_1x_2,\qquad c\ne1,2.
\]
The intrinsic torus has weights \((0,1,3,4)\) downstairs and weight two
on \(z\). It preserves this cover and each reduced lift of \(C\).
Consequently the two quadratic-conductor lengths of a pure supported
curve are individually constant along its punctured torus orbit.

A full Hilbert limit can nevertheless change those lengths after taking
its pure quotient. The comparison below measures the difference by
embedded structure on both sides of the selected-root graph.
The comparison and both controls hold in this characteristic range;
applications of characteristic-zero CI postulation or exclusion results
retain those separate hypotheses.

## Simultaneous graph and the exact correction

Let \(R=k[[\tau]]\), with fraction field \(K\). Let
\(\mathcal Y\subset\mathbf P^3_R\) be a projective flat family, with
\(\mathcal I_{C_R}^N\subset\mathcal I_{\mathcal Y}\subset
\mathcal I_{C_R}\) for some \(N\). Assume its generic fiber is pure
and its special fiber is pure away from the two marked endpoints.
These conditions hold for the full intrinsic torus degeneration of a
pure supported curve by [R-0070](../../results/intrinsic_limits_endpoint_fibers.md).
Write \(\mathcal A=\mathcal O_{\mathcal Y}\).

On the complement of \(x_0x_3=0\), choose the root restricting to
\(s^2t^2\) on the positive reduced lift. In an affine trivialization,
its reduced value is a unit, so it lifts uniquely through the nilpotent
ideal because the derivative \(2z\) is invertible. Equivalently, the finite
square-root recursion divides only by two.
Define, chartwise and with the degree-one transition for \(z\),
\[
\mathcal B=\operatorname{im}\bigl(
\mathcal A[z]/(z^2-r_c)\longrightarrow
\mathcal A[1/(x_0x_3)]\bigr),\qquad
\mathcal Q=\mathcal B/\mathcal A.                         \tag{1}
\]
The localization notation uses the corresponding affine trivialization.
This is the finite closure of the selected graph, not normalization.

The map from \(\mathcal A\) is injective. Indeed an element killed by
a power of \(x_0x_3\) vanishes on the pure generic fiber, where that
function avoids the curve's associated prime; \(R\)-flatness then kills
the element already. The algebra \(\mathcal B\) is finite, being a
quotient of the double-cover algebra, and is \(R\)-flat as a submodule
of the \(R\)-flat localization. The construction glues because the
selected root and its transition functions agree on overlaps.
Moreover \(\mathcal Q\) is supported on the two endpoint sections and
is finite over \(R\). These are the same graph-closure arguments as in
the [equation-layer comparison](../torus/chow_matched_equation_layers.md#the-global-cech-kernel-and-the-scope-of-kummer-comparisons).

Put \(A_0=\mathcal A/\tau\mathcal A\) and
\(B_0=\mathcal B/\tau\mathcal B\). Let \(T_A,T_B\) be their maximal
zero-dimensional subsheaves, and set
\(\bar A=A_0/T_A\), \(\bar B=B_0/T_B\). Both subsheaves are supported
at the endpoints, since the graph is unchanged off them. At endpoint
\(i\), define
\[
\alpha_i=\ell(T_{A,i}),\quad\beta_i=\ell(T_{B,i}),\quad
\delta_i^{\rm gen}=\ell_K(\mathcal Q_{K,i}),\quad
\delta_i^{\rm pure}=\ell_k((\bar B/\bar A)_i).
\]
For a torus degeneration \(\delta_i^{\rm gen}\) is the original
quadratic-conductor length. These \(\delta\)'s are graph-quotient
lengths; they are not the indices of the entire finite modification
used in R-0070. Locally the graph is generated by \(1,z\), so its
quotient by the base is also the base's conductor quotient.

**Proposition.** The pure quotients form the selected quadratic graph
\(\bar A\subset\bar B\), and
\[
\boxed{\delta_i^{\rm pure}
=\delta_i^{\rm gen}+\alpha_i-\beta_i.}                  \tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** Reduction of (1) gives
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal Q[\tau]\longrightarrow A_0
\longrightarrow B_0\longrightarrow\mathcal Q/\tau\mathcal Q
\longrightarrow0.                                    \tag{3}
\]
Its kernel is punctual and lies in \(T_A\). The preimage of \(T_B\)
is exactly \(T_A\): the map is an isomorphism on the interior, and
sections vanishing there are precisely the punctual torsion. Thus
\(\bar A\to\bar B\) is injective. Both pure quotients inject into
their common interior localization. The second is generated by the
first and the specialized selected root, since this holds before
reduction. It is therefore the selected graph over \(\bar A\), retaining
the full generic nilpotent algebra rather than only the reduced curve.

Set \(e_i=\ell(\mathcal Q[\tau]_i)\). The structure theorem for the
finite \(R\)-module \(\mathcal Q\) on each endpoint gives
\[
\ell((\mathcal Q/\tau\mathcal Q)_i)
=\delta_i^{\rm gen}+e_i.
\]
In (3) the image of \(T_{A,i}\) has length \(\alpha_i-e_i\).
Passing to the pure quotients therefore subtracts
\(\beta_i-(\alpha_i-e_i)\) from the displayed length, proving (2).
No complete-intersection hypothesis on the special fiber was used.
The length argument itself is characteristic-free once the finite flat
graph with the stated support and purity properties is given.

## A pure-image control with an increased limiting conductor

Use the existing [length-three moving image](../torus_hilbert_boundary.md#a-finite-image-with-embedded-points-in-the-limit).
On its zero chart, with \(\mathcal O=k[[t]]\), the finite modification
is \(M=\mathcal O[U]/U^3\), with \(V=tU^2\), and its pure image is
\[
A=\mathcal O\oplus t^3\mathcal O U\oplus t^5\mathcal O U^2.
\]
The index \(\ell(M/A)=8\) belongs to the entire finite modification.
For the fixed cover \(c=0\), the chosen root is
\[
z=t^2\left(1+U-\frac{1+t}{2}U^2\right).
\]
The graph \(B=A+Az\) has \(\mathcal O\)-basis
\(1,t^2[U-(1+t)U^2/2],t^3U^2\). Its index in \(M\) is five, hence
its quadratic-conductor length is \(8-5=3\).

In the matched torus family \(V=\tau tU^2\), the full image module is
\[
R[[t]]\oplus t^3R[[t]]U
\oplus(t^6,\tau t^5)R[[t]]U^2.
\]
Its special fiber has the known embedded length \(\alpha_0=1\).
The graph remains free, with basis
\(1,t^2[U-(1+\tau t)U^2/2],t^3U^2\): the last vector follows by
subtracting \(t(z-t^2)\) from \(t^3U\), since \(1+\tau t\) is a unit.
Thus \(\beta_0=0\). The pure special image has index nine and the
graph still has index five, giving
\[
\delta_0^{\rm pure}=4=3+1-0.
\]
This is a pure-supported projective control, not a complete-intersection
example. The local calculation concerns its zero endpoint only.

## A local CI control with a decreased limiting conductor

There is also a [global moving-cover control](moving_quadratic_covers.md#the-flat-graph-at-the-endpoint)
on every fixed pure thickening of the quartic: its whole generic conductor
becomes punctual torsion when the osculating cone specializes to a square.
It has \(\alpha=0\) and \(\beta=\delta_{\rm generic}\), without any
CI hypothesis. The local example below gives a separate explicit algebra.

In formal ambient coordinates \((t,v,h)\) of weights \((1,3,4)\),
the reverse-torus degeneration of \((h-v,v^2)\) is
\[
h=\tau v,\qquad v^2=0.
\]
Its base algebra \(\mathcal A=R[[t]][v]/v^2\) is free, with
\(\alpha=0\). For the fixed quadratic function
\(r=t^4+2tv-h\),
\[
z=t^2+\frac{2t-\tau}{2t^2}v,\qquad
\mathcal B=R[[t]]\oplus t^{-2}(t^2,2t-\tau)R[[t]]v.
\]
Generically \(2t-\tau\) is a unit in \(K[[t]]\), giving conductor
length two. The coefficient ideal has generators \(e_1=t^2\),
\(e_2=2t-\tau\) and relation
\((2t-\tau)e_1-t^2e_2=0\). In the special fiber the nonzero class
\(2e_1-te_2\) is killed by \(t\), and generates its entire torsion.
Hence \(\beta=1\); the pure graph is
\(k[[t]]\oplus t^{-1}k[[t]]v\), giving
\[
\delta^{\rm pure}=1=2+0-1.
\]
This is a local torus degeneration of complete intersections. It is not
a construction of a global pair with sole support the quartic.

## What the comparison leaves to determine

R-0070 supplies \(\alpha_i\) for the full Hilbert limit. Applying its
relative-image method with the additional weight-two root coordinate
can compute \(\beta_i\). The controls show why the difference cannot
be assigned a sign from flatness alone. When \(\alpha_i=0\), (2) gives
\(\delta_i^{\rm pure}\le\delta_i^{\rm gen}\), consistent with the
[finite-free endpoint specialization bound](endpoint_lattice_capacity.md#arithmetic-control-and-comparison-of-traits).

The useful next comparison concerns restrictions on
\(\beta_i-\alpha_i\) imposed by actual global equations. A pure limit
need not be a CI, and removing its embedded structure changes its
Hilbert polynomial; the original CI conductor-postulation inequality
does not automatically become an inequality for that pure limit.
Nor does the selected graph recover the global kernels in the
[equation-torsion layers](../../results/endpoint_equation_torsion.md).
Their filtered equation sources remain separate necessary input.
The comparison preserves endpoint concentration as useful information
without identifying it with an unchanged conductor or a defining pair.
