# Saturated quasiprimitive flags and proper residual zero-cycles

This is the complete proof home for [R-0019](../../results/quasiprimitive_residual_cycles.md), retaining its finite-flat, saturated-flag, nonzero generic first-class and proper auxiliary residual-intersection hypotheses. The local determinant and first-normal inequality are characteristic-free; the fixed-quartic illustrations use characteristic zero. They are length-cycle/divisor statements, not scheme decompositions.

## Local determinant statement

Let R be a complete DVR, K its fraction field, and B a finite flat
R-algebra whose reduced quotient is R and whose generic fiber is
K[y]/(y^m). Let B_i be the saturated order flag

    B_i = B intersect (y^i) B_K,  B_0=B, B_m=0.

Suppose its successive quotients are invertible R-modules, globally denoted
E_i=L^i(D_i), with D_0=D_1=0. These are the hypotheses used by the
repository's quasiprimitive BF filtration; an unsaturated I-adic flag is
insufficient. Take q in B_1 whose class in E_1 is nonzero over K. Write its
first-class vanishing order as t_q.

Choose an R-basis b_0=1,b_1,...,b_(m-1) adapted to the saturated flag.
This is possible because every quotient is free over the DVR. Multiplication
by q has zero row indexed by b_0 and zero column indexed by b_(m-1).
The remaining square block, rows 1,...,m-1 and columns 0,...,m-2, is
triangular with nonzero diagonal. Its i-th diagonal entry is the leading
map E_i -> E_(i+1), and therefore has valuation

    t_q + ord(D_(i+1)) - ord(D_i).

All other maximal minors vanish. Thus the cokernel is one free copy of R
plus a finite torsion module whose length is the valuation of this
determinant. Summing proves

    length_R tors(B/qB) = (m-1)t_q + ord(D_(m-1)).       (RC)

Higher coefficients of q affect off-diagonal entries but do not affect
this determinant. No assumption that q itself generates the quotient
direction at the closed point is needed.

## Residual intersection statement

Let C be a smooth curve on a smooth surface Q in a smooth threefold, and
let X=V(F,G) be a complete-intersection multiple curve with reduced support
C satisfying the preceding quasiprimitive hypotheses. Suppose both restrictions
vanish along C and the first class of the equation of Q is nonzero in L
over the function field of C. Locally write

    F|Q=h rho,  G|Q=h sigma,

where h defines C in Q. Assume rho and sigma have no common curve component.
Their finite intersection Z is supported on C, because X has that support.
There is an exact sequence on Q

    0 -> O_Z(-C) -> O_(X intersect Q) -> O_C -> 0.     (EX)

Indeed its kernel is the ideal generated by h in
O_Q/(h rho,h sigma), and cancellation of the nonzerodivisor h identifies
that kernel with O_Q/(rho,sigma), with the indicated twist.

At a point of C choose a completed parameter t on C and lift it to the
ambient smooth threefold. The complete-intersection local algebra B is
finite flat over R=k[[t]]: it is finite because its radical is the ideal
of C, and torsion-free because the complete intersection is CM and has
only that one minimal prime. The completed middle algebra in (EX) is B/qB,
where q is the local equation of Q. Its quotient O_C is R, while its kernel
is finite R-torsion. Consequently that kernel is exactly the torsion
submodule, with its length equal to the local intersection length of Z.
Equation (RC) therefore proves, coefficientwise as cycles on C,

    [Z] = (m-1) T + D_(m-1),                         (CY)

where T is the zero divisor of the first class of q in
L tensor O_C(Q). Twisting the finite kernel does not change its lengths.
This proof allows special-point normal-order jumps of q and nonprimitive
fibers of X. The identity is of length cycles, not of ideals or schemes.

## Application to a quartic carrier on a smooth rational quartic

Work in characteristic zero. Let C=C_0, let Q be its unique smooth quadric,
and suppose X=(F_4,G_b) has reduced support C and generic multiplicity b.
The quartic has generic normal order one. Write L=O(e-7), and write
E_i=L^i(D_i) for the quasiprimitive filtration. The socle is

    E_{b-1}=omega_C tensor O_C(-b)=O(-4b-2),

so deg D_{b-1}=(3-e)(b-1)-6.

The first class of q in L tensor O_C(2) is nonzero: otherwise F_4|Q would
vanish twice along C, but O_Q(4,4)(-2C)=O_Q(2,-2) has no sections, forcing
q to divide F_4 and contradicting the quadric-carrier obstruction.
Its zero divisor T therefore has degree e+1.

On Q write F_4|Q=h rho and G_b|Q=h sigma_b, where h cuts out C and
rho,sigma_b have classes (3,1),(b-1,b-3). Their intersection Z is proper:
a common residual component would be another curve in X. Every point of
Z lies on C. Locally on Q,

    0 -> O_Z(-C) -> O_{X intersect Q} -> O_C -> 0.

Use a completed parameter t on C, lifted to the ambient ring. The torsion
over k[[t]] in the middle algebra is exactly O_Z(-C). Hence the local
determinant formula gives the effective zero-cycle identity

    [rho intersect sigma_b] = (b-1)T + D_{b-1}.

This is an identity of length cycles on C; it does not assert that the
residual zero-dimensional scheme is annihilated by I_C or has the same
scheme structure as the divisor on the right. Taking degrees recovers
(3,1).(b-1,b-3)=4b-10, but the pointwise equality is stronger.

For b=6, the existing numerical types give D_5=D_2+D_3, so

    [rho intersect sigma_6] = 5T + D_2+D_3.

In the regular-ratio branch after subtracting a quadratic multiple of F,
G_6|Q=h^2 sigma with sigma of class (4,0). If H is the quartic vertical
divisor, [rho intersect C]=T+H. Subtracting yields

    [rho intersect sigma] = 4T + D_2+D_3-H.

The right side must be effective and supported at actual intersections of
the four residual rulings with rho. This is a new pointwise constraint;
it does not by itself identify H with a BF defect or exclude all pairs.

## A uniform first-normal bound

Let A be the divisor of the first-normal image I_X -> I_C/I_C^2, with
primitive horizontal kernel line M. Choose local ambient normal coordinates
x,y so x spans M and y maps to a unit of the quotient L. Apply the local
determinant formula to multiplication by y. The quotient has a direct
R-module decomposition B/yB=R plus N, where N is its nilradical and is
finite torsion. Its length is ord D_{m-1}. On the other hand,

    N/N^2 = R x / (t^{ord A}x),

since this quotient is exactly the first-normal cokernel in the kernel
direction, after killing y. Therefore length(N/N^2)=ord A and

    A <= D_{m-1} coefficientwise.

No characteristic restriction is required for this local inequality.
Equality is false in general: (t^a x-y^2,x^3) has A=a[t=0] and
D_5=2a[t=0]. The proof uses finite flatness and the saturated curvilinear
filtration, rather than a selected binomial local normal form.

For a regular-ratio (4,b) pair, A equals the quartic vertical divisor H.
Consequently

    9-e <= (3-e)(b-1)-6.

In characteristic zero the balanced quartic conormal bundle also gives
e in {0,1,2}, and the resulting necessary regular-branch bounds are

    e=0: b>=6;   e=1: b>=8;   e=2: b>=14.

In particular, the entire regular-ratio e=1 branch at (4,6) is impossible.
For its e=0 branch, deg H=deg D_5=9, so the coefficientwise inequality
forces D_5=H and the cycle identity gives [rho intersect sigma]=4T.
Here T is a single point. Because sigma has class (4,0), and each ruling
meets rho in length one unless it is a common component, sigma must be
the fourth power of the ruling through T. This supplies a geometric
normalization for the remaining mixed e=0 analysis.

The inequality does not exclude nonregular quotients: there A is the
common part of two vertical divisors and may have smaller degree.

## Exact non-lci attachment control (historical checker retired)

This local counterexample is over `k[t,x,y]` in **every characteristic**, and is not claimed to arise from homogeneous equations on the fixed quartic. Let `I=(x,y)`, `L=(x^2,xy+t y^2)`, `J=L+(y^3)`, and `K=(x+t y,t^2)`. The historical exact ideal computation establishes `saturate(L,(t))=J`, `L:J=K`, and `J intersect K=L`. A Hilbert–Burch matrix for `J` is
```
H = [ y        0
     -x+t*y  -y^2
     -t^2     x+t*y ].
```
Its maximal minors generate `J` and `gens(J)*H=0`. Restricting `H` to `k[t]=k[t,x,y]/I` leaves only the entry `-t^2` in its third row, first column. After adjoining the two actual equation columns, the resulting attachment module is `k[t]/(t^2)`, with zeroth Fitting ideal `(t^2)`; likewise `(K+I)/I=(t^2)`, giving actual residual intersection length two. This exhibits torsion attachment not captured by the determinant of the torsion-free source, and does not assert that the primary attachment is lci. The exact matrix identities, not the Macaulay2 implementation, are the retained certificate.
