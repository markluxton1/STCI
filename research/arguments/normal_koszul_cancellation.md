# Normal Koszul cancellation and the actual equation filtration

## Scope

The [normal Koszul comparison](../results/normal_koszul_comparison_defect.md)
is local algebra for \(J\subset I\) in a Noetherian local ring \(R\). The
Tor description of the minimal initial equations uses the graded quotient
alone. The spectral sequence uses a regular sequence of two normal
parameters and a finite nilpotent filtration or the complete Noetherian
adic setting specified below. It applies to a marked curve in a
threefold, including the formal neighborhood of a marked component with
residual support. The field-valued dimension statements concern an
actual transverse two-equation complete intersection. The proofs work in
arbitrary characteristic.

The [recursive family](../results/recursive_normal_koszul_cancellation.md)
realizes the page and degree calculation over any transverse field. Its
integral perturbations realize that local model along a marked affine line.
The projective statement concerns the closures of the two affine carriers.

## Normal Koszul comparison and degree bounds

### Graded Tor and the shifted normal filtration

Set \(B=R/I\), \(M=R/J\), \(K=I/J\), \(S=\operatorname{gr}_I R\),
and \(H=\operatorname{in}_I(J)\). By
[R-0030](../results/local_filtration_comparison_defect.md),
\(A=\operatorname{gr}_K M=S/H\). The graded long exact Tor sequence of
\(0\to H\to S\to A\to0\), tensoring with \(B=S/S_+\), starts with
\[
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_1^S(A,B)
 \longrightarrow H/S_+H\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow B
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
The map from \(H/S_+H\) to \(B\) is zero since \(H\subset S_+\).
This identifies \(Q=H/S_+H\) with \(\operatorname{Tor}_1^S(A,B)\)
without any regularity hypothesis on the two ideals.

Suppose now that \(I=(x,y)\) is generated by a regular sequence.
Then \(S=B[X,Y]\), with \(X,Y\) the initial classes of \(x,y\).
The free Koszul complex \(K_R(x,y)\) resolves \(B\), and
\(\mathscr K_\bullet=M\otimes_R K_R(x,y)\) computes
\(\operatorname{Tor}_\bullet^R(M,B)\). Filter its terms by
\[
F^n\mathscr K_i=(K^{n-i})^{\binom{2}{i}},\qquad K^a=M\quad(a\le0).
\]
Multiplication by either normal parameter raises the coefficient order
by at least one. Hence the differential from \(i\) to \(i-1\) preserves
the total weight \(n\), and its associated graded differential uses
multiplication by \(X,Y\). In weight \(n\) the term of homological degree
\(i\) is \(A_{n-i}^{\binom{2}{i}}\), with \(A_j=0\) for \(j<0\).
Thus the graded complex is \(K_A(X,Y)\), with each normal Koszul
generator of weight one. The Koszul complex on \(X,Y\) over \(S\)
resolves \(B\), so the first homology page is
\[
E_1^{i,n}=\operatorname{Tor}_i^S(A,B)_n.
\]
A representative in \(F^n\mathscr K_i\) whose differential belongs to
\(F^{n+r}\mathscr K_{i-1}\) maps under \(d_r\) to the class of that
differential in \(E_r^{i-1,n+r}\).
[Filtered-complex construction](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/012K)

### Stabilization and the induced homology filtration

If \(K\) is nilpotent, each term has a finite filtration, and the
spectral sequence converges to the associated graded of the induced
filtration on homology. For the complete Noetherian \(I\)-adic case,
each term is a finite \(R\)-module and has a shifted \(I\)-adic filtration
because \(K^a=I^aM\) for \(a\ge0\). By Artin–Rees for each submodule
\(d\mathscr K_i\subset\mathscr K_{i-1}\), one can choose a uniform
integer \(c\ge0\) with
\[
d\mathscr K_i\cap F^p\mathscr K_{i-1}\ \subset\
d(F^{p-c}\mathscr K_i)
\]
for all \(i,p\). Indeed, if \(c_i\) works for the unshifted powers on
\(\mathscr K_{i-1}\), then for \(p-i+1\ge c_i\) the left side is
contained in \(I^{p-i+1-c_i}d\mathscr K_i\). Choose \(c\ge c_i+1\)
for both differentials. When \(p-i+1<c_i\), this choice makes
\(F^{p-c}\mathscr K_i\) the whole term.

For \(r\ge c+1\) and \(z\in F^p\mathscr K_i\) with
\(dz\in F^{p+r}\mathscr K_{i-1}\), choose \(w\in F^{p+r-c}\mathscr K_i\)
with \(dw=dz\). Then \(z-w\) is a cycle with the same class as \(z\)
modulo \(F^{p+1}\mathscr K_i\). The page-\(r\) boundary symbols are the
images of \(F^p\mathscr K_i\cap d(F^{p-r+1}\mathscr K_{i+1})\) in
\(\operatorname{gr}_F^p\mathscr K_i\). Every actual boundary in
\(F^p\mathscr K_i\) lies in \(d(F^{p-c}\mathscr K_{i+1})\), hence already
contributes a page-\(r\) boundary symbol when \(r\ge c+1\).
Both the cycle and boundary symbols have therefore stabilized by
\(E_{c+1}\). Their quotient is the weight-\(p\) graded piece of
homology for the filtration by images of actual cycles in
\(F^p\mathscr K_i\).

This filtration is exhaustive by the definition of \(F\). For
separatedness, Artin–Rees for \(\ker d\subset\mathscr K_i\) bounds the
image of \(\ker d\cap F^p\mathscr K_i\) in homology by
\(I^{p-i-c'_i}H_i(\mathscr K_\bullet)\) for \(p\) large and a constant
\(c'_i\). The homology modules are finite, and Krull intersection for
the local adic powers gives separatedness. This gives the claimed
abutment with its induced filtration in the complete adic case.

### The comparison on representatives

The connecting homomorphism for \(0\to J\to R\to M\to0\), computed
with \(K_R(x,y)\), identifies \(\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(M,B)\) with
\(D=J/IJ\), since \(J\subset I\) and \(R\) is free over itself.
Use the convention that the connecting map takes the differential of
a lift. A cycle in \(\mathscr K_1\) lifts to \((a,b)\in R^2\) with
\(xa+yb\in J\), and the map gives \([xa+yb]\in D\). Changing the lifts
adds an element of \(IJ\), and a Koszul boundary gives zero in \(D\).
If the cycle has weight at least \(n\ge1\), the lifts can be chosen
in \(I^{n-1}\), so its equation lies in \(J\cap I^n\). Conversely,
\(I^n=xI^{n-1}+yI^{n-1}\) gives such a representative for every
\(j\in J\cap I^n\). This proves
\[
F^nD=((J\cap I^n)+IJ)/IJ.
\]
When \(J=(f,g)\) is generated by a regular sequence, the carrier Koszul
resolution of \(M\) computes the underlying Tor modules. The two
generators vanish in \(B\), leaving \(B\), \(D\), and \(\bigwedge_B^2D\)
in degrees zero, one and two. The exterior-square identification is
compatible with changes of regular generators, by the exterior powers
of the corresponding maps of carrier Koszul complexes.

For the graded connecting map, compute with \(K_S(X,Y)\) and use
the same sign convention. Let \((\bar a,\bar b)\in A_{n-1}^2\) be a
cycle of weight \(n\), and choose \(a,b\in I^{n-1}\) lifting it.
Then \(xa+yb\bmod I^{n+1}\) lies in \(H_n\), and its class in
\(H_n/(S_+H)_n\) is the graded connecting image. By the definition
of \(H_n\) there is \(j\in J\cap I^n\) with
\(xa+yb-j\in I^{n+1}\). Write the difference as \(xu+yv\) with
\(u,v\in I^n\). Replacing \((a,b)\) by \((a-u,b-v)\) preserves its
graded class and gives an actual cycle in \(F^n\mathscr K_1\).
Its connecting image in \(D\) is \([j]\).
By R-0030 the class of \([j]\) in \(G_n=\operatorname{gr}_F^nD\)
is \(j\bmod I^{n+1}\) taken modulo \(T_n\), for
\(T=\operatorname{in}_I(IJ)\). This identifies the spectral-sequence
map with the quotient \(H_n/(S_+H)_n\to H_n/T_n\), including the
signs of the two connecting maps.

### Incoming images and degree bounds

The only \(E_1^{0,n}\) is \(E_1^{0,0}=B\). For \(n\ge1\)
the target of every outgoing \(d_r\) from \(E_r^{1,n}\) is zero.
Consequently
\[
Q_n=E_1^{1,n}\twoheadrightarrow E_2^{1,n}
 \twoheadrightarrow\cdots\twoheadrightarrow E_\infty^{1,n}=G_n.
\]
Set \(C_{r,n}=\ker(Q_n\to E_{r+1}^{1,n})\) and \(C_{0,n}=0\).
The kernel of \(E_r^{1,n}\to E_{r+1}^{1,n}\) is the incoming image
from \(E_r^{2,n-r}\). Pulling back along \(Q_n\to E_r^{1,n}\) gives
\[
C_{r,n}/C_{r-1,n}
 \simeq\operatorname{im}(d_r:E_r^{2,n-r}\to E_r^{1,n}).
\]
R-0030 identifies \(C_{\infty,n}\) with \(\Delta_n\). Geometrically,
a class of a minimal full initial equation is erased exactly when
its normal Koszul class has a representative that is the leading
symbol of an actual boundary. The filtration records the order gap
through which such a boundary appears.

The weight-two term \(\mathscr K_2\) has zero associated graded below
degree two, so a contribution to \(\Delta_n\) has \(1\le r\le n-2\).
This proves \(\Delta_1=\Delta_2=0\). If \(J\subset I^e\), then
\(IJ\subset I^{e+1}\). In degree \(e+1\), any element of \(IJ\)
is a sum of products whose degree-\((e+1)\) classes belong to
\(S_1H_e\). Each such product class comes from \(IJ\). Hence
\(T_{e+1}=S_1H_e=(S_+H)_{e+1}\), giving vanishing through \(e+1\).

The normal Koszul calculation uses \(K_A(X,Y)\) on the entire
initial algebra. In the filtered carrier Koszul calculation on
\(f,g\), the graded complex comes from the leading forms of the
carrier equations. A syzygy of those two forms can produce an
additional generator of the full initial ideal, as in the
[degree-four example](filtered_equation_comparison.md#a-minimal-initial-equation-erased-by-the-comparison).
The normal calculation starts with that generator already present in
\(Q\) and tracks whether it survives in \(G\).

### Consecutive cancellations over a transverse field

Let \(R=L[[x,y]]\), \(I=(x,y)\), and let \(J\) be an \(I\)-primary
two-generator complete intersection. If \(h=\mu(H)\), the minimal
graded resolution of the finite-length algebra \(S/H\) over
\(S=L[X,Y]\) has Betti totals \((1,h,h-1)\). Indeed the length-two
resolution has \(h\) minimal first generators, and its alternating
rank sum is zero over the fraction field of \(S\). The underlying
actual Tor has dimensions \((1,2,1)\) by the carrier resolution.
Each differential of rank \(s\) removes \(s\) dimensions from
each of the source and target. Only the differentials from
homological degree two to one can be nonzero, so they remove
\(h-2\) pairs, in agreement with the generator excess in R-0030.
A syzygy in degree \(a\) cancels with an equation in degree \(a+r\)
on page \(r\). These are negative consecutive cancellations in the
field-valued Betti numbers.
[Rossi–Sharifan, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.1086) ·
[Sammartano, Theorem 2 and Remark 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.05220)

If either carrier equation has order one, choose it as a formal
parameter \(u\) and complete it to \(I=(u,v)\). Reducing the other
equation modulo \(u\) gives a nonzero power of \(v\) times a unit,
so \(J=(u,v^m)\) for some \(m\ge1\). The full initial ideals
in these coordinates are \(H=(U,V^m)\) and
\(T=(U^2,UV,V^{m+1})=S_+H\), as in the straightening argument
of R-0030. Thus \(\Delta=0\). If both carrier orders are at least
two, \(J\subset I^2\) and the degree bound gives vanishing through
degree three.

## The recursive family and its cancellation page

### Full initial ideals over a field

Let \(L\) be a field, \(n\ge2\), \(q\ge3\), \(R=L[[x,y]]\), and \(I=(x,y)\).
Set \(D_0=1\), \(D_1=x\), \(D_j=xD_{j-1}-y^qD_{j-2}\), and
\(h_i=y^{qi}D_{n-i}\) for \(0\le i\le n\). The recurrence gives
\[
y^qh_{i-1}-xh_i+h_{i+1}=0\quad(1\le i\le n),\qquad h_{n+1}=0.
\]
In particular the \(h_i\) generate \(J=(h_0,h_1)\), and \(h_i\in IJ\) for
\(i\ge2\). Since \(q>2\), the least-degree term of \(D_j\) is \(x^j\), by
induction on \(j\). Write \(m_i=X^{n-i}Y^{qi}=\operatorname{in}_I(h_i)\).
The ideal \((X,Y^q)^n=(m_0,\ldots,m_n)\) is contained in \(H\).
The monomials \(X^aY^b\) with \(0\le a<n\) and \(0\le b<q(n-a)\)
form a basis of its quotient, of dimension \(qn(n+1)/2\).

The monic polynomials \(D_j\) have degree \(j\) in \(x\). Reducing
\(D_j\) modulo \(D_{j-1}\) gives \(-y^qD_{j-2}\), so the monic resultant
recurrence multiplies \(\operatorname{Res}_x(D_{j-1},D_{j-2})\) by
\((-y^q)^{j-1}\), up to sign. The starting resultant
\(\operatorname{Res}_x(D_1,D_0)\) is one. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Res}_x(D_n,D_{n-1})=\pm y^{qn(n-1)/2},\qquad
\operatorname{Res}_x(D_n,y^qD_{n-1})=\pm y^{qn(n+1)/2}.
\]
Since \(D_n\bmod y=x^n\), division by this monic distinguished
polynomial makes \(R/(D_n)\) free of rank \(n\) over \(L[[y]]\).
The second resultant is the determinant of multiplication by
\(y^qD_{n-1}\) on this free module. It is nonzero, and its valuation
gives the length of the cokernel over the DVR \(L[[y]]\). This
length is also \(\dim_L R/J\). In particular \(J\) is \(I\)-primary
and its two generators form a regular sequence in the two-dimensional
regular local ring. The associated graded of \(R/J\) has the same
length. The inclusion of \((X,Y^q)^n\) in \(H\) and equality of
colengths give \(H=(X,Y^q)^n\).

Set \(a_i=n+(q-1)i\). The \(m_i\) are a minimal homogeneous generating
set for \(H\), so they give a basis of \(Q\) in weights \(a_i\).
For \(i\ge2\) they belong to \(T=\operatorname{in}_I(IJ)\) by the
recurrence. The count in R-0030 gives \(\dim_L\Delta=(n+1)-2=n-1\).
Consequently these \(n-1\) classes are a basis of \(\Delta\), and
\[
T=S_+H+(m_2,\ldots,m_n),\qquad
\Delta=\bigoplus_{i=2}^n L(-a_i).
\]
The images of \(h_0,h_1\) in \(D\) survive in weights \(a_0,a_1\).

### Strict shifted resolution and the page

Give a copy \(R\langle s\rangle\) of \(R\) the decreasing filtration
\(F^pR\langle s\rangle=I^{\max(p-s,0)}R\), so its generator has weight \(s\).
Use \(P_0=R\langle0\rangle\), \(P_1=\bigoplus_{i=0}^n Re_i\) with
weights \(a_i\), and \(P_2=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n Ru_i\) with weights
\(b_i=a_i+1\). Define the differentials by
\[
d_1(e_i)=h_i,\qquad
d_2(u_i)=y^qe_{i-1}-xe_i+e_{i+1}\ (i<n),\qquad
d_2(u_n)=y^qe_{n-1}-xe_n.
\]
The recurrence makes this a complex. In column \(i\) the first two
terms have weight \(a_{i-1}+q=a_i+1=b_i\). The unit in row \(i+1\)
has strictly higher weight \(a_{i+1}=b_i+q-2\). The associated graded
matrix therefore has just \(Y^q,-X\) in rows \(i-1,i\) of column \(i\).
Its signed maximal minors are the monomials \(m_i\), up to a common
sign, and generate a height-two ideal. The graded Hilbert–Burch
theorem gives the minimal free resolution of \(A=S/H\), with the
given shifts.

To see strictness over \(R\), augment \(P_\bullet\) to \(M=R/J\)
with its quotient filtration. The graded augmented complex is exact.
For a cycle \(z\in F^pP_i\), exactness in weight \(p\) gives a preimage
modulo \(F^{p+1}P_i\) with weight at least \(p\). Subtract its boundary
and repeat in weights \(p+1,p+2,\ldots\). The preimages sum to an element
of \(F^pP_{i+1}\) by completeness of the shifted finite free modules,
and separatedness identifies its boundary with \(z\). At the top
term, a nonzero cycle would have a nonzero leading class in the kernel
of the injective graded map. This proves exactness and strictness,
meaning that each image meets \(F^p\) in the image of the source
filtration at \(p\). The augmentation is strict by the quotient
filtration. The resulting Hilbert–Burch resolution over \(R\) has unit
entries, while its associated graded resolution is minimal.

For the page calculation use the total complex of
\(P_\bullet\otimes_R K_R(x,y)\), with the sum of the generator weights
on each tensor summand. Its graded complex is the total complex of
\(\operatorname{gr}P_\bullet\otimes_S K_S(X,Y)\). The augmentations
give filtered maps to \(M\otimes_R K_R(x,y)\) and to \(P_\bullet\otimes_R L\),
using the quotient filtrations on \(M,L\). On associated gradeds the
maps are the two augmentations of the graded tensor complex. Each is
a quasi-isomorphism by exactness of the corresponding graded
resolution and freeness of the terms of the other one. Thus both maps
induce isomorphisms on \(E_1\) and on every subsequent page, with the
normal degrees preserved.

In \(P_\bullet\otimes_R L\), all differential entries except the
units in row \(i+1\) of column \(i<n\) vanish. These units raise
weight by exactly \(q-2\). In the displayed resolution bases the
only nonzero higher differential is therefore
\[
d_{q-2}(u_i)=e_{i+1}\quad(1\le i<n),\qquad d_{q-2}(u_n)=0.
\]
The image classes are independent, and the surviving generators
\(e_0,e_1,u_n\) are actual cycles with zero differential. The
pagewise comparison identifies this with the normal Koszul
calculation. Its incoming images account for every class of
\(\Delta\) in the filtration of R-0065. This gives unbounded defect
dimension and degrees over a field, with any prescribed page
\(q-2\ge1\).

### Integral carriers along the affine line

For \(N\ge qn+1\), put
\[
F=D_n+t\,y^N,\qquad G=y^qD_{n-1}+t(x^N+y^N)
\]
in \(L[t,x,y]\), with marked line \(C\) given by \(x=y=0\).
The coefficients of \(F\), viewed as a polynomial in \(t\), are
coprime because \(D_n\bmod y=x^n\). For \(G\), every linear factor of
\(x^N+y^N\) over an algebraic closure is \(x-\lambda y\) with
\(\lambda\ne0\). On this line \(D_{n-1}\) has lowest term
\(\lambda^{n-1}y^{n-1}\), because all corrections have higher total
order when \(q>2\). Also \(y\) does not divide \(x^N+y^N\).
Thus the two coefficients of \(G\) are coprime, including when some
linear factors are repeated. The primitive linear polynomials in
\(t\) are irreducible by Gauss's lemma, giving the two integral carriers.

The monomial basis of \(S/H\) above has maximum degree \(qn-1\).
Since \(K=IM\) is nilpotent, the vanishing of \(K^{qn}/K^{qn+1}\)
implies \(I^{qn}\subset J\). Extending to \(L[t][[x,y]]\), the
added terms of \(F,G\) belong to \(I^{qn+1}\subset IJ\). The
generator change from \((h_0,h_1)\) to \((F,G)\) is the identity
modulo \(I\), hence invertible in this completion. This proves
equality of the completed ideals along \(C\).

The full initial ideals of \(J\) and \(IJ\) commute with this extension
of coefficients from \(L\) to \(L[t]\). In each degree the defining
images can be formed in a finite polynomial truncation modulo
\(I^{p+1}\), where the constant ideal and its images extend by flat
scalar extension. The normal Koszul complex of the quotient has
finite filtration and extends with it, since \(M\) is killed by
\(I^{qn}\). Its pages extend by flatness as well. In particular the
field calculation applies over \(L(t)\) at the generic point of the line.

### Projective scope

Ordinary homogenization gives the integral projective closures
in \(\mathbf P^3_L\). The affine normal orders \(a_0\) and \(a_1\)
are distinct, so the carriers are distinct and have a proper
intersection containing the marked line. The equality of completed
ideals proves the calculation along the affine part of that line.
Residual global components are permitted. The calculation concerns
a line and supplies no fixed-quartic pair or analysis of the point
at infinity.

### Finite replays and their coverage

The [singular-identity Python replay](../computations/verify_delta_singular_identities_2026_10_24.py)
passed on 35 recursive samples (\(2\le n\le8,\ 3\le q\le7\)).
It checks full integer polynomial expansions, including the Cassini
and lifted syzygy identities, the displayed normal initials, the
weight gaps, and the staircase counts. It also checks 24 samples of
the separate punctual family (\(2\le m\le7,\ 1\le r\le4\)).

The [singular-family Macaulay2 replay](../computations/verify_delta_singular_families_2026_10_24.m2)
passed on 12 recursive samples (\(2\le n\le5,\ 3\le q\le5\)).
It uses Rees saturation over \(\mathbb Q[t]\) to verify both full
initial ideals, and checks the colength, membership of the
perturbations in \(IJ\), and the lifted matrix identity and reduced
rank. The displayed page in the output is computed from \(q\). The
script also checks 12 samples of the punctual family
(\(2\le m\le5,\ 1\le r\le3\)), including the actual closed-fiber
initial ideal, and one supported quadruple with zero relative defect.
The arguments above supply all-parameter coverage, strictness,
the pagewise identification and integrality.

## Conclusion

The [comparison defect](../results/normal_koszul_comparison_defect.md)
has a filtration by successive incoming images in the normal Koszul
spectral sequence. Over a transverse field this refines the
generator excess by page and by normal degree. Over a curve it
retains the actual coefficient modules and the filtration induced
on \(J/IJ\). The recursive family realizes a single page of incoming
cancellations with arbitrary prescribed positive page and unbounded rank.

The [extended Rees torsion comparison](../supplements/DELTA_EMBEDDING_NORMAL_AND_REES_GEOMETRY.md#7-consequences-limits-and-next-questions)
still needs a general identification of its \(u\)-power filtration
with the incoming differential filtration. The
[punctual relative-defect question under sole support](../FRONTIER.md#punctual-relative-defect-under-sole-support)
also remains open.
