# Sign conjugates and the arithmetic group-scheme orbit

**Scope and review.** This is a comparison of actual source algebras over
\(R=\mathbf Z_2\) and its two field characteristics. The explicit flat
line control and the coaction kernel were independently reconstructed on
November 23, 2026. The control is not supported on the fixed quartic and
does not extend the characteristic-different-from-two moment theorem.

## The full coaction retains parity at the prime two

Put \(S=R[z,w,x,y]\), with all coordinates of degree one, and use the
parity grading in which \(z,w\) are even and \(x,y\) are odd. The
group scheme
\[
 \mu_{2,R}=\operatorname{Spec}R[t]/(t^2-1)
\]
acts by this grading. For a homogeneous ideal \(J\), its schematic orbit
image has ideal
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 U_{\mu_2}=
 \ker\!\left[S\longrightarrow(S/J)\otimes_R R[t]/(t^2-1)\right]
 =(J\cap S_{\rm even})\oplus(J\cap S_{\rm odd}).
 \quad}                                                     \tag{AO1}
\]
Indeed the image of \(f_{\rm even}+f_{\rm odd}\) is
\(\overline f_{\rm even}\otimes1+\overline f_{\rm odd}\otimes t\),
and \(1,t\) are a free basis in every characteristic. This is the
largest parity-graded ideal contained in \(J\). The ideal generated by
all parity parts of elements of \(J\) is the smallest parity-graded
ideal containing it.

After inverting two, these ideals are respectively \(J\cap\tau J\)
and \(J+\tau J\), where
\(\tau(z,w,x,y)=(z,w,-x,-y)\). In characteristic two the pointwise
action of \(\tau\) is the identity, while the coaction (AO1) still
retains the two graded pieces. Thus the
[actual sign-conjugate source theorem](../torus/sign_symmetry_and_actual_sources.md#2-symmetrizing-an-actual-acm-source)
cannot simply be specialized using that single point action. Its
characteristic-different-from-two scope remains in force.

## A flat double line with a triple-line orbit

Take
\[
 J=(z+y,y^2),\qquad \tau J=(z-y,y^2).
\]
Each quotient is free on \(1,y\) over \(R[x,w]\), so each is a flat
homogeneous double-line CI. Direct calculation gives
\[
 J\cap\tau J=(z^2,zy,y^2),\qquad
 J+\tau J=(z+y,2y,y^2).                                  \tag{AO2}
\]
For the intersection, reduce modulo \((z^2,zy,y^2)\). A remainder
\(a+bz+cy\) vanishes modulo both \(J\) and \(\tau J\) exactly when
\(a=0\), \(c-b=0\), and \(c+b=0\). Since two is regular in
\(R[x,w]\), this forces \(b=c=0\). The sum formula is immediate.
Consequently the intersection quotient is free on \(1,z,y\), whereas
\[
 S/(J+\tau J)
 \simeq R[x,w]\oplus\bigl(R[x,w]/2\bigr)y,
 \qquad y^2=0.                                         \tag{AO3}
\]
The torsion is supported along the line, rather than at a cone vertex.

Modulo two, \(J_0=\tau J_0\). Its pointwise union is therefore only
the double line \(J_0\), while the reduction of the flat integral union
in (AO2) is the triple-line ideal \((z^2,zy,y^2)\). The schematic
\(\mu_2\) orbit of \(J_0\) is also that triple line: its coaction sends
\(z\) to \(y\otimes1\) and \(y\) to \(y\otimes t\). These images
are independent, although evaluation at the only geometric group point
identifies them. Evaluation of the integral group algebra at \(t=1,-1\)
has determinant \(-2\), which explains the lost direction after reduction.

This is an explicit failure of specializing the split pointwise
description. In this control the full group-scheme orbit image itself
does commute with reduction, as the displayed basis proves. No general
image-base-change theorem follows from this example.

## Relation to the prescribed quartic arithmetic orbit

The existing
[arithmetic four-section orbit](arithmetic_four_section_family.md#3-the-group-scheme-its-orbit-map-and-endpoint-base-change)
uses \(\mu_4\) and proves its schematic image from the actual free basis
\(1,\lambda,\lambda^2,\lambda^3\). In that specified family arithmetic
specialization preserves the image, while restriction to an endpoint
does not. The present control shows why retaining the finite group
scheme, its actual coordinate action and the image algebra also matters
when comparing sign-conjugate sources at two.

Neither comparison supplies a characteristic-two section/Rao obstruction.
The finite vertex defect in the sign-source theorem has different support
and hypotheses from the curve-supported torsion in (AO3). A coaction
retaining parity does not by itself extend the separable quartet or
matrix-moment argument to characteristic two.
