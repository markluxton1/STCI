# Supported Hilbert boundaries and generation of moving ideals

## Scope

The geometric application fixes an algebraically closed field \(k\),
the embedded curve
\(C=C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3_k\), its ideal
sheaf \(\mathcal I\), positive integers \(a,b,N\), and
\[
P_{a,b}(n)=abn+\frac{ab(4-a-b)}2.
\]
The action is
\(\lambda(t)[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]=[x_0:tx_1:t^3x_2:t^4x_3]\).
The supported-limit and graded-generator arguments work in every characteristic; the graded algebra
lemmas are stated over any field with a split torus where required.
The first three proof sections use no trace obstruction or
finite-modification theorem. The invariant-CI corollary additionally
uses the actual eigenform trace theorem STCI-R-0042.

This Argument supplies complete direct proof routes for
[STCI-R-0037](../results/supported_hilbert_full_limits.md),
[STCI-R-0038](../results/torus_eigenvector_generator_lifting.md), and
[STCI-R-0039](../results/moving_ideal_generator_criterion.md).
They are separate direct routes, with no circular dependence among
these Results. The invariant-supported-CI section gives the composite route for
[STCI-R-0040](../results/invariant_supported_ci_exclusion.md), jointly
using STCI-R-0038 and
[STCI-R-0042](../results/eigenform_trace_obstruction.md).
The shared geometric question is what happens to a whole
embedded ideal at its torus boundary, and what must be tested on a
moving ideal to obtain two actual equations. The attracting-locus
classification and the existence of a supported CI deformation are not
proved here.

The endpoint comparison R-0070 applies to any pure curve with reduced
support \(C\), with its own polynomial and no CI hypothesis. The subsequent
sextic application uses characteristic zero and the actual degree-six
equation space.

## Closed incidence and both full limits

We prove STCI-R-0037. Let
\(H=\operatorname{Hilb}^{P_{a,b}}(\mathbf P^3_k)\), with its universal
flat family \(\mathcal Y\subset\mathbf P^3\times H\) and universal
ideal \(\mathcal J\). The standard projective Hilbert theorem says
that \(H\) is projective and represents this flat-family functor. The
ideal \(\mathcal J\) is flat over \(H\): in its defining exact
sequence both the ambient structure sheaf and the universal quotient
are flat over \(H\).

Choose a sufficiently large integer \(m\). Relative Serre vanishing,
relative generation, and cohomology and base change give the following
properties simultaneously over all of \(H\):
\(\mathcal J(m)\) is relatively generated by its sections;
\(E=q_*\mathcal J(m)\) is a vector bundle whose fibers are
\(H^0(\mathcal I_Y(m))\); and
\[
0\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow S_m\otimes_k\mathcal O_H
 \longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow0
\]
is an exact sequence of vector bundles, where
\(S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\).
Indeed sufficiently large twists kill higher cohomology of every
fiber of the flat universal ideal and quotient, and their Euler
characteristics are fixed. The same \(m\) can be chosen so that the
fixed sheaves \(\mathcal I^N(m)\) and \(\mathcal I(m)\) are
globally generated and their degree-\(m\) sections are their usual
homogeneous ideal spaces. All these facts persist on base change.

Put \(V_N=H^0(\mathcal I^N(m))\) and
\(V_C=H^0(\mathcal I(m))\), as subspaces of \(S_m\). The vanishing
of the bundle maps
\[
V_N\otimes\mathcal O_H\longrightarrow Q,
\qquad
E\longrightarrow(S_m/V_C)\otimes\mathcal O_H
\tag{1}
\]
defines a closed subscheme \(H_N\subset H\). These equations express
the desired sheaf inclusions, not merely necessary conditions on
sections. For the first map, generation of \(\mathcal I^N(m)\)
shows that vanishing of its images in the quotient by \(\mathcal J\)
is equivalent to \(\mathcal I^N\subset\mathcal J\).
For the second map, relative generation of \(\mathcal J(m)\) shows
that all its sections lying in \(V_C\) is equivalent to
\(\mathcal J\subset\mathcal I\). Conversely either sheaf
inclusion induces the corresponding inclusion of section spaces and
makes the relevant map in (1) vanish. The evaluation maps and exact
bundle sequences commute with arbitrary base change, so this proves
the assertion for the incidence functor. In particular \(H_N\) is
projective, not just a closed set of geometric points. The use of a
sufficiently large \(m\) proves closedness; no explicit numerical
regularity bound for a proposed computational family is supplied by
this argument.

The parametrization shows
\(\lambda(t)C=C\), since the parameter changes from \([s:u]\) to
\([s:tu]\). Therefore \(\mathcal I\) and \(\mathcal I^N\)
are preserved. The functor just constructed is \(T\)-stable. Its
containments imply
\[
C\subset Y\subset V(\mathcal I^N),
\]
and the outer schemes have the same underlying support. Hence
\(Y_{\mathrm{red}}=C\) for every geometric point.

For any \(Y\in H_N(k)\), the orbit morphism
\(o_Y:\mathbf G_m\to H_N\) extends uniquely over \(0\) and
\(\infty\). Explicitly, each missing point of \(\mathbf P^1\)
has a discrete valuation local ring; properness gives an extension
over that ring, and separatedness gives uniqueness. These extensions
glue with \(o_Y\) to a morphism
\(\overline o_Y:\mathbf P^1\to H_N\). For \(s\in k^*\), the
two maps \(t\mapsto\lambda(s)\overline o_Y(t)\) and
\(t\mapsto\overline o_Y(st)\) agree on \(\mathbf G_m\), so agree
everywhere by separatedness. Evaluating at \(0\) and \(\infty\)
shows that both limits are \(T\)-fixed. Over the algebraically closed
field this is equivalently invariance under the algebraic torus, since
the action and orbit maps are morphisms and \(k^*\) is dense in
\(\mathbf G_m\). Pulling back the universal family gives a flat
embedded family over \(\mathbf P^1\). Its fibers have the prescribed
polynomial and the containments defining \(H_N\), including at the
two limits. The image of the connected scheme \(\mathbf P^1\)
is connected, so the original point and both limits lie in one
connected component of \(H_N\).

### Nilpotence and generic length

We complete the individual support and numerical assertions in
STCI-R-0037. If a projective scheme \(Y\) has reduced support \(C\),
then \(\sqrt{\mathcal I_Y}=\mathcal I\), so
\(\mathcal I_Y\subset\mathcal I\). On each affine chart the image
of \(\mathcal I\) in \(\mathcal O_Y\) is a finitely generated
nil ideal. If its generators \(u_i\) satisfy \(u_i^{e_i}=0\),
then every monomial of total degree
\(1+\sum_i(e_i-1)\) in these generators is zero. Thus that ideal
is nilpotent. A finite affine cover and the maximum of its exponents
give \(\mathcal I^N\subset\mathcal I_Y\) globally. The exponent
can depend on \(Y\). A proper pair of degrees \((a,b)\) is a
regular sequence in \(S\), with Koszul series
\((1-z^a)(1-z^b)/(1-z)^4\), hence polynomial \(P_{a,b}\).
If its reduced support is \(C\), the preceding argument places it
in some \(H_N\).

For completeness the generic multiplicity calculation does not require
that a boundary scheme be pure. Let \(J\) be the saturated homogeneous
ideal of any point of \(H_N\), and \(P=I_C\). Since its support is
\(C\), the radical of \(J\) is \(P\). A finite homogeneous prime
filtration of \(S/J\) has top-dimensional factors \(S/P\), up to
degree shifts; all other factors have dimension at most one as graded
modules and contribute only constants or zero to the projective Hilbert
polynomial. The number of top-dimensional factors is
\(\ell_{S_P}((S/J)_P)\), because localization at \(P\) kills the
other factors and each surviving factor has length one. Since \(C\)
has degree four, the linear coefficient of the Hilbert polynomial of
\(S/J\) is four times this length. Localization of the corresponding
degree-zero affine chart at the generic point of \(C\) has the same
length: passing to the homogeneous localization only adjoins the
invertible grading parameter and extends its residue field. Thus this
is the generic length \(m_C\) of \(\mathcal O_Y\) along \(C\).
Comparison with \(P_{a,b}\) gives \(4m_C=ab\). Substitution of
\(a=b=6\) gives \(P(n)=36n-144\), arithmetic genus \(145\),
and \(m_C=9\).

## Equivariant minimal-generator lifting

We prove STCI-R-0038 independently. Let a split torus act rationally
by graded automorphisms of \(S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]\), preserving the
homogeneous ideal \(J\). The spaces \(J_d\) and
\((S_+J)_d\) are finite-dimensional rational representations, and
their quotient is \(M(J)_d\). A rational representation of a split
torus is the direct sum of its character spaces: in coaction notation
the coefficient of each Laurent monomial in
\(k[T]=k[t_1^{\pm1},\ldots,t_q^{\pm1}]\) gives its weight
component. The coaction identity makes each component an eigenvector
and the counit makes their sum the original vector. This works over
any field and in every characteristic.

Choose a character basis of each \(M(J)_d\). Given a basis vector of
character \(\chi\), lift it first to any vector of \(J_d\), then
take the \(\chi\)-component of that lift. Equivariance of the quotient
map makes this new lift map to exactly the chosen basis vector. There
are only finitely many basis vectors: \(J\) is finitely generated,
and the images of any finite homogeneous generating set span
\(J/S_+J\).

Let \(K\) be the ideal generated by the chosen eigenvector lifts.
Their images span \(M(J)\), hence \(J=K+S_+J\). We verify equality
without a local or completed replacement. Induct on ordinary degree.
For \(f\in J_d\), its class modulo \(S_+J\) is a scalar linear
combination of the chosen degree-\(d\) lifts. Subtract this combination.
The remainder lies in \((S_+J)_d=S_1J_{d-1}\), because \(S\) is
standard graded. By induction its factors in \(J_{d-1}\) belong to
\(K\), so the remainder belongs to \(K\). The base degree is
immediate, and therefore \(J=K\). Removing one lift would fail to
span its nonzero basis class in \(M(J)\), so the generating set is
minimal and has exactly the degrees and characters claimed. Integer
characters remain distinct Laurent monomials in characteristic \(p\);
they have not been replaced by eigenvalues of a differentiated action.

## The moving ideal generator test

We prove STCI-R-0039 independently. For every homogeneous ideal of a
standard graded polynomial ring,
\[
(S_+J)_d=S_1J_{d-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
To see this, write a positive-degree polynomial multiplying an element
of \(J\) as a sum of monomials, and factor one variable from each
monomial. The remaining product already belongs to \(J_{d-1}\).
The reverse inclusion is immediate. Thus the module used in the
Statement is exactly \(J/S_+J\).

Suppose its graded dimensions count precisely the multiset
\(\{a,b\}\). Choose homogeneous lifts \(F,G\) of a graded basis.
The ordinary-degree induction just given can be repeated without any
torus: subtract the basis part in degree \(d\), and express the
remainder using (2) and the already generated degree \(d-1\).
It follows that \(J=(F,G)\), with the required degrees. Conversely,
if \(J=(F,G)\) has height two, both generators are minimal. Were one
redundant, \(J\) would be principal and have height at most one.
For equal degrees a nontrivial scalar dependence of their classes in
\(M(J)\) would make one redundant; for unequal degrees a vanishing
class of the larger generator would express it using the smaller.
Hence their classes are a graded basis and have exactly the asserted
dimensions.

Height two also proves that these equations are a regular sequence.
In the unique factorization domain \(S\), a common nonconstant
irreducible factor of \(F,G\) would put \(J\) inside a height-one
principal prime, contradicting its height. Thus their greatest common
divisor is one. The nonzero polynomial \(F\) is a nonzerodivisor in
\(S\). If \(GH\in(F)\), unique factorization and
\(\gcd(F,G)=1\) imply \(F\mid H\); this says precisely that
\(G\) is a nonzerodivisor modulo \(F\). The Koszul resolution
therefore gives
\[
P(n)=\binom{n+3}{3}-\binom{n-a+3}{3}
 -\binom{n-b+3}{3}+\binom{n-a-b+3}{3}
 =abn+\frac{ab(4-a-b)}2
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

For the conditional finite test, take a minimal graded free resolution
of the module \(J\). We use the usual convention
\(\operatorname{reg}_S J=\max\{j-i:\operatorname{Tor}_i^S(J,k)_j
\ne0\}\). Its degree-zero homological term records
\(\operatorname{Tor}_0^S(J,k)=J/S_+J=M(J)\). Thus a declared
bound \(\operatorname{reg}_S J\le r\) forces \(M(J)_d=0\) for
\(d>r\). Since \(r\ge\max(a,b)\), the required dimensions in
these degrees are also zero. Testing \(0\le d\le r\) is therefore
equivalent to testing every degree. Formula (2) gives the stated
matrix-rank expression. If, on a chosen base stratum, graded ideal
pieces are vector bundles commuting with base change, then the
multiplication maps specialize to precisely these fiber maps. Without
that qualification a rank computed in a relative presentation need
not be the stated fiberwise generator count. No uniform numerical
regularity bound has been computed here.

## Complete-intersection polynomial and a proper pencil

There is a useful simplification of the moving-generator test when the
**whole** saturated homogeneous ideal \(J\subset S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\)
has the complete-intersection Hilbert polynomial \(P_{a,b}\).
Suppose \(k\) is infinite and \(a\le b\). Then the following are
equivalent:

1. \(J\) is generated by a regular sequence of degrees \(a,b\).
2. There exist \(F\in J_a\), \(G\in J_b\) with no common
   irreducible factor.
3. \(J_a\ne0\) and the nonzero degree-\(b\) forms in \(J_b\)
   have no common irreducible factor.

For (2), \(K=(F,G)\) is a saturated codimension-two homogeneous
complete intersection with the same Hilbert polynomial as \(J\).
The inclusion \(K\subseteq J\) yields a coherent quotient
\(\widetilde J/\widetilde K\) with zero Hilbert polynomial, hence
zero sheaf. Saturation then gives \(K=J\). For (3), fix a nonzero
\(F\in J_a\). None of its finitely many irreducible factors divides
every form of \(J_b\). Over an infinite field, a member \(G\) can be
chosen outside the finite union of the corresponding proper linear
subspaces, proving (2). The remaining implications are immediate.

For \(a=b=6\), this says that on the **full CI-polynomial Hilbert
locus**, a proper pencil contained in \(J_6\) suffices to recover
the entire ideal. It does not say that a pair of limiting sextic
forms is proper, or that the degree-six piece alone always generates.
The criterion is already implicit in the standard Hilbert-polynomial
argument; its value here is to replace an unrestricted generator
search by a proper-pencil incidence on a specified Hilbert family.
This is distinct from checking the Chow cycle of an improper
equation limit.

## Clean cancellation and residual descent

The algebraic cancellation proof below also applies without a torus
action. Its [common-quadric application](equations/common_quadric_chow_boundary.md)
determines the full special scheme in every degree on the regular
cofactor locus; the associated local resultant graph is an ordinary blowup.

A torus limit of a proper degree-\((a,b)\) pair may acquire a
common initial factor even though its **full** weight-initial ideal
is flat. Write the two extreme forms as \(F_0=Hu\), \(G_0=Hv\),
where \(H\) is their full gcd, \(h=\deg H>0\), and
\(\gcd(u,v)=1\). The first nonzero coefficient of the normalized
cancellation \(vF_t-uG_t\) is a homogeneous eigenform
\(D_0\in(u,v)\) of ordinary degree \(a+b-h\); it belongs to the
full weight-initial ideal \(J_0\).

**Clean-cancellation proposition.** If \(\gcd(H,D_0)=1\), then
\[
 J_0=(Hu,Hv,D_0),\qquad
 [\operatorname{Proj}(S/J_0)]
   =[\operatorname{Proj}(S/(u,v))]
    +[\operatorname{Proj}(S/(H,D_0))].
\]
The first cycle is omitted if one of \(u,v\) is a unit.
The ideal \(J_0\) is saturated and arithmetically
Cohen--Macaulay, without embedded components.

Indeed, put \(U=(u,v)\), \(K=(HU,D_0)\).
The assumption that \((H,D_0)\) is a regular sequence,
together with \(D_0\in U\), gives the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow S/U(-h)
   \xrightarrow{\ H\ }S/K
   \longrightarrow S/(H,D_0)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The outer quotients are codimension-two complete intersections
when nonzero; the depth lemma makes \(S/K\) Cohen--Macaulay
of dimension two. Its Hilbert numerator is
\[
 z^h(1-z^{a-h})(1-z^{b-h})
 +(1-z^h)(1-z^{a+b-h})
 =1-z^a-z^b+z^{a+b}.
\]
Since \(K\subseteq J_0\) and the latter is the flat weight
initial ideal of the original complete intersection, equality
of Hilbert series forces \(K=J_0\).

The membership \(D_0\in U\) is essential in this exact sequence.
More generally its first term is \(S/(U+(D_0))(-h)\). The
[local length identity and counterexample](torus/equivariant_set_theoretic_descent.md#the-exact-local-length-identity-and-its-membership-hypothesis)
give the corrected arbitrary-ideal formulation; a generic local length
calculation alone still does not establish the full ideal equality above.

For the integral quartic \(C\), if an actual sole-supported
pair existed, both full torus limits would have radical
\(I_C\). In the clean case each nonempty summand above would
then be an eigenform complete intersection with reduced support
\(C\), contrary to the established eigenform trace obstruction
[R-0042](../results/eigenform_trace_obstruction.md) in
characteristic zero. Consequently, **in each direction**, the
first nonzero cancellation must satisfy
\(\gcd(H,D_0)\ne1\). This is a conditional necessary condition,
not an exclusion of moving pairs. When \(h=\min(a,b)\), the
unit-quotient case does not decrease the degree product;
even in the other cases, common factors can require further
parameter divisions and invalidate a naive residual induction.

The proposition is a full-ideal statement: merely taking the
intersection of extreme equation forms would miss the
parameter-torsion corrections. For the normalized family
\(A_t=S[t]/(F_t,G_t)\), its \(t\)-torsion
\(\mathcal D=H^0_{(t)}(A_t)\) fits into
\(0\to\mathcal D\to A_t\to B_t\to0\), where
\(B_t\) is the flat contracted family. Since \(B_t\) is
\(t\)-flat, specialization yields
\(\mathcal D/t\mathcal D\simeq J_0/(F_0,G_0)\).
This **presentation-dependent vertical excess** is not the
intrinsic \(I_C\)-adic comparison defect R-0030; the two
filtrations must not be identified without a compatible
double-Rees comparison.

## A four-generator full limit with an embedded point

A useful control, distinct from the existing **ACM** sextic
three-generator residual example, uses the same weights
\((0,1,3,4)\) and the renaming
\(v=x_0,\ x=x_1,\ z=x_2,\ y=x_3\).
The proper \((2,2)\) intersection
\[
 J=(x^2,xy+z^2)
\]
has radical \((x,z)\), the invariant line \(L\). Its normalized
minimum-weight family begins with \((x^2,xy+t z^2)\).
Saturation with respect to \(t\) gives
\[
 \mathcal K=(x^2,xy+t z^2,xz^2,z^4),\qquad
 K=\mathcal K|_{t=0}=(x^2,xy,xz^2,z^4).
\]
The identities
\(yx^2-x(xy+t z^2)=-t xz^2\) and
\(z^2(xy+t z^2)-y(xz^2)=t z^4\)
produce the extra generators. A monic Gröbner-basis
calculation over \(k[t]\) verifies that this is the entire
\(t\)-saturated family, in any characteristic.

The special ideal is saturated but not Cohen--Macaulay:
\[
 K=(x,z^4)\cap(x^2,y,z^2).
\]
Its pure hull is \((x,z^4)\), while the second component
is an embedded point at \([v:x:y:z]=[1:0:0:0]\)
of length two. As a graded \(k[v,y]\)-module,
\[
 S/K\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^3 \bigl(k[v,y]\bigr)(-i)
 \ \oplus\ \bigl(k[v]\bigr)(-1)\ \oplus\ \bigl(k[v]\bigr)(-2).
\]
Consequently the pure hull has Hilbert polynomial \(4n-2\),
but the full limit has the original CI polynomial \(4n\).
Its minimal graded free resolution has shifts
\[
 F_1=S(-2)^2\oplus S(-3)\oplus S(-4),\quad
 F_2=S(-3)\oplus S(-4)^2\oplus S(-5),\quad
 F_3=S(-5).
\]
Thus the Betti totals change from \((1,2,1)\) to
\((1,4,4,1)\), with consecutive cancellations restoring
the generic Koszul table. The opposite full limit is
\((x^2,z^2)\), so the two directions have different
embedded and generator behavior.

This example is sole-supported **on a line**, not on our
quartic. It demonstrates that the full Hilbert polynomial
can require punctual embedded structure and that the
least degree product of a proper homogeneous pair inside
the limit can increase: from \(4\) in \(J\) to \(8\) in \(K\),
attained by \((x^2,z^4)\). In particular it invalidates
any unconditional degree-product descent through torus
limits. It does **not** contradict weak set-theoretic
descent: \((x^2,z^4)\subset K\) still has radical \((x,z)\).
It also does not replace the supported-quartic
generator-incidence test.

## Equation–Hilbert–Chow graph and its top fiber

For fixed degrees \((a,b)\), let \(U\) be the open locus of proper
pairs containing \(C\), in the ordered equation space (or
\(\operatorname{Gr}(2,I_a)\) when \(a=b\)). The intersection map
\(f:U\to\operatorname{Hilb}^{P_{a,b}}(\mathbf P^3)\) is a morphism.
Its reduced graph closure in the product with the projective
equation space retains **both** the limiting equations and the
full limiting ideal. A boundary equation pair can be improper
even when the Hilbert coordinate is a flat limit of proper CIs.
If the boundary pair is proper, the inclusion of its ideal in
the Hilbert-coordinate ideal and equality of Hilbert polynomials
force equality; hence every extra-generator graph limit lies
over the improper-pair boundary.

Let \(\chi:U\to\mathbf P(E_{ab})\) be the universal-line Chow
resultant map, with \(E_e=H^0(\operatorname{Gr}(2,4),
\mathcal O(e))\), and let \(Q\in E_4\) be the Chow form of
\(C\). In characteristic zero the reduced **triple graph**
\[
 \widehat\Gamma=
 \overline{\{(x,f(x),\chi(x)):x\in U\}}
 \subset X\times\operatorname{Hilb}^{P_{a,b}}
               \times\mathbf P(E_{ab})
\]
retains the equation coordinate, full embedded scheme and
specialized fundamental cycle. When \(4\mid ab\), put
\(m=ab/4\) and take the top Chow fiber over \([Q^m]\).

This top fiber must not be confused with the closure of the
graph of **supported** proper pairs. A residual pair can
specialize to the top Chow cycle without its generic fibers
being supported. Nevertheless, each Hilbert coordinate of the
top fiber has connected underlying scheme: over a DVR trait
through a graph boundary, the proper flat family of connected
complete intersections has
\(H^0(Y_\eta,\mathcal O)=K\); the finite \(A\)-algebra
\(H^0(Y,\mathcal O)\) embeds in \(K\) and equals the
integrally closed DVR \(A\). Stein factorization yields
geometrically connected fibers. In characteristic zero the
top Chow coordinate gives one-dimensional support \(C\);
connectedness then excludes isolated components away from
\(C\). This does not imply primary structure or the uniform
containment \(I_C^m\subset J\) for every boundary point.

The **CI locus** inside the top fiber is exactly the
fixed-degree supported-CI existence test: if its Hilbert
coordinate is an actual CI, purity and the top cycle force
sole support \(C\), even if the displayed equation coordinate
is improper. Conversely every actual supported pair already
lies in the open graph. Thus the incidence
\[
 \widehat\Gamma_{[Q^m]}\cap
 p_H^{-1}(\mathrm{CI}_{a,b})
\]
is nonempty exactly when a supported type-\((a,b)\)
complete intersection exists. This is an exact reformulation,
not a proof of nonemptiness or emptiness.

For an **actual** sole-supported proper CI, a stronger uniform
power bound holds: its ideal is \(I_C\)-primary with generic
transverse length \(m\), hence
\(I_C^{(m)}\subset J\) and \(I_C^m\subset J\).
The first inclusion uses primary contraction and the fact that
the \(m\)-th power of the maximal ideal of a length-\(m\)
Artinian local algebra vanishes. The containment persists in
flat limits **within this supported family**, not for arbitrary
points of the top Chow fiber. This is the distinction between
the supported graph closure and the larger top-cycle fiber.

## Reconciled Chow filtration and finite sextic weight partition

These fixed-degree statements concern **actual proper pairs**, not arbitrary
Hilbert-boundary ideals. They reconcile the November 1 torus-homogeneous-descent
work with the current equation–Hilbert–Chow argument.

Let \(C\subset\mathbf P^3\) be integral of degree \(\delta\),
\(D=ab\), \(G=\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)\), and
\(E_n=H^0(G,\mathcal O_G(n))\). Write \(Q=\operatorname{Ch}_C\in E_\delta\).
On the open locus \(U\) of coprime ordered containing pairs, the
universal-line resultant defines \(\chi:U\to\mathbf P(E_D)\).
For \(r\delta\le D\), the locus of generic multiplicity at least \(r\)
along \(C\) is exactly
\[
 U_{\ge r}=\chi^{-1}\bigl(\mathbf P(Q^r E_{D-r\delta})\bigr).
\]
Multiplication by \(Q^r\) is injective because \(G\) is integral;
factorization into irreducible Chow divisors identifies divisibility
by \(Q^r\) with the generic cycle multiplicity. Thus \(U_{\ge r}\)
is closed **relative to \(U\)**, multiplicity is upper semicontinuous,
and residual degree \(D-\delta m_C\) is lower semicontinuous there.
The exact multiplicity strata are locally closed. No assertion extends
across the common-factor boundary.

In bases of \(E_D/(Q^r E_{D-r\delta})\), these conditions become
finitely many polynomial resultant-coefficient equations. If \(B\)
is the ideal of all resultant coefficients and \(L_r\) the ideal of
the quotient coefficients on the projective presentation space,
the scheme-theoretic closure of the incidence subscheme on
\(X\setminus V(B)=U\) is given by \(L_r:B^\infty\), with the
usual homogeneous sheaf interpretation. Its reduction is the
set-theoretic closure of the geometric incidence locus; the
incidence subscheme itself need not be reduced. Using \(V(L_r)\)
alone admits spurious improper-boundary components.

If \(\delta\mid D\), put \(M=D/\delta\). Choose a linear functional
\(L:E_D\to k\) with \(L(Q^M)\ne0\). The section
\(s=L(R_{F,G})\) of the ample bundle \(\mathcal O(b,a)\) on
\(X=\mathbf P(I_C(a))\times\mathbf P(I_C(b))\) has affine
nonvanishing locus \(X_s\). Every supported proper pair lies in
this **single** chart, and its locus is the closed fiber
\(\chi^{-1}([Q^M])\cap X_s\), hence affine. This is an exact fixed-degree existence problem entirely
inside an affine scheme excluding improper pairs; projective equation
and Hilbert graph closures remain useful for degeneration obstructions,
not prerequisites for defining this incidence. For equal degrees
\(R_{A(F,G)}=\det(A)^dR_{F,G}\) gives the analogous assertion
on \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,I_C(d))\) with Plücker power
\(\mathcal O(d)\). This does not imply affineness of the supported
Hilbert locus or an unspecified unequal-degree quotient.

For the fixed quartic with centered torus weights \((-2,-1,1,2)\),
the sextic equation space \(V_6=I_C(6)\) has dimension \(59\).
Its nonzero **uncentered** weight-space dimensions, from weights
\(3\) through \(21\), are
\[
(1,2,2,3,3,4,4,4,4,5,4,4,4,4,3,3,2,2,1).
\]
Indeed a degree-six monomial of weight \(j\) restricts to the same
binary monomial \(s^{24-j}t^j\); the weight-\(j\) kernel has
dimension \(n_j-1\), where \(n_j\) counts such monomials.
Thus \(X_6=\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_6)\) has dimension \(114\).
Nonzero Plücker weights cover all integers \(-17,\ldots,17\)
in centered coordinates, allowing a repeated summand only when
its weight space has dimension at least two. These are 35
*ambient candidate* walls, with 34 intervening chambers and
at most \(35\cdot36/2=630\) extremal-weight blocks. They
are not a classification of supported geometric families.

For a pencil \(W\), let \(\ell(W)\) and \(u(W)\) be the extreme
weights of its nonzero Plücker components. With linearization
convention that weight \(r\) in \(\mathcal O(q)\) shifts to
\(qr-k\), and \(\theta=k/q\), the torus criterion gives
\[
 W\text{ is }\theta\text{-semistable}
 \iff \ell(W)\le\theta\le u(W).
\]
Strict inequalities give stability. The existing centered Chow
argument forces \(\ell(W)\le0\le u(W)\) for supported proper
sextic pencils, strictly in characteristic zero by the established eigenform
obstruction R-0042. Consequently supported proper pencils are stable
for the centered character-zero linearization in characteristic zero;
stability is necessary, not sufficient for support. This conclusion
concerns moving proper pencils, not improper limiting equation
coordinates. Thus a full chamber sweep is **not** needed to cover
supported pairs. The finite partition can guide targeted residual
and graph-boundary comparisons, but neither certifies support nor
classifies full Hilbert limits.

## Invariant residual supports and absorption limits

For the intrinsic diagonal torus with distinct weights
\((0,1,3,4)\), every reduced irreducible invariant curve
is the closure of a nonfixed orbit. If its nonzero coordinate
support is \(I\), its degree is
\[
 \frac{\max_{i\in I}w_i-\min_{i\in I}w_i}
      {\gcd_{i\in I}(w_i-\min_{j\in I}w_j)}.
\]
Its normalization is \(\mathbf P^1\), with boundary points
the coordinate points at the two extreme weights. The
possible support degrees are \(1\) (two coordinates),
\(3\) (supports \(012,123\)), and \(4\) (supports \(013,023,0123\)).
The coefficients of a full-support orbit quartic still
vary in a two-dimensional family; this is not a finite
classification of fixed ideals.

If \(D\ne C\) is an invariant irreducible curve, its
intersection with \(C\) is a finite invariant subset
and hence is contained in \(\{p_0,p_\infty\}\).
The **only** invariant irreducible curve disjoint from
\(C\) is the coordinate line \(L_{12}=V(x_0,x_3)\).
This does not imply that every residual component of
an invariant proper intersection meets \(C\): connectedness
requires only a chain of components linking it to \(C\).

An explicit counterexample is the proper invariant
\((3,4)\) complete intersection
\[
 F=x_0q,\quad G=x_3A,\qquad
 q=x_0x_3-x_1x_2,\quad A=x_0^2x_2-x_1^3.
\]
Its fundamental cycle is
\[
 [V(F,G)]=[C]+5[L_{23}]+[L_{02}]+[L_{01}]+[L_{12}].
\]
Indeed, distributivity of the two effective Cartier
divisors gives \(V(x_0,x_3)=L_{12}\),
\(V(x_0,A)=3L_{23}\),
\(V(q,x_3)=L_{02}+L_{01}\), and
\(V(q,A)=C+2L_{23}\). The disjoint line
\(L_{12}\) is connected to \(C\) through other lines.
Since the ideal is itself invariant, repeating this
torus degeneration cannot absorb its residual curves.

The marked multiplicity of \(C\) is **nondecreasing**
under flat specialization of a containing intersection:
if the generic cycle is \(m[C]+R\), then
\[
 [Y_0]=m[C]+\operatorname{sp}(R),\qquad
 m_0=m+\operatorname{coeff}_C\operatorname{sp}(R).
\]
Residual degree therefore drops by a multiple of four
when marked multiplicity increases. This is a statement
about fundamental cycles, not primary components or
embedded Hilbert structure. Invariant full-support
quartics can move in a two-dimensional diagonal-torus
family and specialize to \(C\) **as cycles**, but such
motion does not automatically lift to a flat family of
proper two-equation intersections. This is the actual
absorption-realization gap.

## What the boundary retains and what remains open

All assertions about full limits above concern the embedded Hilbert
family and its whole ideal sheaf. At a fiber the homogeneous ideal is
the saturated ideal of that scheme. In a coordinate weight
calculation one must retain all cancellation relations and any new
generators, and then the relevant projective saturation. Taking just
two extreme forms has no such guarantee. Even the invariant ideal
\((x_0,x_1)\), displayed as \((x_0+x_1,x_1)\), has two highest
weight forms equal to \(x_1\) under weights \((0,1,3,4)\), although
the full ideal contains \(x_0\) and its orbit is constant. This simple
control concerns a line, not a supported quartic example.

An \(\mathcal I\)-adic normal-cone construction uses a different
filtration and lives in the associated graded normal algebra. A
fundamental cycle keeps generic multiplicities but discards embedded
points and much nilpotent structure. Neither object replaces the full
Hilbert limit used in STCI-R-0037. The boundary may lose two-generation;
STCI-R-0038 concerns only ideals already invariant, and STCI-R-0039
must be applied to the moving ideal when the desired equations are
moving. Excluding invariant eigenform pairs therefore does not by
itself exclude a moving supported CI whose full limit has additional
generators.

For a fixed \(H_N\), one can label each point set-theoretically by
its two opposite limits and ask where the moving generator criterion
holds. No proof here makes those labels into a finite or locally
closed stratification, realizes any prescribed pair of limits, or
shows that the two-generator subset is empty or nonempty. The comparison
below determines the pure parts and embedded lengths of the two limits.
Recovery of the full ideals and a coverage argument for moving generation
remain separate problems.

## Invariant supported complete intersections

We prove the composite corollary STCI-R-0040. Let \(k\) be
algebraically closed and let \(J\subset k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\) be a
homogeneous height-two complete-intersection ideal whose radical is
the prime of the fixed quartic \(C\). Assume \(J\) itself is
invariant under the torus of weights \((0,1,3,4)\), and let
\(\{a,b\}\) be the positive ordinary degrees of its minimal
homogeneous generators. A homogeneous regular sequence of length
two is minimal: redundancy would make the height at most one.
Consequently its graded minimal-generator module has the two basis
degrees \(\{a,b\}\).

Apply STCI-R-0038 to this stable ideal. It lifts a character basis of
the minimal-generator module to weight-eigenforms \(F,G\) of precisely
those ordinary degrees, generating the entire ideal \(J\). This
step preserves the ideal and the degrees, rather than replacing the
equations by initial forms. Since \((F,G)=J\) and \(\sqrt J=I_C\),
their actual projective intersection has reduced support exactly
\(C\). The field is algebraically closed, the degrees are positive,
and both equations are eigenforms for the required integer weights.
Thus they satisfy the actual-pair eigenform hypotheses of
STCI-R-0042. Its trace conclusion is \(ab=0\) in \(k\).

In characteristic zero this contradicts the positive integers
\(a,b\). In characteristic \(p>0\), it gives \(p\mid ab\);
since \(p\) is prime, at least one of \(a,b\) is a positive
multiple of \(p\), so \(\max(a,b)\ge p\). This proves the
corollary in exactly the asserted range. No factor-only trace
variant is used. In characteristic two, no inference about the
parity of the generic multiplicity is made.

This conclusion requires ideal invariance. The radical \(I_C\)
is invariant for every sole-supported thickening, but that says
nothing about a weight decomposition of its own ideal. The full
fixed limits in STCI-R-0037 can need more than two generators, so
their invariance does not contradict a hypothetical moving supported
CI. The corollary supplies no reduction from arbitrary pairs to
invariant pairs.

## Endpoint fibers in the two intrinsic limits

Take a pure \(Z\) with \(Z_{\mathrm{red}}=C\) and generic length \(m\).
Use \(r,u,v\) on \(\Omega\) as in
[the torus chart](../frameworks/torus-supported-boundaries/weights_and_limits.md#intrinsic-and-transverse-actions).
Since \((u-1,v-1)^m\) vanishes on the generic normal algebra, it vanishes
on \(Z\cap\Omega\) by purity: any resulting section supported on
finitely many points of the pure curve is zero. Its full closure \(W\) in
\(\mathbf P^1_r\times\mathbf G_m^2\) lies in this fixed finite neighborhood.
On the interior purity gives torsion-freeness over \(k[r,r^{-1}]\), and
contraction to the endpoint charts gives torsion-freeness there. Thus \(W\)
is finite flat of rank \(m\). The
[projective parametrization](torus/tropical_thickenings_and_endpoint_duality.md#a-reduced-tropical-model-and-the-exact-normal-map)
factors through \(Z\) because the equations of \(Z\) vanish on the
schematically dense open of \(W\) over the torus. The map \(\rho:W\to Z\)
is proper and is an isomorphism on reduced supports, so it is finite.
The kernel of
\(\mathcal O_Z\to\rho_*\mathcal O_W\) is zero by purity. The pullback of
\(\mathcal O_Z(1)\) is \(\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(4)\). In the notation
of [R-0070](../results/intrinsic_limits_endpoint_fibers.md), this gives

\[
P_Z(n)=4mn+m+h-\delta_0-\delta_\infty.
\]

For the pullback \(r\mapsto\lambda r\), pull \(W\) back by
\((r,\lambda)\mapsto\lambda r\) on the finite \(r\)-chart. This family is
finite locally free. On \(\Omega\times\mathbf A^1_\lambda\) it agrees with
the projective orbit family over \(\lambda\ne0\). Both are the
\(\lambda\)-torsion-free closure there, so the restriction of the full
limit \(L_0\) is the constant family of \(B_0\). For the inverse pullback
use the map \((r,\lambda)\mapsto\tau=\lambda/r\) on \(\Omega\), giving \(B_\infty\).
Choose \(\mathcal I^N\subset\mathcal I_Z\subset\mathcal I\). The
intrinsic action preserves \(\mathcal I\), so contraction from the
punctured parameter trait preserves both containments, as does
specialization. Thus the support stays \(C\). Schematic
closure of the common dense open gives \(Y_i\subset L_i\), where \(Y_i\)
is the pure constant projective image of R-0051. The kernel \(T_i\) of
\(\mathcal O_{L_i}\to\mathcal O_{Y_i}\) is supported on the endpoints.
Purity of \(Y_i\) makes this the maximal finite-length subsheaf. By R-0052
and flatness, its total length is

\[
\ell(T_i)=D_0(B_i)+D_\infty(B_i)-\delta_0-\delta_\infty+h. \tag{3}
\]

To recover the two lengths separately, use \(p_0\) for the pullback
\(r\mapsto\lambda r\). Put \(R=k[\lambda,r]\) and let \(M\) be the finite
free \(R\)-algebra obtained from \(W\) by the pullback on the finite chart.
Write \(U=u-1,\ V=v-1\) in it. The image of the affine ambient coordinate
ring is

\[
A=R[r^3U,r^4V]\subset M,\qquad N=M/A.
\]

Over \(\lambda\ne0\), the substitution \(r'=\lambda r\) identifies this
with the moving affine image. Its weighted generators differ from the
original ones by powers of the unit \(\lambda\). The subalgebra \(A\)
of \(M\) is \(\lambda\)-torsion-free. Thus the kernel of the ambient
coordinate map to \(A\) is the contraction of its localization at
\(\lambda\), and \(A\) is the coordinate algebra of the full orbit
closure on this chart. The algebra \(M\) is generated as an \(R\)-module by
the monomials \(U^aV^b\) with \(a+b<m\): the nilpotent ideal has
its \(m\)-th power zero in the rank-\(m\) fibers. Multiplying each
such monomial by \(r^{4(m-1)}\) puts it in \(A\). Hence \(N\)
is supported on \(r=0\) and is finite over \(k[\lambda]\). The sequence after reduction is

\[
0\to N[\lambda]\longrightarrow A/\lambda A
 \longrightarrow M/\lambda M\longrightarrow N/\lambda N\to0. \tag{4}
\]

The connecting map takes a class \([b]\in N[\lambda]\) represented
by \(b\in M\) to \([\lambda b]\in A/\lambda A\). The image in
\(M/\lambda M=B_0[r]\) is the constant-image chart of \(Y_0\), so
\(N[\lambda]\) is the actual kernel there. The generic
\(k[\lambda]\)-rank of \(N\) is \(\delta_0\) by rescaling \(r\).
Its fiber at zero has dimension \(D_0(B_0)\). The free and cyclic
torsion decomposition over \(k[\lambda]_{(\lambda)}\) gives
\(\ell(T_{0,p_0})=D_0(B_0)-\delta_0\). Subtracting this length from (3)
gives \(\ell(T_{0,p_\infty})=D_\infty(B_0)-\delta_\infty+h\).

For the inverse pullback near \(p_\infty\), use the actual projective
parameter \(z=\tau u/v=x_2/x_3\) and the transverse units
\(\alpha=v^2/u^3,\ \beta=v^3/u^4\). This invertible change on the
upstairs chart preserves the ideal of the reduced section, giving
finiteness over \(k[z]\). For each nonzero polynomial \(P\), the
difference \(P(z)-P(\tau)\) is nilpotent on \(W\). Since \(P(\tau)\)
acts injectively, so does \(P(z)\), by taking a power of \(P(\tau)\)
on any element annihilated by \(P(z)\). The algebra is therefore
free over \(k[z]\). Its fiber at \(z=0\)
equals the fiber at \(\tau=0\), since \(u/v\) is a unit. The action scales
\(z\) by \(\lambda\) and fixes \(\alpha,\beta\). Repeating (4) with
\(z^3(\alpha-1),z^4(\beta-1)\) gives \(D_\infty(B_\infty)-\delta_\infty\)
at \(p_\infty\), and (3) gives \(D_0(B_\infty)-\delta_0+h\) at \(p_0\).
In particular (4) tests image base change on the chart whose parameter
is multiplied by \(\lambda\).

For computation over either endpoint DVR with parameter \(z_*\), take
the column vectors \(e_{ab}(z_*)\) of the local monomials \(U_*^aV_*^b\)
in a basis of the free algebra. Here \((U_*,V_*)\) is the chart's pair
of transverse coordinates centered at the support. The local index is
the valuation of \(\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M/A)\). It is the minimum, over
all \(m\)-element sets of columns, of
\(\sum(3a+4b)+\operatorname{ord}_{z_*}\det(e_{ab}(z_*))\), with zero
minors omitted. The formula follows from the Smith form of the lattice
generated by the weighted columns. The constant index uses the same
minimum with only the minors nonzero on the fiber and with no determinant
valuation. This recovers the inequality at the matched endpoint directly
and retains information about relations which become dependent there.
[Fitting ideals and base change](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07ZA).

### A finite image with embedded points in the limit

Over characteristic zero take the moving length-three algebra
\[
k[r,U,V]/(U^3,V-rU^2),\qquad
k[\tau,U,V]/(U^2-\tau V,UV,V^2)
\]
on the two charts with \(\tau=r^{-1}\). The bases \((1,U,U^2)\) and
\((1,U,V)\) show freeness and \(h=1\), using \(V=rU^2\) on the overlap.
Its pure projective image has \((\delta_0,\delta_\infty)=(8,7)\).
At zero the nonconstant summands are generated by \(r^3U,r^5U^2\).
At infinity \(U^2=zV\), and \(\alpha-1=-3U+(2+6z)V\),
\(\beta-1=-4U+(3+10z)V\) form a basis of the nilpotent summand over
the endpoint DVR. All their products have weight at least six, so
the index there is \(3+4=7\).

The constant indices are \((D_0,D_\infty)(B_0)=(9,7)\) and
\((D_0,D_\infty)(B_\infty)=(7,7)\). For \(B_0\), the basis at zero
uses weights \(3,6\). At infinity the coefficients of \(\alpha-1,\beta-1\)
in \((U,U^2)\) have determinant \(-6\) and give weights \(3,4\).
For \(B_\infty\) the maximal ideal is square-zero of dimension two
and both frames give weights \(3,4\). Thus \(L_0\) has embedded length
one at each endpoint, whereas \(L_\infty\) is pure. At the matched chart
of \(L_0\) the image in \(k[\lambda,r,U]/(U^3)\) is
\[
k[\lambda,r]\oplus r^3k[\lambda,r]U
     \oplus(r^6,\lambda r^5)k[\lambda,r]U^2.
\]
The class of \(\lambda r^5U^2\) in its fiber is nonzero and killed by \(r\).
This example uses a pure scheme image and makes no CI hypothesis.

### Necessary endpoint conditions for a sextic pair

Suppose an actual characteristic-zero \((6,6)\) CI has sole reduced
support \(C\). Then \(m=9\), \(P_Z(n)=36n-144\), and
\(\delta_0+\delta_\infty=153+h\) with \(h>0\) by the
[strict movement theorem](torus/tropical_thickenings_and_endpoint_duality.md#positive-movement-and-the-global-presentation-barrier).
By (3) each embedded endpoint fiber \(B\), viewed in both quotient
frames, must satisfy
\[
D_0(B)+D_\infty(B)\ \ge\ 153. \tag{5}
\]
In particular its maximal ideal has nonzero fourth power. Otherwise the
monomials of total degree at most three span in each frame and their
weights sum to \(70\), giving a total index at most \(140\).

Suppose \(B\) is curvilinear of length nine. In either frame write
\((x,y)=(u-1,v-1)\) or \((\alpha-1,\beta-1)\), with weights \(3,4\).
If \(x\) is a parameter, choose \(c\in k\) so that \(y-cx\) has order
at least two, and write \(j=\operatorname{ord}(y-cx)\), with \(j=9\)
for the zero element. The nine elements \(x^a(y-cx)^b\) indexed by
\(a+jb=0,\ldots,8,\ 0\le a<j\), form a basis by their distinct
orders. Their image weights are at most \(3a+4b\). If \(x\) has zero
linear term, use \(y\) as parameter, set \(j=\operatorname{ord}x\)
with \(j=9\) for \(x=0\), and use \(y^ax^b\) of weights \(4a+3b\).
Summing gives the following upper
bounds for the index of the indicated frame.

| \(j\) | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(x\) a parameter | 76 | 63 | 60 | 64 | 66 | 74 | 88 | 108 |
| \(x\) of order at least two | 64 | 63 | 66 | 76 | 81 | 94 | 115 | 144 |

If the slower coordinate is a parameter in both frames, (5) forces
\(j\ge8\) in at least one frame. If the slower coordinate in one frame has
order \(j\ge2\), the slower coordinate in the other frame has nonzero
linear coefficient \(2\) in \(y\). For \(j=2\), (5) requires \(j'=9\)
in that other frame, since \(64+88=152\). For \(j\ge3\), the opposite
coordinates \((X,Y)\) agree to second order with
\(((1+y)^2-1,(1+y)^3-1)\), so the opposite order is two and \(j=3,4,5\)
are excluded. For \(j=6\) the same agreement through order five yields
a better bound. Put \(d=Y-\tfrac32X\), \(d_3=X^2-\tfrac83d\),
\(d_4=Xd-\tfrac94d_3\), \(d_5=d^2-\tfrac95d_4\).
The elements \(1,X,d,d_3,d_4,d_5,d^3,Xd^3,d^4\) have orders \(0\)
through \(8\) and weights bounded by \(0,3,4,6,7,8,12,15,16\).
Their sum is \(71\), so \(81+71=152\) also excludes \(j=6\).
The change of frames is involutive, so these tests apply at either end.

The degree-six equation space gives an additional restriction. Let \(K\)
be the saturated ideal of the pure constant image of \(B\). The full
homogeneous initial ideal of a \((6,6)\) pair has two independent sextics,
by taking the associated graded of its degree-six piece. They survive
irrelevant saturation and vanish on the pure quotient. Thus
\(\dim K_6\ge2\). Suppose in a frame with \(x\) a parameter the order
\(j\) is eight, with \(c\ne1\). By reversal it is enough to use the
\((u,v)\)-frame. Each weight sextic restricts to \(r^wP(u,v)\) with
\(\deg P\le6\). If it vanishes on \(B\), substituting \(v=1+cx\),
\(u=1+x\) gives a polynomial divisible by \(x^8\), hence identically
zero. The constant image is invariant, so this applies separately to
every weight component. For \(c\ne1\), the inverse image of the line
\(v=1+c(u-1)\) in \(\Omega\) is dense in \(V(H)\), where
\[
H=x_0^3x_3-cx_0^2x_1x_2-(1-c)x_1^4 .
\]
The equation \(H\) is irreducible by primitivity and linearity in
\(x_3\), so every sextic in \(K_6\) is divisible by \(H\).
Its restriction to the embedded family is \(r^4(y-cx)\), of exact
order eight. A quadric multiplier annihilates that class precisely
when it vanishes on \(C\). Since \((I_C)_2\) is spanned by the containing
quadric \(q=x_0x_3-x_1x_2\), this gives \(K_6=kHq\), a contradiction.
For \(c=1\) the orbit surface is the quadric itself and the same
argument gives \(K_6=q(I_C)_4\). Thus the order-eight case in a frame
with \(x\) a parameter can occur only in the quadric direction.

More generally the whole space \(K_6\) of either endpoint image has a
fixed divisor through \(C\). A coprime pair in \(K_6\) would realize
the invariant pure image as a CI by degree exhaustion, contradicting
R-0040. A linear space of forms of dimension at least two over the
infinite field \(k\) has a coprime pair if its gcd is a unit, by fixing
one nonzero form and avoiding the finitely many subspaces imposed by
its irreducible factors. If the gcd \(D\) did not vanish on \(C\),
it would be regular on the pure image and \(K_6/D\) would still consist
of equations through that image. A coprime pair there would contain a
degree-\(36\) curve in a proper intersection of degree
\((6-\deg D)^2<36\). This proves that \(D\) vanishes on \(C\).

Independent reconstruction checked (5), the two weighted basis bounds,
the opposite-frame refinements, and the degree-six exclusion for
\(j=8,\ c\ne1\). These are necessary conditions for each embedded
endpoint fiber. The all-pair
[sextic reduction](carriers/sextic_pair_reduction.md#why-every-pair-is-generically-curvilinear)
gives generic curvilinearity on the curve, so noncurvilinear endpoint
fibers with nonzero fourth power remain among the possibilities.

## Conclusion

For each fixed \((a,b),N\), the supported incidence is a closed
projective torus-stable Hilbert locus, and both full flat torus limits
remain in it with unchanged polynomial and reduced support. A
torus-invariant ideal admits minimal eigenform generators, whereas
two-generation of a moving height-two ideal is exactly its full
graded minimal-generator condition, with a finite cutoff only under
the stated bound. These three direct proofs require no coordinate
computation. Combining equivariant lifting with the separate trace
theorem excludes invariant supported CI ideals in characteristic
zero and forces \(p\mid ab\) in characteristic \(p>0\).
For a pure supported scheme the two intrinsic limits have pure quotients
given by the two embedded endpoint fibers, with explicit embedded lengths.
This imposes both defect and source-space conditions on a sole-support
sextic pair. The full opposite-endpoint algebra and moving generator
incidence remain open.

## Scope and references

The supported Hilbert construction gives neither a uniform nilpotence exponent nor a numerical generator cutoff, classification, nonempty incidence, or quotient comparison. The invariant-ideal exclusion does not reduce arbitrary supported pairs to invariant ideals.

## Degree-12 relation persistence for the residual sextic pencil

This finite calculation is a **residual control**, not a supported STCI
construction. Work over \(\mathbb Q\) with centered weights
\((-2,-1,1,2)\), and set
\[
q=x_0x_3-x_1x_2,\quad A=x_0^2x_2-x_1^3,\quad
g=x_0^3x_2q,\quad
a_\tau=x_0^2x_3A+\tau qx_0A.
\]
The proper sextic pencils are
\(W_\tau=\langle q^3+a_\tau,g\rangle\), for \(\tau=0,1\).
Both have coarse Plücker extremes \([-10,-5]\). Their common
negative full limit is \((q^3,g,x_0^5x_2x_3A)\), since
\((a_1-a_0)x_0^3x_2=(x_0A)g\); this is the already established
Hilbert--Burch limit. Their positive degree-12 characters differ.

The smaller invariant is the degeneration of multiplication on the
fixed hypersurface \(D=V(g)\). Write \(R=S/(g)\) and
\[
\mu_\tau(v):R_6[[v]]\longrightarrow R_{12}[[v]],\qquad
h\longmapsto(a_\tau+vq^3)h.
\]
Here \(\dim R_6=83\), \(\dim R_{12}=371\), and the generic
map is injective. Its positive Smith exponents over the DVR
\(\mathbb Q[[v]]\) measure the lengths of the parameter-torsion
summands of its cokernel, or equivalently the persistence of limiting
relations through successive lifting orders. They are *family*
invariants, not invariants of the limiting pencil alone. Under
\(v=u^m\) they scale by \(m\); comparisons use the primitive
parameter \(v=t^5\).

At \(v=0\), the gcds \(\gcd(a_0,g)=x_0^2\) and
\(\gcd(a_1,g)=x_0\) yield kernel dimensions 9 and 3.
For \(a_\tau=d_\tau A_\tau\), \(g=d_\tau B_\tau\),
the limiting relations are represented by
\(B_\tau S_{\deg d_\tau}/d_\tau S_0\).
The first obstruction is the induced map
\[
\delta_\tau:\ker\mu_\tau(0)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{coker}\mu_\tau(0),\qquad
[B_\tau h]\longmapsto[B_\tau hq^3].
\]
Exact rational elimination in degree 12 gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
\tau & \operatorname{rank}\mu(0)&
\operatorname{rank}\mu\bmod v^2&
\operatorname{rank}\mu\bmod v^3&
\text{positive Smith exponents}\\\hline
0&74&156&239&(1^8,2)\\
1&80&162&245&(1^2,2)
\end{array}
\]
where ranks in the middle columns are ranks of the underlying
\(\mathbb Q\)-linear maps on the truncated parameter modules.
Indeed for source rank 83,
\(83k-\operatorname{rank}(\mu\bmod v^k)
=\sum_i\min(k,e_i)\). The rank defects are respectively
\((9,10,10)\) and \((3,4,4)\). Equality of the defects at truncation orders two and three
shows directly that no exponent exceeds two; hence the total
torsion lengths are 10 and 4 **without using the old state-weight
certificate**. The independently
reproducible degree-12 exact calculation is
[`verify_positive_smith_Q.py`](torus-sextic-controls.support/verify_positive_smith_Q.py).
The first obstruction has ranks 8 and 2 and one-dimensional kernel
in each case. Indeed, the kernel of the special-fiber map is generated by
classes of \\(B_\\tau h\\) for \\(h\\in S_{\\deg d_\\tau}\\), modulo
\\(d_\\tau S_0\\); a class lifts to first order exactly when
\\(B_\\tau hq^3\\in a_\\tau R_6\\). The ranks of the obstruction
are the differences between the number of positive elementary divisors
and those of exponent at least two: \\(9-1=8\\) and \\(3-1=2\\).
The rank computation proves these values; the factorization identifies
the relation space on which the obstruction acts.

The determinant character before removing parameter torsion is
\(-830\): the \(gS_6\) contribution is \(-420\), and
\(a_\tau R_6\) contributes \(-410\). Each parameter
division changes the centered character by \(+5\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{wt}\det(J_{+,\tau})_{12}
=-830+5\sum_i e_i
=\begin{cases}-780&\tau=0,\\-810&\tau=1.\end{cases}
\]
This is a **degree-12** statement, not a claim that degree 12
is a uniform Hilbert embedding or that the entire positive
limit ideal has been classified.

The profile is attached to the specified multiplication presentation
on \\(S/(g)\\), together with its effective orbit parameter. It is not
an intrinsic invariant of a Hilbert boundary point; different fixed
hypersurfaces or presentations need not give comparable profiles.

The conceptual intermediate invariant is the relation-persistence
filtration (the standard DVR elementary-divisor filtration of a
multiplication map). It refines the limiting gcd and coarse VGIT
weights before one constructs the entire flat initial ideal.
The sum of exponents is the valuation of the ideal of maximal
minors, equivalently the determinantal/Fitting contact order.
It is not the intrinsic \(I_C\)-adic defect or the endpoint
image-lattice defect: comparing those requires a compatible
construction, not a numerical identification.

The [endpoint comparison for the first pencil](../results/endpoint_equation_torsion.md#a-relation-missed-at-both-endpoints)
exhibits two independent degree-12 first-layer classes missed by both
completed endpoints. The general result identifies the joint kernel and
relates its generic length on the disjoint coordinate line to the
residual degree of an actual proper pair.


## Involutivity persists through the full torus boundary

**New consequence, pending independent review.**  Combine the supported
Hilbert-limit theorem above with the
[actual contact identity](torus/contact_jacobians_and_hamiltonian_actions.md).
In characteristic zero, if a sole-supported complete intersection
\(J=(F,G)\) existed, then **both full intrinsic torus limits** would be
torus-fixed, sole-supported, involutive schemes with the same Hilbert
polynomial \(P_{a,b}\).  The special fibers need not remain complete
intersections and may acquire additional generators.

The point requiring proof is preservation of involutivity by the full
weight limit.  For the intrinsic weights
\[
 w=(0,1,3,4),
\]
the Poisson bracket is homogeneous of weight \(-4\):
\[
 \{S_p,S_q\}\subset S_{p+q-4}.                         \tag{HB-P1}
\]
Let \(I\) be any homogeneous involutive ideal and
\(\operatorname{in}_w I\) its full weight initial ideal.  If
\(f^\circ,g^\circ\) are leading weight forms of \(f,g\in I\), then either
\(\{f^\circ,g^\circ\}=0\), or it is the leading form of
\(\{f,g\}\in I\).  Hence
\[
 \{\operatorname{in}_wI,\operatorname{in}_wI\}
 \subset\operatorname{in}_wI.                         \tag{HB-P2}
\]
This is the elementary ideal version of the standard fact that an
associated graded of a compatible filtered Poisson algebra is again
Poisson.

The projective full limit uses the saturated initial ideal.  Saturation
does not destroy self-involutivity.  Indeed, localization of an involutive
ideal is involutive: in the bracket of \(a/s^m\) and \(b/s^n\), the only
term without an explicit factor \(a\) or \(b\) is a unit multiple of
\(\{a,b\}\).  Contraction from the localization preserves involutivity, and
\[
 I^{\rm sat}
 =\bigcap_i (I S_{x_i})\cap S.
\]
Intersections of involutive ideals are involutive.  Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\ 
 \bigl(\operatorname{in}_w I\bigr)^{\rm sat}
 \text{ is involutive whenever }I\text{ is involutive}.
 \ }                                                   \tag{HB-P3}
\]
The opposite torus limit is identical with \(w\) replaced by \(-w\).

For a hypothetical STCI pair, the contact theorem gives
\(\{J,J\}\subset J\); the already-proved closed supported incidence gives
both full saturated limits with unchanged Hilbert polynomial and reduced
support \(C\).  Hence (HB-P3) supplies the asserted two involutive boundary
points.

The
[constant-orbit theorem](torus_embedded_quotients.md#sole-support-constant-orbit-images-are-automatically-involutive-in-characteristic-zero)
shows that this extra boundary condition is **automatic on the pure
constant-orbit image**, even when its transverse algebra is not a complete
intersection.  Consequently contact can distinguish a full sole-supported
torus boundary only through

1. the finite embedded endpoint correction by which the full Hilbert limit
   differs from its pure orbit image, or
2. the global homogeneous presentation of that full ideal.

This prevents a false shortcut: one cannot combine contact with the
eigenform trace obstruction merely by taking a torus limit, because the
limit may require more than two generators and its pure thick curve is
already involutive.  The sharpened boundary problem is to impose
(HB-P3) on the **embedded endpoint correction module** together with the
known Hilbert and source constraints.

There is a finite exact formulation of that last condition.  Write
\(L\) for a candidate full limit, \(Y=L^{\rm pure}\) for its pure
constant-orbit quotient, and
\[
 I_L\subset I_Y,\qquad T=I_Y/I_L.
\]
If \(Y\) is sole-supported then the constant-orbit theorem makes \(I_Y\)
involutive.  Hence the bracket defines
\[
 \kappa_L:\bigwedge\nolimits^2(I_L/I_L^2)
 \longrightarrow T,\qquad
 \bar f\wedge\bar g\longmapsto\{f,g\}\bmod I_L.        \tag{HB-P4}
\]
This is well defined and \(\mathcal O_L\)-linear: replacing either
argument by an element of \(I_L^2\) inserts an explicit factor from
\(I_L\), and the Leibniz correction in multiplying an argument by a
function likewise vanishes modulo \(I_L\).  Because
\(\{I_Y,I_Y\}\subset I_Y\), the target really is \(T\).  Moreover
\[
 \boxed{\quad L\text{ is involutive}\iff\kappa_L=0.\quad} \tag{HB-P5}
\]
For the intrinsic limits of R-0070, \(T\) is supported at the two
endpoints and its two lengths are already known.  Thus (HB-P4) converts
the remaining contact condition into a **finite endpoint conormal map**.
The existing length formulas alone do not determine this map; one needs
the actual endpoint algebra or the weight-graded minimal generators of
the full ideal.  This identifies exactly which extra datum R-0070 would
have to retain for contact to produce a new boundary exclusion.


## Pure-quotient cohomology is a flat-boundary obstruction

**New consequence of standard semicontinuity, pending independent review.**
The endpoint-length formula of R-0070 records the Euler-characteristic
difference between a full torus limit and its pure quotient.  There is a
strictly stronger necessary condition: a zero-dimensional endpoint correction
can alter \(\chi\) and \(h^0\), but it cannot alter \(H^1\).

Let \(\mathcal X\to\operatorname{Spec}R\) be a flat projective family over a
DVR whose generic fiber \(X_\eta\) is a degree-\((a,b)\) complete
intersection.  Let \(L=\mathcal X_0\) be the special fiber.  Suppose \(Y\)
is a pure quotient of \(L\) with finite kernel
\[
 0\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow\mathcal O_L
 \longrightarrow\mathcal O_Y\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \dim T=0.                                     \tag{HB-C1}
\]
Then for every \(d\),
\[
 H^1(L,\mathcal O_L(d))
 \simeq H^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y(d)),                        \tag{HB-C2}
\]
because \(H^i(T(d))=0\) for \(i>0\).  On the other hand, the standard
upper-semicontinuity theorem for cohomology in a proper flat family gives
\[
 h^1(L,\mathcal O_L(d))
 \ge h^1(X_\eta,\mathcal O_{X_\eta}(d)).               \tag{HB-C3}
\]
Therefore every pure quotient of a flat CI boundary satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 h^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y(d))
 \ge h^1(X_{a,b},\mathcal O_{X_{a,b}}(d))
 \quad\text{for all }d.
 \quad}                                                \tag{HB-C4}
\]
Here the right side depends only on the CI type.  The same argument gives
\[
 h^0(L,\mathcal O_L(d))
 =h^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(d))+\operatorname{length}T,
 \tag{HB-C5}
\]
while
\[
 P_L=P_Y+\operatorname{length}T.
\]
Thus endpoint length can repair the constant term of the Hilbert polynomial
but cannot repair an \(H^1\)-deficit of the pure quotient.

The only external input in (HB-C3) is the classical semicontinuity theorem;
see Stacks Project, Lemma 36.32.1 (Tag 0BDN).  No special property of the
quartic is used in (HB-C1)--(HB-C5).

### Cartier controls are excluded as pure CI boundaries

Apply (HB-C4) to the Cartier multiples \(nC\subset Q\).  From
\[
 0\to\mathcal O_Q(dH-nC)\to\mathcal O_Q(dH)
 \to\mathcal O_{nC}(d)\to0
\]
and \(C=(3,1)\) up to ruling exchange, one obtains
\[
 h^1(nC,\mathcal O_{nC}(d))
 =
 \begin{cases}
  (3n-d-1)(n-d-1),&0\le d\le n-2,\\
  0,&d\ge n-1.
 \end{cases}                                           \tag{HB-C6}
\]
For \(4C\) and the matching \((4,4)\) polynomial,
\[
 h^1(4C,\mathcal O_{4C}(4))=0,
 \qquad
 h^1(X_{4,4},\mathcal O_{X_{4,4}}(4))=1.               \tag{HB-C7}
\]
Hence \(4C\) is not merely non-CI: it cannot be the pure quotient of **any**
flat \((4,4)\) CI boundary, even after arbitrary finite endpoint correction.

Likewise \(6C\) has the matching \((3,8)\) polynomial, but
\[
 h^1(6C,\mathcal O_{6C}(3))=28,
 \qquad
 h^1(X_{3,8},\mathcal O_{X_{3,8}}(3))=31.              \tag{HB-C8}
\]
So \(6C\) cannot be the pure quotient of a flat \((3,8)\) CI boundary.

This is stronger than comparing Hilbert polynomials or endpoint lengths and
corrects a tempting but false shortcut: the mere presence of a quadric
equation in a special saturated ideal does **not** by itself contradict
flatness, because \(H^1(I_L(d))\) can compensate.  The actual invariant is
the fiber cohomology in (HB-C4).
