# Supported Hilbert boundaries and generation of moving ideals

## Scope

The geometric application fixes an algebraically closed field \(k\),
the embedded curve
\(C=C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3_k\), its ideal
sheaf \(\mathcal I\), positive integers \(a,b,N\), and
\[
P_{a,b}(n)=abn+\frac{ab(4-a-b)}2.
\]
The action is
\(\lambda(t)[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]=[x_0:tx_1:t^3x_2:t^4x_3]\).
The arguments below work in every characteristic; the graded algebra
lemmas are stated over any field with a split torus where required.
The first three proof sections use no trace obstruction or
finite-modification theorem. The final invariant-CI corollary additionally
uses the actual eigenform trace theorem STCI-R-0042.

This Argument supplies complete direct proof routes for
[STCI-R-0037](../results/supported_hilbert_full_limits.md),
[STCI-R-0038](../results/torus_eigenvector_generator_lifting.md), and
[STCI-R-0039](../results/moving_ideal_generator_criterion.md).
They are separate direct routes, with no circular dependence among
these Results. The final proof section gives the composite route for
[STCI-R-0040](../results/invariant_supported_ci_exclusion.md), jointly
using STCI-R-0038 and
[STCI-R-0042](../results/eigenform_trace_obstruction.md).
The shared geometric question is what happens to a whole
embedded ideal at its torus boundary, and what must be tested on a
moving ideal to obtain two actual equations. The attracting-locus
classification and the existence of a supported CI deformation are not
proved here.

## Closed incidence and both full limits

We prove STCI-R-0037. Let
\(H=\operatorname{Hilb}^{P_{a,b}}(\mathbf P^3_k)\), with its universal
flat family \(\mathcal Y\subset\mathbf P^3\times H\) and universal
ideal \(\mathcal J\). The standard projective Hilbert theorem says
that \(H\) is projective and represents this flat-family functor. The
ideal \(\mathcal J\) is flat over \(H\): in its defining exact
sequence both the ambient structure sheaf and the universal quotient
are flat over \(H\).

Choose a sufficiently large integer \(m\). Relative Serre vanishing,
relative generation, and cohomology and base change give the following
properties simultaneously over all of \(H\):
\(\mathcal J(m)\) is relatively generated by its sections;
\(E=q_*\mathcal J(m)\) is a vector bundle whose fibers are
\(H^0(\mathcal I_Y(m))\); and
\[
0\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow S_m\otimes_k\mathcal O_H
 \longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow0
\]
is an exact sequence of vector bundles, where
\(S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\).
Indeed sufficiently large twists kill higher cohomology of every
fiber of the flat universal ideal and quotient, and their Euler
characteristics are fixed. The same \(m\) can be chosen so that the
fixed sheaves \(\mathcal I^N(m)\) and \(\mathcal I(m)\) are
globally generated and their degree-\(m\) sections are their usual
homogeneous ideal spaces. All these facts persist on base change.

Put \(V_N=H^0(\mathcal I^N(m))\) and
\(V_C=H^0(\mathcal I(m))\), as subspaces of \(S_m\). The vanishing
of the bundle maps
\[
V_N\otimes\mathcal O_H\longrightarrow Q,
\qquad
E\longrightarrow(S_m/V_C)\otimes\mathcal O_H
\tag{1}
\]
defines a closed subscheme \(H_N\subset H\). These equations express
the desired sheaf inclusions, not merely necessary conditions on
sections. For the first map, generation of \(\mathcal I^N(m)\)
shows that vanishing of its images in the quotient by \(\mathcal J\)
is equivalent to \(\mathcal I^N\subset\mathcal J\).
For the second map, relative generation of \(\mathcal J(m)\) shows
that all its sections lying in \(V_C\) is equivalent to
\(\mathcal J\subset\mathcal I\). Conversely either sheaf
inclusion induces the corresponding inclusion of section spaces and
makes the relevant map in (1) vanish. The evaluation maps and exact
bundle sequences commute with arbitrary base change, so this proves
the assertion for the incidence functor. In particular \(H_N\) is
projective, not just a closed set of geometric points. The use of a
sufficiently large \(m\) proves closedness; no explicit numerical
regularity bound for a proposed computational family is supplied by
this argument.

The parametrization shows
\(\lambda(t)C=C\), since the parameter changes from \([s:u]\) to
\([s:tu]\). Therefore \(\mathcal I\) and \(\mathcal I^N\)
are preserved. The functor just constructed is \(T\)-stable. Its
containments imply
\[
C\subset Y\subset V(\mathcal I^N),
\]
and the outer schemes have the same underlying support. Hence
\(Y_{\mathrm{red}}=C\) for every geometric point.

For any \(Y\in H_N(k)\), the orbit morphism
\(o_Y:\mathbf G_m\to H_N\) extends uniquely over \(0\) and
\(\infty\). Explicitly, each missing point of \(\mathbf P^1\)
has a discrete valuation local ring; properness gives an extension
over that ring, and separatedness gives uniqueness. These extensions
glue with \(o_Y\) to a morphism
\(\overline o_Y:\mathbf P^1\to H_N\). For \(s\in k^*\), the
two maps \(t\mapsto\lambda(s)\overline o_Y(t)\) and
\(t\mapsto\overline o_Y(st)\) agree on \(\mathbf G_m\), so agree
everywhere by separatedness. Evaluating at \(0\) and \(\infty\)
shows that both limits are \(T\)-fixed. Over the algebraically closed
field this is equivalently invariance under the algebraic torus, since
the action and orbit maps are morphisms and \(k^*\) is dense in
\(\mathbf G_m\). Pulling back the universal family gives a flat
embedded family over \(\mathbf P^1\). Its fibers have the prescribed
polynomial and the containments defining \(H_N\), including at the
two limits. The image of the connected scheme \(\mathbf P^1\)
is connected, so the original point and both limits lie in one
connected component of \(H_N\).

### Nilpotence and generic length

We complete the individual support and numerical assertions in
STCI-R-0037. If a projective scheme \(Y\) has reduced support \(C\),
then \(\sqrt{\mathcal I_Y}=\mathcal I\), so
\(\mathcal I_Y\subset\mathcal I\). On each affine chart the image
of \(\mathcal I\) in \(\mathcal O_Y\) is a finitely generated
nil ideal. If its generators \(u_i\) satisfy \(u_i^{e_i}=0\),
then every monomial of total degree
\(1+\sum_i(e_i-1)\) in these generators is zero. Thus that ideal
is nilpotent. A finite affine cover and the maximum of its exponents
give \(\mathcal I^N\subset\mathcal I_Y\) globally. The exponent
can depend on \(Y\). A proper pair of degrees \((a,b)\) is a
regular sequence in \(S\), with Koszul series
\((1-z^a)(1-z^b)/(1-z)^4\), hence polynomial \(P_{a,b}\).
If its reduced support is \(C\), the preceding argument places it
in some \(H_N\).

For completeness the generic multiplicity calculation does not require
that a boundary scheme be pure. Let \(J\) be the saturated homogeneous
ideal of any point of \(H_N\), and \(P=I_C\). Since its support is
\(C\), the radical of \(J\) is \(P\). A finite homogeneous prime
filtration of \(S/J\) has top-dimensional factors \(S/P\), up to
degree shifts; all other factors have dimension at most one as graded
modules and contribute only constants or zero to the projective Hilbert
polynomial. The number of top-dimensional factors is
\(\ell_{S_P}((S/J)_P)\), because localization at \(P\) kills the
other factors and each surviving factor has length one. Since \(C\)
has degree four, the linear coefficient of the Hilbert polynomial of
\(S/J\) is four times this length. Localization of the corresponding
degree-zero affine chart at the generic point of \(C\) has the same
length: passing to the homogeneous localization only adjoins the
invertible grading parameter and extends its residue field. Thus this
is the generic length \(m_C\) of \(\mathcal O_Y\) along \(C\).
Comparison with \(P_{a,b}\) gives \(4m_C=ab\). Substitution of
\(a=b=6\) gives \(P(n)=36n-144\), arithmetic genus \(145\),
and \(m_C=9\).

## Equivariant minimal-generator lifting

We prove STCI-R-0038 independently. Let a split torus act rationally
by graded automorphisms of \(S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]\), preserving the
homogeneous ideal \(J\). The spaces \(J_d\) and
\((S_+J)_d\) are finite-dimensional rational representations, and
their quotient is \(M(J)_d\). A rational representation of a split
torus is the direct sum of its character spaces: in coaction notation
the coefficient of each Laurent monomial in
\(k[T]=k[t_1^{\pm1},\ldots,t_q^{\pm1}]\) gives its weight
component. The coaction identity makes each component an eigenvector
and the counit makes their sum the original vector. This works over
any field and in every characteristic.

Choose a character basis of each \(M(J)_d\). Given a basis vector of
character \(\chi\), lift it first to any vector of \(J_d\), then
take the \(\chi\)-component of that lift. Equivariance of the quotient
map makes this new lift map to exactly the chosen basis vector. There
are only finitely many basis vectors: \(J\) is finitely generated,
and the images of any finite homogeneous generating set span
\(J/S_+J\).

Let \(K\) be the ideal generated by the chosen eigenvector lifts.
Their images span \(M(J)\), hence \(J=K+S_+J\). We verify equality
without a local or completed replacement. Induct on ordinary degree.
For \(f\in J_d\), its class modulo \(S_+J\) is a scalar linear
combination of the chosen degree-\(d\) lifts. Subtract this combination.
The remainder lies in \((S_+J)_d=S_1J_{d-1}\), because \(S\) is
standard graded. By induction its factors in \(J_{d-1}\) belong to
\(K\), so the remainder belongs to \(K\). The base degree is
immediate, and therefore \(J=K\). Removing one lift would fail to
span its nonzero basis class in \(M(J)\), so the generating set is
minimal and has exactly the degrees and characters claimed. Integer
characters remain distinct Laurent monomials in characteristic \(p\);
they have not been replaced by eigenvalues of a differentiated action.

## The moving ideal generator test

We prove STCI-R-0039 independently. For every homogeneous ideal of a
standard graded polynomial ring,
\[
(S_+J)_d=S_1J_{d-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
To see this, write a positive-degree polynomial multiplying an element
of \(J\) as a sum of monomials, and factor one variable from each
monomial. The remaining product already belongs to \(J_{d-1}\).
The reverse inclusion is immediate. Thus the module used in the
Statement is exactly \(J/S_+J\).

Suppose its graded dimensions count precisely the multiset
\(\{a,b\}\). Choose homogeneous lifts \(F,G\) of a graded basis.
The ordinary-degree induction just given can be repeated without any
torus: subtract the basis part in degree \(d\), and express the
remainder using (2) and the already generated degree \(d-1\).
It follows that \(J=(F,G)\), with the required degrees. Conversely,
if \(J=(F,G)\) has height two, both generators are minimal. Were one
redundant, \(J\) would be principal and have height at most one.
For equal degrees a nontrivial scalar dependence of their classes in
\(M(J)\) would make one redundant; for unequal degrees a vanishing
class of the larger generator would express it using the smaller.
Hence their classes are a graded basis and have exactly the asserted
dimensions.

Height two also proves that these equations are a regular sequence.
In the unique factorization domain \(S\), a common nonconstant
irreducible factor of \(F,G\) would put \(J\) inside a height-one
principal prime, contradicting its height. Thus their greatest common
divisor is one. The nonzero polynomial \(F\) is a nonzerodivisor in
\(S\). If \(GH\in(F)\), unique factorization and
\(\gcd(F,G)=1\) imply \(F\mid H\); this says precisely that
\(G\) is a nonzerodivisor modulo \(F\). The Koszul resolution
therefore gives
\[
P(n)=\binom{n+3}{3}-\binom{n-a+3}{3}
 -\binom{n-b+3}{3}+\binom{n-a-b+3}{3}
 =abn+\frac{ab(4-a-b)}2
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

For the conditional finite test, take a minimal graded free resolution
of the module \(J\). We use the usual convention
\(\operatorname{reg}_S J=\max\{j-i:\operatorname{Tor}_i^S(J,k)_j
\ne0\}\). Its degree-zero homological term records
\(\operatorname{Tor}_0^S(J,k)=J/S_+J=M(J)\). Thus a declared
bound \(\operatorname{reg}_S J\le r\) forces \(M(J)_d=0\) for
\(d>r\). Since \(r\ge\max(a,b)\), the required dimensions in
these degrees are also zero. Testing \(0\le d\le r\) is therefore
equivalent to testing every degree. Formula (2) gives the stated
matrix-rank expression. If, on a chosen base stratum, graded ideal
pieces are vector bundles commuting with base change, then the
multiplication maps specialize to precisely these fiber maps. Without
that qualification a rank computed in a relative presentation need
not be the stated fiberwise generator count. No uniform numerical
regularity bound has been computed here.

## What the boundary retains and what remains open

All assertions about full limits above concern the embedded Hilbert
family and its whole ideal sheaf. At a fiber the homogeneous ideal is
the saturated ideal of that scheme. In a coordinate weight
calculation one must retain all cancellation relations and any new
generators, and then the relevant projective saturation. Taking just
two extreme forms has no such guarantee. Even the invariant ideal
\((x_0,x_1)\), displayed as \((x_0+x_1,x_1)\), has two highest
weight forms equal to \(x_1\) under weights \((0,1,3,4)\), although
the full ideal contains \(x_0\) and its orbit is constant. This simple
control concerns a line, not a supported quartic example.

An \(\mathcal I\)-adic normal-cone construction uses a different
filtration and lives in the associated graded normal algebra. A
fundamental cycle keeps generic multiplicities but discards embedded
points and much nilpotent structure. Neither object replaces the full
Hilbert limit used in STCI-R-0037. The boundary may lose two-generation;
STCI-R-0038 concerns only ideals already invariant, and STCI-R-0039
must be applied to the moving ideal when the desired equations are
moving. Excluding invariant eigenform pairs therefore does not by
itself exclude a moving supported CI whose full limit has additional
generators.

For a fixed \(H_N\), one can label each point set-theoretically by
its two opposite limits and ask where the moving generator criterion
holds. No proof here makes those labels into a finite or locally
closed stratification, realizes any prescribed pair of limits, or
shows that the two-generator subset is empty or nonempty. Nor is
there a comparison morphism here from endpoint lattices of a moving
finite transverse modification to the two opposite Hilbert limits.
These are separate research interfaces requiring actual families,
generation, and coverage arguments.

## Invariant supported complete intersections

We prove the composite corollary STCI-R-0040. Let \(k\) be
algebraically closed and let \(J\subset k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\) be a
homogeneous height-two complete-intersection ideal whose radical is
the prime of the fixed quartic \(C\). Assume \(J\) itself is
invariant under the torus of weights \((0,1,3,4)\), and let
\(\{a,b\}\) be the positive ordinary degrees of its minimal
homogeneous generators. A homogeneous regular sequence of length
two is minimal: redundancy would make the height at most one.
Consequently its graded minimal-generator module has the two basis
degrees \(\{a,b\}\).

Apply STCI-R-0038 to this stable ideal. It lifts a character basis of
the minimal-generator module to weight-eigenforms \(F,G\) of precisely
those ordinary degrees, generating the entire ideal \(J\). This
step preserves the ideal and the degrees, rather than replacing the
equations by initial forms. Since \((F,G)=J\) and \(\sqrt J=I_C\),
their actual projective intersection has reduced support exactly
\(C\). The field is algebraically closed, the degrees are positive,
and both equations are eigenforms for the required integer weights.
Thus they satisfy the actual-pair eigenform hypotheses of
STCI-R-0042. Its trace conclusion is \(ab=0\) in \(k\).

In characteristic zero this contradicts the positive integers
\(a,b\). In characteristic \(p>0\), it gives \(p\mid ab\);
since \(p\) is prime, at least one of \(a,b\) is a positive
multiple of \(p\), so \(\max(a,b)\ge p\). This proves the
corollary in exactly the asserted range. No factor-only trace
variant is used. In characteristic two, no inference about the
parity of the generic multiplicity is made.

This conclusion requires ideal invariance. The radical \(I_C\)
is invariant for every sole-supported thickening, but that says
nothing about a weight decomposition of its own ideal. The full
fixed limits in STCI-R-0037 can need more than two generators, so
their invariance does not contradict a hypothetical moving supported
CI. The corollary supplies no reduction from arbitrary pairs to
invariant pairs.

## Conclusion

For each fixed \((a,b),N\), the supported incidence is a closed
projective torus-stable Hilbert locus, and both full flat torus limits
remain in it with unchanged polynomial and reduced support. A
torus-invariant ideal admits minimal eigenform generators, whereas
two-generation of a moving height-two ideal is exactly its full
graded minimal-generator condition, with a finite cutoff only under
the stated bound. These three direct proofs require no coordinate
computation. Combining equivariant lifting with the separate trace
theorem excludes invariant supported CI ideals in characteristic
zero and forces \(p\mid ab\) in characteristic \(p>0\).
None of these arguments constructs supported CI equations or decides
the unrestricted characteristic-zero STCI question.
