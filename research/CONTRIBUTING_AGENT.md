# STCI research contribution

## Exact claim or question
State the curve, characteristic, quantifiers, carrier and other hypotheses.

## Proposed status
Provisional / exact reduction / conditional / proposed theorem.

## Dependencies and evidence
List stable result IDs, source paths, computation commands and expected outputs.

## Geometric content
Identify the actual geometric objects and maps. Explain what a rank, kernel, discriminant, degree, parameter condition, or certificate measures about them. State the geometric mechanism that permits or obstructs the proposed equations, including the full scheme and boundary behavior needed by the claim. Distinguish this explanation from a suggestive picture or an unproved analogy.

For every example or result, state how that geometry changes the general STCI question: what construction becomes plausible, what class is excluded, what reusable principle is exposed, and what missing implication remains. If the geometry behind a calculation is not understood, preserve the exact calculation and formulate the geometric question explicitly.

## Independent audit
The reviewer must independently reconstruct the argument and its geometric meaning, not merely endorse the proposing agent or reproduce a number. Actively test counterexamples, omitted cases, hidden assumptions, dependency status and exact scope. In particular, distinguish generic statements over k(C) from claims at every point of C, and formal/local constructions from global projective existence. Record reviewer, tested hypotheses, failures, evidence paths, and outcome. Preserve rejected approaches with the specific defect identified. Do not mark accepted before independent review; computations and finite reductions are not automatically proofs of global claims.

## Frontier impact
State exactly what closes, remains open, or changes; distinguish a reduction from a completed calculation.
