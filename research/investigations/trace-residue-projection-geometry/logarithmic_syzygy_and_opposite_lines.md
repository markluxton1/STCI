# One logarithmic differential, a syzygy, and the two coordinate lines

Exploratory calculation, awaiting independent review. Characteristic zero over algebraically closed k. The governing task remains calculation of M_1 on the full translated-cycle CI realization locus. No universal vanishing asserted. Companion to [adjunction and coordinate quotients](adjunction_and_coordinate_quotients.md). Classical inputs: Rosenlicht's regular differential criterion (see [Gorenstein curve singularities of genus three](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11565-024-00495-8)), CI adjunction, and Koszul resolutions for regular sequences ([Stacks 15.31](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/062D)). The computation specializes these inputs, not a new theorem about Koszul homology.

Let S=k[z,x,y,w], E=S/(F_a,G_b) be an actual proper translated-cycle CI, D its reduced arrangement, Sigma the annihilator of the nilradical, m=sum n_i, d=a+b-2. Identify Sigma(d-2) with omega_D. On the normalization of branch i put tau_i=n_i dr/r, r=t/s. This is a tuple of meromorphic differentials, not itself a regular section of omega_D. Via the identification of the generic socle with rational differentials, the six minors are
\[
(D01,D02,D03,D12,D13,D23)
=(yw,-3xw,2xy,4zw,-3zy,zx)\tau .
\]
The equality here is in the rational extension of the socle module with the appropriate twists. It is the complementary tangent formula already established, with its normalization via relative trace.

## Two lower degree sections

Because x,y vanish on every branch at both endpoints, P=x tau, Q=y tau are regular sections of omega_D(1). By the CI's ACM section dictionary these have unique classes in Sigma_(d-1) in E. In E,
\[
 D01=wQ,\ D02=-3wP,\ D03=2yP=2xQ,\ D13=-3zQ,\ D23=zP,\quad
 4zw P=xD12,\ 4zw Q=yD12 .
\]
All coordinate forms are nonzerodivisors in E (no component in a coordinate plane).

## Nontrivial syzygy and exact cofactor restriction on K

The relation yP-xQ=0 defines a degree-d class sigma in H_1(x,y;E), represented by (-Q,P). For any homogeneous h in B=k[z,w], h sigma is a boundary iff there is R in E_(d-2+deg h) with hP=xR,hQ=yR. Regularity of x identifies R uniquely with h tau as a rational socle section, and implies R is in Sigma. Conversely any regular section h tau lifts to the corresponding homogeneous class by adjunction and ACMness. The residues of the meromorphic twisted differential h tau at the two endpoints, in the z and w frames, sum to m h(1,0) and -m h(0,1), respectively. There are only simple poles, so Rosenlicht's condition is exactly the vanishing of these two numbers. Thus
\[
 \operatorname{Ann}_B(\sigma)=(zw).
\]
The statement for nonhomogeneous h follows by homogeneity. The class is nonzero, even in balanced realizations.

Let K=V(x,y), F_K=F(z,0,0,w), G_K=G(z,0,0,w). They both vanish at each endpoint and have no common projective zeros away from them. They cannot both vanish identically, since K is not a component. Their gcd H is thus a nonzero scalar times z^r w^s with r,s>=1 (gcd also defined when exactly one restriction vanishes). Standard comparison of the Koszul resolutions for (x,y) and (F,G) computes the same Tor_1^S(E,B):
\[
 H_1(x,y;E)\simeq (B/(H))(-(a+b-\deg H)).
\]
Indeed on B the syzygies of F_K,G_K are generated by (G_K/H,-F_K/H) and boundaries multiply this generator by H. The comparison is explicit: choose homogeneous lifts of P,Q to S, write yP-xQ=A F+B' G, with deg A=b-2, deg B'=a-2 (unique for those lifts because the difference of cofactors would be a Koszul relation with multiplier of degree -2). Restriction of the coefficient pair (A,B') to K represents the same class up to the overall Koszul sign. Write
\[
 (A_K,B'_K)=J(G_K/H,-F_K/H),\quad \deg J=\deg H-2.
\]
The annihilator of the class of J in B/(H) is (H/gcd(H,J)); hence the above residue calculation gives J=c H/(zw), c in k^*. In particular
\[
 A_K=c G_K/(zw),\qquad B'_K=-c F_K/(zw)
\]
for a common nonzero scalar. This is independent of homogeneous lifts since modifying P,Q by F,G changes their cofactors by terms in (x,y); the Koszul sign is absorbed in c. No value for c is asserted here. This constraint includes the case that one of F_K,G_K is identically zero.

## Restriction to the complementary line L=V(z,w)

The binary Artin CI E/(z,w)=k[x,y]/(f,g) has top degree d and perfect graded residue pairing. The trace-Jacobian formula and the relation 4zw P=xD12 (and analogous relation for Q) give Res(xP)=Res(yQ)=0 and Res(yP)=Res(xQ)=M_1 (using Tr(x^2)=Tr(y^2)=0, Tr(xy)=4zw M_1). These equations hold in the relative residue over B and specialize at z=w=0. Thus the images of P,Q in degree d-1 are respectively M_1 times the basis dual to y,x under the residue pairing with degree one (a,b>=2 since the support is nonplanar). In particular M_1=0 iff both P,Q belong to (z,w)E. The very same two sections have a fixed nontrivial syzygy along K but have vanishing central restriction along L exactly when the configuration is balanced. The cofactor restriction on K alone does not establish this vanishing.

Next natural comparison: use the actual differential expressions for P,Q and the homogeneous cofactor equation yP-xQ=AF+B'G simultaneously with the two line restrictions. Seek constraints on the central classes, not merely a new restatement or a classification of individual carriers. The logarithmic residue and syzygy calculations are necessary consequences of the assumed realization, not sufficient data to reconstruct it.
