# Adjunction from actual equations and coordinate quotients

Exploratory synthesis, characteristic zero, pending independent review. Governing question: M_1 on the entire translated-cycle CI realization locus. No universal vanishing asserted. Companion to [endpoint divisor reconstruction](endpoint_divisor_reconstruction.md).

Given an actual proper homogeneous CI Y=V(F_a,G_b), CI adjunction (or the dual of the Koszul resolution) gives omega_Y=O_Y(a+b-4). For D=Y_red, closed immersion duality gives Sigma(a+b-4)=omega_D, where Sigma=Ann(I_(D/Y)). See [Stacks 48.19](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AU3). The six Jacobian minors have degree a+b-2 and belong to Sigma by the established generic mixed-minor/socle lemma and purity. They are sections of omega_D(2). The dualizing sheaf of D need not be invertible. On each normalized branch these particular sections are regular sections of L_i=omega_P1 tensor O_P1(8)=O_P1(6), not a claim that normalization pullback of omega_D equals omega_P1.

Canonical normalization identifies D12 on branch i with n_i(z dw-w dz), by the trace/Jacobian formula. The generic tangent-minor relations and Euler give the other five complementary twisted forms, with signs: D01=n_i(y dw-w dy), D02=-n_i(x dw-w dx), D03=n_i(x dy-y dx), D13=-n_i(z dy-y dz), D23=n_i(z dx-x dz). Here equality means the adjunction identification with weighted differentials on normalized branches; not equality of ambient forms. Substitute [s^4:s^3t:u st^3:v t^4] to obtain the six endpoint divisors in the companion note. The two extreme sections have disjoint zeros and hence the six generate L_i. This is a direct construction of the common normalized line bundle from the actual equations.

## Systematic quadratic coordinate quotients

Divide each minor by any quadratic coordinate monomial. On z=1, r=t/s, write the minor differential as n_i c_i r^e dr, with (c,e) respectively (uv,6),(-3v,4),(2u,3),(4v,3),(-3u,2),(1,0). If the monomial denominator restricts to d_i r^k (use weights 0,1,3,4 and coordinate coefficients 1,1,u,v), the quotient is n_i(c_i/d_i) r^(e-k) dr. Its orders at 0 and infinity are e-k and k-e-2. Logarithmic at both precisely when k=e+1. Enumerating the ten quadratic monomials gives exactly eight minor/denominator pairs: D01/yw, D02/xw, D03/xy, D12/zw, D13/zy, D23/zx, D03/zw, D12/xy. Their total residues at zero are m,-3m,2m,4m,-3m,m,2M_1,4M_{-1}, respectively; opposite at infinity. Only the last two depend on the configuration beyond m.

Rosenlicht regularity (Altman-Kleiman, Introduction to Grothendieck Duality Theory, VIII.1.16; [reference](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/hyperelliptic-gorenstein-curves-and-logarithmic-differentials/65CD261998F6F4C3A71626FA564D16B5)) for simple poles is cancellation of total residues at each common endpoint. For higher poles test against every local coordinate monomial of the requisite weight: at zero monomials in r,u_i r^3,v_i r^4 of weight -(e-k)-1; at infinity monomials in r'^3/v_i,q_i r',r'^4/v_i of weight e-k+1 (r'=1/r). A nonnegative required weight is necessary for any test. Coefficients of local functions are shared across branches; the residue sums are weighted by their branch monomial coefficients. This is an exhaustive finite gluing test for each of the sixty quadratic quotients, not a claim that each quotient is regular.

For actual CI pairs each quadratic coordinate monomial is a nonzerodivisor in E=S/(F,G) (no component lies in a coordinate plane). By saturation and the ACM section dictionary, D_ij/Q lies in H^0(D,omega_D) iff D_ij belongs to Q E. In particular M_1=0 iff D03 in zw E; inverse balance M_{-1}=0 iff D12 in xy E. The centered quotient (D03-M_1 D12/(2m))/(zw) is regular automatically. No reason that all quotients must be regular: their regularity is equivalent to additional actual ideal memberships, not consequences of merely having the common line bundle.
