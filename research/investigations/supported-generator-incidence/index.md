# Two-equation incidence on bounded supported attracting families

## Scope

The initial target is the fixed embedded quartic over an algebraically closed
field of characteristic zero, type \((6,6)\), and polynomial \(36d-144\)
(degree \(36\), arithmetic genus \(145\)).
For an actual bounded test choose and state \(N>0\), a specified family inside
\(H_N\), and any regularity bound used for finite generation checks. This
record does not supply an uncomputed value of \(N\) or an actual supported
boundary ideal. Positive-characteristic comparisons are separate cases with
their characteristic stated.

## Question

For a specified nonempty family of moving ideals in \(H_N\), which points
have their whole saturated homogeneous ideal generated by two sextics? Can
one construct such a point, or exclude it on that entire declared family?
When using opposite limits, the family must realize the proposed pair of
limits simultaneously, not through two unrelated one-sided deformations.
Can normal-filtration layers, endpoint modifications, or conormal modules
label the loci where the moving generator counts jump?

## Purpose

[F-0001](../../frameworks/torus-supported-boundaries/index.md) preserves support and
Hilbert data at a full torus boundary while testing equations on moving ideals.
This investigation turns that strategy into a bounded realization problem.
Its success can be a construction or an informative family-specific exclusion;
neither outcome alone classifies all supported intersections.

## Inputs

[R-0037](../../results/supported_hilbert_full_limits.md) gives the bounded
incidence and the generic length nine forced by this polynomial.
[R-0039](../../results/moving_ideal_generator_criterion.md) is the exact

Its degree-ten generator warns against testing only the sextic piece.

## Method

First specify actual ideals or an actual flat family, verifying
\(\mathcal I_C^N\subset\mathcal J\subset\mathcal I_C\), the polynomial,
and any endpoint or embedded structure. Compute both full torus limits when
this helps describe the family. Such limits need not be two-generated.

On strata with correct graded fibers, calculate multiplication maps
\(S_1\otimes J_{d-1}\to J_d\). The target is
\(\dim M(J)_6=2\) and \(M(J)_d=0\) otherwise. A finite range requires
a justified uniform bound \(\operatorname{reg}_S J\le r\), \(r\ge6\),
on this family. Two independent sextic sections, a tangent vector, or an
abstract Gorenstein algebra is insufficient. A proposed equation pair must
generate the entire ideal scheme-theoretically.

Where \(J_6\) is a vector bundle commuting with base change, the relative
Grassmannian of two-planes in \(J_6\) parametrizes candidate sextic pencils
symmetrically. Impose fiberwise equality of the ideal generated by the
universal pencil with \(J\), using all generator degrees or a justified
regularity cutoff.

An arbitrary supported deformation to the desired CI is useful even if it
does not realize a prescribed torus orbit history. Keep that construction
problem distinct from the stronger prescribed-boundary problem.

## Cycle-to-ideal realization gate

For any proposed sextic pencil or degeneration, distinguish:

1. **Actual proper equations and cycle.** Verify height two,
   containment of \(C\), and marked multiplicity. For an **actual
   proper** \((6,6)\) pair, the terminal condition \([Y]=9[C]\)
   is equivalent to sole support, by purity and degree exhaustion.
   The [existing rank-strata argument](../../arguments/residuals/marked_deformation_and_absorption.md#fixed-degree-multiplicity-strata-of-actual-pairs)
   gives equations for this locus; the Chow divisibility description
   gives a second, cycle-level presentation of the same condition.
2. **Full supported ideal.** When working with a limit or graph
   boundary instead of an actual proper pair, verify the prescribed
   Hilbert polynomial and support in an actual flat family (or some
   \(H_N\)), retaining embedded structure and the saturated ideal
   at both limits. A limiting cycle equal to \(9[C]\) does not
   determine the limiting generators.
3. **Moving generator incidence.** On a proposed supported Hilbert
   boundary, impose the full two-sextic generator criterion from
   R-0039, with a justified regularity bound if using finitely many
   multiplication matrices. For a saturated ideal with the exact
   \((6,6)\) CI Hilbert polynomial, the existence of a **proper**
   sextic pencil inside its degree-six part already implies equality
   of ideals (see the complete-intersection-polynomial criterion in
   the torus Hilbert-boundary argument).

The concrete question is whether Chow or torus-weight strata cut down
the **same** supported moving-ideal family enough to make its
two-generator incidence computable. Otherwise keep the bounded
Hilbert-family question rather than launch a parallel VGIT
classification. An invariant cycle or extreme-form pair does not
determine a supported boundary ideal; conversely, a supported
boundary with extra generators does not exclude a moving supported
CI until its attracting family is tested.

## ADE primitive-triple contact as a bounded input

The [surviving primitive-triple calculation](../../arguments/carriers/sextic_ade_reduction.md#interpretation-of-the-cubic-contact-ranks)
gives a 27-dimensional sextic source on each of three orbits and
joint \((h,\Theta)\) ranks 23 (principal) and 24 (algebraic).
These are **necessary linear source conditions**, not a supported
Hilbert family and not a solution of the present Investigation.

A bounded next test, if an actual family is specified, is to intersect
the decomposable-wedge cubic-contact condition with its higher-order
normal-contact equations, then verify properness, the terminal Chow
condition and both full limits on that **same** family. The proposed
principal-orbit rank of the full wedge map needs an independently
checkable nonvanishing witness before it is used as a geometric rank
certificate. A zero or nonzero incidence result must be stated for the
declared family; no coverage of all sextic pencils follows automatically.

## First-cancellation obstruction

For a proposed supported characteristic-zero sextic pair, the
[clean-cancellation theorem](../../arguments/torus_hilbert_boundary.md#clean-cancellation-and-residual-descent)
requires the first nonzero cancellation coefficient \(D_0\)
to share a factor with the full common factor \(H\) of the
extreme sextics, **in both torus directions**. Otherwise the
full initial ideal is explicitly ACM and splits at cycle level
into eigenform complete intersections, contradicting the
known eigenform obstruction. This is a necessary gate before
any higher cancellation or endpoint analysis.

The surviving case \(\gcd(H,D_0)\ne1\) does not yield a
smaller supported pair: additional generators, embedded
structure, or a unit quotient can defeat descent. The
relevant unresolved question is whether the surviving
first-cancellation strata intersect the same bounded
supported Hilbert attracting family and its actual proper
two-sextic locus. Do not replace that incidence problem
with a numerical degree-product induction.

## Top-cycle and weight gates

The [triple-graph formulation](../../arguments/torus_hilbert_boundary.md#equationhilbertchow-graph-and-its-top-fiber)
provides a second, equivalent fixed-degree existence test:
does the top Chow fiber meet the open CI locus in the
**Hilbert coordinate**? This is not the same as asking
whether the limiting displayed pencil is proper. The
top-cycle fiber may include limits of residual pairs;
the closure of supported approximants is smaller or equal,
with stronger power containment.

The centered-weight semistability condition excludes
one-sided weight blocks for **actual** supported proper
sextic pencils. It does not exclude improper limiting
pencils, so it should be applied to the moving equation
coordinate, alongside the first-cancellation and full
generator gates. The next genuine calculation would
identify a nonempty top-cycle graph stratum and determine
whether its Hilbert CI locus is empty or nonempty, rather
than classify all abstract VGIT chambers.

### Reconciled fixed-degree decision target

For actual proper sextic pencils, the terminal incidence is precisely
\[
\chi^{-1}([Q^9])\subset U_{6,6},
\]
where \(Q=\operatorname{Ch}_C\) and \(U_{6,6}\) is the open locus of
proper pencils in \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,I_C(6))\). It lies in a **single
affine Chow-resultant chart**, with finite coefficient equations; see the
[reconciled argument](../../arguments/torus_hilbert_boundary.md#reconciled-chow-filtration-and-finite-sextic-weight-partition).
Nonemptiness in characteristic zero remains open. This formulation
is an alternative exact description of the same set-theoretic terminal
normal-jet rank condition, not an assertion that the two incidence
scheme structures agree and not a new existence theorem. Work inside
this affine proper-pair chart for fixed-degree existence; retain
projective and Hilbert graph closures for degeneration obstructions.

For a bounded attracting-family test, distinguish three gates on the
same declared family: (1) **cycle incidence**, the terminal Chow
condition on actual proper pencils; (2) **torus obstructions**, centered
stability and first cancellation in both directions on the same moving
family; and (3) **full-ideal realization** for Hilbert-boundary points,
including the saturated ideal and prescribed-degree generators. For an
actual proper pencil, the terminal Chow condition already proves sole
support and the pair itself generates its CI ideal: do not repeat a
full generator test there. On a Hilbert boundary, the full two-sextic
generator criterion remains essential.
A graph-boundary point with top cycle is not an actual proper pencil,
and an improper displayed limit cannot be inserted into \(U_{6,6}\).
Record explicitly whether the test is on the moving proper equation
coordinate or the full Hilbert ideal. The centered character-zero stability gate in characteristic zero
removes one-sided proper pencils (using R-0042); the other 34
ambient VGIT chambers are not a priority unless needed to separate
specific residual or full-limit strata. A targeted next test is to
intersect existing two-direction endpoint-constrained sextic families
with the terminal Chow incidence, keeping both endpoint limits tied
to the same moving family. This is a proposed incidence test, not an
assertion that such a family has been realized or the equations solved.
The general fixed-degree Chow filtration is separate from the sextic-only
VGIT counts; in particular it neither resolves the (6,16) cubic-jet
gate nor establishes an unrestricted characteristic-zero exclusion.

## Local leading ideals are not a global generator certificate

The [GHK reconciliation](../../arguments/normal-geometry/actual_equation_filtration.md#generic-leading-ideals-versus-actual-global-equations)
separates local realizability of a two-dimensional parameter
ideal from realizability by two **prescribed-degree global
equations**. A locally normalized generating pair may use
\(k(C)\)-valued coefficients unavailable to homogeneous
global row operations. Even a locally realizable full
initial ideal must pass actual symbol-source maps,
higher-jet syzygy compatibility, and the projective
sole-support test.

For the sextic \((6,6)\) search, retain the global
two-sextic incidence rather than substituting the
locally minimized order/excess. The conditional
\((6,16)\) two-node cubic-jet zero locus is a
**separate** fixed-degree investigation, already developed
in the spectral Arguments; its unresolved compatibility
does not provide an exclusion of \((6,6)\) or an
unrestricted quartic STCI.

## Findings

This is an unperformed mathematical investigation. The pilot has reconstructed
the criteria and controls, not produced a supported invariant boundary of this
polynomial, an attracting-family classification, or an exhaustive incidence
calculation. The residual three-generator example is not such a boundary.
There is no inferred nonemptiness or universal sextic exclusion.

## Completion and next decisions

Complete one declared family by an actual supported pair, a proof of emptiness
of its full two-equation incidence, or a precisely identified missing lifting
or generator condition. A local failure can redirect that family search.
An all-pairs conclusion still needs coverage of the relevant fixed limits and
power bounds. An unbounded-degree conclusion needs a further argument.
Keep any exploration and useful data in this directory when work starts;
catalog independently reusable findings as Results and preserve complete proofs.
