Their absence of special-point exceptional divisors for the *full lci curve ideal* is not an absence of special-point defects in a different, filtered intersection ideal. The explicit unbounded-defect family is a necessary scope warning.

The [Delta embedding analysis](DELTA_EMBEDDING_NORMAL_AND_REES_GEOMETRY.md#delta-embedding-geometry-report) keeps track of additional marked-embedding and conormal-torsion data; [normal flatness](NORMAL_FLATNESS_AND_FILTERED_CI.md#2026-10-19-filtered-generic-ci-normal-flatness) only propagates generic complete-intersection behavior under saturation and flatness hypotheses. **Do not infer:** fixed Segre numbers or the generic transverse algebra alone force a bound on the boundary correction divisor, or imply that a pair has no residual components. The counterexamples and exact hypotheses in the original sections remain part of the argument.

# Full-ideal Rees divisors and unbounded intrinsic normal-filtration defects

Date: 2026-10-19. Status: **provisional structural propositions with proofs;
independently reconstructed in the companion audit; not promoted**.

Independent review: [Rees/residual audit](2026-10-19-rees-residual-independent-audit.md).

## 1. Exact question and inherited premises

The motivating curve is the characteristic-zero rational quartic
\(C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3\). The question is whether
generator-independent divisorial Rees data or ordinary Segre numbers can
control special-point degeneration of a hypothetical two-hypersurface
thickening of \(C_0\).

The retrieval interface is
[formal normal geometry and residual intersections](../synthesis/formal-normal-intersection.md).
The accepted hypotheses and conclusion of
[STCI-R-0010](../results/saturated_first_relation_normal_flatness.md)
are retained literally: a saturated **actual** first relation, together
with the prescribed full generic binary complete-intersection initial
algebra, forces ordinary normal flatness. No part of that result is
reproved here. In particular, the saturation hypothesis is not silently
dropped.

The exact first-blowup balance, residual-degree bounds, moving-direction
refinements, and generator-dependent vertical counterexamples were
already proved and independently reconstructed in the
[exceptional-cycle note](2026-10-19-exceptional-cycle-balance-and-vertical-counterexamples.md)
and its
[audit](2026-10-19-exceptional-cycle-and-vertical-family-independent-audit.md).
Those statements remain **reviewed evidence, not promoted canonical
results**. They are not new contributions of this note.

The new statements below concern the blowup of the **full ideal**
\(J=(F,G)\), not the intersection of the individual strict transforms on
\(\operatorname{Bl}_{C_0}\mathbf P^3\). These are different constructions.
The local family in section 5 uses the earlier family but computes a new
**intrinsic** normal-filtration defect and a precise generic-CI
specialization of it. It is not a projective construction for \(C_0\).

## 2. No special-point divisorial Rees valuations for an lci curve ideal

**Proposition 1.** Let \(X\) be a smooth integral threefold of finite type
over a field, and let \(Y\hookrightarrow X\) be a codimension-two regular
immersion. Suppose \(Y_{\mathrm{red}}=C\) is an irreducible curve. Put
\[
 b:B=\operatorname{Bl}_{I_Y}X\longrightarrow X,
 \qquad \nu:\overline B\longrightarrow B
\]
for the blowup and its normalization. Every exceptional prime divisor of
\(\overline B\to X\) dominates \(C\). In particular, no divisorial Rees
valuation of \(I_Y\) has center equal to a closed point of \(C\).

Moreover localization at \(\eta_C\) identifies these prime divisors with
the divisorial Rees valuations of the transverse parameter ideal
\((I_Y)_{\eta_C}\) in the two-dimensional regular local ring
\(\mathcal O_{X,\eta_C}\).

**Proof.** Locally write \(I_Y=(f,g)\), with \(f,g\) a regular sequence.
Its Rees algebra has presentation
\[
 \mathcal R(I_Y)=\mathcal O_X[A,B]/(gA-fB).
\]
This is the regular-sequence linear-type presentation; equivalently,
relations among the homogeneous monomials in \(f,g\) are generated by
the Koszul relation. Reducing the presentation modulo \(I_Y\) shows
that the exceptional Cartier divisor is
\[
 E_Y=\mathbf P_Y(I_Y/I_Y^2).
\]
Since \(I_Y/I_Y^2\) has rank two, \((E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is an
irreducible ruled surface over \(C\). The fiber over a closed point of
\(C\) has dimension one.

The blowup is integral, since its Rees algebra sits inside
\(\mathcal O_X[z]\), and its normalization is finite: all schemes are of
finite type over a field. A finite morphism preserves the dimension of
a closed irreducible subset. Therefore the inverse image in
\(\overline B\) of an exceptional fiber over a closed point has dimension
one and contains no prime divisor of the threefold. All exceptional
prime divisors are contained in \(\nu^{-1}(E_Y)\); their images in \(B\)
have dimension two and hence equal \((E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}\). They
therefore dominate \(C\).

Blowup and finite normalization commute with localization. Since every
exceptional prime survives localization at \(\eta_C\), and taking the
closure recovers it, the stated bijection follows. \(\square\)

The same dimension argument applies when \(Y_{\mathrm{red}}\) has
several curve components, including residual components: every
exceptional prime divisor dominates a curve component of the reduced
support. There is still no exceptional divisor over a closed point.

This does **not** say that the global normalized exceptional surfaces
carry no special-point information. Their curves, finite-cover geometry,
ramification, conductor, and special fibers can carry such information.
It says that the list of divisorial valuations itself is already visible
over the generic points of the support curves.

## 3. The exact Rees multiplicity budget

Retain Proposition 1 and put
\[
 m=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{X,\eta_C}}
                  (\mathcal O_{Y,\eta_C}).
\]
Write
\[
 \nu^*E_Y=\sum_i a_iD_i,
 \qquad
 f_i=[k(D_i):k((E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}})].
\]

**Proposition 2.** The intrinsic Rees coefficients satisfy
\[
 \boxed{\sum_i a_if_i=m.}
\]

**Proof.** A rank-two projective bundle over \(Y\) has the same generic
cycle multiplicity along its reduced support as \(Y\) has along \(C\).
Thus the Cartier divisor \(E_Y\) has cycle
\(m[(E_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}]\). Since \(\nu\) is finite birational, the
projection formula for Cartier divisors gives
\[
 \nu_*[\nu^*E_Y]=[E_Y].
\]
The pushforward of \([D_i]\) has coefficient \(f_i\) on the reduced
exceptional surface. Comparing coefficients proves the identity.
\(\square\)

For the transverse ideal \((u^a,v^b)\), let \(h=\gcd(a,b)\). Its
single monomial Rees valuation has primitive weights
\((b/h,a/h)\). The vanishing coefficient of the ideal is \(ab/h\),
and the map of exceptional direction curves has degree \(h\): it is
the map with rational function \(u^a/v^b\), the \(h\)-th power of a
primitive direction parameter. Hence \(a_1f_1=ab\), as required.

For a hypothetical \((d,e)\) STCI of \(C_0\), this identity reads
\(\sum_i a_if_i=de/4\). It is a genuine generator-independent
decomposition of the transverse multiplicity. No restriction on the
global exceptional finite covers has yet been extracted from it.

## 4. Ordinary Segre numbers are fixed by the CI degrees

**Proposition 3.** If \(Y=V(F_d,G_e)\subset\mathbf P^3\) is a proper
complete intersection, then
\[
 i_*s(Y,\mathbf P^3)
 =de\,H^2-de(d+e)\,H^3.
\]

**Proof.** The regular immersion has normal bundle
\(\mathcal O_Y(d)\oplus\mathcal O_Y(e)\). Its normal cone is that
bundle, so the definition of the Segre class gives
\[
 s(Y,\mathbf P^3)
 =c(\mathcal O_Y(d)\oplus\mathcal O_Y(e))^{-1}\cap[Y].
\]
Pushing forward and expanding in dimensions one and zero proves the
formula. \(\square\)

Consequently these ordinary global Segre numbers cannot distinguish
special-point normal-filtration defects among proper CIs of fixed
degrees. One must use finer data, for example the filtration relative to
\(I_C\), rather than only the Segre class of \(Y\) in the ambient space.
The proposition supplies no additional STCI obstruction.

## 5. An intrinsic saturation defect unbounded with fixed generic algebra

Let \(A=k[t]\), choose integers \(p\ge2\), \(a>p\), and \(b>p\), and
work in the smooth affine threefold
\(X=\operatorname{Spec}A[u,v]\). For \(N\ge1\), set
\[
 C=V(u,v),\quad \mathfrak q=(u,v),\qquad
 J_N=(u^b,v^a+t^Nu^p),\quad Y_N=V(J_N).
\]
The earlier exceptional-cycle note proves that \(Y_N\) is a
\(C\)-supported lci, finite free of rank \(ab\) over \(A\), with basis
\(u^iv^j\), \(0\le i<b,0\le j<a\). We use this verified description
without re-auditing its vertical chosen-generator calculation.

Let \(\mathfrak q_N=\mathfrak q\mathcal O_{Y_N}\). The first actual
relation is
\[
 R_p=\ker\!\left(
  \operatorname{Sym}^p(\mathfrak q/\mathfrak q^2)
      \longrightarrow \mathfrak q_N^p/\mathfrak q_N^{p+1}
 \right).
\]

**Proposition 4.** In the monomial basis of the symmetric power,
\[
 R_p=A\,t^Nu^p,\qquad
 R_p^{\mathrm{sat}}=A\,u^p,\qquad
 R_p^{\mathrm{sat}}/R_p\simeq A/(t^N).
\]
Thus the intrinsic first-relation saturation defect has length exactly
\(N\). Equivalently,
\[
 \mathfrak q_N^p/\mathfrak q_N^{p+1}
       \simeq A^{\oplus p}\oplus A/(t^N).
\]

**Proof.** Since \(a,b>p\), reduce the defining ideal modulo
\(\mathfrak q^{p+1}\). Its image in the degree-\(p\) summand is precisely
\(A\,t^Nu^p\): both \(u^b\) and \(v^a\) vanish, and every
positive-normal-degree coefficient of the remaining generator also
vanishes. There is no lower-degree relation. Saturation over the PID
\(A\) replaces \(t^Nu^p\) by \(u^p\). The quotient assertions follow
immediately. \(\square\)

This defect depends on the full ideal and the embedding of \(C\); it
does not depend on replacing the displayed generators by another
generating pair.

**Corollary 4a (fixed generic filtered algebra).** On the subsequence
\(N=aL\), \(L\ge1\), all generic filtered algebras are isomorphic over
\(K=k(t)\). Indeed the filtration-preserving change of normal coordinates
\(v=t^Lv'\) identifies
\[
 K[u,v]/(u^b,v^a+t^{aL}u^p)
 \simeq K[u,v']/(u^b,v'^a+u^p).
\]
The maximal-ideal filtration is preserved because \(t^L\) is a unit in
\(K\). The intrinsic saturation-defect lengths \(aL\), nevertheless,
are unbounded. The same generic coordinate identifications identify the
generic Rees valuations of the full ideals, including their Rees
coefficients and finite generic residue degrees. Proposition 1 says
there are no additional special-point divisorial Rees valuations that
could supply the missing defect length.

This compares generic data under explicit coordinate identifications;
it does not claim that the valuations, viewed as valuations of one fixed
ambient rational-function field without those identifications, are
literally equal.

**Corollary 4b (the full generic CI initial algebra can also be fixed).**
Choose \(b=2p\). Over \(K\), the defining ideal is equivalently
\[
 (u^p+t^{-N}v^a,\ v^{2a}).
\]
Its full ordinary initial ideal is
\[
 (u^p,v^{2a}).
\]
To see equality of initial ideals, both displayed initial forms belong
to the initial ideal, are coprime, and define an algebra of length
\(2pa\), which is already the length of the original quotient. An
additional initial relation would strictly reduce that length.

Therefore one fixed coprime binary-CI generic initial type \((p,2a)\),
one fixed multiplicity \(2pa\), and even one fixed generic filtered
algebra admit arbitrarily long intrinsic first-relation saturation
defects. For instance \(p=2,a=3,b=4,N=3L\) has fixed generic initial
type \((2,6)\), multiplicity \(12\), and defect length \(3L\).

This is compatible with STCI-R-0010: its saturated-first-relation
hypothesis fails for every \(N>0\). It shows exactly why a replacement
of that hypothesis by generic initial-CI data, generic Rees data, or
constant transverse length would be false. It does not rule out a
global bound involving the projective degrees of an actual \(C_0\)
presentation; this affine family has no such fixed global degree data.

**Corollary 4c (a fixed non-CI full generic initial ideal).** Choose
\(b=p+1\) instead. The full generic ordinary initial ideal is then
\[
 (u^p,\ uv^a,\ v^{2a}),
\]
which has three minimal generators. Indeed over \(K\), write
\(F=u^p+t^{-N}v^a\). Subtracting \(u^{p+1}\) from \(uF\) produces
\(uv^a\); then subtracting \(u^{p-1}(uv^a)\) from \(v^aF\) produces
\(v^{2a}\). Their initial monomials have total colength
\(2a+(p-1)a=(p+1)a\), exactly the original quotient length, proving
equality of initial ideals. Thus the same unbounded intrinsic defect
also occurs for a fixed three-generator generic initial algebra. For
\(p=2,a=3,b=3,N=3L\), this fixed initial ideal is
\((u^2,uv^3,v^6)\), of length nine.

The graded algebra in Corollary 4c has exactly two socle monomials,
\(u^{p-1}v^{a-1}\) and \(v^{2a-1}\), of respective degrees
\(p+a-2\) and \(2a-1\). In particular, the original local algebra is
Gorenstein (it is a complete intersection), while its ordinary tangent
graded algebra is not Gorenstein. A graded self-duality argument that
uses one socle degree cannot be transferred to this non-CI tangent type
without an additional construction of the dual filtration.

## 6. Frontier impact and unresolved step

There is no new unrestricted STCI exclusion here. The structural gain is
a precise separation:

1. Every divisorial valuation on the normalized blowup of a proper lci
   curve ideal is generically transverse; none is a special-point
   divisor.
2. Their intrinsic coefficient/residue-degree budget is exactly the
   generic multiplicity.
3. Ordinary Segre numbers of a complete intersection are fixed by its
   two degrees.
4. A first-relation saturation defect is intrinsic and can grow without
   bound while the full generic filtered algebra, the coprime-CI initial
   type, and the generic divisorial Rees data stay fixed.

Consequently any successful Rees continuation must retain **global
geometry** of the normalized exceptional surfaces (finite maps,
ramification, conductor, special-fiber curves) or the actual filtration
relative to \(I_{C_0}\). The generic divisorial list and ordinary Segre
numbers alone cannot recover the missing saturation data.

For the fixed quartic, the unresolved dependency is a global constraint
on these intrinsic defect lengths or on the finite exceptional covers
that uses the ambient degrees and the balanced normal bundle. The local
counterexample neither supplies nor forbids such a global constraint.
No canonical result/question record, inherited proof, or historical
acceptance record is modified by this note.

# Independent audit: intrinsic Rees divisors and an unbounded first-relation defect

Date: 2026-10-19.

Reviewer: independent `audit_contact_budget` research agent.
Status: **independently reconstructed structural statements on the hypotheses below; not a promoted result**. No canonical result or question record is modified. The conclusions are limitations on proposed invariants, not an STCI exclusion.

## 1. Exact scope, dependencies, and outcome

The blowup statements concern a smooth integral threefold of finite type over a field, and a codimension-two complete-intersection subscheme whose reduced support is one integral curve. The Segre calculation specializes to two homogeneous surfaces in projective three-space with no common surface component. The degeneration family is an affine local model over the affine line; it makes no existence claim for a global homogeneous presentation of the fixed rational quartic.

The companion proposal audited here is [the full-ideal Rees investigation](2026-10-19-rees-residual-investigation.md). The relevant prior evidence is the [exceptional-cycle and vertical-family proposal](2026-10-19-exceptional-cycle-balance-and-vertical-counterexamples.md), especially its local family, and its [independent audit](2026-10-19-exceptional-cycle-and-vertical-family-independent-audit.md). Those records already establish the chosen-pair vertical degeneration. This audit does not repeat that multiplicity calculation or reopen the inherited entirely-thick numerical inequality. It reconstructs the intrinsic full-ideal blowup claims and adds the exact first-relation defect calculation.

The audit outcomes are:

1. Every exceptional prime divisor of the normalized blowup of the full complete-intersection ideal dominates the support curve. Localization at the generic point identifies these divisors with the divisorial Rees valuations of the generic transverse ideal.
2. The coefficients and finite degrees of these divisors satisfy an exact multiplicity identity, proved below.
3. The ordinary Segre class pushed to projective three-space is fixed by the two surface degrees.
4. In the displayed local family, the actual first degree-\(p\) relation has saturation defect of length \(N\). On the subsequence \(N=aL\), this grows without bound while the complete generic ideal, and hence its divisorial Rees data, is fixed up to an explicit filtration-preserving coordinate isomorphism.

The fourth item concerns the degree-\(p\) relation; it does not assume saturated first relations. No assertion is made that valuations are identical in an ambient ring with its coordinates fixed.

## 2. Normalized full-ideal blowup: no exceptional divisors over closed points

Let \(X\) be a smooth integral threefold of finite type over a field. Let \(I\subset\mathcal O_X\) define a codimension-two complete intersection \(Y\), and suppose \(Y_{\mathrm{red}}=C\) is one integral curve. Put

\[
b:B=\operatorname{Bl}_I X\longrightarrow X.
\]

The ideal \(I\mathcal O_B\) is invertible. Write \(I\mathcal O_B=\mathcal O_B(-E)\); its effective Cartier divisor is the scheme-theoretic inverse image of \(Y\). Since the immersion is regular,

\[
\operatorname{gr}_I\mathcal O_X
\simeq\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal O_Y}(I/I^2),
\qquad
E\simeq\operatorname{Proj}_Y
\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal O_Y}(I/I^2).
\tag{R1}
\]

The conormal bundle \(I/I^2\) has rank two over \(Y\). Thus \(E\to Y\) is a projective-line bundle, including its nonreduced base structure. Its reduction \(E_{\mathrm{red}}\to C\) is an irreducible ruled surface. In particular, the fiber of \(b\) over a closed point of \(C\) has dimension one. This assertion does not require the scheme \(Y\) to be reduced or normally flat relative to \(C\).

The scheme \(B\) is integral: locally its Rees algebra is a subring of the polynomial ring over the integral coordinate ring of \(X\). Its normalization

\[
\nu:\overline B\longrightarrow B
\]

is finite, because schemes of finite type over a field are excellent. Outside \(Y\), the map \(b\) is an isomorphism onto a smooth open subscheme, so normalization is an isomorphism there as well. Every prime divisor exceptional for \(b\nu\) is consequently contained in \(\nu^{-1}(E)\).

Let \(D\) be such a prime divisor. Since \(\nu\) is finite, its image has the same dimension as \(D\), namely two. Its image is contained in the irreducible surface \(E_{\mathrm{red}}\), so that image is all of \(E_{\mathrm{red}}\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{every exceptional prime divisor of }\overline B\to X
\text{ dominates }C.}
\tag{R2}
\]

Equivalently, the inverse image of a closed point of \(C\), being finite over a one-dimensional fiber of \(B\), cannot contain a divisor of \(\overline B\). This conclusion concerns divisors of the normalized blowup of the full ideal. It does not forbid vertical curves in \(B\), singularities over special points, or vertical components in intersections of two individually transformed equations on another blowup.

If a proper complete intersection has several reduced curve components, the same reasoning component by component shows that each exceptional prime divisor dominates a curve component of the reduced center. There is still no exceptional prime divisor mapping to an isolated closed point.

## 3. Localization and the generic Rees valuations

Let \(\eta\) be the generic point of \(C\), let

\[
R=\mathcal O_{X,\eta},\qquad J=I R,\qquad
m=\operatorname{length}_R(R/J).
\]

The ring \(R\) is a two-dimensional regular local ring, and \(J\) is a parameter ideal primary to its maximal ideal. Formation of the Rees algebra and blowup commutes with localization. Normalization of a domain also commutes with localization. Consequently

\[
\overline B\times_X\operatorname{Spec}R
\simeq\operatorname{Nor}\bigl(\operatorname{Bl}_J\operatorname{Spec}R\bigr).
\tag{R3}
\]

Every global exceptional divisor from (R2) survives this localization, because its generic point maps to \(\eta\). Conversely, every exceptional prime divisor of the localized normalized blowup has a unique closure among the global exceptional prime divisors. One may see this directly as the localization bijection on primes above the generic point of \(E_{\mathrm{red}}\). Hence the exceptional prime divisors in (R2) are in bijection with the divisorial Rees valuations of \(J\).

This proves that their valuation-theoretic data come from the generic transverse ideal. It does not say that their global exceptional surfaces or the degeneration of those surfaces over closed points are determined by generic data.

## 4. Exact coefficient identity

Write

\[
\nu^*E=\sum_i a_iD_i,
\qquad
f_i=[k(D_i):k(E_{\mathrm{red}})].
\]

The integers \(a_i>0\) are the orders of the full ideal along the corresponding normalized exceptional divisors. By (R2), each map \(D_i\to E_{\mathrm{red}}\) is finite and dominant, so \(f_i\) is a positive finite integer.

The coefficient of the fundamental cycle of \(E\) along \(E_{\mathrm{red}}\) is exactly \(m\): by its projective-bundle description (R1), the local ring at the generic point is a polynomial-localization extension of the Artinian ring \(\mathcal O_{Y,\eta}\) with the same length. Thus

\[
[E]=m[E_{\mathrm{red}}].
\tag{R4}
\]

Since \(\nu\) is finite birational, \(\nu_*[\overline B]=[B]\). Applying the projection formula for the effective Cartier divisor \(E\) gives

\[
\nu_*\bigl(\nu^*E\cap[\overline B]\bigr)
=E\cap[B]=[E].
\]

Comparison of the coefficient of \(E_{\mathrm{red}}\) proves

\[
\boxed{\sum_i a_if_i=m.}
\tag{R5}
\]

For example, over an algebraically closed field, the parameter ideal \((u^a,v^b)\) has one primitive monomial Rees valuation with

\[
v(u)=b/g,\qquad v(v)=a/g,\qquad
a_1=ab/g,\qquad f_1=g,\quad g=\gcd(a,b).
\]

Its exceptional map has degree \(g\), given on the torus by a constant multiple of the \(g\)-th power map. Formula (R5) gives \(a_1f_1=ab\), its transverse complete-intersection length. This example illustrates why the coefficient identity alone permits arbitrarily large multiplicity.

## 5. Segre pushforward for two projective surfaces

Specialize to \(X=\mathbf P^3\), and let \(Y=V(F_d,G_e)\) be the proper scheme intersection of surfaces with no common surface component. It is a regular immersion, with

\[
N_{Y/\mathbf P^3}
\simeq\mathcal O_Y(d)\oplus\mathcal O_Y(e).
\]

For the inclusion \(i:Y\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^3\), the regular-immersion Segre formula gives

\[
s(Y,\mathbf P^3)
=c(N_{Y/\mathbf P^3})^{-1}\cap[Y].
\]

With \(H\) the hyperplane class and \(i_*[Y]=deH^2\), its pushforward is

\[
\boxed{i_*s(Y,\mathbf P^3)
=deH^2-de(d+e)H^3.}
\tag{R6}
\]

Higher terms vanish for dimension reasons. Thus the ordinary Segre numbers pushed to projective three-space are determined by \(d,e\). This computation does not provide a new numerical distinction among thickenings of fixed complete-intersection type. It makes no claim that all finer Segre constructions relative to the smooth reduced support, or normalization and conductor data, are redundant.

## 6. Exact first-relation kernel in the degeneration family

Let \(A=k[t]\), let \(S=A[u,v]\), and put \(\mathfrak q=(u,v)\). Fix

\[
p\ge2,\qquad a>p,\qquad b>p,\qquad N\ge1,
\]

and define

\[
J_N=(u^b,v^a+t^Nu^p),\qquad
B_N=S/J_N,\qquad M_N=\mathfrak q B_N.
\tag{R7}
\]

The original family is a complete intersection with radical \(\mathfrak q\). Indeed \(u^b\) is a nonzerodivisor in \(S\), and the second equation is monic in \(v\), hence is a nonzerodivisor modulo \(u^b\). Successive monic division gives the free \(A\)-basis

\[
u^iv^j,\qquad 0\le i<b,\quad0\le j<a.
\tag{R8}
\]

Thus the original family is finite flat of rank \(ab\). Nilpotence of \(u\), followed by the displayed relation for \(v^a\), gives its stated radical.

There are no relations below degree \(p\), because \(J_N\subset\mathfrak q^p\). The actual degree-\(p\) relation module is

\[
\mathcal R_p
=\ker\left(
\operatorname{Sym}_A^p(\mathfrak q/\mathfrak q^2)
\longrightarrow M_N^p/M_N^{p+1}
\right).
\tag{R9}
\]

Identify the source with \(\mathfrak q^p/\mathfrak q^{p+1}\). Since \(J_N\subset\mathfrak q^p\), its kernel is the image of \(J_N\) in this module. Both \(u^b\) and \(v^a\) vanish modulo \(\mathfrak q^{p+1}\), because \(a,b>p\). For an arbitrary coefficient \(h(t,u,v)\), only its degree-zero term \(h(t,0,0)\) can contribute to the degree-\(p\) image of \(h(t,u,v)t^Nu^p\); every other term belongs to \(\mathfrak q^{p+1}\). Consequently the whole image of the ideal, with all possible coefficient combinations included, is exactly

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_p=A\,t^Nu^p.}
\tag{R10}
\]

In particular there is no additional degree-\(p\) relation arising from cancellation between higher-order terms. The computation is the image of the entire ideal in the quotient, not just the leading term of a selected generator.

Its saturation in the free module \(\operatorname{Sym}_A^p(A^2)\), with respect to the generic point of \(\operatorname{Spec}A\), is \(A u^p\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_p^{\mathrm{sat}}/\mathcal R_p
\simeq A/(t^N),\qquad
\operatorname{length}_A(
\mathcal R_p^{\mathrm{sat}}/\mathcal R_p)=N.}
\tag{R11}
\]

Equivalently, \(M_N^p/M_N^{p+1}\) has a torsion summand \((A/(t^N))u^p\), while its other degree-\(p\) monomials form a free summand. Thus flatness of the original algebra (R8) does not imply flatness of its normal associated graded algebra.

## 7. Fixed generic filtered ideal, with the exact coordinate qualification

Let \(K=k(t)\) and restrict to \(N=aL\), \(L\ge1\). The invertible \(K\)-linear coordinate substitution

\[
u\longmapsto u,\qquad v\longmapsto t^L v'
\]

preserves \((u,v)\) and all of its powers. It takes the generic ideal in (R7) to

\[
(u^b,t^{aL}(v'^a+u^p))
=(u^b,v'^a+u^p),
\tag{R12}
\]

since \(t\) is a unit in \(K\). Hence the complete generic ideals, not just their quotient lengths, are identified by this filtration-preserving coordinate isomorphism. Their Rees algebras, normalized blowups, divisorial Rees valuations, orders \(a_i\), and finite degrees \(f_i\) correspond under that isomorphism. Nevertheless (R11) has length \(aL\), which is unbounded.

This proves the precise limitation:

\[
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
\text{The generic filtered ideal, up to a filtration-preserving}\
\text{coordinate isomorphism, and its corresponding divisorial}\
\text{Rees data do not bound the first-relation saturation defect}\
\text{in this affine local family.}
\end{gathered}}
\tag{R13}
\]

One must not strengthen (R12) to equality in a fixed ambient coordinate ring. For example, take \(p=2,a=b=3\). In \(K[[s]]\), the arc

\[
u=s^3,\qquad v=-t^Ls^2
\]

has order nine on \(J_{3L}=(u^3,v^3+t^{3L}u^2)\), while \(v^3+u^2\) has order six, since \(1-t^{3L}\ne0\). The valuative criterion for integral dependence therefore shows

\[
v^3+u^2\notin\overline{J_{3L}},
\]

although it belongs to \((u^3,v^3+u^2)\). Thus even the integral closures of these ideals need not agree in the fixed ambient coordinates. The comparison made in (R12)--(R13) is explicitly up to the coordinate isomorphism; no identical-ambient-valuations claim is needed.

## 8. Additional audit: fixed full generic initial ideals

The companion proposal's Corollaries 4b and 4c both pass independent reconstruction. In this section, the initial ideal means the full ordinary initial ideal for the maximal-ideal filtration over \(K=k(t)\), using lowest total normal degree. It does not mean a chosen monomial-order initial ideal.

Write \(c=t^{-N}\in K^\times\) and \(F=u^p+cv^a\), so that the generic ideal is \((u^b,F)\).

For \(b=2p\), the identity

\[
(u^p-cv^a)F=u^{2p}-c^2v^{2a}
\]

shows that

\[
(u^{2p},F)=(F,v^{2a}).
\]

The two latter generators have initial forms \(u^p\) and \(v^{2a}\), so

\[
(u^p,v^{2a})\subseteq\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(J_NK[u,v]).
\]

The left ideal has colength \(2pa\), and the original quotient has exactly that length by (R8). Passage to the full associated graded algebra preserves the length of an Artinian local algebra. A strict additional initial relation would reduce this length. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(J_NK[u,v])
=(u^p,v^{2a})\quad\text{when }b=2p.}
\tag{R14}
\]

This is a coprime binary complete-intersection initial type \((p,2a)\). In particular \(p=2,a=3,b=4,N=3L\) has fixed generic full initial type \((2,6)\), length twelve, and unbounded degree-two relation defect \(3L\).

For \(b=p+1\), the exact cancellations

\[
uF-u^{p+1}=cuv^a,
\qquad
v^aF-u^{p-1}(uv^a)=cv^{2a}
\]

give \(uv^a,v^{2a}\in J_NK[u,v]\). Together with the initial form \(u^p\) of \(F\), they give inclusion of

\[
L=(u^p,uv^a,v^{2a})
\]

in the full initial ideal. Its standard monomials are \(v^j\), \(0\le j<2a\), and \(u^iv^j\), \(1\le i<p\), \(0\le j<a\). Hence

\[
\operatorname{length}(K[u,v]/L)
=2a+(p-1)a=(p+1)a,
\]

equal to the original length. The same length argument proves

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(J_NK[u,v])
=(u^p,uv^a,v^{2a})\quad\text{when }b=p+1.}
\tag{R15}
\]

All three monomials are minimal generators since \(p\ge2\). This second initial algebra is not a complete intersection. For \(p=2,a=3,b=3,N=3L\), it is \(K[u,v]/(u^2,uv^3,v^6)\), of length nine, again with unbounded first-relation defect \(3L\).

The additional socle observation also passes. In the quotient of (R15), its socle has exactly the monomial basis

\[
u^{p-1}v^{a-1},\qquad v^{2a-1},
\]

in degrees \(p+a-2\) and \(2a-1\). Indeed a standard monomial with positive \(u\)-exponent is killed by both variables precisely when its \(u\)-exponent is \(p-1\) and its \(v\)-exponent is \(a-1\). Among the standard monomials having zero \(u\)-exponent, only \(v^{2a-1}\) is killed by both variables. Since multiplication by each variable sends distinct surviving monomials to distinct monomials, cancellation supplies no additional socle elements. Thus this associated graded algebra has socle dimension two, whereas the original generic local algebra is a complete intersection and hence Gorenstein. A one-dimensional graded-socle pairing therefore cannot be transferred from the original algebra to its full ordinary associated graded algebra in this example.

These two subfamilies show that the limitation (R13) persists both with a fixed coprime-CI full generic initial type and with a fixed three-generator full generic initial ideal. They do not weaken the saturated-actual-first-relation hypothesis of [STCI-R-0010](../results/saturated_first_relation_normal_flatness.md): by (R11), that hypothesis fails for every \(N>0\). No proof of that promoted theorem is repeated here.

## 9. Frontier impact and unresolved dependencies

The full-ideal normalized blowup removes the dependence on a chosen pair of generators, but its divisorial valuations over a pure curve-supported complete intersection all come from the generic transverse ideal. The example proves that those generic data do not control even the first-relation saturation defect along the base. The ordinary Segre pushforward supplies only the degree expression (R6).

These conclusions leave open invariants retaining global variation: the exceptional surfaces as finite covers of the reduced exceptional ruled surface, their degeneration, ramification or conductor data, and the full filtration relative to the reduced support. No inequality for those finer data is proved here. The affine family does not establish their realizability for the fixed rational quartic or a global two-hypersurface STCI presentation. The unrestricted characteristic-zero quartic question remains open.

The evidence in this audit is the exact arguments above. No computational reduction is being reported as a completed calculation, and no promoted theorem or closed quartic-carrier case is reopened.

## 2026-10-19-exceptional-cycle-balance-and-vertical-counterexamples

# Exceptional cycle balance and unbounded vertical degeneration

Status: **proposed structural statements with proofs and an independent reconstruction on the stated hypotheses; not a promoted result**. See the [independent audit](2026-10-19-exceptional-cycle-and-vertical-family-independent-audit.md), including the moving-direction addendum.

The fixed-curve statements concern the characteristic-zero quartic
\(C=C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\), with the accepted inputs
\(N_{C/\mathbf P^3}=\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(7)^{\oplus2}\) and
\(C\subset Q\simeq\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) of type \((1,3)\), after choosing the order of the rulings.
The broader hypothesis is \(C\subset V(F,G)_{\mathrm{red}}\), where the two surfaces have no common surface component. No integral-carrier, normality, smooth-root, or coprime-leading-form hypothesis is imposed.

Accepted numerical restrictions are recorded in [the entirely-thick topic](../results/entirely_thick.md). This note does not re-audit them or claim an unrestricted STCI exclusion. Its new contributions are residual-cycle corrections, support-sensitive conditional STCI exclusions in section 2a, and explicit counterexamples showing that vertical degeneration is unbounded with fixed transverse generic algebra and depends on the chosen generators.

## 1. Exact first-blowup balance

Let \(d=\deg F\), \(e=\deg G\), and let \(p,q\ge1\) be their exact generic orders along \(C\). Write
\[
[V(F,G)]=m[C]+R,\qquad r=\deg R=de-4m.
\]
All cycles below retain their scheme-theoretic generic multiplicities.

Let \(\pi:B=\operatorname{Bl}_C\mathbf P^3\to\mathbf P^3\), let \(H=\pi^*\mathcal O(1)\), and let \(E\) be its exceptional divisor. Use the convention that \(\mathbf P(N)\) parametrizes lines, so that
\[
E\simeq\mathbf P^1_{\rm base}\times\mathbf P^1_{\rm dir},\qquad
H|_E=4\alpha,\quad E|_E=7\alpha-\beta,
\]
where \(\alpha,\beta\) are pullbacks of \(\mathcal O(1)\) from the indicated factors.
Thus \(\alpha^2=\beta^2=0\) and \(\alpha\beta=1\).

The two strict surfaces have divisor classes \(dH-pE,eH-qE\), and have no common surface component. Their intersection is a pure effective one-cycle
\[
X=(dH-pE)(eH-qE)=\widetilde R+Y,
\]
where \(\widetilde R\) consists of the components outside \(E\) and is the strict-transform cycle of \(R\); \(Y\) consists of all components contained in \(E\). Write its divisor class on \(E\) as
\[
[Y]_E=A\alpha+B\beta,\qquad A,B\ge0,
\]
and define the residual contact number
\[
\lambda=E\cdot\widetilde R\ge0.
\]
The horizontal degree is \(B\); \(A\) includes the total multiplicity of vertical fibers as well as the other \(\alpha\)-contributions of horizontal curves.

**Proposition 1 (cycle balance).**
\[
\boxed{B=m-pq},\qquad
\boxed{A=\lambda+7(m+pq)-4(dq+ep)}.
\tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** The restrictions above give
\[
H^2E=0,\quad HE^2=-4,\quad E^3=-14.
\]
Consequently
\[
H\cdot X=de-4pq,
\qquad E\cdot X=4(dq+ep)-14pq.
\]
On the decomposition of \(X\), these two numbers are respectively
\(r+4B\) and \(\lambda+7B-A\). Use \(r=de-4m\) and solve. This proof only uses degrees and proper intersection; it makes no identification with a projectivized normal cone or the zero locus of two first symbols. ∎

In particular the complete strict-transform exceptional cycle, including vertical components, has a controlled numerical class. It is the **entire effective cycle** whose class enters (1); horizontal and vertical parts cannot be interchanged without compensating changes.

For a rational curve of degree \(c\) with explicitly balanced normal bundle \(\mathcal O(n)^{\oplus2}\), the identical proof gives
\[
B=m-pq,\qquad A=\lambda+n(m+pq)-c(dq+ep).
\tag{2}
\]
This extension assumes the balanced bundle and does not infer it from rationality.

## 2. Residual-degree inequalities and quadric rulings

**Proposition 2 (residual correction).** Under Proposition 1,
\[
\boxed{7de+28pq+5r\ge16(dq+ep).}
\tag{3}
\]
If no component of \(R\) lies on the unique quadric \(Q\), then the stronger inequality holds:
\[
\boxed{7de+28pq+r\ge16(dq+ep).}
\tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** The ideal sheaf \(I_C(3)\) is globally generated. One quick proof uses
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3}(1)
\xrightarrow{\cdot Q} I_C(3)
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_Q(2,0)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The outer sheaves are globally generated and \(H^1(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3}(1))=0\), so global sections generate the middle sheaf: away from \(Q\), the multiples of \(Q\) suffice, and on \(Q\), the lifted generators of the quotient together with those multiples suffice. Hence \(3H-E\) is globally generated on the blowup, giving \(\lambda\le3r\). Substituting \(m=(de-r)/4\) into \(A\ge0\) in (1) proves (3).

If no residual component lies in \(Q\), its strict-transform cycle has nonnegative intersection with the effective strict quadric \(2H-E\). Therefore \(\lambda\le2r\), and the same substitution proves (4). ∎

The distinction between (3) and (4) is geometric. If
\[
R=R_{\rm out}+R_Q,\quad
\deg R_{\rm out}=r_{\rm out},\quad [R_Q]_Q=a\alpha_Q+b\beta_Q,
\]
where no component of \(R_{\rm out}\) lies in \(Q\), then
\[
\lambda_Q=C\cdot R_Q=3a+b,\qquad
\lambda_{\rm out}\le2r_{\rm out}.
\]
Indeed the strict transform of \(Q\) is isomorphic to \(Q\), since \(C\) is Cartier on it, and \(E|_{\widetilde Q}=C\). This gives the refined necessary condition
\[
\boxed{7de+28pq+r_{\rm out}+5a-3b\ge16(dq+ep).}
\tag{5}
\]
The ratio \(\lambda/r\) can equal three on a trisecant ruling line of \(Q\), so a universal bound \(\lambda\le2r\) would be false. No claim is made that (3) or (5) is attained by every prescribed collection of intersection data.

For \(r=0\), (3) returns the established entirely-thick numerical condition. It does not improve that STCI specialization. For the broader intersection problem, a positive left-hand deficit gives the explicit lower bound
\[
r\ge\max\left(0,\left\lceil\frac{16(dq+ep)-7de-28pq}{5}\right\rceil\right).
\]
The contact number \(\lambda\) depends on the residual cycle and \(C\), and does not depend on a presentation of its ideal by \(F,G\).

## 2a. A moving-direction refinement and conditional STCI exclusions

For each horizontal irreducible component \(\Gamma_i\) of the common zero locus of the first normal forms on \(E\), write
\[
[\Gamma_i]=a_i\alpha+b_i\beta,\qquad b_i>0.
\]
It is also a component of the proper intersection \(X\), with some positive generic intersection multiplicity \(n_i\). Every horizontal component of \(X\) arises this way. Let \(\nu\) be the total multiplicity of its vertical fibers. Then
\[
A=\nu+\sum_i n_i a_i,\qquad B=\sum_i n_i b_i.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, if every common horizontal component has slope \(a_i/b_i\ge\rho\), then \(A\ge\rho B\). Together with (1), this gives
\[
\lambda+(7-\rho)m+(7+\rho)pq\ge4(dq+ep).
\tag{7}
\]
This is a support-dependent refinement; it does not presume that two first symbols determine their actual intersection multiplicities.

**Proposition 2a (unique moving-section case).** Suppose the only common horizontal root of the first normal forms is the graph \(\Gamma\) of a morphism \(\mathbf P^1\to\mathbf P^1\) of degree \(\epsilon\). Then
\[
\boxed{\nu=\lambda+(7-\epsilon)m+(7+\epsilon)pq-4(dq+ep)\ge0.}
\tag{8}
\]
This follows from \([\Gamma]=\epsilon\alpha+\beta\), since its multiplicity in \(Y\) is \(B=m-pq\). The common root can have arbitrary multiplicities in the two first symbols; neither transverse regularity nor coprime initial forms are assumed.

For an STCI presentation with exact orders \(p,q\ge2\), write \(\alpha_0=d/p\), \(\beta_0=e/q\). The accepted factor reduction rules out a quadric factor of either equation, and the entirely-thick restriction-to-quadric input gives \(\alpha_0,\beta_0\ge3\). This lower bound is **not** asserted for arbitrary residual intersections with a quadric factor. Equation (8) now yields the stronger necessary condition
\[
\boxed{\frac{7-\epsilon}{4}\alpha_0\beta_0+7+\epsilon
\ge4(\alpha_0+\beta_0).}
\tag{9}
\]
Two infinite conditional families are thereby excluded:

- If \(\epsilon\ge2\), no such STCI pair can have
  \(\min(\alpha_0,\beta_0)\le16/5\). Indeed increasing \(\epsilon\) decreases the left side of (9), because \(\alpha_0\beta_0/4>1\); for \(\epsilon=2\), (9) rearranges to
  \(\beta_0(5\alpha_0/4-4)\ge4\alpha_0-9\), impossible when \(3\le\alpha_0\le16/5\).
- If \(\epsilon=1\) and \(\alpha_0=3\), then \(\beta_0\ge8\). The unsloped condition alone only forces \(\beta_0\ge4\) on this boundary.

For example the unique-degree-two-section hypothesis excludes normalized degree pair \((\alpha_0,\beta_0)=(4,4)\), which the unsloped inequality allows. These conclusions are about the **common horizontal root support**, not arbitrary moving first relation sheaves. They allow arbitrary special-point degeneration and vertical fibers, whose effect is retained in \(\nu\ge0\). They do not exclude common-root configurations with additional lower-slope components, nor arbitrary STCI equations.

There is also a necessary condition with no prescribed common-root configuration. For every hypothetical entirely-thick STCI pair,
\[
B=pq(\alpha_0\beta_0/4-1)>0,\qquad
A-7B=pq\bigl(14-4\alpha_0-4\beta_0\bigr)<0.
\]
Equation (6) therefore forces **at least one horizontal common-root component of slope strictly less than seven**. In the unique-section case this implies the degree-independent bound \(\epsilon\le6\). The conclusion concerns the support of the two first normal forms; it is neither a full initial-ideal classification nor a formal-root lift theorem.

## 3. Unbounded vertical fibers with fixed entirely-thick generic data

**Proposition 3 (local counterexample family).** Fix integers \(p\ge2\), \(a>p\), \(b>p\). For each \(N\ge1\), in the smooth threefold \(\mathbf A^3_{t,u,v}\), put
\[
C=V(u,v),\qquad f=t^N u^p+v^a,\qquad g=f+u^b.
\]
Then the following hold, over any field (in particular in characteristic zero).

1. \((f,g)=(u^b,v^a+t^N u^p)\) is a complete-intersection ideal with radical \((u,v)\).
2. Its quotient is finite free of rank \(m=ab\) over \(k[t]\), with basis \(u^i v^j\), \(0\le i<b,0\le j<a\).
3. Both displayed equations have exact generic normal order \(p\).
4. On \(\operatorname{Bl}_C\mathbf A^3\), the intersection cycle of their two strict surfaces consists of the horizontal section \(u=0\) in the exceptional bundle, with coefficient \(ab-p^2\), and the vertical exceptional fiber over \(t=0\), with coefficient \(N(b-p)\).
5. The equally valid generators \((f,g-f)=(f,u^b)\) have orders \(p,b\). Their strict-surface intersection contains no vertical fiber, and has the same horizontal section with coefficient \(b(a-p)\).

**Proof.** Monic division first by \(u^b\), then by \(v^a+t^N u^p\), proves the basis assertion. The quotient is free over \(k[t]\), so this is a flat lci family with constant transverse length. Its radical is \((u,v)\) because \(u\) is nilpotent, followed by \(v\). There is no residual one-dimensional component.

In the blowup chart \(v=uw\), the two displayed strict equations generate
\[
(t^N+u^{a-p}w^a,\ u^{b-p}).
\]
At the generic point of the exceptional fiber \(u=t=0\), \(w\) is a transcendental unit, and this quotient has length \(N(b-p)\): it is free of rank \(N\) over \(k(w)[u]/(u^{b-p})\), by monic division in \(t\). This proves the vertical multiplicity.

In the other chart \(u=vw\), the strict ideal is
\[
(t^Nw^p+v^{a-p},\ v^{b-p}w^b).
\]
Over \(k(t)\), its support is \(v=w=0\), the horizontal section corresponding to the normal direction \(u=0\). Additivity of intersection multiplicity in the factored second equation gives
\[
(b-p)\,\operatorname{length}K[[w]]/(t^Nw^p)
+b\,\operatorname{length}K[[v]]/(v^{a-p})
=(b-p)p+b(a-p)=ab-p^2.
\]
There are no other components: away from the exceptional locus the blowup is an isomorphism and \(V(f,g)\) has support \(C\); within that locus, the two initial forms have the sole generic normal direction \(u=0\), and their only common entire fiber is at \(t=0\).

For the replacement pair, the strict equation of \(u^b\) is the unit one in the first chart, excluding a vertical fiber. In the second chart the strict ideal is
\((t^Nw^p+v^{a-p},w^b)\), free of rank \(b(a-p)\) over \(k[t]\). This proves the final assertion. ∎

With fixed \(p,a,b\), generic normal orders and length remain fixed while the vertical coefficient is unbounded. Thus no upper bound for vertical multiplicity can depend only on \(m,p,q\); even flatness and a pure \(C\)-supported lci do not supply such a bound. This is a local statement: it does **not** construct an STCI of the fixed quartic.

The stronger conclusion with a fixed **generic algebra**, rather than just fixed numerical data, holds on the subsequence \(N=aL\). Over \(K=k(t)\), the substitution \(v=t^L v'\) identifies every such quotient with
\[
K[u,v']/(u^b,v'^a+u^p).
\]
The substitution preserves the maximal-ideal filtration, while the vertical coefficient \(aL(b-p)\) is unbounded. Thus even the isomorphism class of the generic filtered algebra does not bound vertical multiplicity of these chosen-pair strict transforms.

## 4. An actual projective pure-support example

Vertical fibers can occur even for homogeneous two-hypersurface presentations on projective three-space. For \(p\ge2,N\ge1\), take coordinates \([T:U:V:W]\) and
\[
F=T^NU^p+V^{N+p},\qquad
G=F+U^{N+p}.
\]
Both equations have degree \(D=N+p\), no common surface component, and
\[
\sqrt{(F,G)}=(U,V).
\]
Their intersection is a complete-intersection thickening of the line \(L=V(U,V)\), of generic length \(D^2\). Their generic orders along \(L\) are \(p,p\). Near \([0:0:0:1]\), this is Proposition 3 with \(a=b=N+p\), so the first strict-surface intersection contains the exceptional fiber there with coefficient \(N^2\). The leading forms have no other common entire fiber. Replacing \(G\) by \(G-F=U^{N+p}\) removes that vertical fiber. This homogeneous example concerns a line with a different normal bundle; its role is to refute a general vertical-implies-residual principle, not to settle any quartic case.

## 5. Frontier impact and unresolved interface

The numerical class of the entire first-blowup exceptional cycle satisfies an exact residual-contact balance. The contact bound is sensitive to residual curves on the unique quadric and to its two rulings. The slopes of actual common normal directions refine the numerical obstruction and exclude the conditional infinite families in §2a. Vertical exceptional multiplicities can nevertheless be arbitrarily large while the generic filtered algebra is fixed (using the subsequence above), and they can disappear after an invertible generator change.

Consequently a proposed intrinsic invariant built from successive intersections of **individual strict equations** needs an explicit proof of generator invariance, or explicit correction terms. The normal cone of the quotient and the saturated full initial ideal remain distinct from these chosen-pair cycles. The examples do not rule out invariants of the full filtered ideal.

The precise unsolved step relevant to the quartic is to further constrain the distribution of the effective class \((A,B)\) among horizontal curves and vertical fibers using the globally realizable full normal filtration. The residual-correction inequality alone recovers the inherited inequality when \(R=0\); section 2a adds new conditional exclusions using horizontal support slopes. The unrestricted characteristic-zero \(C_0\) STCI problem remains open.

## 1. Dependencies, assumptions, and authority

The fixed projective curve is the characteristic-zero smooth rational quartic

\[
C=C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3.
\]

The accepted inputs used are its normal bundle
\(N_{C/\mathbf P^3}\simeq\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(7)^{\oplus2}\), and its realization as a divisor of type \((1,3)\) on the smooth quadric \(Q\). These are given mathematical premises of the investigation; this audit does not repeat their proofs. The [two-surface synthesis](../synthesis/02-two-surfaces.md) supplies navigation and conventions, not independent theorem authority. The [entirely-thick topic](../results/entirely_thick.md) and its cited historical audit concern the already known residual-free necessary inequality; the proposed work extends its bookkeeping to an actual residual cycle.

Let \(F,G\) have degrees \(d,e\), contain \(C\), and have no common surface component. Their exact generic orders along \(C\) are \(p,q\ge1\). No assumption of integral surfaces, a smooth common root, or saturation of a first-symbol relation is needed. Write the actual intersection cycle as

\[
[V(F,G)]=m[C]+R,\qquad \rho=\deg R=de-4m.
\]

The following argument retains every component of \(R\), with its generic scheme multiplicity. It also retains every horizontal and vertical component on the blowup exceptional divisor. It does not identify any of these objects with the projectivized normal cone of the quotient.

## 2. Reconstruction of the global cycle and all signs

Blow up \(C\), with map \(\pi:B\to\mathbf P^3\), exceptional divisor \(E\), and \(H=\pi^*\mathcal O(1)\). Use the projective bundle of normal lines. Write

\[
E\simeq\mathbf P^1_{\rm base}\times\mathbf P^1_{\rm dir},\quad
\alpha=c_1\mathcal O(1,0),\quad\beta=c_1\mathcal O(0,1).
\]

The tautological normal line of \(E\) in \(B\) is
\(\mathcal O_E(E)=\mathcal O_E(7,-1)\), and
\(\mathcal O_E(H)=\mathcal O_E(4,0)\). Therefore

\[
H^2E=0,\quad HE^2=-4,\quad E^3=-14.
\tag{A1}
\]

The strict surface divisors are \(D=dH-pE\), \(D'=eH-qE\). They have no common surface component: one mapping onto a surface downstairs would contradict the original hypothesis, and neither strict divisor contains \(E\). Their scheme intersection is a codimension-two complete intersection in the smooth threefold, hence its fundamental cycle is effective and pure of dimension one.

Separate this cycle as

\[
X=D\cdot D'=\widetilde R+Y.
\]

Outside \(E\), the blowup is an isomorphism, so the first term is precisely the strict-transform cycle of \(R\), with its original multiplicities. Write

\[
[Y]_E=A\alpha+B\beta,\qquad A,B\ge0,\qquad
\lambda=E\cdot\widetilde R\ge0.
\]

Effectivity gives \(A,B\ge0\) on \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\). Nonnegativity of \(\lambda\) follows because no component of \(\widetilde R\) lies in the effective Cartier divisor \(E\); it does not require \(R\) to be nonsingular or reduced. Using (A1) gives independently

\[
H\cdot X=de-4pq=\rho+4B,
\]

\[
E\cdot X=4(dq+ep)-14pq=\lambda+7B-A.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{B=m-pq},\qquad
\boxed{A=\lambda+7(m+pq)-4(dq+ep)}.
\tag{A2}
\]

This verifies both the coefficient and the sign of the residual contact correction. Here \(B\) is the total degree over the base of the horizontal exceptional cycle. A vertical fiber has class \(\alpha\), but \(A\) also receives \(\alpha\)-degree from horizontal components. Therefore \(A\) must not be equated with the vertical multiplicity alone.

For a smooth rational curve of degree \(c\) with the explicitly assumed balanced normal bundle \(\mathcal O(n)^2\), the same calculation has \(HE^2=-c\), \(E^3=-2n\), and gives

\[
B=m-pq,\qquad A=\lambda+n(m+pq)-c(dq+ep).
\]

Balancedness is an additional hypothesis in this generalization.

## 3. Residual bounds, including the quadric correction

The sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3}(1)
\xrightarrow{\cdot Q} I_C(3)
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_Q(2,0)\longrightarrow0
\]

proves that \(I_C(3)\) is generated by global sections: the two outer sheaves are generated, and \(H^1(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3}(1))=0\) lifts the quotient sections. Thus \(3H-E\) is globally generated and has nonnegative degree on every integral curve. It follows that

\[
\lambda\le3\rho.
\tag{A3}
\]

If no component of \(R\) lies on \(Q\), the effective strict quadric \(\widetilde Q\sim2H-E\) meets every residual component nonnegatively, giving

\[
\lambda\le2\rho.
\tag{A4}
\]

Substitution into \(A\ge0\), using \(4m=de-\rho\), gives respectively

\[
\boxed{7de+28pq+5\rho\ge16(dq+ep)},
\]

\[
\boxed{7de+28pq+\rho\ge16(dq+ep)}\quad
\text{if no residual component lies on }Q.
\]

The refined statement also checks. Decompose \(R=R_{\rm out}+R_Q\), put \(\rho_{\rm out}=\deg R_{\rm out}\), and write the weighted divisor class of \(R_Q\) on \(Q\) as \((a,b)\), with \(C\) of class \((1,3)\). The strict quadric is isomorphic to \(Q\), because blowing up a Cartier divisor is the identity; under this identification, \(E|_{\widetilde Q}=C\). Hence

\[
\lambda_Q=3a+b,\quad \deg R_Q=a+b,\quad
\lambda_{\rm out}\le2\rho_{\rm out}.
\]

Consequently

\[
4\lambda-7\deg R\le\rho_{\rm out}+5a-3b,
\]

which verifies

\[
\boxed{7de+28pq+\rho_{\rm out}+5a-3b\ge16(dq+ep)}.
\]

A ruling line of type \((1,0)\) has \(C\)-intersection three and degree one. Thus \(\lambda\le2\rho\) cannot be asserted for an arbitrary residual cycle meeting \(Q\). None of these formulas by itself gives a new contradiction at \(R=0\): their residual-free specialization is the inherited inequality.

## 4. Independent local scheme calculation

Use different notation from the global generic orders. Fix \(p\ge2\), \(a,b>p\), and \(N\ge1\), and set

\[
f=t^N u^p+v^a,\quad g=f+u^b,\quad
J=(f,g)=(u^b,v^a+t^N u^p)\subset k[t,u,v].
\]

The sequence \(u^b,v^a+t^N u^p\) is regular: after quotienting by \(u^b\), the second polynomial is monic in \(v\), so is a nonzerodivisor. Monic division gives a free \(k[t]\)-basis

\[
u^iv^j,\quad0\le i<b,\quad0\le j<a.
\]

In particular the transverse family is finite flat of rank \(ab\), and its radical is \((u,v)\). Nilpotence of \(u\), followed by the relation for \(v^a\), proves the latter directly. No residual curve downstairs occurs.

On the \(u\)-chart of the blowup of \((u,v)\), put \(v=uw\). The original two strict equations generate

\[
I_u=(t^N+u^{a-p}w^a,u^{b-p}).
\tag{A5}
\]

Its support is the vertical fiber \(u=t=0\). At the generic point of that fiber the residue direction \(w\) is a unit. The quotient in the transverse parameters \(u,t\) is free of rank \(N\) over \(k(w)[u]/u^{b-p}\). Thus its length is exactly

\[
N(b-p).
\tag{A6}
\]

On the \(v\)-chart, put \(u=vz\). The strict ideal is

\[
I_v=(t^Nz^p+v^{a-p},v^{b-p}z^b).
\tag{A7}
\]

Its support is precisely the union of the vertical fiber \(v=t=0\) and the horizontal section \(v=z=0\). At the horizontal generic point, work over \(K=k(t)\). The first equation is divisible by neither \(v\) nor \(z\). Additivity of intersection multiplicity with the factorization of the second equation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
i(t^Nz^p+v^{a-p},v^{b-p}z^b)
&=(b-p)i(t^Nz^p+v^{a-p},v)\\
&\quad+b\,i(t^Nz^p+v^{a-p},z)\\
&=(b-p)p+b(a-p)=ab-p^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A8}
\]

The original surfaces have no common component, and the displayed chart equations have codimension two, so the total strict-surface intersection is a pure one-dimensional complete intersection. There is no hidden additional curve at the junction of the two components. In particular (A6) and (A8) determine its full fundamental cycle.

**Special-fiber distinction.** Setting \(t=0\) in (A5) gives
\((u^{a-p}w^a,u^{b-p})\). This special fiber is one-dimensional. Its scheme thickness is not the definition of (A6): the latter is the length in the threefold at the generic prime \((u,t)\). Thus the flat original family and the nonflat transform intersection are fully compatible.

For the replacement generator \(g-f=u^b\), its strict equation is the unit on the \(u\)-chart and \(z^b\) on the \(v\)-chart. The transformed intersection is therefore

\[
(t^Nz^p+v^{a-p},z^b),
\]

free of rank \(b(a-p)\) over \(k[t]\), with support the horizontal section alone. This verifies that the vertical component can disappear under an invertible change of generators. The generic orders change from \((p,p)\) to \((p,b)\), so this is dependence on the displayed pair of surface divisors; it is not a contradiction to invariance of the ideal \(J\).

For all \(N\), the numerical generic data \((m,p,p)=(ab,p,p)\) remain fixed while (A6) is unbounded. If the stronger statement requires the **same \(K\)-algebra up to \(K\)-isomorphism**, restrict to \(N=aL\), \(L\ge1\). The substitution \(v=t^L v'\) over \(K\) identifies

\[
K[u,v]/(u^b,v^a+t^{aL}u^p)
\simeq K[u,v']/(u^b,v'^a+u^p).
\]

The vertical multiplicity \(aL(b-p)\) remains unbounded along this subsequence. Without this restriction or a field extension, equal lengths and generic orders alone would not justify the phrase “same generic algebra.”

These arguments work in every characteristic. They are exact local algebra and intersection calculations, not finite computational evidence.

## 5. Homogeneous projective line example

For coordinates \([T:U:V:W]\), let \(D=N+p\) and

\[
F=T^NU^p+V^D,\qquad G=F+U^D.
\]

The ideal is \((U^D,V^D+T^NU^p)\), whose radical is \((U,V)\). It is a homogeneous regular sequence with no common surface factor, and its projective support is the line \(L\). Monic division over \(k[T,W]\) gives generic multiplicity \(D^2\) along \(L\). The two generic orders are \(p,p\).

On the affine chart \(W\ne0\), the point \([0:0:0:1]\) and its blowup are the local model above with \(a=b=D\). Its vertical coefficient is \(N(D-p)=N^2\); the horizontal coefficient is \(D^2-p^2\). At all other points of the line the leading coefficient \(T^N\) is a unit, so there is no additional whole exceptional fiber. As a separate sign check, \(N_{L/\mathbf P^3}=\mathcal O(1)^2\) gives \(A=(D-p)^2=N^2\), exactly the local calculation.

The replacement pair \((F,U^D)\) removes that fiber. This is an actual homogeneous projective example of vertical exceptional degeneration with no residual curve downstairs. Its curve is a line, and it supplies no existence claim for the fixed rational quartic.

## 6. Audit conclusion and remaining implication

The proposed identities and inequalities are independently proved on their stated scope. The examples prove that neither flatness, pure support, fixed generic numerical data, nor even a fixed generic algebra bounds the vertical coefficient in the local setting. The homogeneous example also disproves a general projective implication from a vertical exceptional curve to a residual curve downstairs.

The missing fixed-quartic implication is a constraint on which complete effective classes \((A,B)\), including their decomposition into horizontal curves and vertical fibers, arise from global homogeneous equations and their full normal filtration. The present calculations do not supply that constraint, do not produce an unrestricted smooth-root bridge, and do not resolve the characteristic-zero \(C_0\) STCI problem.

## 7. Addendum audit: slopes of horizontal common-root components

This addendum independently audits §2a added to the proposal after the first audit. **Outcome: the general slope refinement, unique-section identity, and conditional STCI exclusions pass.** Their hypotheses concern all horizontal common-root components of the actual first normal forms, rather than a chosen relation sheaf or one selected root among several.

Let the irreducible horizontal components of the common zero support of the two leading forms on \(E\) be \(\Gamma_i\), with

\[
[\Gamma_i]=a_i\alpha+b_i\beta,\qquad b_i>0.
\]

Each \(\Gamma_i\) is contained in both strict surface divisors. Since their intersection is proper and pure of dimension one, it is a component of that intersection with some positive generic multiplicity \(n_i\). Conversely every horizontal component of \(Y\) is contained in both restricted leading divisors. There is no inference here that \(n_i\) is bounded by the root multiplicity in either leading form.

Every irreducible vertical curve in \(E=\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) is a fiber, with class \(\alpha\). Thus, for the total vertical multiplicity \(\nu\), one has exactly

\[
A=\nu+\sum_i n_i a_i,\qquad B=\sum_i n_i b_i.
\tag{A9}
\]

If every \(a_i/b_i\ge s\), then (A9) gives \(A\ge sB\). Together with (A2), this proves

\[
\boxed{\lambda+(7-s)m+(7+s)pq\ge4(dq+ep)}.
\tag{A10}
\]

The notation \(s\) here denotes a lower bound on slopes, not a normal order or a parameter of the curve. The inequality remains valid in the broader residual setting; replacing \(m\) by \(de/4\) requires \(R=0\).

If the sole horizontal common root is the graph of a degree-\(\epsilon\) morphism \(\mathbf P^1\to\mathbf P^1\), then its class is \(\epsilon\alpha+\beta\). Its multiplicity in \(Y\) is exactly \(B\), so (A9) gives the sharper identity

\[
\boxed{\nu=\lambda+(7-\epsilon)m+(7+\epsilon)pq-4(dq+ep)\ge0}.
\tag{A11}
\]

This holds despite arbitrary multiplicities of that root and arbitrary special-point degenerations.

For the STCI specialization, put \(x=d/p\), \(y=e/q\). The [inherited thick audit](2026-10-06-session-thick-independent-audit.md) proves that neither equation can be divisible by \(Q\), and restriction to \(Q\) then gives \(x,y\ge3\). This premise is supported by the linked audited legacy evidence; there is no separate ID-bearing promoted record for that numerical restriction in the current canonical index. This dependence is essential: the lower bounds are not asserted for arbitrary residual pairs containing a quadric factor. Since \(m=de/4\) and \(\lambda=0\), (A10) becomes

\[
\boxed{\frac{7-s}{4}xy+7+s\ge4(x+y)}.
\tag{A12}
\]

Two numerical consequences are checked directly:

1. If \(s\ge2\), the left side of (A12) decreases with \(s\), because \(xy/4\ge9/4>1\). At \(s=2\), the inequality is
   \[
   y(5x/4-4)\ge4x-9.
   \]
   If \(3\le x\le16/5\), its left side is nonpositive and its right side is positive. By symmetry, \(\min(x,y)\le16/5\) is impossible. This proof works for arbitrary horizontal components all having slope at least two, and in particular for a unique degree-\(\epsilon\) section with \(\epsilon\ge2\).
2. If \(s=1\) and \(x=3\), (A12) reads \(y/2\ge4\), hence \(y\ge8\). For a unique degree-one section, this is exactly the proposed boundary restriction.

The additional example \((x,y)=(4,4)\), \(s=2\) is excluded since (A12) would require \(29\ge32\). The unsloped inequality gives \(35\ge32\), so this is a strict strengthening in a nonempty numerical region.

There is also a degree-independent structural consequence without a prescribed slope hypothesis. Under the same entirely-thick STCI assumptions, \(x,y\ge3\) implies

\[
B=pq(xy/4-1)>0,\qquad
A-7B=pq(14-4x-4y)<0.
\]

If every common horizontal root had slope at least seven, (A9) would instead give \(A\ge7B\). Therefore **at least one horizontal common-root component has slope strictly less than seven**. As its bidegree is integral, this means \(a_i\le7b_i-1\). In the unique-section case it gives the unconditional restriction \(\epsilon\le6\). This is a necessary support restriction for hypothetical entirely-thick STCI presentations; it does not assert that the restricted supports are realizable.

The exclusions using a prescribed lower slope remain support-dependent and conditional. They do not prove that arbitrary pairs have that lower bound on every root-component slope. Additional lower-slope horizontal components can defeat their hypothesis even when one highly moving root exists. Special-point vertical fibers cannot defeat (A10), since they contribute nonnegatively to \(A\) and have already been included in \(\nu\).
