# Mixed-characteristic deformation of torus-homogeneous pairs

Fix the integral embedded curve
\(C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3\), with coordinate
weights \((0,1,3,4)\). The
[positive-characteristic trace theorem](EQUIVARIANT_DEGREE_BOUNDS.md)
forces \(p\mid ab\) for a supported pair with a weight-homogeneous
equation. This note studies simultaneous deformations of a pair whose
**two special-fiber equations** are weight-homogeneous. Both lifted
equations may lose this symmetry.

**Status, October 31, 2026:** independently reconstructed, reviewed
provisional deductions; no canonical promotion. These constrain lifts of
specified special fibers. They do not exclude arbitrary characteristic-zero
STCIs or prove that every characteristic-zero pair specializes to this locus.

The later [prime-to-p extraction theorem](PRIME_TO_P_POWER_EXTRACTION.md)
excludes **all** ramified supported lifts of the particular powered-quartic
pairs below when the power is prime to \(p\). It uses full nilpotence
and global primary-ideal membership, beyond the trace argument here.
General eigen special fibers are still subject only to the stated trace
restriction unless another result applies.
The [Frobenius-power extension](FROBENIUS_POWER_LIFTING_PRECISION.md)
also excludes unramified lifts of the prescribed \(p\)-divisible powers,
gives their exact strong Witt precision, and requires ramification index
at least \(p-1\) for any remaining supported trait.

## The valuation obstruction

Let \(R\) be a mixed-characteristic DVR with uniformizer \(\pi\),
residue characteristic \(p\), and \(e=v_\pi(p)\). Suppose two
homogeneous forms \(\widetilde F_a,\widetilde G_b\) have:

- a proper special fiber supported exactly on the fixed curve, with both
  equations weight-homogeneous;
- a characteristic-zero generic fiber supported exactly on the fixed curve.

After exchanging and normalizing the forms, their reductions contain
\(X^a\) and \(Y^b\), respectively, with coefficient one, where
\(X=x_1,Y=x_2\). Their special weights are \(a,3b\).
Normalize these same unit coefficients to one over \(R\).
Let \(J_R\) be the ideal generated by coefficients of monomials of
weights different from \(a\) in the first form or \(3b\) in the
second. If \(J_R\ne0\), put \(r=v_\pi(J_R)\ge1\). Then
\[
 \boxed{ab\in J_R^2,\qquad e\,v_p(ab)\ge2r.} \tag{1}
\]
If \(J_R=0\), such a generic fiber is impossible.

In particular, if \(v_p(ab)=1\), an **unramified** lift with the
stated generic support is impossible. A ramified lift must have
\(e\ge2\), and its coefficients that break the symmetry cannot all
vanish to order greater than \(e/2\). No curvilinearity or prescribed
nilpotence exponent is needed for this DVR statement.

## Integral trace coefficient and its quadratic vanishing

Work first on the unrestricted coefficient space of normalized forms of
degrees \(a,b\), before imposing curve containment or sole support.
The torus acts on a coefficient of monomial \(M\) with character
\(a-\operatorname{wt}(M)\), or \(3b-\operatorname{wt}(M)\)
for the second form. Let \(J\) be the ideal of all nonzero-character
coefficients. The quotient by \(J\) is the full eigenform locus.

There is an integral torus-stable open neighborhood of this locus on which
\[
 B=A[X,Y]/(F,G),\qquad A=S[x_0,x_3],
\]
is free with basis \(X^iY^j\), \(0\le i<a,0\le j<b\).
Here \(S\) is the localized coefficient ring. To construct it, first
set \(x_0=x_3=0\). In each of the finitely many relevant graded
pieces of the binary complete-intersection presentation, choose a minor
exhibiting the rectangular monomials as complement. The degrees through
\(a+b-1\) suffice, including the vanishing of the last quotient piece.
At the eigenlocus this presentation is \((X^a,Y^b)\); the selected
determinants have value \(\pm1\). They are torus semi-invariants and
therefore have character zero. Invert their product. The binary quotient
has the indicated basis; filtered homogeneous division in \(X,Y\)
then gives the same basis over \(S[x_0,x_3]\). This construction works
over \(\mathbb Z\), without a characteristic-zero division. Every DVR
pair above lies in this open, since the determinants reduce to units.

The trace \(\operatorname{Tr}_{B/A}(XY)\) has ordinary degree two
in \(x_0,x_3\). Write \(c\in S\) for its \(x_0x_3\)
coefficient. It has character zero, since both \(XY\) and
\(x_0x_3\) have weight four. On the eigenlocus, the elementary
weight equations give
\[
 F\equiv X^a\pmod{x_0^2},\qquad
 G\equiv Y^b\pmod{x_3^2}.
\]
Reducing the free algebra modulo \((x_0^2,x_3^2)\) makes
multiplication by \(XY\) have zero diagonal. Consequently
\(c\in J\). But \(J/J^2\), over the weight-zero ring \(S/J\),
is generated only in nonzero integer characters. Its character-zero part
is zero. Hence
\[
 \boxed{c\in J^2.} \tag{2}
\]
This is a statement about the full character grading, not the derivative
of a torus action. A nonzero character divisible by \(p\) is still
nonzero and cannot invalidate (2).

For the DVR family, let \(q=XY-x_0x_3\). On the generic fiber
sole support makes \(q\) nilpotent. Its trace is zero over the fraction
field of \(R[x_0,x_3]\), and injectivity returns that equality to
\(R[x_0,x_3]\). The rank of \(B\) is \(ab\), so
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(XY)=ab\,x_0x_3,\qquad c=ab.
\]
Evaluating (2) proves (1). This proof never assumes that a nilpotent
matrix over a nonreduced base has trace zero.

## A stronger infinitesimal statement with explicit support hypotheses

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic \(p\ge7\).
Suppose the special eigenpair has generic transverse algebra
\(k(C_0)[v]/(v^m)\), where \(m=ab/4\), and \(v_p(ab)=1\).
There is no flat complete-intersection lift over \(W_2(k)\) with the
same degrees and reductions satisfying the ideal-sheaf containments
\[
 \mathcal I_{C_{W_2}}^m
 \subset (\widetilde F,\widetilde G)
 \subset\mathcal I_{C_{W_2}}. \tag{3}
\]
The lifted forms are arbitrary; no weight condition is imposed on them.

To prove this, it remains to justify trace zero over \(W_2\).
Localize \(W_2(k)[x_0,x_3]\) generically modulo \(p\), avoiding
the two projection endpoints. The fixed curve is finite étale of degree
four there. After a faithfully flat étale extension it splits into four
sections. The nilpotent kernel in (3) permits lifting the idempotents;
the algebra splits into four augmented free blocks of rank \(m\).
In each block choose \(v\) in the augmentation ideal lifting a
generator of its special curvilinear algebra. By (3), \(v^m=0\).
Nakayama and equality of free ranks identify the block with
\(R'[v]/(v^m)\). Every augmentation-ideal element, in particular
\(q\), has strictly triangular multiplication in this basis.
Its trace is therefore zero. Descent and the injective localization return
\(\operatorname{Tr}(q)=0\) to the original polynomial ring.

Thus again \(c=ab\). All non-eigen coefficients of the lift belong
to \(pW_2(k)\), whose square is zero. Equation (2) gives \(c=0\),
contradicting \(v_p(ab)=1\).

The strong containment and curvilinearity in this infinitesimal statement
are substantive. For example, over \(R=\mathbb Z/p^2\), \(p\)
odd, the free rank-four algebra
\[
 R[u,v]/(u^2-pu,v^2)
\]
has augmentation ideal \(N=(u,v)\) with \(N^4=0\), but
\(\operatorname{Tr}(u)=2p\ne0\). Its special fiber is not
curvilinear. Also, merely retaining topological support modulo each power
of \(p\) does not supply a uniform bound such as (3).

## Examples beyond a quartic equation and characteristic-zero meaning

For \(p\ge7\), choose positive integers \(b_0,c_0\) with
\(3b_0+4c_0=p\), and put \(a_0=p-b_0-c_0\). The known pair is
\[
 F_4=x_0x_3^3-x_2^4,\qquad
 G_p=x_0^{a_0}x_2^{b_0}x_3^{c_0}-x_1^p.
\]
It has sole support \(C_0\), with transverse length \(p\).
For any positive \(n\) prime to \(p\), the pair
\((F_4^n,G_p)\) has degrees \((4n,p)\), sole support \(C_0\),
and length \(np\). It remains generically curvilinear: on \(x_0=1\),
the first normal differential of \(G_p\) along the curve is
\[
 b_0z^{p-3}\,d x_2+c_0z^{p-4}\,d x_3,
\]
which is nonzero. On this smooth carrier, \(F_4\) has order \(p\)
and its \(n\)-th power has order \(np\). For example, at \(p=7\),
\(G_7=YZ-X^7\) and
\(F_4|_{G_7}=(X^3-Y)^7/Y^3\) on the generic chart.
Taking \(n=2\) gives a degree-\((8,p)\) special pair covered by
both lifting statements, with neither equation of degree four.
An arbitrary deformation need not retain its special quartic factor.

## A ramified pair that passes the first trace and retains residual support

The quadratic vanishing order in (2) is attained even by variations that
contain the fixed curve. Write \(Z=x_3\), \(q=XY-x_0Z\), and
consider the integral degree-\((7,8)\) family
\[
 F=X^7-x_0^5YZ+u\,q x_0X^4,\qquad
 G=(Y^4-x_0Z^3)^2+v\,q ZY^5.
\]
Both equations contain \(C_0\). The perturbation weights are eight and
23, against special weights seven and 24; the two coefficient characters
are opposite. The leading forms in \(X,Y\) remain \(X^7,Y^8\), so
the pair is proper and its quotient is free of rank 56 over the base
polynomial ring, for every \(u,v\).

Modulo \((x_0^2,Z^2)\), the equations become
\(X^7+u x_0X^5Y\) and \(Y^8+v ZXY^6\). Multiplication by
\(XY\) has a diagonal contribution \(uv x_0Z\) on exactly the
three basis elements \(X^6Y^6,X^5Y^7,X^6Y^7\). Thus
\(c=3uv\) integrally. The two pure base-square trace coefficients
vanish: setting either base coordinate to zero makes \(X\) or \(Y\)
nilpotent over a domain. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(q)=(3uv-56)x_0Z.
\]
Over the ramified DVR \(\mathbb Z_7[\pi]/(\pi^2-7)\), choose
\(u=\pi,\ v=(8/3)\pi\). The special fiber is the supported
degree-\((7,8)\) pair above and the **entire first trace vanishes**.

Its characteristic-zero generic fiber nevertheless has a residual curve.
For any nonzero \(u,v\) in characteristic zero, on \(x_0=1,X\ne0\)
put \(Y=X^3y,Z=X^4z,w=uX,t=uv\). Away from
\(yz(y-z)(yz-1)=0\), the first equation eliminates
\(w=(yz-1)/(y-z)\); the second is exactly
\[
 H(y,z)=(yz-1)(y^4-z^3)^2+tzy^5(y-z)^2=0.
\]
This is nonconstant and has none of \(y,z,y-z,yz-1\) as a factor:
evaluate at the corresponding divisors, using \(t\ne0\). Thus its
zero set has a curve in that open. Recovering \(X=w/u\) maps it to
an actual residual curve, with \(q=X^4(y-z)\ne0\).

Independent reconstruction checked the integral diagonal calculation,
properness, elimination and nonempty residual support. This control
saturates the valuation bound for the first trace, but is **not** a
supported mixed-characteristic lift. Higher nilpotence/support equations
are essential even at the minimum permitted ramification.

## Remaining characteristic-zero question

The obstruction measures how a characteristic-zero presentation would
have to leave the torus-symmetric locus. It does not prove that a ramified
lift exists when (1) permits it. Conversely, the absence of an unramified
lift does not exclude a horizontal component of the full incidence: the
elementary model \(xy=p\) at \((0,0)\) has no unramified lift but
does have the ramified lift \(x=y=\pi,\ \pi^2=p\).

The [full arithmetic incidence](ARITHMETIC_EQUATIONS_AND_JET_GAPS.md#mixed-characteristic-lifting-on-the-full-incidence)
retains both arbitrary equations, ramification and actual generic support.
The [power-extraction theorem](PRIME_TO_P_POWER_EXTRACTION.md) proves that
no horizontal component passes through the prescribed points
\((G_p,F_4^n)\) when \(p\nmid n\), including \((G_p,F_4^2)\).
The unrestricted horizontal-component question remains open. In particular, the
non-eigen degree-\((16,p)\) examples in that proof home are not covered
by this special-eigen theorem. The field-valued rank incidence is not
silently identified with the stronger infinitesimal condition (3).

Independent reconstruction checked the integral coefficient chart,
character argument, DVR trace descent, the distinct \(W_2\) hypotheses,
and the ramification limitation. The powered example was checked using
the smoothness of \(G_p\); replacing generators before taking a power
would give a different ideal and an incorrect noncurvilinear model.
