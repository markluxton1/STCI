# Quadratic source decomposition of the Z4 triple obstruction

Status: new exact coordinate calculation, **not independently audited**. Fixed raw integral coordinates and first quotient conventions of the 2026-10-20 obstruction report. Does not assert a canonical coordinate-independent splitting.

Let p=a+bu+3hu^2, q=d+2hu; P(c)=3ac^2+(3b-4d)c+h, Q(c)=2ac+2b-3d. Set c0=u^-1, c1=u^-4q-u^-5p, A1=3u^-8p-4u^-7q, B1=2u^-7p-3u^-6q,
A2=-9u^-12p^2+16u^-11pq-6u^-10q^2,
B2=-5u^-11p^2+9u^-10pq-3u^-9q^2.

The quadratic cocycle K=u^8([epsilon^2]m_V+u^-8[epsilon^2]m_U) decomposes as
K_A=u^8 Q(c0) A2,
K_B=-u^8 P(c0) B2,
K_move=u^8 c1(Q'(c0) A1-P'(c0) B1).
Terms involving arbitrary quadratic embedding corrections cancel against the linear kernel transition. These formulas are an **exact equality in the chosen frame**, not an invariant direct sum of cohomology classes.

For the distinguished quotient p=0,q=1,P=-4c,Q=-3:
K_A=18u^-2, K_B=-12u^-2, K_move=-12u^-2;
K=-6u^-2. The -6 in A2 comes directly from binomial(4,2); the other contributions are divisible by four.

For every allowed a,b,h in 2R, d in 1+2R, reduce the quadratic pieces mod 4. Since p=0 mod 2, q=d mod 2, P(c0)=-4dc0 mod 2 plus even terms, Q(c0)=-3d mod 2, and A2=-6u^-10 q^2 mod 4 plus p-dependent terms divisible by 4, we obtain
K_A = 18d^3 u^-2 = 2u^-2 (mod 4).
K_B=0 (mod 4), since P(c0) is even and B2 is even in this family? More carefully B2=-3u^-9 q^2 mod 2 and P(c0)=-4dc0 + terms in 2R, so P is even, but its product need not be 0 mod 4: its even parts include 3b c0+h, and B2 odd. Thus K_B can have mod-4 contributions in outer Laurent degrees, even if the middle coordinate vanishes mod 4. Likewise K_move may contribute outer coordinates. Only the middle-coordinate attribution is claimed.

Exact symbolic extraction gives middle Laurent coefficients:
[K_A]_-2 = -88abh+114adh-18b^3+59b^2d-60bd^2+18d^3;
[K_B]_-2 = 82abh-102adh+15b^3-47b^2d+45bd^2-12d^3;
[K_move]_-2 = 10abh-16adh+6b^3-23b^2d+29bd^2-12d^3.
Their sum is C2=(b-d)(4ah+3b^2-8bd+6d^2).
Modulo 4 the three middle coefficients are respectively 2,0,0. This is a uniform source attribution of the **middle coordinate** in the fixed raw frame, not only at the distinguished quotient.

This does not construct a natural map from divided-power polynomials to H^1(O(-4)). In particular, a change of raw normal coordinates changes the allocation among K_A, K_B and K_move. The total class is intrinsic for the fixed quotient, but the decomposition is not proved invariant.

# Independent mathematical audit: explicit (4,4) family and quartic symbols

Date: 2026-10-23. Scope: the fixed curve C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4], the explicit pair F=x1^4-x0^3*x3, G=x2^4-x0*x3^3, and newly compared symbol maps. This is an independent audit of the new arguments, not a rerun or replacement of inherited quartic-carrier exclusion/lifting results. Repository and Git state were not changed.

## Decision

The stated flatness, four-component characteristic-zero decomposition, contact/delta calculations, characteristic-dependent normal initial ideals, and quartic symbol-image calculations are correct. Two presentation qualifications matter: distinguish the subline generated locally by X from the quadric conormal subline generated by q=X-uY; and write all degree numbers on P1 explicitly, since ambient twist 4 restricts to P1 degree 16.

## 1. Flat family and components: PASS

Over Z the homogeneous quotient is

    Z[x0,x3][x1,x2]/(x1^4-x0^3*x3, x2^4-x0*x3^3).

Successive division by the two monic polynomials gives the free basis x1^a*x2^b, 0<=a,b<4, over Z[x0,x3]. The relations are homogeneous. Each graded component is consequently a free Z-module, and homogeneous localization/degree zero preserves flatness. Thus Proj is flat over Z.

On x0!=0 put u=x1/x0, X=x3/x0-u^4, Y=x2/x0-u^3. Exact elimination gives

    (F,G)=(X,P),
    P=Y^4+4*u^3*Y^3+6*u^6*Y^2+4*u^9*Y.

After inverting u, the substitution Y=u^3*(z-1) gives P=u^12*(z^4-1). In an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, the four distinct components are exactly

    C_zeta=[s^4:s^3*t:zeta*s*t^3:t^4], zeta^4=1.

There are no additional boundary curves: x0=0 forces the endpoint [0:0:0:1], and x3=0 forces [1:0:0:0]. Each displayed component is smooth rational of degree four. The complete intersection is Cohen--Macaulay with total degree 16 and is generically reduced on all four components; hence it is their reduced scheme union, without embedded components. The separate Macaulay2 computation also verifies equality of the homogeneous ideals over QQ(i).

## 2. Contact and genus: PASS

On the x0 chart all four branches lie on the smooth surface F=0 with plane coordinates (u,x2/x0), and their equations are x2/x0=zeta*u^3. Two distinct branches intersect in k[u]/(u^3), so each pair has contact/intersection multiplicity three at [1:0:0:0].

On x3=1 use c=x2/x3. For C_zeta the equations are x0/x3=c^4 and x1/x3=zeta*c^3, since zeta^4=1. Thus the same computation gives pairwise contact three at the other endpoint, now on the smooth surface G=0. All other points belong to only one component.

For a reduced planar union of smooth branches delta is the sum of pairwise intersection multiplicities. There are six pairs, so each endpoint has delta=18 and total delta=36. Normalization is four copies of P1, giving chi=4-36=-32 and arithmetic genus 33, agreeing with the (4,4) complete-intersection formula.

## 3. Full normal initial ideals and base change: PASS

In characteristic zero the curve ideal on this chart is (X,Y). In the quotient X=0 its image is J=(Y) in k[u,Y]/(P). Over the domain k[u], every nonzero multiple hP has lowest Y-degree term equal to the product of the lowest terms of h and P. Thus the full Y-adic initial ideal is exactly (4*u^9*eta), and

    gr_J(k[u,Y]/(P)) = k[u,eta]/(u^9*eta)   (char 0).

After inverting u this becomes k(u), i.e. the fixed component C_1 is generically reduced, of length one. The additional normal-cone component u=0 is vertical over the endpoint; it is not a new curve component downstairs. The actual other three curve components downstairs are the explicit C_zeta above.

In characteristic two P=Y^4, so the exact ideal and its full normal initial ideal are (X,Y^4). By contrast the initial forms of only the displayed generators F,G generate (X), since their first forms are -X and u^8*X. A filtered combination eliminating X supplies Y^4. The distinction between initial forms of chosen generators and the initial ideal of the entire ideal is essential.

There is an additional transparent base-change calculation: over Z the same principal-order argument gives gr=Z[u,eta]/(4*u^9*eta). Its reduction modulo two is F2[u,eta], while the associated graded of the reduced family is F2[u,eta]/(eta^4). Thus the normal associated graded algebra does not commute with this base change, despite flatness of the original complete-intersection family. The associated graded is not Z-flat: u^9*eta is nonzero and killed by 4. In contrast each ideal J^n is a submodule of the Z-flat U, hence Z-torsionfree and Z-flat; the failure concerns successive quotients and compatibility of the filtration with base change, not flatness of those ideal terms. This assertion concerns this explicit filtration and does not imply that the conormal symmetric algebra of the regular embedding C in P3 fails to commute with base change.

## 4. Conormal frames and degrees: PASS, with notation qualification

All O(n) in this section mean O_{P1}(n); ambient O_C(d) is O_{P1}(4d).

The quadric frame e=q=X-uY, f=Y has transition

    e'=u^-8*e,
    f'=u^-6*f-2*u^-7*e.

This gives the quadric conormal sequence 0->O(-8)->E->O(-6)->0 and its characteristic-two splitting. In characteristic zero the balanced frame is U=X-(3/2)*uY, V=Y, with both linear transitions u^-7. The accepted E=O(-7)^2 is used as a premise, not reproved here.

The other agent's frame generated by X has opposite kernel generator 3A-4*u^-1*B=u^-8*X and quotient lift -B/3=u^-6*Y-(2/3)*u^-7*X. Its normalized extension cocycle -(2/3)*u^-1 is valid over Z[1/3], hence in characteristics zero and two, and has the asserted characteristic dependence. This X subline is distinct from the quadric subline q. In particular X is not the first symbol of a global quadratic equation through C.

## 5. Exact source image and target dimensions: PASS

Quartic restriction through Q gives 0->O_Q(-2,2)->O_Q(1,3)->O_{P1}(10)->0; the cokernel on global sections has dimension three. The source H0(O_Q(1,3)) restricts to the eight monomials with exponents 0,1,3,4,6,7,9,10. Quadratics restrict surjectively to H0(O_{P1}(8)), so q times quadratics supplies the nine-dimensional kernel of H0(E(16))->H0(O(10)). Therefore the first-symbol image is exactly the preimage of this eight-dimensional residual space, of dimension 17, in every characteristic.

In characteristic zero its equations in the balanced coefficient pair (P,R) are P1=2R2, P4=2R5, P7=2R8. In characteristic two E=O(-8)+O(-6) and the image is H0(O(8))*e plus span(1,u,u^3,u^4,u^6,u^7,u^9,u^10)*f; its equations are B2=B5=B8=0.

The full quartic ideal has dimension 18 in both characteristics. Its first-symbol kernel is k*q^2, also proved geometrically: restriction of a double-vanishing quartic to Q would be a section of O_Q(-2,2), so it is q times a quadric through C, hence a multiple of q^2. The nonzero second symbol of q^2 proves the actual second-symbol image has dimension one. The full quadratic-symbol target has dimension nine in either characteristic: Sym^2(E)(16)=O(2)^3 in characteristic zero, and O(0)+O(2)+O(4) in characteristic two.

# Independent review: the osculating conormal line and its coincidence divisor

Date: 2026-10-24. Reviewer: `osculating_normal_count`.

Status: **independently reconstructed exploratory geometry; not canonically
promoted**. The count proposed by the parent investigation is correct with the
qualifications below. No STCI obstruction or new bound on actual STCI equations
is proved.

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, for the fixed
embedding

\[
\nu([s:t])=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4],\qquad C=\nu(\mathbf P^1).
\]

The sole retained normal-bundle input is the accepted
[STCI-R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md):
\(E=N^*_{C/\mathbf P^3}=\mathcal O(-7)^2\), with degrees taken on
\(\mathbf P^1\). Its proof is not rerun. No quartic-carrier exclusion or
higher-contact classification is used.

## 1. The intrinsic line defined by osculation

The osculating plane at \([s:t]\) has coefficients

\[
h(s,t)=(-t^4,\ 2st^3,\ -2s^3t,\ s^4).
\]

Indeed, on \(s=1\), its pullback to \(C\) is
\((v-u)^3(v+u)\). The coefficient vector is nowhere zero, including at
the two endpoints. Because this plane contains the tangent line of \(C\),
its differential belongs to \(E(4)\). Its coefficient degree gives a
nowhere-vanishing line inclusion

\[
\mathcal O(-4)\longrightarrow E(4).
\]

Consequently the osculating conormal direction is the saturated subline

\[
L_{\rm osc}\simeq\mathcal O(-8)\subset E.
\tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, the tautological line of the degree-four osculating map
\(\mathbf P^1\to(\mathbf P^3)^\vee\) is
\(L_{\rm osc}(4)=\mathcal O(-4)\). This argument proves (1) directly,
without using a singular auxiliary surface or its normalization.

There is also an independent check using the cubic scroll

\[
R=x_0x_2^2-x_1^2x_3.
\]

On \(x_0=1\), use normal coordinates
\(v=x_2-u^3,\ w=x_3-u^4\), with \(u=x_1\). Then

\[
R=v^2+u^2(2uv-w),\qquad
\sigma_R=u^2(2uv-w).
\]

On \(x_3=1\), use \(z=x_2\),
\(v'=x_1-z^3,\ w'=x_0-z^4\). Then

\[
R=-(v')^2+z^2(w'-2zv').
\]

Thus its first normal symbol vanishes exactly to order two at each
endpoint and nowhere else. Saturating
\(\mathcal O(-12)\xrightarrow{\sigma_R}E\) removes this degree-four
divisor and again gives \(\mathcal O(-8)\). Its limiting direction at
the endpoints is the hyperosculating plane, although \(R\) itself is
singular there. One must use this saturated direction rather than call
the tangent plane of a singular surface uniquely defined.

## 2. Exact coincidence count for an individual surface

Let \(F\in I_C(d)\) have nonzero first normal symbol
\(\sigma_F\in H^0(E(4d))\). Let \(A_F\) be its zero divisor, and
let \(L_F\subset E\) be its saturated image. Then

\[
L_F=\mathcal O(\deg A_F-4d).
\]

The wedge map

\[
L_F\otimes L_{\rm osc}\longrightarrow\det E
\]

is either identically zero, in which case the two saturated line
subbundles agree, or has an effective zero divisor \(D_F^{\rm osc}\)
of degree

\[
\boxed{\deg D_F^{\rm osc}=4d-6-\deg A_F.}
\tag{2}
\]

This follows from \(\deg\det E=-14\) and (1). At a point where
\(F=0\) is smooth, the vanishing means exactly
\(T_pV(F)=H_p\). At a point of \(A_F\), the surface is singular,
so the divisor compares the **limiting saturated normal direction**
with the osculating direction; it does not assign a unique tangent
plane to the singular surface. If \(\sigma_F=0\) identically, neither
\(L_F\) nor (2) is available. If \(V(F)\) is smooth along all of
\(C\), then \(A_F=0\) and the count is \(4d-6\), unless the
directions agree identically. In the latter case degree comparison
would force \(4d=8\); the actual smooth quadric below does not have
identically osculating tangent plane.

This identity uses the actual first normal symbol. It does not assert
that every abstract line subbundle or every prescribed coincidence
divisor comes from a global homogeneous equation.

## 3. An explicit polynomial check, including both endpoints

For any homogeneous \(F\in I_C(d)\), define

\[
P_F(s,t)=\sum_{i=0}^3 F_{x_i}(\nu(s,t))\nu_i(s,-t).
\]

Its degree is \(4d\), and \(s^3t^3\) always divides it. To see this
at \(s=1\), put \(f_i(u)=F_{x_i}(1,u,u^3,u^4)\). Euler's identity
and differentiation along \(C\) give

\[
f_0+uf_1+u^3f_2+u^4f_3=0,\qquad
f_1+3u^2f_2+4u^3f_3=0.
\]

Hence

\[
P_F(1,u)=4u^3(f_2+2uf_3).
\tag{3}
\]

The reversed chart proves divisibility by \(s^3\). In the normal
coordinates above, \(\sigma_F=f_2v+f_3w\), whereas
\(L_{\rm osc}\) has primitive generator \(2uv-w\). Their wedge
is \(-f_2-2uf_3\), so (3) is precisely the local coincidence equation,
with the fixed endpoint factor separated. Thus

\[
\frac{P_F}{4s^3t^3}\in H^0(\mathcal O(4d-6))
\]

records \(A_F+D_F^{\rm osc}\) if the wedge is nonzero. The factor
\(A_F\) must be removed before interpreting zeros as coincidences of
saturated directions.

For \(q=x_0x_3-x_1x_2\), one gets
\(P_q/(4s^3t^3)=st\). Thus the quadric's tangent plane is
osculating exactly at the two endpoints, each once. For the scroll
\(R\), the polynomial is identically zero, as required.

## 4. A global counterexample test for an unscoped small bound

Take the explicit cubic

\[
F=(x_0^2x_2-x_1^3)+(x_1x_3^2-x_2^3).
\tag{4}
\]

Its first normal symbol on \(x_0=1\) is

\[
\sigma_F=(1-3u^6)v+2u^5w.
\]

The two coefficients have no simultaneous zero. The reversed chart
has the identical formula with \(u,v,w\) replaced by \(z,v',w'\).
Therefore this cubic is smooth at every point of \(C\). Moreover

\[
P_F=4s^3t^3(s^6+t^6).
\]

It has six distinct osculating tangent-plane coincidences. Thus a
universal bound of two for the osculating coincidences of an arbitrary
smooth containing surface is false. This is a statement about
individual containing surfaces; it is not an STCI construction and
does not refute any theorem with additional STCI hypotheses.

There is also a useful test for broader containing **pairs**. Let
\(L\) be a nonzero linear form, and set

\[
G=LF+q^2.
\]

Then \((F,G)=(F,q^2)\), a proper homogeneous pair. Its two nonzero
generic first normal symbols have the same saturated direction
\(L_F=\mathcal O(-12)\). In the balanced bundle
\(E(7)=\mathcal O^2\), this direction defines a map to
\(\mathbf P^1\) of degree five. This already rules out an unscoped
degree-at-most-two bound based on proper containment and common normal
direction alone.

The residual support is explicit. In Segre coordinates
\([x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]=[ac:ad:bc:bd]\),

\[
F|_Q=(a^2-b^2)(bc^3-ad^3).
\]

Consequently \(V(F,G)_{\rm red}\) is \(C\) together with the two
trisecant ruling lines \(a=b\) and \(a=-b\). This example **fails
sole support in a visible way**. It cannot be used against a proposed
bound whose hypotheses include \(V(F,G)_{\rm red}=C\).

## 5. Computation and review boundary

The displayed formulas for (4), its reversed chart, the scroll normal
symbols, and \(P_F\) were independently expanded with SymPy 1.14.0,
using `/tmp/stci-z4-env/bin/python`, with exact polynomial comparisons.
The final command returned:

```text
PASS: cubic smooth along C, endpoint-normal checks, six osculating tangencies
D_F = 4*s**3*t**3*(s**2 + t**2)*(s**4 - s**2*t**2 + t**4)
```

The proof above is independent of that expansion. The initial test used
structural equality against a differently factored expression, which
failed; replacing it by expansion of the difference returned zero.
This was a test-harness correction, not a counterexample or change to
the mathematical formulas.

The reviewed conclusion is (1)--(3), including all endpoint and
singular-symbol qualifications, plus the explicit counterexample tests.
What remains unproved is any implication from these identities to an
STCI obstruction, or any bound on the common saturated direction of
actual sole-support equations. Such a bound would need genuinely
global input beyond the incidence geometry used here.

# Dual incidence and the involution secant scroll

Date: 2026-10-24. Author/reviewer role: `dual_incidence`, independent derivation for the special-line investigation.

**Status: provisional research evidence, not a canonical result or an STCI obstruction.** Throughout, the field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero and the embedded curve is exactly
\[
 c(u)=[1:u:u^3:u^4],\qquad c(\infty)=[0:0:0:1].
\]
The calculations below reconstruct this embedding directly. No quartic-carrier exclusion, normal-bundle splitting, or earlier normal-direction inequality is used. The index and authority/contribution rules were consulted. The geometric labels in this note refer to the fixed embedding, not arbitrary smooth rational quartics.

## 1. The hyperplane system and its two discriminant curves

The hyperplane system is the basepoint-free four-dimensional subspace
\[
 V=\langle s^4,s^3t,st^3,t^4\rangle\subset H^0(\mathbf P^1,\mathcal O(4)).
\]
Thus its intrinsic discriminant consists of sections with a multiple zero. In the affine coordinate \(v=t/s\), its sections are binary quartics with zero \(v^2\)-coefficient. The osculating section with a triple zero at \(u\) must have the form
\[
 (v-u)^3(v-r).
\]
Its missing coefficient is \(3u(u+r)\), giving \(r=-u\) away from the endpoints. Homogeneous continuation gives the involution \(\iota(u)=-u\), with fixed points \(0,\infty\), and
\[
 H_u:\quad -u^4x_0+2u^3x_1-2ux_2+x_3=0,
 \qquad H_u\cdot C=3c(u)+c(-u).
\]
At the endpoints this is \(4c(0)\) or \(4c(\infty)\). The normalization map of the osculating curve in dual projective space is
\[
 [s:t]\longmapsto[-t^4:2st^3:-2s^3t:s^4].
\]

A bitangent section is the square of a quadratic. Write that quadratic as \(v^2-wv+z\). The missing coefficient condition is \(w^2+2z=0\), so its section is
\[
 (v^2-wv-w^2/2)^2
   =v^4-2wv^3+w^3v+w^4/4.
\]
Consequently the bitangent curve in dual space is another rational quartic, parametrized by
\[
 B_w=[w^4/4:w^3:-2w:1].
\]
For \(w\ne0,\infty\) its contact points have parameters
\[
 u=\frac{1+\sqrt3}{2}w,\qquad
 v=\frac{1-\sqrt3}{2}w.
\]
Their ratio is one of \(-2\pm\sqrt3\), rather than \(-1\). Equivalently the ordered bitangent correspondence is
\[
 u^2+4uv+v^2=0,
\]
homogenized separately in the two factors of \(C\times C\). It is the union of the graphs of the mutually inverse automorphisms \(u\mapsto\rho u\), where \(\rho=-2+\sqrt3\). In particular, involution pairs are not bitangent pairs. The involution takes the bitangent plane \(B_w\) to \(B_{-w}\).

The two bitangent contacts merge at each endpoint to a quadruple contact; there is no tangent plane with contact divisor \(2c(0)+2c(\infty)\), because \(s^2t^2\notin V\). The osculating and bitangent curves in dual space meet only at these two quadruple-root sections. In the affine chart with last coefficient one near the first endpoint, equality of their coefficients imposes
\[
 u=w,\quad 2u^3=w^3,\quad -u^4=w^4/4.
\]
Their scheme-theoretic intersection there has length three, and similarly at infinity. They share their tangent and quadratic jet, then separate at cubic order.

The dual surface \(C^\vee\subset\mathbf P(V)\) is the restricted binary-quartic discriminant, a reduced irreducible surface of degree six. Its **reduced singular locus** is the union of the osculating and bitangent quartics and the two lines of endpoint triple-contact planes,
\[
\ell_0=\{[0:0:C:D]\},\qquad
\ell_\infty=\{[A:B:0:0]\}.
\]
At a nonendpoint triple root the residual point is forced to be its involution partner, giving the osculating quartic. At zero, however, every section \(v^3(C+Dv)\) has a triple root; the reversed chart supplies the second line. The extra lines are essential. At a quartic with exactly one double root and two other distinct roots, the full discriminant is smooth, and its differential is a nonzero multiple of evaluation at that double root. Restriction to \(V\) cannot annihilate this differential because \(V\) is basepoint free. Thus the hyperplane section introduces no additional singularities at simple double-root quartics. The remaining singular quartics have a triple root or two double roots, exhausted by these four components. This is a statement about the singular set, not the scheme structure of its singular ideal. The usual discriminant degree six follows from the resultant of a quartic and its derivative after removing its leading coefficient.

**Independent-review correction:** the initial draft omitted the two endpoint pencil lines by identifying every triple-root section with a limiting generic osculating plane. Reviewer geometry_final_audit identified the endpoint jet-rank failure. The four-component description above replaces that false two-component description. This correction does not affect the formulas for either quartic or their mutual intersection lengths.

## 2. The paired osculating line is the paired secant

For \(u\ne0,\infty\), both \(H_u\) and \(H_{-u}\) contain \(c(u)\) and \(c(-u)\). They are distinct, so their intersection is exactly
\[
 S_u=\langle c(u),c(-u)\rangle,
 \qquad x_2=u^2x_1,\quad x_3=u^4x_0.
\]
The unordered family is parametrized by the involution quotient \(z=u^2\). Its limiting members at \(z=0,\infty\) are
\[
 L_0=V(x_2,x_3),\qquad L_\infty=V(x_0,x_1).
\]
They are the endpoint tangent lines and have length-three intersection with \(C\). The general paired secant has two distinct simple contacts. It is not a trisecant.

The union of the lines \(S_u\) is the irreducible cubic ruled surface
\[
 R=V(x_0x_2^2-x_1^2x_3).
\]
Indeed its parametrization on this affine ruling chart is
\[
 [A:B]\longmapsto[A:B:zB:z^2A].
\]
Conversely the cubic equation reconstructs \(z=x_2/x_1\) when \(x_1\ne0\), and closure gives the entire surface. Its singular double directrix is \(V(x_1,x_2)\). The two hyperosculating points are its pinch points: at the first endpoint its equation is \(x_2^2=x_1^2x_3\), and the other endpoint has the analogous equation.

This surface supplies a useful geometric identity:
\[
 T_{c(u)}R=H_u\qquad(u\ne0,\infty).
\]
For verification,
\[
 \nabla(x_0x_2^2-x_1^2x_3)|_{c(u)}
 =u^2[u^4:-2u^3:2u:-1],
\]
which gives exactly that tangent plane. **The ruled cubic is not a developable surface.** Along a fixed ruling its tangent plane varies; it is merely the osculating plane along this distinguished section \(C\).

The first normal symbol of its equation has zeros of order two at each endpoint. This can be checked without choosing a conormal splitting. Near \(c(0)\) put
\[
 u=x_1,\qquad a=x_2-u^3,\qquad b=x_3-u^4.
\]
Then
\[
 R=2u^3a-u^2b+a^2,
\]
whose linear normal part is \(u^2(2ua-b)\); the parenthetical direction remains primitive at \(u=0\). The infinity chart is symmetric. This is an explicit surface and a direction calculation, not a hypothetical common-normal constraint for all STCI pairs.

## 3. The unique quadric, rulings, and polarity

On the smooth quadric \(Q=V(x_0x_3-x_1x_2)\), use the Segre coordinates
\[
 (x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)=(ac,ad,bc,bd).
\]
The curve is the divisor \(bc^3-ad^3=0\) of class \((1,3)\), and the restriction of the ruled cubic factors as
\[
 R|_Q=ab(bc^3-ad^3).
\]
Hence, as Cartier divisors on \(Q\),
\[
 R\cap Q=C+L_0+L_\infty.
\]
This is a concrete residual divisor, not a vertical exceptional component.

The ambient involution extending \(u\mapsto-u\) is
\[
 A=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,1).
\]
It preserves \(Q\), its two rulings, and \(R\). On the Segre factors it acts as \(([a:b],[c:d])\mapsto([a:-b],[c:-d])\). Its fixed projective lines are the two eigenspace lines \(V(x_1,x_2)\) and \(V(x_0,x_3)\), and every paired secant joins a point of one fixed line to a point of the other.

For clarity, polarity with respect to \(Q\) sends the plane with coefficients \([a_0:a_1:a_2:a_3]\) to \([a_3:-a_2:-a_1:a_0]\). Thus the pole of \(H_u\) is
\[
 P_u=[1:2u:-2u^3:-u^4].
\]
The pole quartic lies on \(4x_0x_3-x_1x_2=0\); it meets \(Q\) only at the two endpoints, with intersection order four at each. The polar of the paired secant is a **different** line:
\[
 S_u^\perp=T_{c(u)}Q\cap T_{c(-u)}Q
 =V(x_3+u^4x_0,\ x_2+u^2x_1).
\]
In particular one should not identify \(H_u\) with the tangent plane of \(Q\) at \(c(u)\). For general \(u\), \(H_u\cap Q\) is a smooth conic meeting \(C\) in \(3c(u)+c(-u)\). At each endpoint that conic degenerates into the two quadric rulings through the point, contributing lengths three and one respectively.

## 4. Universal plane incidences and their actual compatibility content

On the product of the osculating parameter line and \(C\), the universal plane section is the Cartier divisor
\[
 D_{\mathrm{osc}}=3\Delta+\Gamma_\iota.
\]
This follows from the full factorization \(H_u(c(v))=(v-u)^3(v+u)\), including homogeneous continuation. It has bidegree \((4,4)\), as does the universal bitangent section
\[
 D_{\mathrm{bit}}=2\Gamma_{c_+}+2\Gamma_{c_-},
 \qquad c_\pm=(1\pm\sqrt3)/2.
\]
The endpoint mergers are already included in these Cartier divisors; they do not add vertical components.

Suppose now that \(Z=V(F,G)\) is a projective complete intersection with sole reduced support \(C\), degrees \(d,e\), generic multiplicity \(m\), and \(de=4m\). Either universal plane family defines
\[
 W=(Z\times\mathbf P^1)\cap\mathcal H\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1.
\]
This is finite flat of rank \(de\). Indeed every fiber is a zero-dimensional proper plane section of the Cohen--Macaulay curve \(Z\), of length \(de\). Projectivity and zero-dimensional fibers give finiteness. The universal plane is a nonzerodivisor on the two-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay product, so \(W\) is Cohen--Macaulay of dimension one; its nonconstant irreducible components dominate the smooth base, giving torsion-freeness and hence flatness. More directly the constant fiber length criterion for finite modules on a smooth curve gives the same conclusion.

The fundamental cycles are therefore
\[
 [W_{\mathrm{osc}}]=m(3\Delta+\Gamma_\iota),\qquad
 [W_{\mathrm{bit}}]=2m(\Gamma_{c_+}+\Gamma_{c_-}).
\]
These identities are on the reduced incidence support in the ambient product. The scheme \(W\) generally has a nilpotent structure and is not identified with a divisor on the reduced product. At the endpoints, distinct support branches merge and the entire fiber has length \(de\).

There is also a bundle-level numerical identity, rather than just a support cycle. For any degree-\(k\) family of planes having finite intersection with \(Z\) at every parameter, let \(E=f_*\mathcal O_W\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{rk}E=de,\qquad
 \deg E=-\frac{kde(d+e-2)}2.
\]
In particular both degree-four plane families above give \(\deg E=-2de(d+e-2)\). To verify this without smoothness assumptions on \(Z\), its complete-intersection Hilbert polynomial gives
\[
 \chi(\mathcal O_Z)=de(4-d-e)/2,\qquad
 \chi(\mathcal O_Z(-1))=de(2-d-e)/2.
\]
The universal plane exact sequence on \(Z\times\mathbf P^1\) is
\[
 0\to\mathcal O_Z(-1)\boxtimes\mathcal O(-k)
 \to\mathcal O_{Z\times\mathbf P^1}\to\mathcal O_W\to0.
\]
Taking Euler characteristics yields
\[
 \chi(\mathcal O_W)=de\left(1-\frac{k(d+e-2)}2\right),
\]
and \(\chi(E)=\operatorname{rk}E+\deg E\) on the base gives the formula. It is automatic for an actual complete intersection, so it becomes an obstruction only if some additional argument independently prevents an algebra with these data and the prescribed gluing.

This organizes a genuine family-level compatibility requirement: any actual pair has to produce these finite flat families, with their gluing across the two mergers. The cycles and flatness themselves follow formally from the actual complete intersection and the chosen plane family. They do not establish an extra numerical inequality or rule out its existence. A useful next question is whether the full scheme gluing can impose a constraint not already encoded by the ambient equations. This note has not proved such a constraint. Formal local models or length cycles alone cannot answer that global realization question.

## Verification boundary and research impact

All displayed geometric formulas have direct algebraic proofs above; they are new derivations for this investigation and remain provisional pending the parent investigation's independent review. In particular, the ruled cubic, polarity, and dual discriminant organize the geometry but do not replace any canonical STCI theorem. No normal-bundle calculation or quartic exclusion has been re-run. No assertion is made about arbitrary degrees, unrestricted STCI nonexistence, or a universal bound on common normal directions.

# Independent derivation: special lines and planes of the fixed rational quartic

Date: 2026-10-24. Author: independent geometry agent `special_geometry`.
Status: **new research evidence; not a promoted canonical result**.
Scope: the specified embedding of `P1` in `P3` over an algebraically closed
field of characteristic zero. No STCI hypothesis is used in this note.

The proofs below start from the embedding, rather than from earlier quartic
carrier exclusions or conormal calculations. They distinguish the
scheme-theoretic intersection of a line with the curve from a plane section.

## 1. The intrinsic ruling picture

Write `c(u)=[1:u:u^3:u^4]`. The only quadratic relation is
`q=x0*x3-x1*x2`: the ten quadratic monomials have pullback exponents
`0,1,2,3,4,4,5,6,7,8`, so their kernel is one-dimensional. Its quadric
`Q` is smooth. Under the Segre identification

```
Q=P1_A x P1_B,
x0=A0*B0, x1=A0*B1, x2=A1*B0, x3=A1*B1,
```

the curve is the graph `[A0:A1]=[s^3:t^3]` over `[B0:B1]=[s:t]`.
Thus `C` has divisor class `(1,3)`, the projection to `B` is an isomorphism,
and the projection to `A` has degree three. The two totally ramified points
of the degree-three projection are `p0=c(0)` and `p∞=c(∞)`.
This distinguishes the two ruling families intrinsically.

The degree-three ruling line over `v` is

```
L_v : x2=v*x0, x3=v*x1,
L_v.C = sum_{u^3=v} [c(u)].
```

For nonzero finite `v` the three points are distinct. At `v=0,∞` the
intersection divisor is respectively `3p0,3p∞`. The other ruling line
through `c(a)` is

```
M_a : x1=a*x0, x3=a*x2,
M_a.C = [c(a)].
```

**All lines meeting C in scheme length greater than two are exactly the
lines `L_v`; their length is three, never four.** Indeed, if a line not
contained in `Q` met `C` in length at least three, the nonzero quadratic
`q` on that line would vanish on a subscheme of length at least three,
contradicting degree two. A line contained in the smooth quadric belongs
to one of its two rulings, where the preceding degrees apply.

In particular, the tangent line is a trisecant only at `p0,p∞`. At all
other points it has intersection length exactly two with `C`. To check
the distinction directly, `T_a=<c(a),c'(a)>` and
`q(c(a)+z*c'(a))=-3*a^2*z^2`. At `a=0` the tangent is `L_0`, and the
opposite chart gives `T_∞=L_∞`. A secant through two distinct finite points
`a,b` lies in `Q` exactly when `a^3=b^3`; it then contains the third point
of the cubic fiber. All other ordinary secants have length two.

## 2. Osculation and the intrinsic involution

Hyperplane sections form the four-dimensional subspace
`W=<s^4,s^3*t,s*t^3,t^4>` of `H0(P1,O(4))`. A section with triple zero at
nonzero finite `a` has the form `(u-a)^3*(u-b)`. The missing coefficient
of `u^2` is `3*a*(a+b)`, so `b=-a`. Consequently

```
H_a : -a^4*x0+2*a^3*x1-2*a*x2+x3=0,
H_a.C = 3[c(a)]+[c(-a)].
```

The homogeneous plane map is

```
[s:t] -> [-t^4:2*s*t^3:-2*s^3*t:s^4].
```

Its limits are `H_0:x3=0` and `H_∞:x0=0`, with sections `4p0` and
`4p∞`. These are the two hyperosculating planes. At either endpoint the
2-jet spans only the tangent line; the displayed hyperosculating plane
is the unique plane of contact order four and the limit of generic
osculating planes. Every plane through the endpoint tangent line already
has contact at least three there. This endpoint jet-rank drop should not
be hidden in a generic second-derivative definition of osculation.

The residual-point map is the involution `iota(u)=-u`. Intrinsically it is
the unique nonidentity involution of `C≅P1` fixing each of the two totally
ramified points of the distinguished cubic ruling projection. Its two
fixed points are exactly the hyperosculating points.

It extends to the ambient projective involution

```
sigma[x0:x1:x2:x3]=[x0:-x1:-x2:x3].
```

On `Q` this changes the signs of both `A1` and `B1`, so it preserves
each ruling family. Its ambient fixed locus consists of the two disjoint
lines `E_+=V(x1,x2)` and `E_-=V(x0,x3)`. Its four fixed points on `Q`
are the four Segre coordinate points, only two of which lie on `C`.

For nonfixed `a`, the two osculating planes intersect in the ordinary
secant joining their contact points:

```
H_a ∩ H_-a = S_{a^2},
S_w : x2=w*x1, x3=w^2*x0.
```

This follows either by adding/subtracting their equations or because
each plane contains both points and the planes are distinct. Thus the
paired plane intersection has length two on `C`, not a length-three
osculating-line intersection. The limiting lines at `w=0,∞` are the
two tangent trisecants. The pairing factors through `C/iota`, with
quotient coordinate `w=u^2`.

The union of these paired secants is the irreducible cubic ruled surface

```
R : x0*x2^2-x1^2*x3=0.
```

Its singular locus is the line `E_+`; a general point of that line is a
double point. The other fixed line `E_-` is a directrix too. Indeed,
`S_w` joins `[1:0:0:w^2]∈E_+` to `[0:1:w:0]∈E_-`: the first endpoint
has two possible parameters and the second has one. This explains the
double line without appealing merely to an implicit equation. At `p0,p∞`
the local equation is a pinch-point form `z^2-x^2*y`; these are the two
ramification points of the degree-two map from the ruling parameter to
`E_+`. On `Q` there is the useful divisor identity

```
R|Q=C+L_0+L_∞,
```

since the Segre pullback of its equation factors as
`A0*A1*(A1*B0^3-A0*B1^3)`. The residual conic class `(2,0)` thus
consists precisely of the two endpoint tangent trisecants.

For distinct osculating planes, their intersection line avoids `C`
unless the parameters are interchanged by `iota`. This also follows
from taking the common divisor of `3a+(-a)` and `3b+(-b)`.

## 3. Bitangency gives a different correspondence

A plane bitangent at distinct finite points `a,b` has section
`(u-a)^2*(u-b)^2`. The vanishing middle coefficient is

```
a^2+4*a*b+b^2=0.
```

Thus `b=r*a`, where `r=-2+sqrt(3)` or its inverse
`r^-1=-2-sqrt(3)`. **The two contact points of a bitangent plane are not
paired by `iota`.** Instead `iota` sends one bitangent pair `{a,b}` to
another pair `{-a,-b}`. Relative to the two ramification points, the
unordered multiplicative ratio satisfies `r+r^-1=-4`.

For such `a,b` the plane is

```
B_{a,b}: a^2*b^2*x0-2*a*b*(a+b)*x1-2*(a+b)*x2+x3=0.
```

There are no bitangents at one endpoint and a distinct point: the same
middle-coefficient condition excludes them. The compactified family
has two degenerate members, with sections `4p0` and `4p∞`, exactly the
hyperosculating planes.

A coordinate-free organization uses `Sym^2(C)≅P2`. An unordered
length-two divisor has binary quadratic equation
`h=alpha*s^2+beta*s*t+gamma*t^2`; its doubled divisor is a hyperplane
section exactly when `h^2∈W`, namely

```
beta^2+2*alpha*gamma=0.
```

This is a smooth conic in `Sym^2(C)`. Squaring maps it to a rational
quartic curve of bitangent planes in the dual projective space. It
meets the diagonal (double-point divisors) in exactly the two endpoint
divisors, with intersection multiplicity two at each: the diagonal is
`beta^2-4*alpha*gamma=0`, and the two conics have the same tangent
line at either intersection. The ordered correspondence on `C×C` is the union of the two
graphs `b=r*a` and `b=r^-1*a`, exchanged by interchanging the factors.

## 4. Incidence identities

For a generic `a`, the tangent line `T_a` lies in `H_a`, in the tangent
plane to `Q`, and in the two bitangent planes at `a`. In particular,

```
H_a ∩ B_{a,r*a}=T_a.
```

Any two distinct tangent lines are coplanar, and hence intersect, exactly
when their contact points form a bitangent pair. This is because a plane
containing both tangent lines has two double zeros in its section,
and conversely the bitangent plane contains both lines. In particular
`T_a` and `T_-a` are skew for nonzero finite `a`.

The plane tangent to `Q` at `c(a)` has section

```
(u-a)*(u^3-a^3),
```

and cuts `Q` in `L_{a^3} ∪ M_a`. Thus its section is
`2[a]+[zeta*a]+[zeta^2*a]` for a primitive cube root `zeta`, with the
endpoint limits `4p0,4p∞`. In contrast, a nonendpoint osculating plane
cuts `Q` in a smooth conic with section `3[a]+[-a]`; a nondegenerate
bitangent plane cuts it in a smooth conic with section `2[a]+2[b]`.
Their plane determinants are respectively `3*a^4` and `9*a^2*b^2`,
so neither kind is a tangent plane to `Q` away from the endpoints.

More generally, if distinct planes `H,K` have section divisors `D_H,D_K`,
the scheme `C∩(H∩K)` is exactly their common divisor
`min(D_H,D_K)`. Locally on the smooth curve this is the equality
`(t^h,t^k)=(t^min(h,k))`. It gives the preceding line incidences without
mistaking contact multiplicities in a plane for line-intersection
multiplicities.

## 5. Scope and novelty boundary

These are elementary projective-geometric statements reconstructed here,
not a literature-priority claim. No normal-bundle splitting or accepted
degree-four exclusion is reproved or used. The configurations by
themselves say nothing about the existence of higher-degree STCI pairs.
Their potential application must retain the distinction between the
length-three ruling lines, the length-two paired secants, and the
length-four hyperosculating **plane** sections.

# Special plane and line sections: independent STCI derivation

Date: 2026-10-24. Author: independent agent `stci_sections`.

**Status:** supporting research proposal, not a canonical result or promotion.
All claims below concern the fixed embedded curve
\(C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\) over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
zero. The section-length arguments work more generally. Their elementary proofs
are included so that the new geometric interpretation does not depend on a
historical bound on common normal directions.

## 1. Proper intersections first; sole support is an additional hypothesis

Let \(F,G\) have positive degrees \(d,e\), vanish on \(C\), and have no common
surface component. Then \(Z=V_+(F,G)\) is a pure one-dimensional
Cohen--Macaulay complete intersection. Write

\[
[Z]=m[C]+R,\qquad de=4m+\deg R,
\]

where \(R\) is effective and has no component \(C\). The integer \(m\) is
the length of the generic transverse algebra at \(C\). If a plane \(H\)
contains no component of \(Z\), intersection with \(H\) gives the effective
zero-cycle

\[
[Z\cap H]=m[C\cap H]+R\cdot H.
\tag{1}
\]

Residual components may meet \(H\) at points of \(C\); their contribution
must not be discarded on the basis of support of a single section.
If \(H\) contains a residual component, its restricted equations have an
improper intersection, and this finite-section formula is inapplicable.

Now impose the additional hypothesis \(Z_{\mathrm{red}}=C\). It implies
\(R=0\), \(de=4m\), and every plane is a proper section, because \(C\)
is nonplanar. Neither restricted equation \(F_H,G_H\) vanishes identically:
otherwise a positive-degree plane curve \(V_H(G_H)\), or the whole plane,
would lie in \(Z\). They have no common plane curve component either.

## 2. Exact local lengths of the plane complete intersections

For every \(p\in C\cap H\), put
\(r_p=\operatorname{ord}_p(H|_C)\). Then

\[
\boxed{
 i_p(F_H,G_H)
 =\operatorname{length}\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3,p}/(F,G,H)
 =m r_p.}
\tag{2}
\]

Here the length is that of the actual zero-dimensional plane complete
intersection, not merely a total degree. For completeness, let
\(B=\mathcal O_{Z,p}\) and let \(h\) define \(H\). The ring \(B\) is
one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay, its only minimal prime is the ideal of
\(C\), and \(h\) is a nonzerodivisor. Use a prime filtration of \(B\)
and additivity of the finite Euler characteristic
\(\chi_h(M)=\ell(M/hM)-\ell(\ker h|_M)\).
Factors supported at the closed point have Euler characteristic zero.
The unique dimension-one factor \(\mathcal O_{C,p}\) appears \(m\)
times and has Euler characteristic \(r_p\). This proves (2). Standard
intersection theory identifies the same length with local plane intersection
multiplicity; see [Stacks, tag 0B06](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B06).

Consequently an ordinary osculating plane with section \(3p+\iota(p)\)
gives lengths \(3m,m\); a hyperosculating plane gives one local length
\(4m=de\); and a nondegenerate bitangent plane with section \(2p+2q\)
gives lengths \(2m,2m\). Any other special plane uses its actual divisor
\(H|_C\) in the same formula.

This does **not** identify the plane ideal or the Hilbert function. For a local
test, set \(C=V(x,y)\subset\operatorname{Spec}k[[t,x,y]]\) and
\(H_r=V(y+t^r)\). The supported CIs

\[
J_1=(x,y^4),\qquad J_2=(x^2,y^2)
\]

both have generic length four and give plane-section length \(4r\), but
their plane algebras are
\(k[[t]]/(t^{4r})\) and \(k[[t,x]]/(x^2,t^{2r})\). For \(r\ge2\)
the embedding dimensions are respectively one and two.

## 3. Tangencies that do follow, and singularities that do not

For two plane germs of multiplicities \(a_p,b_p\), equation (2) implies
\(a_pb_p\le mr_p\). Equality holds exactly when their tangent cones
have no common linear factor. The number \(mr_p\) alone does not specify
\(a_p,b_p\), smoothness, or a tangent cone.

If both plane germs are smooth, they have a common tangent precisely when
their intersection multiplicity is greater than one. If an ambient surface
\(X\) is smooth at \(p\), its tangent plane contains \(T_pC\). For a
plane \(H\supset T_pC\) with \(H\ne T_pX\), the section \(X\cap H\)
is smooth with tangent line \(T_pC\). If both surfaces satisfy these
conditions, the plane sections share that tangent and have the exact contact
order \(mr_p\). When \(H=T_pX\), the plane section can be singular;
its tangent cone requires additional information.

Concentration at a hyperosculating point does not force an ambient surface
singularity. The local model

\[
F=x,\quad G=x+y^m,\quad H_r=y+t^r
\tag{3}
\]

has two smooth ambient surfaces, sole reduced support \(C\), and two smooth
plane curves
\(x=0\), \(x+(-1)^m t^{rm}=0\) with contact exactly \(rm\).
It realizes every \(r\), including \(r=4\). This is a local test, not
a projective realization for the fixed quartic.

There is nevertheless a standard **global smooth-carrier restriction**.
If \(X\) were smooth at every point of \(C\), then in a neighborhood
of \(C\) the Cartier divisor cut out by \(G\) on \(X\) would be
\(mC\). Restriction to \(C\) would give
\(4e=m\deg N_{C/X}=m(14-4d)\), where the last equality follows from
the normal sequence and the accepted degree \(\deg N_{C/\mathbf P^3}=14\).
Combining with \(de=4m\) gives
\(16=d(14-4d)\), impossible: for positive integral \(d\) the right side
is at most twelve. Thus **every hypothetical defining surface is singular
somewhere on \(C\)**. This standard adjunction calculation is stronger
than the bare plane-section length identity; it locates neither those
singularities nor a new all-degree obstruction. The local smooth examples
above show why it is essential that smoothness was assumed along the whole
projective curve.

If \(m>1\), the first conormal symbols satisfy
\(\sigma_F\wedge\sigma_G=0\) identically on \(C\): independent
linear terms would generate the maximal ideal of the transverse regular
local ring and give generic length one. At a point where both symbols are
nonzero, the ambient tangent planes therefore coincide. If a symbol is zero,
the corresponding surface is singular there; wedge vanishing in this case
does not describe two smooth tangent surfaces. Entirely thick equations have
zero first symbols and supply no first-order tangent-plane field.

## 4. Lines have a different intersection problem

Let \(L\) be a line, and \(Z_L=C\cap L\) its finite intersection scheme.
If neither restriction vanishes, then

\[
\gcd(F|_L,G|_L)\ \text{has divisor at least }Z_L,
\qquad \deg\gcd(F|_L,G|_L)\le\min(d,e).
\]

Under sole support its support is exactly the support of \(Z_L\); its
multiplicities can exceed those of \(Z_L\). Individual restricted forms
can have additional zeros away from \(C\), provided these zeros are not
common. If \(L\subset X\), then \(L\not\subset Y\) and the degree
\(e\) divisor of \(G|_L\) is entirely supported on \(C\cap L\).
For a line meeting \(C\) only at one point, this is an \(e\)-fold root.
In particular it applies to a contained endpoint tangent trisecant.

There is no line analogue \(\ell(Z\cap L)=m\ell(C\cap L)\) in general.
Even locally, in model (3) a line germ with normal coordinates
\(x=t^r,y=t^r\) gives length \(r\), while the reduced curve intersection
has length \(r\) and the transverse thickness is \(m\). This germ need
not be a linear line in the straightened coordinates; it demonstrates the
missing local algebra implication. For an actual transverse line, take
\(t=y=0\), giving length one for every \(m\).

## 5. Residual divisors on the unique quadric

Write

\[
Q=V(q),\quad q=x_0x_3-x_1x_2,\quad
[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]=[ac:ad:bc:bd].
\]

The equation of \(C\) on \(Q\cong\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\)
is \(c_C=bc^3-ad^3\), so \(C\) has class \((1,3)\) and a hyperplane
has class \((1,1)\). A fibre of the first projection, class \((1,0)\),
is a trisecant; a fibre of the second, class \((0,1)\), is unisecant.

Under sole support, \(q\) cannot divide \(F\): otherwise
\(V_Q(G|_Q)\) would be supported only on \(C\), forcing
\((e,e)=b(1,3)\), which is impossible for \(e>0\). The same holds
for \(G\). Hence there are well-defined effective divisors

\[
\operatorname{div}F_Q=aC+R_F,\quad R_F=(d-a,d-3a),\quad
1\le a\le\lfloor d/3\rfloor,
\]

and similarly \(\operatorname{div}G_Q=bC+R_G\). The orders \(a,b\)
are orders on \(Q\), not automatically the ambient normal orders.
The residual divisors have no common component, since neither contains
\(C\) and any other common curve would be a residual component of \(Z\).
Their proper zero-dimensional intersection is supported on \(C\), with

\[
\boxed{\deg(R_F\cdot R_G)
=2de-4(db+ea)+6ab\ge0.}
\tag{4}
\]

Also \(R_F\cdot C=4d-6a\), and similarly for \(G\). These are
necessary divisor-class and support constraints. They provide no automatic
scheme description of \(R_F\cap R_G\), nor an equality with a first-normal
defect divisor without further hypotheses. The promoted
[STCI-R-0019](../results/quasiprimitive_residual_cycles.md) supplies a stronger
length-cycle identity only with its finite-flat curvilinear algebra, saturated
invertible flag, nonzero generic auxiliary first class, and proper residual
intersection hypotheses. We do not assume those hypotheses here.

If \(d=3a\), then \(R_F=(2a,0)\) is a union of trisecant fibres.
On each fibre component \(L\), the mate restricts to a polynomial all of
whose zeros lie on the degree-three scheme \(L\cap C\). At a generic
fibre these are three distinct points and the zero multiplicities are an
integer allocation of \(e\); at a ramified endpoint fibre all \(e\)
zeros lie at one point. These are interpolation constraints, not an
emptiness proof.

## 6. A precise normal-direction coincidence count

Use the accepted conormal degree/splitting
\(E=N^*_{C/\mathbf P^3}=\mathcal O(-7)^2\) from
[STCI-R-0020](../results/quartic_conormal_characteristic_jump.md), without
reproving it. A first symbol of a degree-\(d\) form is a section of
\(E(4d)\). If nonzero generically, write its common zero divisor as
\(A_F\), with degree \(a_F\); its saturated direction line is
\(L_F=\mathcal O(a_F-4d)\subset E\).

The osculating plane at affine parameter \(u\) has coefficient vector
\([-u^4:2u^3:-2u:1]\), since its restriction to \(C\) is
\((t-u)^3(t+u)\). Homogenizing gives a degree-four, everywhere nonzero
plane vector. A plane tangent to \(C\) gives an element of \(E(4)\),
so this field defines an everywhere saturated line
\(L_{\rm osc}=\mathcal O(-8)\subset E\).

If \(L_F\ne L_{\rm osc}\) generically, the wedge of their two
inclusions has an effective coincidence divisor of degree

\[
\boxed{4d-6-a_F.}
\tag{5}
\]

This follows directly from
\(\deg\operatorname{Hom}(L_F\otimes L_{\rm osc},\det E)
=-14-(a_F-4d)-(-8)\).
At a singular point the count uses the saturated limiting field, not an
actual smooth tangent plane. If the fields agree identically, their wedge
is identically zero and there is no finite coincidence divisor.

For two nonzero proportional first symbols, saturation yields the same
line and therefore
\(4d-a_F=4e-a_G\). Formula (5) then gives the same coincidence divisor
for both equations. This is a global compatibility identity involving actual
first-normal data; special plane-section cycle identities alone do not
determine those data.

## 7. Explicit counterexample to a naive bound of two

Any proposed universal bound of two on such coincidences needs hypotheses
beyond the special-plane geometry. Here is an exact test with residual curves:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F&=x_0^2x_2-x_1^3+x_1x_3^2-x_2^3,\\
G&=x_0F+q^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

The cubic \(F\) is smooth at every point of \(C\). At finite parameter
\(u\), its gradient is

\[
(2u^3,-3u^2+u^8,1-3u^6,2u^5),
\]

which cannot vanish: if its first coordinate is zero, then \(u=0\) and
the third is one. At infinity the gradient is \(dx_1\). The first
symbols of \(F,G\) are proportional generically. Their saturated common
plane field coincides with the osculating field at exactly the six simple
points \(u^6=-1\), in agreement with (5) for \(d=3,a_F=0\).

This is **not** an STCI counterexample. Indeed

\[
(F,G)=(F,q^2),\qquad
F|_Q=(a^2-b^2)(bc^3-ad^3).
\]

Its support is \(C\) together with the two distinct trisecant fibres
\(a^2-b^2=0\). At the generic point of \(C\), the gradients of
\(F,q\) are independent, so \(m=2\); the residual cycle has degree
\(12-4\cdot2=4\), namely twice each of the two lines.
Thus (6) refutes an inference from proportional first symbols and the
incidence geometry to a bound of two. It does not refute a theorem retaining
additional supported-CI, saturation, degree, or defect hypotheses.

## 8. Moving sections and the remaining global question

For the osculating-plane family over its parameter \(B\cong\mathbf P^1\),
the incidence subscheme \(\mathcal D_Z\subset Z\times B\) is a relative
effective Cartier divisor and is finite flat of degree \(de\) over \(B\):
all fibres are finite, the product is flat, and the section is a
nonzerodivisor on every fibre. On \(C\times B\), its reduced-carrier
equation defines the divisor \(3\Delta+\Gamma_\iota\). Its fundamental
cycle after replacing \(C\) by \(Z\) is
\(m(3\Delta+\Gamma_\iota)\). In particular the hyperosculating
collision of lengths \(3m\) and \(m\) is compatible with flatness.

The open issue is whether this family, its actual embedded scheme structure,
and the normal-direction/divisor data can be shown incompatible with global
homogeneous equations in all remaining degrees. Neither (2), (4), (5), the
flat family, nor the counterexample tests prove such incompatibility.
The established quartic-carrier exclusion and its scope are retained from
[STCI-R-0001](../results/quartic_carrier_exclusion.md); no part of that
accepted proof or the accepted normal-bundle computation was rerun here.

# Local filtration audit and promotion boundary — 2026-10-26

Status: mathematical review of local statements, not an independent executable replay, not a global sheafification certification, and not a new canonical theorem. Sources: RECONCILIATION_2026-10-24-IDEAL-FILTRATIONS.md and the October 23–24 investigation and independent review notes.

## 1. Exact comparison from ideals

Let R be a noetherian local ring, I an ideal, J subset I, S=gr_I(R), H=in_I(J), B=in_I(IJ), and S_+ its positive part. The inclusion S_+H subset B is immediate by multiplying representatives and taking leading terms, even when cancellations make B larger. The filtration F^n on D=J/IJ is the image of J intersect I^n. Its nth graded quotient is (J intersect I^n)/(J intersect I^(n+1) + IJ intersect I^n). The initial-symbol identification therefore gives gr_F^n D=H_n/B_n. Consequently

0 -> B_n/(S_+H)_n -> H_n/(S_+H)_n -> H_n/B_n -> 0

is exact. In particular the comparison map is a surjection from Q to gr_F D, not the reverse. This argument is local and requires no global splitting.

## 2. Generic generator excess

At the generic point of a smooth curve, suppose R is a regular transverse two-dimensional local ring and J is a two-generator parameter ideal. Then D=J/IJ is a two-dimensional vector space over the residue field. The induced finite filtration is exhaustive and separated; hence dim gr_F D=2. The initial ideal H is a finite-colength homogeneous ideal in a polynomial ring in two variables, and Q=H/S_+H has dimension mu(H). Summing the exact comparison gives dim Delta=mu(H)-2. This does not imply Delta vanishes whenever J is a complete intersection: the initial ideal can need extra generators.

## 3. Punctual torsion criterion

On a smooth curve, suppose the graded comparison is an isomorphism generically. Each Delta_n is torsion. Over a local DVR, Q_n -> G_n is surjective with equal generic rank. The induced map of torsion-free quotients is an isomorphism (the target is free and the source quotient has equal rank), so there is an exact torsion sequence 0 -> Delta_n -> tors(Q_n) -> tors(G_n) -> 0. Length Delta_n equals length tors(Q_n) minus length tors(G_n). Thus torsion in Q is necessary but not sufficient for a punctual defect. If Q is torsion-free, the defect vanishes.

## 4. Smooth-carrier support and its precise logical role

The independent October 23 review reconstructs the completed-local straightening of a smooth carrier and concludes Delta_p=0 wherever at least one carrier has nonzero first normal symbol. Therefore Supp Delta is contained in C intersect Sing(F) intersect Sing(G), under the regular ambient and two-generator CI hypotheses. Containment is not an existence assertion. The argument is local, and no global line-bundle twist is needed for this support inclusion.

## 5. What the examples do and do not show

The October 24 punctual family has Delta concentrated in one degree with arbitrary torsion length, but its actual complete intersection has residual support, so it does not address radical(J)=I. The supported quadruple J=(x^2,y^2+tx) has radical I and torsion in Q, but Delta=0. These are opposite controls, not a solution to the supported punctual-defect question.

## 6. Still unverified

The characteristic-two quartic claim ker(D -> I/I^2) is isomorphic to O(-24) and to Q_4 relies on global chart frames and transitions; it is supported by the prior independent audit, but has not been rechecked in this turn. The normal-Koszul spectral sequence requires its filtered-complex indexing, convergence and incoming-differential identifications to be retained exactly as in the source; no simplified global spectral-sequence theorem is promoted here. The case radical(J)=I with generic Delta=0 and nonzero isolated Delta remains open.

## Decision

The local exact comparison, generic excess, torsion criterion and support containment have independently reviewed proofs and are suitable for a *separately scoped local-algebra* result after schema/path validation. Do not promote CR-13's global O(-24) assertion or infer a general supported-defect vanishing theorem from this note. This review does not perform a fresh executable replay.
