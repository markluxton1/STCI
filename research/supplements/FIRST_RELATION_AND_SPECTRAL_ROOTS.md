# First-relation defects and the effective resultant budget

Date: 2026-10-19.
Author: `saturation_defects` research worker.
Status: **provisional structural theorem package; independent audit PASS**.
Independent reconstruction: [defect audit](2026-10-19-first-relation-defect-independent-audit.md).

## 1. Scope, dependencies, and proposed contribution

Let `C` be a smooth connected projective curve in a smooth threefold `X`
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let `Y` be an
actual lci thickening with `Y_red=C`. Put

    E=N^*_(C/X), J=I_C O_Y,
    L_j=J^j/J^(j+1), K_j=ker(Sym^j E -> L_j).

Assume the **full** generic ordinary initial algebra is

    k(C)[u,v]/(f_r,g_s),  2<=r<s, gcd(f_r,g_s)=1.

No saturation assumption is made on the actual first relation `K_r`.
Write

    R=saturation(K_r in Sym^r E),
    K_r=R(-D), delta=length(D), g=s-r, d=r+s-2,
    h=deg E, a=deg R.

Here `R` is a line subbundle, and `D` is an effective divisor. This note
produces an effective resultant divisor with an exact degree determined
by saturated-filtration generation defects. It then charges a moving
nonreduced root to those defects, and ultimately to `D`.

Accepted inputs are [STCI-R-0010](../results/saturated_first_relation_normal_flatness.md),
its saturated Gorenstein-duality proof in
[the filtered-duality evidence](2026-10-19-filtered-saturated-duality-moving-purepower-bridge.md),
and [STCI-R-0011](../results/moving_nonreduced_spectral_root_obstruction.md).
The characteristic-zero pure-power quotient formula is an accepted input
of [STCI-R-0009](../results/moving_purepower_boundary.md).
The local exact-order normal-flatness lemma is independently reviewed
supporting evidence in
[the local theorem](2026-10-19-filtered-generic-ci-normal-flatness.md)
and [its audit](2026-10-19-filtered-local-normal-flatness-independent-audit.md);
it is not itself a separate promoted result. Its codimension-two
Gorenstein Hilbert-function drop input is explicitly identified there.

The elementary torsion upper bound in section 3 below is inherited in
substance from section 9 of
[the generator-filtration audit](OCT13_THICK_EQUALITY_SOCLE_AND_FILTRATIONS.md#source-2026-10-13-generalization-thick-generator-filtration-auditmd).
The proposed new contribution is its combination with exact saturated
duality and the quotient resultant, including the formula

    degree(resultant divisor)=(r-1)c_(s-1)-r c_s.

This package remains conditional on the full generic initial CI. It
does not cover non-CI generic initial ideals, residual reduced
components, or arbitrary STCI presentations.

## 2. Saturated layers retain exact Gorenstein duality

Let `P_i` be the saturation of `J^i` in `O_Y`, and set

    B_i=P_i/P_(i+1), S=Ann_Y(J), ell=deg S.

The accepted saturated-duality lemma applies independently of saturation
of `K_r`: its hypotheses are actual lci Gorensteinness, unique smooth
reduced support, and generic graded Gorenstein symmetry. It gives

    J^(d+1)=0, B_i locally free,
    B_(d-i)=Hom_C(B_i,S).

In particular `B_d=S` and actual multiplication gives perfect pairings.

For `j<r`, the kernel `K_j` has generic rank zero and is a subsheaf of
the bundle `Sym^j E`, hence is zero. Consequently `L_j=Sym^j E` in
these degrees. Successive extensions show that `O_Y/J^i` has no
finite-support torsion for `i<=r`, so

    P_i=J^i (i<=r), B_j=Sym^j E (j<r).

The last displayed equality includes `j=r-1`: the quotient is
`P_(r-1)/P_r=J^(r-1)/J^r`. It does not assume that `L_r` is torsion free.

## 3. Early ordinary torsion and generation defects

For every `j>=r-1`, put

    A_j=Sym^j E/(R tensor Sym^(j-r)E),

where the denominator is zero for `j=r-1`. Since `R` is a subbundle,
fiberwise multiplication by its nonzero binary form is injective.
Thus `A_j` is a bundle of rank `r`, and

    deg A_j=[r*j-r*(r-1)/2]h-(j-r+1)a.                 (1)

For `r<=j<s`, the generic full-CI hypothesis says that the entire
generic kernel is generated by multiples of `f_r`. Therefore

    saturation(K_j)=R tensor Sym^(j-r)E.              (2)

Indeed the right side is a saturated subbundle with exactly the required
generic fiber. Furthermore actual multiplication gives

    R(-D) tensor Sym^(j-r)E subset K_j
                            subset R tensor Sym^(j-r)E.

If `T_j=torsion(L_j)` and `tau_j=length(T_j)`, (2) gives

    L_j/T_j=A_j,
    tau_j=length((R Sym^(j-r)E)/K_j)
          <=(j-r+1)delta,                r<=j<s.      (3)

The canonical map `L_j -> B_j` has kernel exactly `T_j`. To see this,
its kernel is generically zero, hence torsion; conversely torsion maps
to zero in the bundle `B_j`. Thus the degree-one generation map induces
an injection

    A_j -> B_j,  c_j=length coker(A_j -> B_j),
                                                r<=j<s. (4)

All the displayed defects can also be treated as effective length
divisors on `C`. Write `d_i=length(P_i/J^i)`. The exact comparison of
ordinary and saturated filtrations gives

    tau_j=d_(j+1)-d_j+c_j, c_j<=d_j, d_r=0.          (5)

In particular `c_r=0`, and summing (3)--(5) gives

    0<=c_(s-1)<=binom(g,2)delta.                     (6)

These inequalities hold stalkwise as well as after summing lengths.

## 4. Exact socle and second-relation identities

Let

    c=c_(s-1).

By (4), `deg B_(s-1)=deg A_(s-1)+c`. Pairing this bundle with
`B_(r-1)=Sym^(r-1)E` gives

    r ell=r(s-1)h-(s-r)a+c.                          (7)

This is the defect-corrected replacement for the saturated case of
STCI-R-0010. The correction is the generation defect of the saturated
layer, rather than the ordinary torsion of the same layer.

The primitive relation `R` vanishes in `B_r`: here `P_r=J^r`, and its
image in `L_r` is torsion. Multiplicativity of the saturated filtration
therefore gives maps `A_j -> B_j` in all higher degrees. At degree `s`
the map has generic rank `r-1`. Let

    T=ker(A_s -> B_s), c_s=length coker(A_s -> B_s).

The kernel is a line subbundle of `A_s`: its quotient is the image
inside a vector bundle and is torsion free. No global polynomial lift
of `T` is assumed. Duality gives

    B_s=Hom_C(Sym^(r-2)E,S),

and degree subtraction using (1) and (7) yields

    t:=deg T=s h-(s/r)a-((r-1)/r)c+c_s.              (8)

The rational expressions in (7)--(8) must be integers in any actual
example; they are consequences of bundle identities, not extra
hypotheses.

## 5. The effective resultant divisor

Locally choose a binary form generating `R` and a polynomial lift of
the quotient line `T` in degree `s`. The homogeneous resultant is
unchanged when the lift is modified by a first-relation multiple.
The local resultants therefore glue to a section of

    L_res=(det E)^(rs) tensor R^(-s) tensor T^(-r).

The section is generically nonzero because the entire generic initial
ideal is the coprime CI `(f_r,g_s)`. Its zero divisor `Delta` is effective,
and (8) gives the exact formula

    deg Delta=rs h-s a-r t=(r-1)c-r c_s.              (9)

In particular

    0<=r c_s<=(r-1)c,
    deg Delta<=(r-1)binom(g,2)delta.                 (10)

The divisor `Delta` is supported on `D`. At a point outside `D`, an
actual order-`r` relation has nonzero symbol. The transverse planar lci
fiber has exact first order `r`; the local exact-order normal-flatness
theorem cited in section 1 makes the entire normal cone a CI of
degrees `(r,s)` at that point. The quotient resultant is consequently
a unit there. This support assertion uses that separately reviewed
local theorem; the purely degree-theoretic identities (7)--(10) do
not require it.

The formula is an effective replacement for the zero-resultant-degree
step in STCI-R-0010. It permits a positive divisor and does not turn
its degree into an existence statement.

There is also a useful zero-defect criterion:

    c_(s-1)=0 if and only if all ordinary layers are locally free. (10a)

The reverse implication is immediate. For the forward implication,
(9) forces `c_s=0` and a nowhere-zero resultant. The first and second
quotient relations define an everywhere fiberwise binary CI algebra
`D_gr -> B`. Its top line has degree `a+t-h=ell` by (7)--(8), so its
generically nonzero map to `B_d=S` is an isomorphism. Perfect CI
fiberwise multiplication then forces each component of `D_gr -> B`
to be an isomorphism, exactly as in the accepted top-socle argument
of STCI-R-0010. Generation of `B` in degree one gives
`P_i=J^i+P_(i+1)`; descending induction yields `P_i=J^i` for all `i`.
In particular a nonzero first-relation defect forces `c_(s-1)>0`.

## 6. Full-root formulation and a general nonreduced-root payment

Specialize to `C=P1` and balanced `E=H^-1 tensor V`, where
`lambda=deg H` and `dim V=2`. Write

    R=H^-r tensor M_r, deg M_r=-epsilon,
    Z subset P1_base x P1_direction, class(Z)=(epsilon,r).

The saturated first form defines the **full** finite-flat root divisor
`Z`, retaining generic multiplicities and all singularities. Relative
restriction identifies

    A_s=H^-s tensor pi_*O_Z(s).

Consequently `T -> A_s` gives a section

    sigma of O_Z(-n,s), n=s lambda+t.                 (11)

It is a unit on the entire generic root algebra. Since `Z` is a
Cartier Cohen--Macaulay curve, `sigma` is a nonzerodivisor, with a
finite-length zero divisor `Q`. Its norm is the resultant, so

    length Q=s epsilon-r n=deg Delta.                (12)

This also follows directly from degrees and Euler characteristics on
the full possibly nonreduced root. Write

    Z=sum_i m_i D_i, class(D_i)=(a_i,b_i), b_i>0.

The section has nonzero reduction on each reduced integral component,
so the following integers are nonnegative:

    rho_i=s a_i-n b_i>=0,
    deg Delta=sum_i m_i rho_i.                       (13)

If `m_i>=2` and `a_i>0`, then in fact `rho_i>=1`. If `rho_i=0`, the
nonzero section of the degree-zero line on the integral curve `D_i`
would be nowhere zero. The same section restricted to `2D_i` would
then be a unit, by the local unit criterion modulo the nilradical.
It would trivialize `O(-n,s)|2D_i`, contrary to the doubled positive
divisor Picard injectivity used in STCI-R-0011, since `s>0`.

It follows that

    deg Delta>=sum_(i: m_i>=2 and a_i>0) m_i.         (14)

If a repeated component is constant (`a_i=0`), (13) forces `n<=0`,
and hence `deg Delta>=s epsilon`. Thus whenever `epsilon>0` and `Z`
is nonreduced, either (14) contains a term at least two, or all
repeated components are constant and the latter stronger bound applies.
In particular

    moving nonreduced full root => deg Delta>=2.      (15)

Unlike the saturated obstruction, this conclusion allows a moving
nonreduced root provided a nonzero defect divisor pays for it. It is
only a necessary condition and retains all component and gluing data.

For the actual quartic `C0`, `lambda=7`, `h=-14`, and for an actual
global CI of surface degrees `A,B`, `ell=14-4(A+B)`. Defining

    mu=7d+14-4(A+B),

equation (7) becomes

    r mu=g epsilon+c.                               (16)

The scalar equation is a consequence of the full bundle model. It
does not substitute for the resultant divisor or ambient lifting.

## 7. Stronger payment for a moving pure power

Suppose now `R=(H^-1 tensor M)^r`, with `M` a moving line subbundle
of the constant `V` and `deg M=-e<0`. Thus `epsilon=r e`. The accepted
characteristic-zero apolar quotient formula gives

    A_s=H^-s tensor M^(-(g+1)) tensor Sym^(r-1)V.

This is a constant rank-`r` bundle tensored with a line of degree
`-s lambda+(g+1)e`. Since `T -> A_s` is nonzero,

    t<=-s lambda+(g+1)e.

Substituting (8) gives

    ((r-1)/r)c-c_s>=(r-1)e,
    deg Delta>=(r-1)r e=(r-1)epsilon.                (17)

The same left side is the degree of the zero divisor obtained by
evaluating `sigma` on the reduced root section. Hence (17) also
recovers the accepted next-relation degeneration bound, now with an
exact actual-Gorenstein expression for the divisor degree.

Combining (6), (10), and (17) yields

    c>=r e,
    delta>=r e/binom(g,2), when g>=2.                 (18)

For an integral statement use the ceiling on the last right side.
For `g=1`, (6) gives `c=0`, already contradicting (17). Section 8
in fact excludes every genuine first defect when `g<=2`.
These conditions quantify the first-relation nonsaturation required
to survive the moving-power obstruction. They do not establish that
such a defect pattern can be lifted to an actual global CI.

## 8. Two auxiliary scope results

### Automatic normal flatness for gaps at most two

For a local transverse special fiber, let `q` be its first ideal
order. Purity gives `I_Y subset I_C^r`, so `q>=r`. A planar Artinian
Gorenstein algebra of first order `q` has length at least `q^2`:
its Hilbert function begins `1,2,...,q`, and the accepted drop bound
of at most one forces at least the tail `q-1,...,1`. Since every
transverse fiber has length `rs`,

    r<=q<=floor(sqrt(rs)).                            (19)

If `s-r<=2`, then `rs<(r+1)^2`, so `q=r` at every point. The local
exact-order normal-flatness theorem therefore gives all ordinary
layers locally free, even without assuming saturation of `K_r`.
The same reasoning covers `r=s`. Genuine first-relation defects in
the full generic CI lane can therefore occur only when `s-r>=3`.
For C0 this is a scoped structural fact, not a new STCI conclusion.

### The gap-three boundary is sharp for local lci families

An independent exact example from the `defect_local_models` worker is

    A=k[[t,x,y]]/(F,G),
    F=t*x*y+x^3+y^4, G=x*y^2.                         (20)

Its radical is `(x,y)`: a prime containing `G` contains `x` or `y`,
and `F` then forces the other normal variable. Thus the two equations
have height two and form a regular sequence. The special intersection
has length

    i(x^3+y^4, x*y^2)
      =i(x^3+y^4,x)+2 i(x^3+y^4,y)=4+2*3=10.

Since `A` is Cohen--Macaulay with support the `t`-axis, `t` is regular;
the finite algebra `A` is consequently free of rank ten over
`k[[t]]`. The explicit identity

    H=t^2 G+(x^2-t*y)F=x^5-t*y^5+x^2*y^4

shows that, over `k((t))`, the ideal is `(F,H)`. Their initial forms
are `txy` and `x^5-t y^5`, a coprime pair. Hence the **full** generic
ordinary initial ideal is the binary CI of degrees `(2,5)`. The
actual first kernel is `k[[t]]*txy`, with primitive saturation
`k[[t]]*xy` and colength one. The special first order is three.
This proves that the gap-three threshold above is sharp for local
lci families. Its primitive first root is constant and reduced.
It is not an example of a moving root or a global C0 construction.

For an explicit filtration certificate, the following is a free
`k[[t]]` basis:

    1,x,y,x^2,xy,y^2,x^2y,y^3,y^4,y^5.

The relations imply `x^3=-txy-y^4`, `x^3y=-y^5`,
`x^4=-t x^2y`, `x^5=t y^5`, and `y^6=0`. Thus

    J^2=<x^2,xy,y^2,x^2y,y^3,y^4,y^5>,
    J^3=<txy,x^2y,y^3,y^4,y^5>,
    J^4=<t x^2y,y^4,y^5>, J^5=<y^5>, J^6=0.

In particular `L_2=R_base^2 directsum R_base/(t)` and
`L_3=R_base^2 directsum R_base/(t)`, where
`R_base=k[[t]]`; the later `L_4,L_5` are free of ranks two and one.
The saturated generation cokernels have
`c_3=c_4=1`, `c_2=c_5=0`. The primitive degree-five quotient
relation is `x^5-t y^5`; its resultant with `xy` has a simple zero
at `t=0`. The special Hilbert function is `(1,2,3,2,1,1)`, while
the generic Hilbert function is `(1,2,2,2,2,1)`.
These are direct exact module calculations for this local example;
they do not assert a general pointwise version of (9).

### A broader elementary local family

An elementary counterexample family, supplied independently by the
`defect_local_models` worker, is

    A=k[[t,x,y]]/(x^a-t y^r, y^(q r)),
    a>r>=2, q>=2.

It is finite free over `k[[t]]` on the monomials
`x^i y^j`, `0<=i<a`, `0<=j<q r`, by the two monic relations. It is
an actual lci with reduced support the `t`-axis. Over `k((t))`, the
ideal equals

    (y^r-t^-1 x^a, x^(a q)),

whose full ordinary initial ideal is the coprime CI
`(y^r,x^(a q))`. The actual first relation is `t y^r`, so its
saturation has colength one. At `t=0`, the planar CI is
`(x^a,y^(q r))`, whose first order exceeds `r`.

For example `(r,a,q)=(2,3,2)` gives generic degrees `(2,6)` and
special degrees `(3,4)`, with defect length one. This is a local
existence example, not a global C0 construction. It shows that actual
lci Gorensteinness and a generic full CI do not force first-relation
saturation in general.

## 9. Review and frontier impact

The proposed exact identities are (7), (8), and (9). The main new
geometric constraints are (13)--(18): repeated moving roots require
an effective resultant divisor, and actual saturated-duality
generation defects determine its total degree. The ordinary first
defect only provides an upper budget; the inequalities do not decide
whether the remaining models exist.

The linked independent review passed reconstruction of the actual kernel of `L_j -> B_j`,
the equality of the saturation in (2), the degree-s kernel as a
quotient relation, all resultant weights and signs, full-root
nonzerodivisor behavior, and the implication from a zero-degree
component section to triviality on its double. Its denominator
qualification at `g=1` has been incorporated in (18). The local example
must not be promoted to global projective existence. The full generic
initial CI, actual lci, and unique-support hypotheses remain explicit.

## 2. Early saturation and the actual maps

Set `P_i=saturation(J^i in O_Y)` and `B_i=P_i/P_(i+1)`. Accepted saturated duality gives bundles `B_i`, the socle line `S=B_d`, `d=r+s-2`, and perfect pairings `B_i tensor B_(d-i)->S`.

For `j<r`, the generic kernel `K_j` is zero. Since it is a subsheaf of the bundle `Sym^j E`, it is zero everywhere. Thus the ordinary layers through degree `r-1` are vector bundles. Their extensions show that `O_Y/J^i` is CM for `i<=r`, so `P_i=J^i` there. In particular the two exact early identifications needed later really are

    B_(r-1)=Sym^(r-1)E,
    B_(r-2)=Sym^(r-2)E.

No saturation of `K_r` was imported into this step.

For `r<=j<s`, multiplication by a primitive local frame of `R` has constant rank in every binary-form fiber. Therefore `R Sym^(j-r)E` is a subbundle of `Sym^j E`. It has exactly the generic fiber of `K_j`, and hence is its saturation. Multiplication of actual relations gives

    R(-D) Sym^(j-r)E subset K_j subset R Sym^(j-r)E.

Consequently the ordinary torsion is precisely

    tors(J^j/J^(j+1))=(R Sym^(j-r)E)/K_j,

with length `tau_j<=(j-r+1)delta`, where `delta=deg D`.

The primitive relation maps to zero in `B_r` because its image is generically zero and `B_r` is torsion free. Thus it and all its multiples vanish in the saturated graded algebra. This justifies `A_j->B_j` without falsely asserting that `A_j` maps to the ordinary layer. For `j<s`, this is an injection of equal-rank bundles with finite cokernel. At `j=s`, its kernel `T` is a line subbundle: the quotient is its image inside the torsion-free bundle `B_s`.

These map assertions pass. In particular, using `c_s` for the **cokernel** length, rather than for a rank-one kernel or its degree, is necessary for the signs below.

## 3. The sharper first-defect upper budget

Write `d_i=length(P_i/J^i)` and let `c_i` denote the generation-cokernel length. The ordinary-to-saturated exact comparison yields

    tau_i=d_(i+1)-d_i+c_i.

Also `C_i=P_i/(J^i+P_(i+1))` is a quotient of `P_i/J^i`, so `c_i<=d_i`. Since `d_r=0`, it follows that `c_r=0` and

    d_j<=sum_(i=r)^(j-1) tau_i.

For `g=s-r`, this independently gives

    c_(s-1)<=d_(s-1)
             <=sum_(i=r)^(s-2)(i-r+1)delta
              =binom(g,2)delta.

Every step is a statement of finite module lengths at a stalk. The inequality therefore holds both pointwise and after summation. No pointwise resultant-divisor identity is being deduced from it.

## 4. Independent reconstruction of the degree identities

Put `h=deg E`, `a=deg R`, `ell=deg S`, and `c=c_(s-1)`. The exact symmetric-power quotient gives

    deg A_j=r(2j-r+1)h/2-(j-r+1)a.

Pairing `B_(s-1)` with the exact early bundle `B_(r-1)` then gives

    r ell=r(s-1)h-(s-r)a+c.

At degree `s` the exact sequence is

    0 -> T -> A_s -> B_s -> C_s -> 0,

and duality identifies `B_s=(Sym^(r-2)E)^vee tensor S`. In particular

    deg T=deg A_s-deg B_s+c_s
         =s h-(s/r)a-((r-1)/r)c+c_s.

The plus sign on `c_s` is correct. Any actual example must satisfy the resulting integrality conditions.

Local lifts of the quotient relation `T` differ by first-relation multiples. Resultant invariance under such changes gives the section of

    (det E)^(rs) tensor R^(-s) tensor T^(-r).

The determinant weight `rs` and generator weights `s,r` agree with the homogeneous binary resultant. Generic coprimality makes this section generically nonzero. Its effective zero divisor `Delta` consequently has

    deg Delta=(r-1)c-r c_s>=0.

This proves all three central identities and the advertised upper bound

    deg Delta<=(r-1)binom(g,2)delta.

Only a total-degree identity is asserted. Although determinant sequences also identify the underlying resultant line with a line formed from cokernel divisors, that alone would not identify its particular section with a canonical divisor section. This audit does **not** claim a pointwise equality `Delta=(r-1)C_(s-1)-r C_s`.

## 5. Support and the zero-generation-defect implication

At a point outside `Supp D`, there is an actual order-`r` relation with nonzero special symbol. The reviewed local exact-order theorem therefore makes the entire ordinary normal cone a CI of degrees `(r,s)` in a neighborhood of that point. Its quotient resultant is a unit. Thus `Supp Delta subset Supp D`; the separate local theorem is an explicit dependency.

There is also a useful global consequence. If `c_(s-1)=0`, effectiveness gives `c_s=0` and `deg Delta=0`. Projectivity forces the generic nonzero resultant to be nowhere zero. The quotient relations then define a relative graded CI mapping to `B`. Its top line is `R tensor T tensor(det E)^(-1)` and has the same degree as `S`; the generic top map is a global isomorphism. Perfect fiberwise CI pairings force every graded map to be an isomorphism. Descending induction gives `P_i=J^i` for all `i`, hence all ordinary torsion, all generation defects, and `D` vanish. This uses the same accepted top-socle comparison as R-0010, with the zero-defect degree identities just verified.

This last statement is global and projective. No local vanishing conclusion was inferred merely from a local value of `c_(s-1)` and the global degree formula.

## 6. General nonreduced-root payment

Now restrict to the rational base with balanced conormal, exactly as in the proposal. The full finite-flat root divisor `Z` has class `(epsilon,r)` and retains all multiplicities. The quotient inclusion is adjoint to a section

    sigma of O_Z(-n,s), n=s lambda+deg T.

Generic coprimality makes it invertible on the full generic root algebra, including nilpotents. Since `Z` is a Cartier CM curve with no vertical components, it is a nonzerodivisor. The finite-length zero scheme has length

    s epsilon-r n=deg Delta.

The norm description agrees with the resultant: locally one can choose a direction point avoiding the finite root fiber, write the first form as a monic polynomial, and identify both sections with the determinant of multiplication by the second quotient relation. Changes of these local choices multiply by units.

Write `Z=sum_i m_i D_i`, with reduced integral `D_i` of class `(a_i,b_i)` and `b_i>0`. Restriction of the section to each component is nonzero, so

    rho_i=s a_i-n b_i>=0,
    deg Delta=sum_i m_i rho_i.

For an integral singular component this uses the degree of a line bundle and the finite cokernel of a nonzero section; smoothness of `D_i` is unnecessary.

Suppose `m_i>=2`, `a_i>0`, and `rho_i=0`. The component section would be nowhere zero on `D_i`. Its restriction to the contained subscheme `2D_i` is then a unit by the local unit criterion modulo a nilpotent ideal. It would trivialize `O(-n,s)|_(2D_i)`. The accepted doubled-positive-divisor Picard injectivity lemma used by R-0011 forbids this because `s>0`. Therefore such a component has `rho_i>=1` and contributes at least `m_i` to `deg Delta`.

A repeated constant component has `a_i=0`, hence `n<=0` by nonnegativity of `rho_i`. If the full root moves (`epsilon>0`), this yields `deg Delta>=s epsilon`, which is at least three under `s>r>=2`. Thus every moving nonreduced full root indeed satisfies `deg Delta>=2`, whether its repeated components move or remain constant. This distinction was tested explicitly and is handled correctly.

The result constrains repeated components of the **total root divisor**. It does not exclude a reduced total root with nonreduced special fibers.

## 7. Pure-power strengthening

For `R=(H^-1 M)^r` and `deg M=-e<0`, the accepted characteristic-zero apolar formula makes `A_s` a constant rank-`r` bundle tensored with a line of degree `-s lambda+(g+1)e`. A nonzero map from the kernel line `T` to this bundle gives

    deg T<=-s lambda+(g+1)e.

Substituting the independently verified degree of `T` gives

    ((r-1)/r)c-c_s>=(r-1)e,
    deg Delta>=r(r-1)e.

This is also consistent with the exact divisor identity for a pure power: locally `Res(m^r,g)=Res(m,g)^r`, so `Delta` is `r` times the evaluation zero divisor. Consequently the left side of the first inequality is an actual integer divisor degree in this case, not merely a rational numerical expression.

The inequalities `c>=r e` and, when its denominator is nonzero, `delta>=r e/binom(g,2)` follow. They are necessary conditions; no ambient construction or unrestricted STCI exclusion follows.

## 2026-10-19-spectral-nonreduced-root-obstruction

# A doubled-divisor obstruction to nonreduced moving spectral roots

Date: 2026-10-19. Author: primary research agent.
Status: **independently reviewed proposal; audit PASS**. The review
reconstructed the full argument and component corollaries. This file
does not promote a canonical result.

## 1. Exact question, accepted inputs, and scope

Work over an algebraically closed field `k` of characteristic zero. Write
`S=P1_base x P1_direction`, with projections `p,q`, and use
`O_S(a,b)=p*O(a) tensor q*O(b)`. A full root divisor is an effective
Cartier divisor `Z` finite flat of degree `r` over the base, of bidegree
`(epsilon,r)`. In particular, it has no vertical component.

The accepted [spectral criterion STCI-R-0008](../results/spectral_root_picard_criterion.md)
says, for a saturated first relation in a balanced split conormal bundle,
that an everywhere-coprime second **quotient** graded relation of degree
`s>r` exists if and only if

    O_Z(0,s) = p*O(n)|Z                              (1)

for an integer `n`. It retains the full, possibly nonreduced scheme `Z`.
No lifting of the second quotient line to a global polynomial line is
assumed here. The accepted
[moving-power result STCI-R-0009](../results/moving_purepower_boundary.md)
already excludes (1) for a multiple of a moving section. The new argument
excludes (1) for **every nonreduced moving root divisor**, including
repeated irreducible multisections and mixtures of components.

The primary STCI application has `C=C0`, `N_C=O(7)^2`. The proof below
concerns the spectral ruled surface and does not use an assertion about
the actual ambient formal neighborhood or arbitrary defining surfaces.

## 2. Differential restriction lemma

**Lemma 1.** Let `X` be a smooth scheme over a field, `D` an effective
Cartier divisor, and `L` a line bundle. If `L|2D` is trivial, then the
Hodge first Chern class

    c1(L) in H1(X,Omega_X)

restricts to zero in `H1(D,Omega_X|D)`.

**Proof.** Let `I=I_D`. On a sufficiently fine affine cover of `D`, choose
frames of `L` lifting a fixed trivialization on `2D`. Such frames exist
after shrinking ambient open sets: a lift of a unit modulo `I^2` is a
unit near `D`. The resulting transition functions satisfy
`g_ij=1 mod I^2`. The Leibniz rule gives `d(I^2) subset I Omega_X`.
Consequently `(dg_ij)/g_ij` restricts to zero in `Omega_X|D`.
These logarithmic differentials represent the restriction of `c1(L)`,
proving the claim. Equivalently, differentiation of a lift defines a
map `O_2D -> Omega_X|D`, independent of the lift because
`d(I^2) subset I Omega_X`, and it annihilates the transition cocycle of
a trivial line bundle. This argument does not require `D` to be smooth
or reduced. Square.

The use of **ambient** differentials restricted to `D`, rather than
`Omega_D`, is essential. Triviality only on `D` would not prove the lemma.

## 3. Picard injectivity on a doubled ample divisor

**Theorem 2.** Let `D subset S=P1 x P1` be an effective Cartier divisor of
bidegree `(a,b)` with `a,b>0`, over characteristic zero. Then

    Pic(S) -> Pic(2D)

is injective. More explicitly, if `O_S(u,v)|2D` is trivial, then
`u=v=0`. The divisor `D` may be singular, reducible, or nonreduced.

**Proof.** The restriction sequence for ambient differentials is exact:

    0 -> Omega_S(-D) -> Omega_S -> Omega_S|D -> 0.

Since

    Omega_S(-D)=O(-a-2,-b) direct sum O(-a,-b-2),

Kunneth and the usual cohomology of `P1` give
`H1(S,Omega_S(-D))=0`. Indeed, each Kunneth summand contributing to `H1`
has an `H0(P1,O(c))` factor with `c<0`. Hence

    H1(S,Omega_S) -> H1(D,Omega_S|D)

is injective. By Lemma 1, triviality on `2D` forces
`c1(O_S(u,v))=0` in `H1(S,Omega_S)`.

For completeness, `H1(S,Omega_S)=k direct sum k`, one summand for each
ruling. On a standard two-chart cover of `P1`, the transition `z` of
`O(1)` gives the nonzero Cech class `dz/z` in `H1(P1,Omega_P1)`.
The two ruling classes therefore form a basis, and
`c1(O_S(u,v))=(u,v)` in this basis, up to simultaneous conventions for
sign. Characteristic zero implies `u=v=0` as integers. Square.

This is a restriction theorem for line bundles on `S`. It is not an
assertion that all line bundles on `2D` come from `S`.

## 4. Nonreduced moving roots cannot have spectral descent

**Theorem 3.** Let `Z subset S` be an effective Cartier divisor, finite
flat over `P1_base`, of bidegree `(epsilon,r)` with `epsilon>0`.
Suppose there are integers `s>0,n` satisfying (1). Then:

1. `n r=s epsilon`, in particular `n>0`.
2. Every irreducible component `D_i` of `Z`, written with its reduced
   structure and bidegree `(a_i,b_i)`, satisfies
   `a_i>0`, `b_i>0`, and `s a_i=n b_i`.
3. `Z` is reduced as a divisor on `S`.

**Proof.** Write `Z=sum m_i D_i`, with `m_i>=1`. Finite flatness excludes
a vertical component, so `b_i>0`. Restricting (1) to the integral curve
`D_i` and taking degree gives

    s a_i=n b_i.                                      (2)

The degree computations are intersection computations on the smooth
surface: `deg O_Di(0,1)=a_i` and `deg O_Di(1,0)=b_i`.
Multiplying (2) by `m_i` and summing yields
`s epsilon=n r`. This proves `n>0`, hence `a_i>0` in (2).

If `m_i>=2` for any `i`, then `2D_i` is a closed subscheme of `Z`:
locally the equation of `Z` is divisible by the square of the equation
of `D_i`, so `I_Z subset I_Di^2`. Thus

    O_S(-n,s)|2D_i is trivial.

Theorem 2 applies to `D_i`, since both of its bidegrees are positive,
and forces `n=s=0`, contrary to `s>0`. All `m_i` equal one. An effective
Cartier divisor on a smooth surface with every prime multiplicity one
is reduced, so the claim follows. Square.

**Corollary 4 (graded obstruction).** Under the hypotheses of
STCI-R-0008 over the rational base in characteristic zero, a moving
first relation (`epsilon>0`) with nonreduced full root cannot have an
everywhere-coprime second quotient graded relation of any degree
`s>r`. Equivalently, the class of `O_Z(0,1)` has infinite order in
`Pic(Z)/p*Pic(P1)` for such a nonreduced moving root.

This includes repeated irreducible multisections, not only powers of
linear binary forms. It works uniformly in `epsilon,r,s`, and uses no
smoothness or irreducibility assumption on the root.

## 5. Reduced roots: a component bound and the movement-two boundary

The same proof records more than reducedness. Put

    g=gcd(epsilon,r),  epsilon0=epsilon/g,  r0=r/g.

If (1) holds, each component is of bidegree

    (a_i,b_i)=t_i (epsilon0,r0),  t_i a positive integer,
    sum t_i=g.                                       (3)

Indeed (2) gives the common slope, and coprimality of `epsilon0,r0`
gives integrality of `t_i`. Reducedness then gives the sum in (3).
In particular the number of irreducible components is at most `g`.
If `g=1`, the full root is integral.

For movement `epsilon=2`, any spectral-descent survivor therefore has
one of the following forms:

- an integral reduced divisor of class `(2,r)`, which may be singular;
- if `r` is even, a reduced union of two distinct divisors of class
  `(1,r/2)`.

An integral divisor of class `(1,k)` is smooth and rational: its
projection to the direction ruling is finite of degree one, and hence
is an isomorphism to the normal curve `P1`. The two components in the
second alternative have intersection length `r`; their intersections
and the full gluing data still enter the Picard descent test. No
torsion computation or ambient liftability is claimed for this locus.

## 6. Limits and precise STCI consequence

Theorem 3 eliminates **generic repeated factors** in a moving spectral
root whenever the uniform second-quotient-relation hypotheses hold.
It does not eliminate multiple roots occurring only in special fibers:
an integral ramified cover can have a nonreduced fiber while its total
root divisor is reduced.

For a hypothetical actual STCI on `C0`, if its full normal algebra has
a saturated moving first relation and a globally defined second
quotient generator coprime in every fiber as in STCI-R-0008, its root
must be reduced and satisfy (3). In particular, this excludes actual
pairs in that precisely specified uniform graded class with repeated
generic moving roots. It does **not** prove that an arbitrary actual
pair satisfies this uniformity assumption. Special-point initial-ideal
degeneration, nonsaturation, and additional initial generators remain
possible. No common singular point of the original surfaces is
assumed or deduced.

The separate [actual filtered-algebra bridge](2026-10-19-saturated-first-relation-spectral-bridge.md)
proves that an actual lci thickening with a saturated actual first
relation and full generic binary CI initial ideal satisfies precisely
the uniform graded hypotheses used here. Combining the two arguments
therefore excludes **actual** thickenings of `C0` in that class when the
full first root is moving and nonreduced. Saturation and the generic
two-generator initial-ideal pattern remain essential hypotheses.

If `epsilon=0`, the root is constant and may be nonreduced; the positivity
step above fails as it must. In positive characteristic, the final
Chern-class argument detects the integer bidegrees only modulo the
characteristic. No positive-characteristic exclusion is asserted.

The proofs above are conceptual; no finite parameter scan is used.
The standard Hodge Chern class via `dlog` and its Cech representative
are documented in the primary reference
[Stacks Project, Section 50.9, tag 0FLE](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FLE).

Independent reconstruction and failure tests are recorded in

The review outcome is PASS, including the component bound and the

## 2. Independent proof of the double-divisor claim

Write `Omega=Omega^1_(S/k)` and let `L=O_S(u,v)`.

**Infinitesimal triviality kills the restricted logarithmic Chern class.**
Choose a trivialization of `L|_(2D)`. Locally along `D`, lift the chosen
nowhere-zero local section to a local frame of `L`: a lift is again a
frame after shrinking, because its reduction modulo `I_D^2` is a frame.
Transition functions for these frames then have the form

    g_ij = 1+h_ij,              h_ij in I_D^2.

The Leibniz rule gives

    d(I_D^2) subset I_D Omega.

Consequently `(dg_ij)/g_ij` is zero in `Omega|_D`. The cocycle defining
the Hodge Chern class `c1^H(L)` therefore has zero restriction in
`H^1(D,Omega|_D)`.

This argument uses only the ideal-square condition and the smoothness of
the ambient surface. It does **not** require smoothness or reducedness
of `D`. The relevant target is the ambient restriction `Omega|_D`, not
`Omega_D`; replacing it by `Omega_D` would lose the needed information.

**The restriction map is injective.** Since `D` is effective Cartier and
`Omega` is locally free, its restriction sequence is exact:

    0 -> Omega(-D) -> Omega -> Omega|_D -> 0.

On the product surface,

    Omega(-D) = O_S(-a-2,-b) direct_sum O_S(-a,-b-2).

For `p<0` and `q<0`, the Kunneth formula gives

    H^1(S,O_S(p,q))
      = H^1(P1,O(p)) tensor H^0(P1,O(q))
        direct_sum H^0(P1,O(p)) tensor H^1(P1,O(q))
      = 0.

Both indices of both summands of `Omega(-D)` are strictly negative, so
`H^1(S,Omega(-D))=0`. The long exact sequence therefore makes

    H^1(S,Omega) -> H^1(D,Omega|_D)

injective. The previous paragraph implies `c1^H(L)=0` in
`H^1(S,Omega)`.

**The ambient class detects both integers.** The two ruling summands
identify `H^1(S,Omega)` with `k direct_sum k`. Under this identification,
the classes of `O_S(1,0)` and `O_S(0,1)` are the two basis vectors, up to
the common harmless choice of transition-sign convention. This can be
seen directly on each `P1`: the transition function of `O(1)` gives the
nonzero Cech differential `dz/z`, the generator of
`H^1(P1,Omega_P1)=k`. Additivity of `dlog` now gives

    c1^H(O_S(u,v)) = (u,v).

Characteristic zero makes the map `Z -> k` injective. Thus `u=v=0`,
proving (A).

For source verification of the standard constructions only, the
[Stacks discussion of logarithmic Hodge Chern classes](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FLE)
and [the field-base Kunneth formula](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BEC)
were checked. The needed calculation and ideal-square argument are
given explicitly above; no external nonreduced-root exclusion was used.

## 3. Scheme-theoretic audit of the spectral consequence

Set `L=O_S(-n,s)`. R-0008 gives `L|_Z=O_Z`.

**The positive descent degree remains valid on a nonreduced root.**
For any effective Cartier divisor `Z` of bidegree `(epsilon,r)` and
any integers `x,y`, the divisor sequence and
`chi(O_S(x,y))=(x+1)(y+1)` give

    chi(O_Z(x,y))-chi(O_Z) = r*x+epsilon*y.

This calculation works for singular, reducible, and nonreduced `Z`.
Applying it to the trivial bundle `L|_Z` gives

    -n*r+s*epsilon=0,
    n=s*epsilon/r > 0.                                  (1)

There is no use here of a degree formula valid only for smooth curves.
It also follows by taking the finite-flat norm, as in the accepted
R-0008 evidence.

**Every reduced component moves in both rulings.** Write the effective
divisor on the smooth surface as

    Z = sum_i m_i D_i,

where each `D_i` is a reduced irreducible component of bidegree
`(a_i,b_i)`. Finiteness of `pi:Z->P1` excludes vertical components:
`b_i>0`. More explicitly, an irreducible curve of bidegree `(a_i,0)`
would be a fiber of the projection to the first factor over the
algebraically closed field, and hence could not map finitely onto the
base. This is precisely where finite flatness of the **full** root
scheme is used, rather than merely a generic binary form.

Restricting `L|_Z=O_Z` further to `D_i` gives

    0 = deg(L|_D_i) = -n*b_i+s*a_i.

The degree formula is an intersection number on the smooth surface and
is valid for a singular integral `D_i`. Since `n>0`, `s>0`, and `b_i>0`,
it follows that `a_i>0` as well.

**A repeated component contains its full double.** If some `m_i>=2`,
then `Z-2D_i` is effective. The corresponding inclusion of divisor
ideals is

    I_Z subset I_(2D_i),

so `2D_i` is a closed subscheme of `Z`. Hence `L|_(2D_i)` is trivial.
Both bidegree entries of `D_i` are positive, and (A) applies with
`(u,v)=(-n,s)`. It would force `n=s=0`, contradicting `s>r>=2`.

Thus all multiplicities `m_i` equal one. Finally, an effective Cartier
divisor on the smooth surface has no embedded associated components;
equivalently, its local equations factor in the regular local UFDs
with exponents given by these divisor multiplicities. Multiplicity one
along every prime component therefore means the scheme `Z` is reduced.
This proves (B), including the exclusion of nilpotents supported only
at special points; there are no hidden embedded nilpotents left after
the multiplicity check.

The proof actually needs only `s>0` and a descent
`O_Z(0,s)=pi^*O(n)` with `epsilon>0`; the stronger `s>r>=2` is retained
because it is the accepted second-relation interface being applied.

## 4. Audit of the component bound and movement-two additions

Put `g=gcd(epsilon,r)`, `epsilon0=epsilon/g`, and `r0=r/g`. Equations

Consequently the number of components is at most `g`, and `g=1` forces
an integral full root. Positivity of both primitive entries uses

No unrecorded irreducibility assumption enters this deduction.

class `(1,r/2)`; the second possibility requires even `r`. This is an

a sufficient condition for descent or ambient realization.

`D` of class `(1,k)` does not assume that `D` is already normal. Its

6. **Reduced need not mean smooth or irreducible.** The conclusion leaves

## 3. Saturated filtration and exact early layers

Let `P_i` be the saturation of `J^i` in `O_Y`, and let

    B_i=P_i/P_(i+1),       S=Ann_Y(J).

Purity gives `J^(d+1)=0` globally. The saturation lemma gives vector
bundles `B_i`, a multiplicative saturated filtration, and perfect
actual multiplication pairings

    B_i tensor B_(d-i) -> S,
    B_(d-i)=B_i^* tensor S.                              (3)

For `j<r`, the actual initial relation kernel `K_j` is zero because it
is a rank-zero subsheaf of a vector bundle. For `r<=j<s`, one has

    R tensor Sym^(j-r)E subset K_j.

This product is a saturated subbundle of `Sym^j E`: in every fiber,
multiplication by the nonzero binary form `f_r` is injective with the
same rank. The generic rank of `K_j` is exactly that of this subbundle
by (1). The quotient between them is therefore zero. Hence

    K_j=R tensor Sym^(j-r)E,                 r<=j<s.     (4)

All ordinary layers through degree `s-1` are consequently vector bundles,
and successive extension gives

    P_i=J^i for i<=s.                                   (5)

Set, for `j>=r-1`,

    Q_j=Sym^j V/(M tensor Sym^(j-r)V),

where the denominator is zero at `j=r-1`. These are vector bundles with

    rank Q_j=r,       deg Q_j=(j-r+1)*epsilon.

For `r-1<=j<s`, equations (4)--(5) identify

    B_j=H^(-j) tensor Q_j.                              (6)

## 4. Duality fixes the socle degree and second quotient degree

Pair `B_(r-1)=H^(-(r-1)) tensor Sym^(r-1)V` with `B_(s-1)` in (3).
Taking degrees and using (6) gives

    deg S=-d*lambda+(s-r)*epsilon/r.                    (7)

Now `P_s=J^s` implies a SURJECTION

    A_s := Sym^s E/(R tensor Sym^(s-r)E) -> B_s.         (8)

The source is `H^(-s) tensor Q_s`, rank `r`, and the target, by (3), is

    B_s=H^(r-2) tensor S tensor (Sym^(r-2)V)^*,

rank `r-1`. The kernel `T` of (8) is a line subbundle. Degree subtraction
using (7) gives

    deg T=-s*lambda+s*epsilon/r.                        (9)

In particular the displayed rational quantities must be integral in
any actual example. This is a consequence, not an extra assumption.

## 5. The full root section has degree zero and no zeros

For `s>=r-1`, restriction to the root divisor gives

    Q_s = pi_* O_Z(s),
    A_s = H^(-s) tensor pi_* O_Z(s).                     (10)

Indeed the restriction sequence on `C x P1` has kernel
`M tensor O_(P1)(s-r)` and has vanishing relative first cohomology in
this range. It holds for the FULL Cartier root, including nilpotents.

By finite adjunction, the inclusion `T -> A_s` corresponds to a section

    sigma of L_Z := O_Z(s) tensor pi^*(H^(-s) tensor T^(-1)). (11)

At the generic point of `C`, its image is the unique second quotient
relation supplied by the full initial ideal in (1). Coprimality there
means that `sigma` is a UNIT on the entire generic root algebra. This
retains its nilpotents and each of its generic components.

The scheme `Z` is an effective Cartier divisor in the smooth surface
`C x P1`, hence is a pure Cohen--Macaulay curve. It is finite flat over
`C` and has no vertical associated points. A section that is a unit at
all generic points is therefore a nonzerodivisor and yields

    0 -> O_Z -> L_Z -> D_sigma -> 0,

where `D_sigma` has finite length. The coefficient root class gives

    deg O_Z(1)=epsilon,
    deg pi^*A=r deg A

for every line bundle `A` on `C`, with degrees and lengths computed on
the full root scheme. Therefore (9) gives

    deg L_Z=s*epsilon+r(-s*lambda-deg T)=0.

By the exact sequence, this degree is `length D_sigma`. Thus the
cokernel is zero and `sigma` is nowhere zero, including on nonreduced
or singular fibers. It trivializes `L_Z`, proving (2).

Consequently `T` is everywhere coprime to the first quotient relation.
No finite-support degeneration divisor survives. The degree-zero
argument, rather than generic coprimality alone, removes that divisor.

## 6. Further rigidity: recovering the full ordinary graded algebra

Let `K'_s` be the kernel of `Sym^s E -> B_s`. Its quotient by first
relation multiples is exactly `T`. Define the graded algebra

    D = Sym(E)/(ideal generated by R and K'_s).

Locally lift `T` to a second polynomial generator; different lifts
differ by a multiple of the first relation. Section 5 makes the two
local generators a regular sequence of degrees `(r,s)` in EVERY fiber.
Thus `D` is a finite locally free graded CI family. This construction
uses the generator extension, which need not split globally.

Both `R` and `K'_s` vanish in the multiplicative saturated graded algebra
`B=direct_sum B_i`, so there is a natural algebra morphism

    D -> B.                                             (12)

It is an isomorphism at the generic point and both sides have the same
graded ranks. The graded CI socle line is

    D_d=R tensor T tensor det(E)^(-1).

This determinant formula is invariant under changing the local second
generator by a multiple of the first; it does not assume a split
generator extension. Equations (7) and (9) give

    deg D_d=deg S=deg B_d.

The top component of (12) is generically nonzero. A nonzero map between
line bundles of equal degree on a smooth projective connected curve
is an isomorphism. Therefore the top map is an isomorphism everywhere.

At a closed point of `C`, suppose an element of `D_i` maps to zero in
`B_i`. Perfect fiberwise Gorenstein multiplication in `D` pairs it with
an element of `D_(d-i)` to a nonzero element of `D_d`, unless it was zero
already. The latter nonzero top element cannot map to zero because the
top map is an isomorphism. This contradiction proves that each
`D_i -> B_i` is fiberwise injective. Equal ranks now give an isomorphism
of every graded bundle.

Hence `B` is generated in degree one. In particular `J^i -> B_i` is
surjective for every `i`, or

    P_i=J^i+P_(i+1).

Descending induction from `P_(d+1)=0` gives `P_i=J^i` for all `i`. Thus
all ordinary layers are bundles and every ordinary graded fiber is the
binary CI just constructed. This proves conclusion 3 without an
external Hilbert-function classification.

bridge. This section is an independently reviewed proposal within the

**General theorem proposed.** Let `C` be a smooth connected projective

with `Y_red=C`. Let `E=N_(C/X)^*` be ANY rank-two conormal bundle.

`(r,s)`.

    h=deg E,      a=deg R,      d=r+s-2.

    deg A_j=[r*j-r*(r-1)/2]*h-(j-r+1)*a.                (14)

This follows by subtracting the degrees in its defining exact sequence,

    B_s=(Sym^(r-2)E)^* tensor S.

    t := deg T=s*h-(s/r)*a.                            (16)

QUOTIENT relation and does not require a global second polynomial line.

    L_Res=(det E)^(rs) tensor R^(-s) tensor T^(-r).     (17)

    D_d=R tensor T tensor (det E)^(-1),

and, by (15)--(16),

    deg D_d=a+t-h=ell=deg S.

The generic top map is nonzero and is therefore an isomorphism everywhere.
Perfect graded multiplication in EVERY fiber of `D` then forces every
map `D_i -> B_i` to be fiberwise injective, hence an isomorphism. Exactly

`P_i=J^i+P_(i+1)`; descending induction yields `P_i=J^i` for every `i`.
This proves the general theorem.

`(det N_C^*)^(rs) R^(-s) T^(-r)`, of degree zero. Its invariance on adding
a first-relation multiple to the second relation avoids any splitting
assumption.

Generic transverse length of the full initial CI is rs, so the degree of
the actual projective CI and determinants in (A1) give

    ab=4rs,
    4(a+b)=A+B=7(r+s)-epsilon*(s-r)/r.                (A2)

The length computation uses support exactly C. If residual curves are
allowed, its equality `ab=4rs` cannot be retained.

## 3. Nonsplitting and the orders of the original equations

If (A1) split, uniqueness of splitting type on P1 would imply

    {4a,4b}={A,B}.

Therefore

    16ab=A*B=rs*(49-(epsilon/r)^2)<=49rs,

whereas (A2) gives `16ab=64rs`. Since r,s are positive, this is impossible.
This argument also works when epsilon=0, and does not require first knowing
which line bundle is the more negative one.

Write the quotient map in (A1) as `(alpha,beta)`. Its entries are sections
of `O(4a-A)` and `O(4b-A)` without a common zero. If either entry were zero,
the other would be nowhere zero, providing a right inverse and splitting
the sequence. A nonzero degree-zero entry would also split it. Hence both
entries are nonzero of positive degree. Their identification with the
original degree-r symbols proves:

    ord_C(F)=ord_C(G)=r generically,
    their degree-r symbols jointly generate R at every point.

In particular

    A<4a,4b<B,       0<=epsilon<7r.                   (A3)

This deduction was tested against the tempting countercase of one original
equation having order s: that would make one order-r quotient entry zero,
which forces the already excluded splitting. It also retains special
points at which one original coefficient vanishes; the two cannot vanish
there simultaneously. No claim is made that either coefficient is
individually nowhere zero.

## 4. The quadric bound, including reducible equations

Let `q=0` be the smooth unique quadric Q containing C, where C has class
`(1,3)`. If q divides F, STCI support would require the nonzero divisor
`G|Q` of class `(b,b)` to have support C. Such a divisor would have to be
a positive integral multiple of `(1,3)`, impossible for b>0. If `G|Q=0`
instead, Q is a common surface component. Thus q cannot divide F, and
symmetrically cannot divide G. This direct argument uses only the STCI
support condition and works for reducible or nonreduced F,G.

Since F has generic ambient order r, its nonzero restriction to Q vanishes
along C at least r times. The remaining effective divisor has class
`(a-r,a-3r)`. Effectivity on P1 x P1 forces `a>=3r`; likewise `b>=3r`.
The residual divisor can contain further copies of C, so no equality of
ambient and restricted normal orders is assumed.

## 5. An independent exhaustive arithmetic reduction

    a+b<=3r+4tr/3.                                  (A4)

For the second inequality, `x+ab/x` decreases on
`3r<=x<=sqrt(ab)`. Combining it with the sum in (A2) gives

    (5r-3epsilon)*t<=15r-3epsilon.                   (A5)

Also B integral requires `r | epsilon*s`.

For epsilon=1 this makes t an integer. Hence (A4) gives t>=3. Since r>=2,

    t<=3+6/(5r-3)<4.

all-degree movement-one exclusion under precisely the stated hypotheses.
