# A uniform obstruction tower for moving power relations

Date: 2026-10-14. Status: **PROVED NEW STRUCTURAL THEOREM, independently reviewed in
[the tower audit](2026-10-14-generalization-moving-purepower-tower-independent-audit.md).**
The accepted balanced conormal bundle and embedded transition of `C0` are
premises. The statement concerns the compatibility of a single first normal
relation, and proves a boundary of that method rather than an STCI exclusion.
No accepted computation is rerun.

## 1. The quotient bundles in every normal order

Let `C` be a smooth curve in a smooth threefold, in characteristic zero, with
balanced conormal bundle

    E=N_C^*=H^-1 tensor V,  dim V=2.

Choose a line subbundle `M subset V tensor O_C`, so that its fibers never
vanish. Write `W=H^-1 tensor M subset E`. The corresponding normal direction
may move along the curve. Suppose the first relation under consideration is
the pure power `m^r`, with actual saturated relation line

    R=W^r=H^-r tensor M^r.

This section has no divisor where the actual relation fails to saturate.
Adding such a divisor changes the obstruction bundle and must be retained
separately. Let `k>=1`. If no independent relation has yet appeared, the
degree-`r+k` relation space is `R tensor Sym^k E`. The compatibility obstruction
for correcting the first relation at that order belongs to

    Q_k=Hom(R, Sym^(r+k)E / (R tensor Sym^k E)).             (1)

After fixing a nonzero determinant of the constant vector space `V`, there is
a uniform bundle isomorphism

    Q_k = H^-k tensor M^-(r+k+1) tensor Sym^(r-1)V.          (2)

Thus `Q_k` has rank `r`. Formula (2) does not depend on the degrees of the
two projective hypersurfaces in a hypothetical STCI presentation.

Here is a direct proof. Let `M_perp subset V^* tensor O_C` be the line
annihilating `M`. The constant determinant identifies `M_perp` with `M`.
Under the perfect characteristic-zero symmetric pairing, the annihilator of

    M^r tensor Sym^k V subset Sym^(r+k)V

is exactly

    M_perp^(k+1) tensor Sym^(r-1)V^*.                       (3)

At any fiber choose a basis in which `m` is the first vector. The first
space consists of monomials with first-vector exponent at least `r`.
Its annihilator consists of dual monomials with second-vector exponent at
least `k+1`, which is precisely (3). Multiplication by a nonzero linear form
is injective at every fiber, so both sides of (3) are vector bundles of
rank `r`; this fiberwise equality is a bundle equality. Dualizing proves

    Sym^(r+k)V / (M^r tensor Sym^k V)
       =M^-(k+1) tensor Sym^(r-1)V.

Restoring the conormal twist and tensoring with `R^*` gives (2).
The characteristic-zero hypothesis makes the symmetric pairing perfect
without any factorial qualification. No assertion for arbitrary positive
characteristic is made here.

## 2. What vanishing cohomology actually constructs

Let `I=I_C` and fix `g>=2`. If

    H^1(C,Q_k)=0 for every 1<=k<g,                          (4)

the pure-power first relation can be lifted successively to a globally
patched ideal in `O_X/I^(r+g)`. On every affine normal chart this ideal has
the form

    (P, I^(r+g)),  P=m^r+terms of strictly higher normal order.   (5)

It therefore defines an actual finite-order thickening supported on `C`.
Its full initial ideal through degrees below `r+g` is generated by the
single moving relation `m^r`.

To justify the lifting assertion, start with the ideal determined by `R`
modulo `I^(r+1)`. Suppose local generators have been patched through degree
`r+k-1`. Choose arbitrary higher local lifts and higher lifts of their
gluing units. Their next discrepancies, modulo multiplication of `m^r` by
degree-`k` normal forms, are a Cech class in (1). All nonlinear normal
transition and base-coordinate motion are included in this discrepancy;
they are not frozen or omitted. If its cohomology class is zero, local
degree-`r+k` corrections remove it. The residual discrepancy belongs to
`R tensor Sym^k E` and is absorbed by changing the gluing unit. This produces
the next patched ideal. Repeating the construction proves (5).

For `C=P1` this can be performed on the two standard affine charts, so the
generator and its unit on their overlap give the patched ideal directly.
On each local normal chart, the first symbol is a nonzerodivisor in the
polynomial normal algebra. Consequently the actual initial ideal of the
principal generator is generated by that first symbol. Multiplication by
`m^r` has constant fiber rank, so every associated graded layer of (5) is
locally free on `C`. The resulting thickening is locally Cohen--Macaulay.
This argument does not assert that the thickening admits a global
retraction to `C`.

The ideal (5) contains the truncation ideal `I^(r+g)`, which generally has
many generators. It is not claimed to be a complete intersection. Its
existence says that this part of the normal obstruction tower cannot by
itself supply an STCI contradiction. Compatibility with a second relation,
the full socle budget, and projective equations remain separate conditions.

If all the groups in (4) vanish for every `k>=1`, one obtains compatible
formal lifts of the first relation at all orders. This is a statement in
the formal neighborhood of `C`; it does not assert algebraization to an
actual projective hypersurface.

## 3. A sharp cohomology threshold on a rational balanced curve

For `C=P1`, put `H=O(lambda)`, `M=O(-e)`, `e>=0`. Formula (2) becomes

    Q_k=O(q_k)^r,  q_k=(r+k+1)e-lambda*k.                 (6)

Therefore `H^1(Q_k)=0` exactly when `q_k>=-1`. If `e<lambda`, the numbers
`q_k` decrease with `k`, so all groups through a gap `g` vanish exactly when

    (r+g)e >= lambda*(g-1)-1.                             (7)

If `e>=lambda`, all the groups vanish at every order. The assertion here
is the exact vanishing threshold for these bundles, and the sufficient
lifting condition of Section 2. Below that threshold an actual obstruction
class may still vanish; a nonzero cohomology group is not an obstruction
by itself.

For `C0`, `lambda=7`. The quadratic step has

    Q_1=O((r+2)e-7)^r.

In particular:

- Every moving linear relation of degree `e>=2` has zero quadratic
  obstruction group.
- Every degree-one moving direction raised to a power `r>=4` has zero
  quadratic obstruction group.
- A degree-one moving direction raised to a power `r>=6g-8` has zero
  obstruction groups through all orders before a gap `g`.

For example, `e=1,r=4,g=2` gives an actual finite-order thickening of the
form `(P,I^6)` with first relation `m(z)^4`, for every basepoint-free moving
degree-one direction. This has fixed normal layers and no independent
relation through normal degree five. It supplies an explicit structural
counterboundary to a universal quadratic exclusion of moving first
relations, rather than a global STCI example.

The movement degree in the previously accepted gap-cost theorem is
`epsilon=r e`. Thus the constructed first-relation compatibility still
pays the accepted cost

    Delta >= r e*g(g+1)/2

when it occurs in a full thickening with the specified next-relation gap.
Vanishing obstruction groups do not remove that global cost.

## 4. The degree-one moving linear branch has an exact zero locus

The new independently derived calculation in

    f=(A+Cz)u+(B+Dz)v,  AD-BC != 0.

The correct operator retains the base derivative from the accepted

precisely at three diagonal points

    [A:B:C:D]=[t:0:0:1],
    t=2, (2+4 sqrt(-2))/3, (2-4 sqrt(-2))/3,

and on the open conic

    A=2D,  BC=8D^2,  D!=0.

The first diagonal point is the direction of the actual quadric
`q=x0*x3-x1*x2`, namely `q=u+(z/2)v` in the accepted normal chart.
The other points and the conic are newly identified quadratic-compatible
directions. They are not asserted to extend to the next normal order.
The cubic compatibility calculation is a separate live research task.

The actual equation `q^r` supplies all higher lifts of its own pure-power
first relation. Therefore even a full tower of first-relation tests needs

not directly exclude an entirely thick presentation having first symbol
`q^r`.

## 2026-10-14-generalization-moving-purepower-tower-independent-audit

# Moving pure-power relations: uniform obstruction bundles and finite-order lifts

Date: 2026-10-14. Status: **independently proved new finite-order lifting
theorem**, with the scope stated below. The accepted conormal identification
for the rational quartic is used as a premise. No accepted chart-transition,
normal-bundle, or constant-direction computation is repeated.

This note audits the proposed generalization of the next-order calculation in
[the moving linear relation review](2026-10-14-moving-linear-quadratic-obstruction-independent-audit.md).
The apolar quotient, the successive gluing obstruction, the vanishing range,
and the Cohen--Macaulay assertion all pass. The construction supplies embedded
finite-order thickenings; it does not supply a global carrier or an STCI pair.

## 1. Hypotheses and the precise truncation

Let `C` be a smooth curve embedded in a smooth threefold `X` over a field of
characteristic zero. Write `I=I_C` and suppose its actual conormal bundle is

    E=I/I^2=H^-1 tensor V,

where `H` is a line bundle and `V` is a constant two-dimensional vector space.
Let `M` be a line subbundle of `V tensor O_C`, and put

    W=H^-1 tensor M subset E,
    A=W^r subset Sym^r E,       r>=1.

The subbundle assumption means that the moving direction has no base points.
The initial relation is the pure power `m^r`; there is no divisor of zeros
attached to its saturation. Fix a desired truncation `N=r+g`, with `g>=1`.
The construction seeks an ideal sheaf `J_N` in the actual ambient threefold,
containing `I^N`, locally of the form

    J_N=(p,I^N),       initial(p)=m^r.

Its initial relation sheaves in every degree `j<N` must be precisely

    0                                      if j<r,
    W^r tensor Sym^(j-r)E                   if r<=j<N.

Thus the last prescribed principal-relation degree is `N-1=r+g-1`. There
is no asserted second independent relation before that degree. A new full
truncation relation appears in degree `N`.

## 2. The obstruction bundle in every order

For the passage from truncation `r+k` to truncation `r+k+1`, with `k>=1`, put

    B_k=Sym^(r+k)E / (W^r tensor Sym^k E),
    Q_k=Hom(W^r,B_k).

The multiplication image is a subbundle: locally a basis with the first
vector spanning `W` makes its image the span of the `k+1` monomials divisible
by the first vector to power `r`. Its cokernel therefore has rank `r` and
has no torsion.

Let `M_perp` be the annihilator line of `M` inside `V^* tensor O_C`. The
determinant sequence gives

    M_perp=M tensor det(V)^*.

Under the characteristic-zero perfect symmetric pairing, the annihilator of
`M^r Sym^k V` is exactly

    M_perp^(k+1) tensor Sym^(r-1)V^*.

To verify this equality, choose a fiber basis `(x,y)` with `x` spanning `M`.
The relation image has monomials `x^r` times every monomial of degree `k`.
Its annihilator consists of dual monomials with dual `y`-exponent at least
`k+1`. These are exactly `(y^*)^(k+1)` times all degree-`r-1` dual binary
forms. Both sides are rank-`r` bundles, so this fiber calculation proves the
bundle equality, rather than merely a pointwise dimension count.

Restoring the conormal twist and dualizing gives the canonical formula

    Q_k = H^-k tensor M^(-(r+k+1))
          tensor det(V)^(k+1) tensor Sym^(r-1)V.

The determinant factor is constant. Consequently the claimed formula
without this factor is correct after choosing a nonzero element of `det(V)`;
it is the same bundle up to a constant one-dimensional vector-space factor.
The coefficient `r+k+1` of the movement degree is essential.

## 3. Why H^1(Q_k) controls gluing ideals in the actual ambient space

The initial truncation exists without an obstruction: take the preimage of
`W^r` under `I^r -> I^r/I^(r+1)`. It is an ideal because multiplication by
`I` lands in `I^(r+1)`. This gives `J_(r+1)`.

Suppose `J_n`, with `n=r+k`, has already been constructed. On a suitable
open cover choose its local relation generators `p_i` and lift each to the
next ambient truncation. On overlaps there are units `a_ij` such that

    p_i-a_ij p_j belongs to I^n.

The degree-`n` discrepancy, normalized by the leading relation frame, defines
a section of `Hom(W^r,Sym^n E)`. Its image in `Q_k` is independent of the
choice of overlap unit, because a correction of degree `k` to that unit adds
exactly a multiple of the leading relation in `W^r Sym^k E`.

On a triple overlap the discrepancy is a multiple of `p_l`. Modulo
`I^(n+1)`, its degree-`n` part lies in `W^r Sym^k E`. Indeed, multiplication
by the leading form `m^r` is strict for the normal filtration: that form is
a nonzerodivisor in the local symmetric algebra. It follows that the images
of the discrepancies form a Cech 1-cocycle in `Q_k`.

If `H^1(Q_k)=0`, choose local degree-`n` corrections whose coboundary kills
this cocycle. The remaining discrepancies are in `W^r Sym^k E`. They are
absorbed by degree-`k` corrections to the overlap units, which remain units
since `k>=1`. The corrected local ideals `(p_i,I^(n+1))` therefore glue to
an actual ideal sheaf `J_(n+1)` on `X`.

Every step uses the canonical sheaves `I^n/I^(n+1)` and local choices of
generators. It assumes neither a global projection to `C` nor a global
splitting of the formal neighborhood. At each step, local lifting exists
simply by lifting the local ambient equation; the obstruction is its gluing.

This proves the following finite-order statement: if

    H^1(C,Q_k)=0 for every 1<=k<g,

there exists an embedded thickening `Y_N=V(J_N)` with `N=r+g` and precisely
the principal initial relation sheaves specified in Section 1. Vanishing
does not make the choices canonical; the corresponding `H^0(Q_k)` controls
available changes to each lift.

## 4. The initial algebra and Cohen--Macaulay property

Locally, the leading form of `p` is a nonzerodivisor in `Sym E`. Thus a
product with `p` has precisely the sum of its factors' normal orders.
Truncating by `I^N` gives

    gr_I(O_X/J_N)
      = Sym E / (W^r Sym E, all degrees >=N).

Every remaining homogeneous layer is locally free on `C`, because `W` is
a subbundle. This verifies the claimed initial relation sheaves and retains
the full torsion statement: there is no hidden torsion in any layer below
the truncation.

At a closed point of `C`, each nonzero layer has depth one as an ambient
module, since it is locally free over the smooth curve. Successive uses of
the depth lemma show that `O_X/J_N` has depth at least one. Its support has
dimension one, so `Y_N` is Cohen--Macaulay. This argument is intrinsic to
the filtration and does not need a retraction even for the flatness claim
one might otherwise make in local coordinates.

## 5. Uniform vanishing range on the rational quartic

On `C=P^1`, write `H=O(lambda)` and `M=O(-e)`, with `e>=0`. The constant
factor in Section 2 has dimension `r`, and hence

    Q_k=O(q_k)^r,
    q_k=(r+k+1)e-lambda k.

For the accepted rational-quartic conormal splitting, `lambda=7`. Since
`H^1(P^1,O(q))=0` exactly when `q>=-1`, every prescribed lift up to
truncation `r+g` is unobstructed whenever

    (r+k+1)e-7k>=-1 for every 1<=k<g.

If `e<7`, this affine function decreases with `k`, so the uniform condition
is exactly

    (r+g)e>=7(g-1)-1.

If `e>=7`, all these obstruction groups vanish for every finite `g`. The
case `g=1` has no lifting step and is automatically allowed. For `e=0`,
the formula gives `q_k=-7k`; no vanishing conclusion applies beyond the
initial truncation, in agreement with the accepted constant-direction
obstructions.

For example, a pure power moving with degree one has all prescribed steps
through gap `g` unobstructed whenever `r>=6g-8`. This extends the previously
reviewed one-step threshold `r>=4`. For degree two the corresponding
condition is `2r>=5g-8`. These are sufficient vanishing thresholds, not
necessary compatibility conditions: a nonzero obstruction group can still
contain a zero actual obstruction.

On a curve of positive genus the bundle formula remains valid, but the
`q>=-1` vanishing criterion is specific to `P^1`. The appropriate hypothesis
is the actual vanishing of each line-bundle `H^1` in Section 3.

## 6. Scope and consequences for the research frontier

This proves that finite-order normal compatibility cannot uniformly exclude
moving pure-power first relations in the vanishing range. For any fixed
finite gap, sufficiently large first relation order makes the entire tower
of these obstruction groups vanish, including the actual nonlinear ambient
transition effects encoded by the successive discrepancy cocycles.

The construction gives genuine embedded Cohen--Macaulay finite-order
thickenings, but it does not impose the full-algebra conditions of a complete
intersection presentation. In particular, if `r>1`, the generic top normal
layer of `Y_N` has dimension `r` and is annihilated by the maximal ideal of
the generic Artin algebra. Its socle therefore has dimension at least `r`;
this constructed truncated algebra cannot itself be a zero-dimensional
complete intersection. If `r=1`, it is curvilinear locally, but a global
STCI presentation is still not supplied.

No carrier degree, global equation degree, projective complete-intersection
pair, or algebraization of an infinite compatible tower is claimed. Even
when all finite steps have vanishing obstruction groups, the assertion here
is restricted to finite orders. To progress toward an STCI exclusion, one
must use a new independent relation, Gorenstein/socle constraints, actual
global equations, or another condition beyond this principal-relation tower.

## 7. Audit verdict

**Pass**, with the constant determinant factor made explicit and with the
truncation understood as `J_N=(p,I^N)` for `N=r+g`. The claimed uniform
obstruction bundle, vanishing inequality, induction in the actual ambient
space, exact initial relation sheaves, and Cohen--Macaulay conclusion are
proved above. The stronger global carrier/STCI conclusions are not part of
the theorem and do not follow from it.

## 2026-10-14-generalization-moving-purepower-second-relation-independent-audit

# Moving pure-power first relations: a uniform second-relation obstruction

Date: 2026-10-14. Status: **independently proved new obstruction and exact
degree identity** under the hypotheses stated below. The previously accepted
normal-layer defect convention and first-relation gap cost are premises; they
are not replayed here. The new apolar quotient is independently established in
[the finite-order tower audit](2026-10-14-generalization-moving-purepower-tower-independent-audit.md).

## 1. Bundle setting and the new second relation

Let `C` be a smooth integral projective curve in characteristic zero, with
balanced conormal bundle

    E=H^-1 tensor V,

where `V` is a constant two-dimensional vector space. Let

    M subset V tensor O_C,   deg M=-e<0,
    W=H^-1 tensor M,
    L=E/W=H^-1 tensor det(V) tensor M^-1.

Assume the first generic initial relation is the pure power `m^r`, with
`r>=2`. Let `s>r` be the next independent generic relation degree, and put
`g=s-r`. At the generic point, assume that this new binary form is coprime
to `m`; in particular this holds when the generic initial ideal is the
complete intersection `(m^r,g_s)`.

Write `K_s` for the actual degree-`s` relation kernel inside `Sym^s E`.
The saturated space of multiples of the first relation is

    A_prior=W^r tensor Sym^g E subset Sym^s E.

It is a rank-`g+1` subbundle. Define

    U_s=K_s intersection A_prior,
    T=K_s/U_s subset A_s,
    A_s=Sym^s E/A_prior.

The intersection and the quotient are taken as actual sheaves. The map to
`A_s` identifies `T` with a torsion-free rank-one subsheaf, so `T` is a line
bundle on the smooth curve. It need not be a subbundle at special points.
This definition does not identify a generic generator with a globally
saturated special-fiber generator.

The apolar quotient formula, including its constant factor, is

    A_s=H^-s tensor M^(-(g+1))
        tensor det(V)^(g+1) tensor Sym^(r-1)V.

Restriction to the quotient direction `E -> L` induces a canonical map

    A_s -> L^s.

The assumed generic coprimality makes its restriction to `T` nonzero.
Consequently there is an effective divisor `D` such that

    T=L^s(-D).

Here `D` is the actual zero divisor of this map, including any failure of the
subsheaf `T` to remain a subbundle. It is not discarded by saturation.

## 2. Uniform coprimality is impossible for a moving power of order at least two

Suppose the full initial algebra is a complete intersection of degrees
`r<s` on every fiber, with its first generator the line `W^r` and first
generator `m^r`. Then `T` is the second generator line and the map
`T -> L^s` is nowhere zero: a binary form is coprime to `m` exactly when
its restriction to `m=0` is nonzero. Thus `D=0` and `T=L^s`.

But the natural inclusion `T -> A_s` would then be a nonzero section of

    Hom(L^s,A_s)
      = M^(r-1) tensor det(V)^(1-r) tensor Sym^(r-1)V.

The final two factors are constant. Since `(r-1)deg M<0`, this bundle has
no global section on an integral projective curve. This is a contradiction.

Therefore a pure-power first relation of order `r>=2` in a genuinely moving
basepoint-free direction cannot extend to a fiberwise complete-intersection
initial algebra of two distinct generator degrees. The conclusion is
uniform in `r` and `s`. It does not exclude merely generic complete
intersection initial algebras with special-point defects, nor the linear
case `r=1`.

## 3. Generic coprimality forces an effective degeneration cost

In the generic situation of Section 1, the nonzero inclusion `T -> A_s`
instead defines a section of

    Hom(T,A_s)
      = M^(r-1)(D) tensor det(V)^(1-r) tensor Sym^(r-1)V.

A line bundle carrying a nonzero section has nonnegative degree. Hence,
writing `delta=deg D`,

    delta >= (r-1)e.

This includes subsheaf zeros and the special-point loss of coprimality in
one intrinsic divisor. No special fiber is assumed to retain its generic
minimal-generator presentation. If equality holds, the line bundle
`M^(r-1)(D)` is trivial; this follows because its nonzero section has an
effective zero divisor of degree zero. On `P^1`, its corresponding map into
the constant factor of `A_s` is a constant binary form of degree `r-1`.
Its evaluation divisor is the pullback of that form's root divisor by the
moving direction map. This equality description is not needed for the
inequality.

## 4. Exact degree-s defect, retaining the first relation's pole divisor

The first relation may itself have an effective defect divisor:

    K_r=W^r(-D0),       delta0=deg D0.

Multiplication of this actual relation by degree-`g` normal forms gives

    A_prior(-D0) subset U_s subset A_prior.

Both `U_s` and `A_prior` have rank `g+1`, so put

    tau=length(A_prior/U_s)>=0.

This retains the defect of the old relation multiples at the new-generator
degree. For example `D0=0` forces `tau=0`; in general
`tau<=(g+1)delta0`.

Let `lambda=deg H`. By definition of the accepted normal-layer defect,
with `rank K_s=g+2`,

    Delta_s=-deg K_s-lambda s(g+2).

The exact sequence `0 -> U_s -> K_s -> T -> 0` gives

    deg A_prior=-lambda s(g+1)-r e(g+1),
    deg U_s=deg A_prior-tau,
    deg T=-lambda s+s e-delta.

Adding these degrees yields the new exact identity

    Delta_s=e g(r-1)+delta+tau.

Consequently,

    Delta_s >= e(r-1)(g+1)+tau
             >= e(r-1)(g+1).

This calculation uses the actual degree-`s` kernel and the actual divisor
`D`. It does not silently replace either by its saturation. The identity
holds on any smooth projective support curve with the stated balanced
conormal and generic relation ranks.

## 5. Combination with the accepted earlier-gap cost

The accepted first-relation gap calculation has movement degree
`epsilon=r e` for the pure power. The sum of its defects in degrees
`r,...,s-1` is therefore at least

    r e g(g+1)/2+delta0.

The remaining normal-layer defects are nonnegative. Adding the new
degree-`s` bound gives

    Delta >= r e g(g+1)/2+delta0
             +e(r-1)(g+1)+tau.

In particular, omitting only the explicitly nonnegative `tau` proves the
proposed lower bound

    Delta >= r e g(g+1)/2+delta0+e(r-1)(g+1).

For the accepted rational-quartic exact socle budget
`n mu=7 Asym+2 Delta`, this entails the necessary condition

    n mu >= 7 Asym+r e g(g+1)+2delta0
            +2e(r-1)(g+1)+2tau.

It is a uniform obstruction cost, rather than a complete exclusion of all
moving generic initial complete intersections. The gap can be one; unlike
the separate constant-direction quadratic pole obstruction, no gap-at-least-
two hypothesis is needed here.

## 6. Audit verdict and frontier meaning

**Pass.** The new fiberwise exclusion, generic divisor bound, exact
`Delta_s` identity, and combination with the already accepted gap cost
follow from the quotient bundle and the actual sheaf degree sequences.

The first relation's divisor `D0`, the old-multiple defect `tau`, and the
evaluation divisor `D` are distinct and are all retained. The finite-order
existence theorem in the companion audit is compatible with this exclusion:
it adds a full truncation relation at its last degree, and for `r>1` its
top layer already has socle dimension at least `r`. A future complete-
intersection second relation must pay the new divisor cost; it cannot be
inserted with fiberwise coprimality and zero defects.

# Root-curve descent and the geometry of moving binary relations

Date: 2026-10-14. Status: **new structural theorem, independently reviewed**.
This note generalizes the relation-bundle viewpoint, rather
than enumerating further carrier degrees. The accepted moving-power
second-relation obstruction and its independent audit are premises. No
accepted calculation is repeated.

## 1. The root divisor remembers the entire first binary relation

Let `C` be a smooth integral projective curve, and let the actual conormal
bundle in a smooth threefold be

    E=H^-1 tensor V,             dim V=2,

where `V` is constant. Suppose the first saturated generic initial relation
is a line subbundle

    R=H^-r tensor M subset Sym^r E,        r>=2.

Thus `M subset Sym^r V tensor O_C` is a line subbundle. Fixing the constant
determinant factors does not affect the following statements. Put
`P=C x P(V^*)`, with projections `p` and `q`. The universal binary form
defines a divisor

    Z=V(f) subset P,
    O_P(Z)=p^*M^-1 tensor q^*O(r).

The subbundle condition says that no fiber polynomial is zero. Hence
`pi=p|Z` is finite flat of degree `r`, including at repeated roots. No
reducedness or irreducibility of `Z` is assumed in the general theorem.

For `j>=r-1`, pushing forward the divisor sequence gives

    0 -> M tensor Sym^(j-r)V
      -> Sym^j V tensor O_C
      -> pi_*O_Z(j) -> 0,                              (1)

where the first term is zero for `j=r-1`. The vanishing needed here is
`H^1(P1,O(j-r))=0`. In particular the saturated quotient by the first
relation at a later degree `s>r` is

    A_s=Sym^s E/(R Sym^(s-r)E)
       =H^-s tensor pi_*O_Z(s).                        (2)

The notation `O_Z(j)` always means restriction of the **direction** ruling
`q^*O(j)`, not the base ruling.

## 2. Exact criterion for a second fiberwise coprime relation

Let `T` be a line bundle on `C`. By adjunction, a morphism

    T -> A_s

is the same as a section of the line bundle

    O_Z(s) tensor pi^*(H^-s tensor T^-1).               (3)

In each fiber, the proposed second binary relation is coprime to the first
one precisely when this section is a unit on the finite root scheme. This
statement includes a repeated root with its full scheme structure: an
element of its Artin local algebra is a unit exactly when its value on the
reduced root is nonzero. Consequently the second relation is coprime in
**every** fiber if and only if (3) has a nowhere-vanishing section, or
equivalently

    O_Z(s) is isomorphic to pi^*N,
    N=H^s tensor T.                                    (4)

This is both necessary and sufficient for a full **graded** family. Given
an isomorphism in (4), it gives a line `T subset A_s` whose restriction is
coprime on every fiber. In the graded algebra `Sym E/(R)`, take the ideal
generated by this line in degree `s`, and take its inverse image in
`Sym E`. Locally on the base the quotient line lifts to a binary form, so
the inverse image is locally generated by a regular sequence `(f_r,g_s)`.
Every fiber is a graded Artin complete intersection of degrees `(r,s)`.
The Hilbert function is constant, and the graded layers are locally free
over `C`.

It is essential that the second generator lives **modulo multiples of the
first generator**. A global lift `T -> Sym^s E` is not needed. Such a lift
can be impossible even when (4) holds. The graded family constructed here
also has not been lifted into the actual formal neighborhood of `C`.

## 3. The criterion becomes a precise torsion problem on a rational base

Now let `C=P1`, `M=O(-epsilon)`, with `epsilon>=0`. The root divisor has
bidegree `(epsilon,r)`, where the first entry is the base degree. Its
direction ruling has degree `epsilon`, and its base ruling has degree
`r`. If (4) holds, taking total degrees on `Z` gives

    r deg N=s epsilon.                                 (5)

Put

    d=gcd(r,epsilon),     r0=r/d,     epsilon0=epsilon/d,
    L=O_Z(r0) tensor pi^*O(-epsilon0).

The convention `gcd(r,0)=r` covers the constant relation case. Condition
(4) is equivalent to

    r0 divides s,     L^(s/r0) is trivial in Pic(Z).      (6)

Indeed the only possible descended bundle in (5) is
`N=O(s epsilon/r)`. Thus, if `L` has finite order `t`, the possible later
degrees are exactly the positive multiples of `r0*t`, subject to `s>r`.
If `L` has infinite order, no such uniform graded second relation exists.
This uses `Pic(Z)`, including all nilpotent information when `Z` is
nonreduced. It does not replace the root scheme by its support.

For `epsilon>0`, any such `N` has positive degree, whereas `Sym^s V` is
constant. Thus

    Hom_C(N,Sym^s V tensor O_C)=0.

The second generator line in (4) cannot lift globally to `Sym^s E`.
The generator extension is necessarily nonsplit. Discarding that
extension would incorrectly exclude the valid graded families supplied
by (6).

The previously proved pure-power obstruction is consistent with (6).
For a moving power, `Z` is a nonreduced multiple of its moving graph.
The obstruction lies in the full nonreduced Picard group. Passing merely
to the graph loses the decisive information. General root curves need
not have the same obstruction.

## 4. A smooth elliptic root curve defeats a naive pure-power extension

Let `B` be an elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero. Choose a line bundle `A` of degree two and a
nontrivial torsion line bundle `eta` of exact order `m>2`. Put
`D=A tensor eta`. Both complete degree-two systems define maps to `P1`.
Their product embeds `B` in `P1_base x P1_direction`.

For completeness, the product map has degree dividing two onto its image.
If it had degree two, its image would project with degree one to each
factor, so would be a `(1,1)` graph and would imply `A=D`. This contradicts
`eta!=O`. Thus the map is birational onto its integral `(2,2)` image. That
image has arithmetic genus one; its smooth normalization also has genus
one. The normalization quotient therefore has length zero, so the image
is smooth and the map is an isomorphism.

Take this image as `Z`. Then

    O_Z(1)=D,     pi^*O_base(1)=A,
    O_Z(-1,1)=eta.

Here `r=epsilon=2`, and (6) says exactly `m divides s`. Taking `s=m`
gives a graded complete-intersection family of degrees `(2,m)` with a
moving first binary relation and no loss of coprimality at any fiber.
The order `m` is arbitrary. Thus the exclusion for a moving pure power
cannot be extended to an arbitrary moving binary relation.

This is a structural counterboundary, **not** an STCI example. On `C0`,
where `H=O(7)`, this graded algebra has length `2m`, top degree `m`, and
socle line `O(-6m-2)`. The general socle formula is

    Soc=R tensor T tensor (det E)^-1.

Its layers are Gorenstein symmetric, and its full normal-layer defect is
`Delta=m(m-2)`. In particular, some members can even match the accepted
numerical socle constraints for projective complete intersections. The
missing condition is realization in the actual ambient neighborhood and
then by two projective equations; a graded construction alone supplies
neither.

## 5. The actual ambient obstruction lives naturally on the root curve

Suppose no independent relation has yet appeared by degree `r+k`, with
`k>=1`. The first-relation correction bundle is

    Q_k=Hom(R, Sym^(r+k)E/(R Sym^k E))
       =pi_*[O_Z(r+k) tensor pi^*(H^-k tensor M^-1)].   (7)

The identity follows directly from (1), the projection formula, and the
definition of `R`. Finite pushforward gives the exact cohomology identity

    H^1(C,Q_k)
      =H^1(Z,O_Z(r+k) tensor pi^*(H^-k tensor M^-1)).    (8)

There is a geometric interpretation. Blow up the actual threefold along
`C`. Its exceptional divisor is `P=C x P1`, and its conormal bundle is

    O_P(-Exceptional)=p^*H^-1 tensor q^*O(1).

Since `Z` is a Cartier divisor in `P`,

    N_(Z/P)=O_Z(r) tensor pi^*M^-1.

The line bundle in (8) is consequently

    N_(Z/P) tensor (O_P(-Exceptional)|Z)^k.              (9)

It is the obstruction bundle for extending the Cartier divisor `Z` into
successive neighborhoods of the exceptional divisor. In coordinates, the
first class is the restriction to `f=0` of the nonlinear ambient normal
transition applied to `f`. The differential operator includes the change
of the base coordinate as well as the quadratic change of the normal
coordinates. A pointwise evaluation of a constant-direction obstruction
would omit the base derivative and is not this class.

For a smooth root curve of bidegree `(epsilon,r)`, its genus and the degree
of the line bundle in (8) are

    genus(Z)=(epsilon-1)(r-1),
    degree=2r epsilon+k(epsilon-r lambda),

where `H=O(lambda)`. For the elliptic `(2,2)` example on `C0`, the first
obstruction space is

    H^1(Z,O_Z(3) tensor pi^*O(-5)).

This line bundle has degree `-4`; the obstruction space therefore has
dimension four. A nonzero obstruction group does not show that the actual
ambient class is nonzero. Conversely, torsion of the ruling difference
does not make this class vanish.

Adjunction also gives a uniform dual description:

    omega_Z tensor L_k^-1
      =O_Z(-k-2) tensor pi^*(omega_C tensor H^k),

where `L_k` is the line bundle in (8). The first relation's coefficient
bundle `M` cancels from the formula. Its geometry still enters through
the actual root divisor `Z`; the cancellation does not make the
obstruction independent of the first relation.

The first class has a coordinate-free description. Write
`J=O_P(-Exceptional)`. The actual first neighborhood of `P` in the blowup
has a square-zero extension class

    kappa in Ext^1_P(Omega_P,J).

Restricting to `Z` and pulling back along its conormal differential
`df:O_Z(-Z)->Omega_P|Z` produces the first obstruction in
`H^1(Z,N_(Z/P) tensor J|Z)`. In local splittings of the square-zero
extension, their differences are derivations; applying those derivations
to local equations `f` and restricting to `Z` gives exactly this cocycle.
This also proves that the base-coordinate derivative must be included.

For `C0`, the two tangent directions of `P=P1 x P1` decompose the
ambient class into

    H^1(P,O(-5,1)) and H^1(P,O(-7,3)).

For a smooth root divisor of bidegree `(2,2)`, the divisor exact sequence
identifies the four-dimensional target as

    H^1(Z,L_1)
      =coker[ H^1(P,O(-7,1)) --multiply f-->
                                      H^1(P,O(-5,3)) ].

The two spaces have dimensions twelve and sixteen, and the map is
injective because `H^0(Z,L_1)=0`. This gives a small intrinsic obstruction
incidence for the entire moving quadratic family, rather than one
calculation for each projective degree.

This identifies the next broad research problem: relate the ambient
formal-neighborhood extension class of `Z` to the torsion-descent condition
(6), while retaining the second-generator extension. Solving that bridge
would impose geometry beyond all graded Hilbert-function and socle-degree
tests at once.

## 2026-10-14-generalization-spectral-root-curve-independent-audit

# Moving first relations: spectral descent criterion and elliptic gap examples

Date: 2026-10-14. Author: `moving_second_general/spectral_audit`.
Status: **NEW PROOF, independently derived for the parent moving-relation lane.**
No inherited pure-power, tower, or normal-carrier certificate was rerun.
The statement being audited is [Root-curve descent and the geometry of
moving binary relations](2026-10-14-generalization-spectral-root-curve-relations.md).

This note proves a criterion for an abstract graded algebra on a curve.
It does not construct a multiple structure in the actual formal neighborhood
of `C0`, and it does not produce a set-theoretic complete intersection in
projective space. In particular, a second generator in the quotient by the
first relation must be distinguished from a global line of polynomials.

## 1. Setup and the root divisor

Let `C` be a smooth connected projective curve over an algebraically closed
field, let `V` be a fixed two-dimensional vector space, and put

    E = H^(-1) tensor V,       S = Sym(E).

Suppose a saturated first relation is the line subbundle

    R = H^(-r) tensor M  ->  Sym^r(E),
    M -> Sym^r(V),             r >= 1.

Here subbundle means that the map is nonzero in every fiber; equivalently,
its cokernel is locally free. Write `deg(M)=-epsilon`.
Use the convention that the second factor in

    P = C x P(V*),       p:P->C,       q:P->P(V*)

has `H^0(O(1))=V`. The relation is a section

    f in H^0(P, p^*M^(-1) tensor q^*O(r)).

Its zero scheme `Z` is an effective Cartier divisor, finite flat of degree
`r` over `C`: no fiber of `p` is a component, and every fiber divisor has
length `r`. This assertion includes nonreduced root divisors. Denote the
finite flat projection by `pi:Z->C`, and set

    N = q^*O(1)|Z.

For `s>=r-1`, pushing forward the divisor sequence, with the usual
cohomology of `O(s-r)` on `P1`, gives an exact sequence

    0 -> M tensor Sym^(s-r)V -> Sym^s V -> pi_*N^s -> 0,       (1)

where the left term is zero when `s=r-1`. Consequently, for the degree-`s`
part of the quotient by the first relation,

    A = S/(R),
    A_s = H^(-s) tensor pi_*N^s,                s>=r-1.        (2)

The order convention in (1) makes the sign of the later descent degree
unambiguous.

## 2. Exact criterion for a coprime quotient relation

Fix `s>r` and a line bundle `T` on `C`. By finite adjunction and (2), a
map `T->A_s` is equivalent to a section

    sigma in H^0(Z, N^s tensor pi^*(H^(-s) tensor T^(-1))).   (3)

At a point `c in C`, choose local trivializations and a polynomial lift
`g` of this quotient relation. The following are equivalent:

1. `f_c` and `g_c` have no common projective root, including scheme
   structure at multiple roots of `f_c`.
2. The restriction of `g_c` to the finite scheme `Z_c` is a unit in its
   corresponding invertible module.
3. `sigma` is nonvanishing at every point of `Z_c`.

For a local Artinian ring an element is a unit exactly when its residue
at its closed point is nonzero, so this equivalence retains the full
nonreduced root scheme. It is unaffected by changing `g` by a multiple of
`f`.

It follows that a fiberwise coprime quotient relation `T->A_s` exists if
and only if

    N^s tensor pi^*(H^(-s) tensor T^(-1)) is trivial,

or, equivalently,

    N^s = pi^*(H^s tensor T) in Pic(Z).                      (4)

A trivialization gives such a map, and its image is a subbundle of `A_s`:
on every base fiber the adjoint map is nonzero, hence the cokernel of the
map from a line bundle to a vector bundle is locally free.

Allowing `T` to vary, the exact obstruction is the class

    alpha = [N] in Pic(Z)/pi^*Pic(C).

There is a coprime second quotient generator in degree `s` precisely when
`s alpha=0`. If `alpha` has finite order `m`, the possible degrees are
exactly the multiples of `m` that exceed `r`. If `alpha` has infinite
order, no such degree exists. This is a Picard-group obstruction, not
merely a numerical degree obstruction.

The norm provides a useful necessary condition. For this root divisor,
the determinant of (1), together with its degree-zero analogue, gives

    Norm_pi(N) = M^(-1),

up to a fixed one-dimensional `k`-vector space, which is a trivial line
bundle on `C`. Taking norms in (4) therefore gives

    (H^s tensor T)^r = M^(-s).                              (5)

The norm identity `Norm_pi(pi^*D)=D^r` for finite locally free maps is
also recorded in [Stacks, Norms, Lemmas 31.18.2 and
31.18.6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BCX).
Equation (5) is necessary; it does not replace (4).

## 3. On `P1`: the sign and the global-lift obstruction

Now take `C=P1` and `deg H=h`. Since `Z` has bidegree
`(epsilon,r)` in `P1 x P1`,

    deg N = epsilon,       deg(pi^*O(d)) = r d.

Thus (4) forces

    H^s tensor T = O(d),
    d = s epsilon/r,
    deg T = -s h + s epsilon/r.                            (6)

In particular `r` must divide `s epsilon`. The sign of `d` is positive
when the first relation moves (`epsilon>0`). This numerical divisibility
is only necessary; (4) also controls a degree-zero class on `Z`.

The exact sequence of degree-`s` relations of the resulting ideal is

    0 -> R tensor Sym^(s-r)E -> I_s -> T -> 0.              (7)

A global second polynomial line would be a lift `T->I_s->Sym^sE`.
Such a lift exists exactly when (7) splits. For `epsilon>0`, (6) shows
that a lift is impossible:

    Hom(T,Sym^sE)
      = Hom(O(s epsilon/r), Sym^sV tensor O)
      = 0.                                                (8)

Thus a moving first relation and a fiberwise coprime quotient second
relation necessarily have a nonsplit relation extension. An argument
that treats `T` as a subline of `Sym^sE` would discard these families.

Nevertheless, (4) does define a global graded ideal. Form in `A` the
ideal generated by the quotient line `T->A_s`, and take its inverse image
in `S`. Locally on `C` the map lifts to a polynomial `g`, so this ideal
is `(f,g)` locally; on overlaps two such lifts differ by a multiple of
`f`. Every fiber is a homogeneous complete intersection of degrees
`r,s`. The graded quotient is finite locally free of rank `rs`, with
the standard complete-intersection Hilbert function. This follows also
from the local Koszul resolution and fiberwise regularity. No assertion
of a global split two-generator resolution is needed.

## 4. Elliptic `(2,2)` examples with arbitrary second degree

Work in characteristic zero. Fix any integer `m>2`. Choose a smooth
elliptic curve `E0`, a line bundle `A0` of degree two, and a line bundle
`Q` of exact order `m` in `Pic^0(E0)`. Put

    B0 = A0 tensor Q.

Such `Q` exists because, over an algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero, the elliptic `m`-torsion is `(Z/mZ)^2`.
For the standard degree/separability input see [MIT 18.783, Lecture 6,
multiplication and torsion](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2022/LectureSlides6.pdf).
The construction can also be made in characteristic `p` for `m` prime
to `p`; the characteristic-zero version suffices here.

Riemann--Roch gives `h^0(A0)=h^0(B0)=2`, and both line bundles are
base-point-free: after removing a putative base point, a degree-one line
bundle would still have two sections, contrary to Riemann--Roch on a
genus-one curve. This is also the degree-`2g` global-generation case in
[Stacks, Lemma 53.22.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0E3C).
Their complete linear systems give two degree-two maps and hence

    j:E0 -> P1 x P1.

Here is a proof that `j` is a closed immersion, rather than an assumption
about a generic pair. The degree of `E0` over its image divides two. If
it were two, both image projections would have degree one, so the image
would be a `(1,1)` graph and `A0=B0`, contradicting `Q!=O`. Thus `j` is
finite birational onto an integral `(2,2)` curve. A `(2,2)` curve has
arithmetic genus `(2-1)(2-1)=1`, while its normalization has genus one.
The normalization exact sequence therefore has zero quotient, proving
that the image is normal and `j` is an isomorphism onto that smooth
image. In particular the embedded curve is a smooth root divisor `Z`
with

    r=2,       epsilon=2,
    pi^*O(1)=A0,       N=B0.

For any `s>2`, descent of `N^s` to the first `P1` forces its descended
degree to be `s`. Hence

    N^s in pi^*Pic(P1)
      iff B0^s = A0^s
      iff Q^s = O
      iff m divides s.                                    (9)

The first possible coprime second quotient relation is therefore exactly
degree `m`. Choosing a trivialization for `s=m` and applying section 3
gives a finite locally free graded algebra with fiber type `(2,m)`.
The first relation has fixed order two and moving degree two, while the
gap `m-2` is arbitrarily large. These examples demonstrate that no bound
on the second degree can follow from `r` and `epsilon` alone.

## 5. Exact `C0` numerical boundary for these abstract families

Take `H=O(7)` on `P1`, as in the accepted balanced conormal bundle of
`C0`. In the elliptic construction at second degree `s=m`,

    E=O(-7)^2,      R=O(-16),      T=O(-6m),
    n=2m,          top graded degree=m.

The relative Artin complete-intersection socle line is

    Soc = R tensor T tensor det(E)^(-1) = O(-6m-2).        (10)

This formula is valid with the nonsplit relation extension (7): local
changes of the second generator by multiples of the first do not change
the determinant of the relation generators, and the local Koszul socle
identifications glue.

The Hilbert function is

    h_0=h_m=1,       h_j=2 for 1<=j<=m-1,

so its first moment is `m^2` and the accepted asymmetry is zero. The
normal-layer defects can be computed directly. For `2<=j<m`, sequence
(1) gives the normalized degree `2(j-1)` in rank two. In the top degree,
(10) gives defect `m-2`. The total is therefore

    Delta = 2 sum_(j=2)^(m-1)(j-1) + (m-2)
          = m(m-2).                                      (11)

Were this abstract algebra the associated graded algebra of an actual
complete intersection of degrees `a,b` supported on `C0`, its length and
socle would require

    ab=8m,
    14-4(a+b)=-6m-2,
    equivalently (3a-16)(3b-16)=64.                        (12)

For integers `a,b>=5`, the only solutions are

    (a,b,m)=(8,8,8), (6,16,12), (16,6,12).                (13)

Indeed the two factors in (12) are each congruent to two modulo three;
among positive factor pairs of 64 this leaves `(8,8)` and `(2,32)` and
their interchange. The negative-factor possibility with `a,b>=5` gives
only `(-1,-1)`, whose product is not 64.

For (13), the accepted full socle budget is satisfied exactly:

    mu=7m+14-4(a+b)=m-2,
    n mu=2m(m-2)=2 Delta,
    Asym=0.

Thus the full scalar socle budget and the abstract graded-CI condition
alone do not remove these two unordered degree pairs. Their existence
as abstract algebras is a boundary for that method. Realization in the
actual normal-neighborhood transitions, and then by two projective
equations, remain entirely unproved and may be obstructed.

## 6. The actual ambient lifting problem has a spectral obstruction group

The abstract examples identify an obstruction that the next step must use:
compatibility with the actual formal-neighborhood transitions. Here is
the target group for that problem, with the distinction between the
group and its actual obstruction class retained.

Fix local lifts of the first relation and suppose they have been made
compatible through normal degree `r+k-1`. Suppose also that the next
independent relation has degree `s>r+k`. In normal degree `r+k`, the
remaining mismatch, modulo multiples of the first relation, is a
1-cocycle with coefficients in

    Hom(R,A_(r+k))
      = pi_*(N^(r+k) tensor pi^*(H^(-k) tensor M^(-1))).

Changing local lifts changes the cocycle by a coboundary. Thus the
order-`k` first-relation obstruction lies in

    H^1(Z,L_k),
    L_k = N^(r+k) tensor pi^*(H^(-k) tensor M^(-1)).       (14)

Vanishing of this particular class is equivalent to correcting this
one order, once the lower-order choices have been fixed. Formula (14)
is a linearized lifting statement, not a claim that all such classes
occur for the fixed embedding of `C0`. At and above degree `s`, the
second relation changes the quotient target and must be included.

For `C=P1`, `deg H=h`, its degree is

    deg L_k = (2r+k) epsilon - r k h.                     (15)

Adjunction for the Cartier divisor `Z` gives the useful dual expression

    omega_Z = N^(r-2) tensor pi^*(omega_C tensor M^(-1)),
    omega_Z tensor L_k^(-1)
      = N^(-k-2) tensor pi^*(omega_C tensor H^k).         (16)

In the elliptic example with `r=epsilon=2`, `h=7`, and `k=1`,

    L_1 = N^3 tensor pi^*O(-5),
    deg L_1=-4,          h^0(L_1)=0,          h^1(L_1)=4.  (17)

There are four obstruction coordinates, and no global first-order
correction once a particular lower lift has been fixed. The existence
of this four-dimensional group does not say that the actual obstruction
class is nonzero. Conversely, the abstract algebra construction of
sections 3--5 does not force this class to vanish.

The contribution of the quadratic normal-coordinate transition has a
geometric factorization. Assume here that `Z` is smooth and `pi` is
generically separable. Restricting the relative derivative of `f` to `Z`
gives a section

    rho in H^0(Z,N^(r-2) tensor pi^*M^(-1)),              (18)

whose zero divisor is the ramification divisor of `pi`. A quadratic
normal vector field, modulo its radial component, gives a cubic
projective displacement. After restriction to `Z`, its coefficient is a
section of `N^3 tensor pi^*H^(-1)`. Applying the derivative to `f` is
multiplication by `rho`, producing a section of `L_1` exactly as in
(14). In binary coordinates this is the familiar factorization through
the cubic `x delta(y)-y delta(x)`; the radial part is a multiple of `f`
and vanishes in the quotient.

For a moving relation, normal-coordinate changes are not the entire
ambient cocycle. Changes in the base coordinate contribute derivatives
of the coefficient functions of `f`, and previous correction terms
contribute at later orders. Those terms must be retained in the actual
class in (14). The factorization (18) describes the pure normal
quadratic contribution only. For a nonreduced root divisor, it is not
permissible to treat `rho` as a nonzero divisor or replace the full
scheme by its reduced support.

This supplies a structural route forward: compute the actual class in
(14), or its Serre-dual pairing using (16), on the root curve. Counting
the dimension of its target, or using only the numerical defect, cannot
settle the lifting problem. The smooth elliptic examples have generically
two distinct root directions, so they also show why a theorem for a
moving pure power does not automatically cover an arbitrary moving
first relation.

## 7. Coordinate-free first class on the blowup

The owner's blowup interpretation gives a precise way to name that
actual first class. Let `X` be the actual smooth ambient threefold,
`Xtilde=Bl_C X`, and identify its exceptional divisor with
`P=C x P(V*)`. The conormal line of this divisor is

    J=O_P(-Exceptional)=p^*H^(-1) tensor q^*O(1).

This sign follows from the tautological normal line:
`N_(P/Xtilde)=p^*H tensor q^*O(-1)`. The square-zero first neighborhood
of `P` has an extension class

    kappa in Ext^1_P(Omega_P,J).

Since `P` is smooth, this is `H^1(P,T_P tensor J)`. Restrict this
extension to `Z` and pull it back along the conormal differential

    df:O_Z(-Z) -> Omega_P|Z.

The resulting class lies in

    Ext^1_Z(O_Z(-Z),J|Z)
      = H^1(Z,O_Z(Z) tensor J|Z)
      = H^1(Z,L_1).                                    (19)

It is the obstruction to extending the Cartier divisor `Z` into the
first neighborhood of `P`. A local equation proof identifies it with
the mismatch in section 6: lifting each local equation for `Z` and
comparing on overlaps applies the transition derivation to that
equation. Changing its lift changes only a coboundary. Consequently
(19) is the actual geometric class, rather than an unspecified element
of a group of the right dimension. It still needs to be evaluated for
the fixed ambient embedding.

For `C=P1` and `H=O(7)`, the product tangent splitting gives

    kappa_base in H^1(P,O(-5,1)),       dimension 8,
    kappa_direction in H^1(P,O(-7,3)),  dimension 24.     (20)

Both components contribute through `df`; retaining only the direction
component omits the derivative of a moving relation in the base.

For a smooth `(2,2)` root divisor, `L_1=O_Z(-5,3)` and its divisor
sequence is

    0 -> O_P(-7,1) --f--> O_P(-5,3) -> L_1 -> 0.

Both ambient `H^0` groups vanish, `H^2(P,O(-7,1))=0`, and
`H^0(Z,L_1)=0` by its negative degree. Thus there is an exact sequence

    0 -> H^1(P,O(-7,1)) --f--> H^1(P,O(-5,3))
      -> H^1(Z,L_1) -> 0,

with dimensions `12 -> 16 -> 4`. In particular, the four-dimensional
obstruction space in (17) is exactly this quotient. The middle
cohomology class, taken modulo the image under multiplication by `f`,
must be built from the actual `kappa` and `df`. The quotient description
does not itself say that the class vanishes or is nonzero.

## Audit conclusion

The spectral criterion (4), the positive sign in (6), the mandatory
nonsplitting (8), the arbitrary elliptic gap construction (9), and the
numerical boundary (13) all pass this independent derivation. The
criterion concerns a second relation modulo the first. It must not be
reported as the existence of two global polynomial line subbundles or
as an ambient STCI construction. The spectral obstruction bundle
