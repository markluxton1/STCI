# Local leading ideals and global normal-symbol lifting

For the fixed quartic $C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3$ over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the generic local
optimization is settled. The minimum excess is the length defect in the
Goto–Heinzer–Kim formula for a normalized generating pair. The unresolved
comparison is whether prescribed local cancellations can be realized by two
actual homogeneous equations of fixed degrees, with compatible jets along
the whole curve. The cubic-jet test on the conditional two-node $(6,16)$
stratum isolates one such question.

## A. Established mathematics

Write $K=k(C)$, $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3,\eta_C}$, and let $J\subset R$
be an $\mathfrak m$-primary ideal generated by two elements. In the chart
$x_0=1$, use $z=x_1/x_0$ and subtract $z^3,z^4$ from $x_2/x_0,x_3/x_0$.
The resulting parameters $u,v$ identify $R$ with $K[u,v]_{(u,v)}$.
Its residue field is infinite. Put $S=\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}R\simeq K[u,v]$,
$J^*=\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak m}(J)$, and
$m=\ell_R(R/J)$.

- **GHK Part I, J. Algebra 298 (2006), 238–247.** In a Noetherian local
  ring with factorial associated graded ring, take a two-element regular
  sequence with normalized orders $a\le b$ and $f^*\nmid g^*$. Lemma 2.1
  identifies the first possible cancellation from the primitive syzygy
  $(-g^*/D,f^*/D)$, where $D=\gcd(f^*,g^*)$. Proposition 2.2 constructs a
  cancellation producing a new initial generator in the complete local ring.
  Proposition 2.4 identifies its least order and the resulting
  three-generator prefix for fixed $f,g$. Theorem 1.2 shows that a
  three-generated full leading ideal is perfect. [Part I](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~heinzer/preprints/GHK2.pdf)
- **GHK Part II, J. Algebra 312 (2007), 709–732.** For a parameter ideal
  in a two-dimensional regular local ring with infinite residue field,
  Theorems 1.2–1.3 and Lemma 3.2 give minimal initial generators $\xi_i$
  starting with the normalized pair. Put $c_i=\deg\xi_i$,
  $D_i=\gcd(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_i)$ and $d_i=\deg D_i$. Then
  $c_1=a$, $c_2=b$, $a=d_1>\cdots>d_n=0$, and
  $c_{i+1}-c_i>d_{i-1}-d_i$ for $2\le i<n$. The prefixes from $i=2$
  onward are intrinsic degree truncations of the leading ideal. If $c_1=c_2$,
  the first generator line can vary while $d_1=c_1$ is fixed. The Hilbert
  series is

  $$
  H_{S/J^*}(t)=
  \frac{\sum_{i=2}^{n}t^{d_i}(1-t^{d_{i-1}-d_i})(1-t^{c_i-d_i})}{(1-t)^2}.
  $$

  The Hilbert function rises as $1,2,\ldots,a$, then descends to zero by
  steps of size zero or one. In higher dimension Theorem 1.6 uses the
  additional hypothesis that the tangent cone is Cohen–Macaulay.
  [Part II, Setting 1.1 and §§3–4](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~heinzer/preprints/6ghk2.pdf)
- **GHK’s two-dimensional realization paper, Comm. Algebra 36 (2008),
  1901–1910.** Theorem 2.3 constructs a parameter ideal in the given
  two-dimensional regular local ring with infinite residue field for each
  admissible numerical $(c_i,d_i)$. The proof uses parameter choices and the
  transformations of Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2. It applies in any
  characteristic and does not require algebraic closure or a coefficient
  field. The theorem prescribes the numerical sequences.
  [Two-dimensional paper, §2](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~heinzer/preprints/7ghk91.pdf)
- **A specified graded ideal.** Mandal–Rossi, *Acta Math. Vietnam.* 40
  (2015), 85–100, Theorems 2.1–2.2 and Remark 2.3 recover the generator
  and syzygy shifts from the Hilbert function of a two-dimensional local CI.
  The syzygy shifts are $e_i=c_i+d_{i-1}-d_i$, with
  $c_i<e_i<c_{i+1}$ for $2\le i<n$ and $c_n<e_n$. Conversely, a
  finite-colength homogeneous ideal in $K[u,v]$ with these shifts is itself
  realizable as a leading ideal by the filtered Hilbert–Burch construction.
  Add units in the matrix positions from $e_i$ to $c_{i+1}$. Each affected
  term has higher filtration degree, so the maximal minors retain their
  initial forms. The matrix columns lift the graded syzygies, and the
  unit block reduces the local presentation to two generators. This is a
  direct application of the method in Rossi–Sharifan. GHK credit
  Iarrobino’s *Tangent cone of a Gorenstein singularity* (1986),
  Theorem 2.2.A, with the fixed-graded-ideal characterization. The audit
  verified this attribution in GHK. Iarrobino’s original statement and
  field hypotheses were inaccessible.
  [Mandal–Rossi, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2756) ·
  [Rossi–Sharifan, Theorem 2.2 and Remark 4.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.1086)
- **Filtered lifting and the normal cone.** Valabrega–Valla,
  *Nagoya Math. J.* 72 (1978), 93–101, Theorem 1.1 gives the strict
  filtration criterion for generators of a form ideal. Proposition 2.1
  gives it for regular initial forms, and Corollary 2.4 lifts a regular
  initial generating sequence in the local setting. Rossi–Sharifan,
  *Proc. AMS* 138 (2010), Theorems 2.2–2.3 characterize a standard basis
  by lifting all graded first syzygies and lift a graded resolution to a
  filtered resolution of an already given module with a good filtration over
  a regular local ring with infinite residue field. Their Theorem 3.1 and
  Sammartano’s Theorem 2 and Remark 3 describe consecutive Betti
  cancellations and their spectral-sequence pages. The normal-cone
  construction starts from a given embedding. For a regular immersion the
  conormal symmetric algebra is the ambient normal algebra.
  [Valabrega–Valla](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/F91724E40BE6AE7101C7A959AFC3DA05/S0027763000018225a.pdf/form-rings-and-regular-sequences-1.pdf) ·
  [Rossi–Sharifan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.1086) ·
  [Sammartano](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.05220) ·
  [Stacks, Lemma 31.22.5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/063M)

The full text of **GHK Part III, Comm. Algebra 40 (2012), 2523–2539**,
was inaccessible in the audit. The publisher describes criteria for a
perfect height-two prefix of the leading ideal of a fixed CI to exhaust that
ideal, under the standing assumption $\dim S>2$. The two-dimensional
calculation above uses the independently verified Parts I–II and the
two-dimensional paper. [Part III publisher record](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00927872.2011.580482)

## B. Repository correspondence

- **Blow-up excess measures the chosen pair.** For displayed orders $r,s$,
  Deligne’s SGA 7 II, Exposé X, Theorem 2.9 and formula (2.9.2) give
  $m-rs$ as the sum of the strict-transform intersection lengths on the first
  exceptional line, weighted by $[\kappa(p):K]$. The sum vanishes exactly
  when the displayed binary symbols are coprime. For a proper global
  intersection, the pushforward of the strict-transform cycle is
  $(m-rs)[C]+R_{\mathrm{res}}$, where $R_{\mathrm{res}}$ is the residual
  cycle downstairs. Horizontal exceptional components contribute generic
  contact. Vertical exceptional components push to points and require
  separate analysis. [Deligne](https://grothendiecksga.com/read/sga7/fr/10-2.html) ·
  [Two surfaces](../synthesis/02-two-surfaces.md#22-the-common-graph-and-its-two-contractions)
- **The generic comparison with the equation module is determined
  numerically.** R-0030 compares $J^*/S_+J^*$, where $S_+=(u,v)$, with the associated graded
  of $J/\mathfrak mJ$ for its induced filtration. In a normalized pair the
  two surviving degrees are $c_1,c_2$. The kernel comprises the extra
  generator degrees $c_3,\ldots,c_n$. Combining the GHK shifts with the
  normal Koszul proof gives the page numbers. Interlacing forces $e_i$ to
  cancel against $c_{i+1}$, working backwards from $e_{n-1}$, so
  cancellation occurs on page $c_{i+1}-e_i$. The relative equation
  module, saturation and punctual torsion are additional data along $C$.

Classical theory treats the last stages together once the whole formal pair
exists. Hironaka–Matsumura’s Theorem (3.3), with Hartshorne’s §1 and

containment $I_C^N\subset(\widehat F,\widehat G)\subset I_C$ and CI
connectedness to obtain sole support. The unsolved step is producing those
glued sections and the containment from the proposed local data.

## D. Minimum excess

The scalar is redundant as a primary invariant. In any sorted local
generating pair the first order is $c_1$. If the second exceeded $c_2$,
every initial form through degree $c_2$ would be a multiple of the first,
contradicting the second minimal initial generator. Subtracting lifts of
successive initial quotients reaches a normalized pair with orders
$c_1,c_2$. Chalmovianská–Chalmovianský call the fixed-first-generator
version a *max-order basis* in Definition 3.6 and Lemma 3.8. The orders
are intrinsic and the pair need not be unique. GHK Part II, Theorem 1.3(2)
therefore computes

$$
\epsilon_{\min}(J)=m-c_1c_2=\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}d_i(c_{i+1}-e_i).
$$

It is zero exactly when $J^*$ has two minimal generators, equivalently
when some generating pair has coprime initial forms. The Hilbert function
recovers all the numerical shifts and $d_i$. The actual gcd polynomials
additionally record the directions and multiplicities, while the full
initial ideal records the initial equations themselves.
[Max-order basis](https://gradmath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/GJM2022-Chalmoviansky.pdf) ·
[GHK Part II](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~heinzer/preprints/6ghk2.pdf) ·
[Mandal–Rossi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2756)

For a fixed homogeneous CI ideal, equal degrees allow $\mathrm{GL}_2(k)$.
For $a<b$, the permitted changes are scalar rescalings and addition of
$AF$ to the degree-$b$ mate, with $A$ homogeneous of degree $b-a$.
Local coefficients can have poles along $C$. The hypothetical $(6,6)$
reduction makes the distinction concrete. Its generic curvilinear length
is nine, so the local optimal orders are $(1,9)$ and $\epsilon_{\min}=0$.
Every homogeneous sextic presentation in that setting has one order one
and the other at most two, hence displayed excess at least seven. A
coprime full generic initial ideal is compatible with the global
obstruction to coprime displayed symbols.

## E. Recommended next step

Decide whether the actual cubic-jet obstruction vanishes anywhere on the
retained zero-defect two-node stratum. Use the conditional characteristic-zero
$(6,16)$ setting with full generic initial CI degrees $(2,12)$ and a
saturated first relation of movement two. On this stratum $f=\ell_1\ell_2$,
the forced multiplier is $\alpha=\alpha_1\alpha_2$, and the two branch
symbols lift uniquely to actual cubics $H_1,H_2$. Every permitted sextic
has the form $H_1H_2+\eta q^3$, with $\eta\ne0$ required by the
lower-degree carrier exclusion.

Ask whether the first incidence, all 25 second pairings, and full spectral
torsion including both node gluings admit a point satisfying

$$
\big[(H_1H_2+\eta q^3)_3-\alpha G_0\big]\bmod f=0 ,
$$

where $G_0$ is the cubic correction of the embedded root. The rank-certified
source map already gives exact equations and uniqueness of the sextic at a
successful datum. What remains is emptiness or an exact point on the whole
declared stratum, including its retained endpoint and branch boundaries and
the zeros of $\alpha$. Emptiness would exclude this conditional stratum.
A point would give an actual sextic through the cubic stage, with higher
compatibility and a degree-sixteen mate still to establish.
