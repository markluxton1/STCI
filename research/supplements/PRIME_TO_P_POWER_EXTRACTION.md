# Extracting prime-to-p powers from supported mixed-characteristic lifts

Fix the integral embedded curve
\(C=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3\).
The [mixed-characteristic trace calculation](MIXED_CHARACTERISTIC_TRACE_OBSTRUCTION.md)
obstructs unramified lifts near eigenpairs but leaves ramification possible.
The argument here uses the full nilpotence condition. It excludes all
ramified lifts of the known powered-quartic special pairs when the power
is prime to the residue characteristic.

**Status, October 31, 2026:** independently reconstructed, reviewed
provisional theorem. No canonical promotion or unrestricted
characteristic-zero conclusion.

The [Frobenius-power extension](FROBENIUS_POWER_LIFTING_PRECISION.md)
quantifies finite unramified precision and extracts powers divisible by
\(p\) when the absolute ramification index is below \(p-1\).

## Extraction theorem

Let \(R\) be a complete mixed-characteristic DVR, with uniformizer
\(\pi\), residue field \(k\) of characteristic \(p\), and fraction
field \(K\). Scalar extension of \(k\) is allowed. Suppose
\(F_0,H_0\) are homogeneous forms of degrees \(a,b\) over \(k\),
with sole geometric reduced support \(C_k\). Assume the carrier
\(F_0=0\) is smooth at the generic point of \(C_k\). Put
\(r=ab/4\).

Let \(n\ge2\) be prime to \(p\). Suppose a flat complete-intersection
pair \(F,G\) over \(R\), of degrees \((a,nb)\), reduces to
\((F_0,H_0^n)\), contains the fixed relative curve \(C_R\), and has
generic reduced support exactly \(C_K\). Then there are homogeneous
forms \(H,L\) over \(R\) such that
\[
 \boxed{G=H^n+FL,\qquad H|_{C_R}=0,\qquad H\bmod\pi=H_0,}
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(\deg H=b\), \(\deg L=nb-a\), and a negative-degree
\(L\) means zero. In particular,
\((F,G)=(F,H^n)\), and \((F,H)\) is itself a supported
degree-\((a,b)\) lift on the generic fiber.

An arbitrary DVR may first be completed. No restriction on the ramification
index occurs. No smoothness assumption on \(H_0\) is required.
Both original lifted equations are arbitrary; the conclusion reconstructs
a lower-degree equation from their support.

## The exact global correction space

Write \(S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\), \(P=I_{C_k}\), and use
\(S_d=0\) for \(d<0\). On the smooth generic carrier \(F_0\),
the order of \(H_0\) along \(C\) is \(r\), by the complete-
intersection cycle degree. The following is an equality of homogeneous
polynomial spaces:
\[
 \boxed{
 \{g\in S_{nb}:\operatorname{ord}_C(g|_{F_0})
       \ge(n-1)r+1\}
 =H_0^{n-1}P_b+F_0S_{nb-a}.}
 \tag{2}
\]

Indeed, \(J=(F_0,H_0^{n-1})\) is a height-two complete intersection
with sole reduced projective support \(C\). Its affine cone has radical
\(P\): the origin is already in the cone. The quotient is
[Cohen–Macaulay](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00SB)
and unmixed, with \(P\) its only associated prime. In particular there
is no embedded vertex component, and \(J\) is \(P\)-primary.
Thus \(J_P\cap S=J\).

The generic order in (2) first implies \(g\in J_P\), hence
\(g\in J\) globally. Homogeneity gives
\(g=H_0^{n-1}h+F_0\ell\), with \(h\in S_b\).
The one additional order forces \(h\in P_P\cap S=P\).
This proves one inclusion; the other follows by adding generic orders.
There is no degree-gap hypothesis and no missing saturation calculation.

For the characteristic-seven pair \((F_7,H_4^2)\), (2) reads
\(V_8=H_4(I_C)_4\oplus F_7S_1\), of dimension \(18+4=22\).
The intersection is zero because the relatively prime forms \(F_7,H_4\)
have degrees seven and four. Formula (2) proves the full space directly;
no finite computation is needed.

## The algebra supplied by an actual supported trait

Set \(m=nr\). On the generic fiber, the complete intersection has
generic transverse length \(m\). Therefore \(I_{C_K}^m\) vanishes
at the generic point of \(C_K\). Purity of the complete-intersection
curve makes it vanish everywhere: a surviving image would have finite
support inside a pure one-dimensional sheaf. Flatness over the DVR then
gives
\[
 \mathcal I_{C_R}^{m}\mathcal O_{V(F,G)}=0. \tag{3}
\]
This conclusion uses an actual supported generic fiber. Topological support
on a dual-number fiber alone does not imply (3).

On \(x_0=1\), take \(x_1=t\); the fixed curve is
\((x_2,x_3)=(t^3,t^4)\). Complete the DVR \(R[t]_{(\pi)}\)
to \(D\), whose residue field is \(k(t)\), and complete in the
two normal coordinates. The generic special derivative of \(F_0\)
in one normal coordinate is a unit, so the lifted \(F\) eliminates
that coordinate. The fixed curve supplies the augmentation, and its ideal
on the resulting formal carrier is generated by the remaining parameter
\(v\).

The remaining equation reduces to a unit times \(v^m\).
[Weierstrass preparation and division](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~heinzer/preprints/talkgenfib.pdf#page=8)
give a finite free quotient of rank \(m\) over \(D\). The powers
\(1,v,\ldots,v^{m-1}\) lift its residue-field basis and generate by
Nakayama. By (3), \(v^m=0\) in it. Equality of free ranks gives
the augmented algebra
\[
 B\simeq D[v]/(v^m). \tag{4}
\]
The reduction of the polynomial \(H_0\) in this algebra has the form
\(v^rU_0(v)\), with \(U_0\) a unit. This construction uses only
the generic point of the curve; it makes no claim that the same coordinates
or a regular retraction work at its exceptional points.

## Divided powers without division by p

In \(B=D[v]/(v^{nr})\), suppose \(x\) has augmentation zero,
reduces to \(v^rU_0\), and \(x^n\in\pi^jB\), \(j\ge1\).
Then
\[
 x=v^rU+\pi^jA,\qquad A(0)=0,
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{ord}_v\left(\frac{x^n}{\pi^j}\bmod\pi\right)
 \ge(n-1)r+1.} \tag{5}
\]

For the proof, suppose inductively \(x=v^rU+\pi^qL\), with
\(1\le q<j\), and absorb all terms of \(L\) of degree at least
\(r\) into \(U\). Modulo \(\pi^{q+1}\),
\[
 x^n=n\pi^qv^{(n-1)r}U_0^{n-1}L_0.
\]
Multiplication by \(v^{(n-1)r}U_0^{n-1}\) is injective on
polynomials of degree below \(r\) in \(k(t)[v]/(v^{nr})\).
Since \(n\) is a unit, the assumed divisibility forces \(L_0=0\).
This inductively proves \(x\in(v^r)+\pi^jB\).
Augmentation zero and torsionfreeness imply \(A(0)=0\).
Dividing the binomial expansion by \(\pi^j\) and reducing gives
\(n v^{(n-1)r}U_0^{n-1}A_0\), proving (5).
The only integer inverted is \(n\).

## Global induction on the coefficients

Start with a homogeneous lift \(H_1\in(I_{C_R})_b\) of \(H_0\),
and \(L_1=0\). Such curve-containing lifts exist in every degree:
restriction under the fixed parametrization sends each ambient monomial
to one parameter monomial, and its integral kernel has a basis of
differences of monomials with the same image. This also proves residue
base change for the coefficient kernel used below.

Suppose
\[
 G-H_j^n-FL_j=\pi^j g_j.
\]
In (4), both \(F\) and \(G\) vanish, so
\(g_j=-H_j^n/\pi^j\). Division is unambiguous in this free
\(D\)-module. Equation (5) puts \(\overline g_j\) in the left
side of (2); its order threshold is at most \(nr\), so quotienting
by \(v^{nr}\) loses none of these conditions. Consequently
\[
 \overline g_j=H_0^{n-1}h_j+F_0\ell_j,
 \qquad h_j\in(I_{C_k})_b.
\]
Lift \(h_j\) inside the integral curve ideal, lift \(\ell_j\)
arbitrarily in its degree, and set
\[
 H_{j+1}=H_j+\pi^j\widetilde h_j/n,\qquad
 L_{j+1}=L_j+\pi^j\widetilde\ell_j.
\]
The binomial expansion advances the polynomial congruence to
\(\pi^{j+1}\). Completeness and the finite number of coefficients
give limits \(H,L\) satisfying (1).

## Consequence for the known positive-characteristic equations

For every \(p\ge7\), choose positive \(b_0,c_0\) with
\(3b_0+4c_0=p\), put \(a_0=p-b_0-c_0\), and use
\[
 F_0=x_1^p-x_0^{a_0}x_2^{b_0}x_3^{c_0},\qquad
 H_0=x_2^4-x_0x_3^3.
\]
The [known construction](POSITIVE_CHARACTERISTIC_CONSTRUCTIONS.md#all-mate-degrees-on-a-fixed-cuspidal-quartic)
has sole support \(C\). The normal differential of \(F_0\) is
nonzero generically, since \(b_0,c_0\) are nonzero modulo \(p\).
Hence the extraction theorem applies to every pair
\((F_0,H_0^n)\) with \(p\nmid n\).

A supported mixed-characteristic lift would therefore supply a
characteristic-zero pair with a quartic equation. The
[promoted quartic-carrier exclusion](../results/quartic_carrier_exclusion.md)
and its lower-degree/factor reduction exclude this. Thus
\[
 \boxed{(F_0,H_0^n)\text{ has no supported mixed-characteristic lift
 on fixed }C_R\text{ when }p\nmid n.}
\]
This includes \((p,8)\) for every \(p\ge7\), without a ramification
restriction. Via the [trait criterion](ARITHMETIC_EQUATIONS_AND_JET_GAPS.md#mixed-characteristic-lifting-on-the-full-incidence),
these specified points lie on no horizontal component of the full
degree-\((p,4n)\) incidence: \(p\) is nilpotent in their local rings.
A trait in the proper-pair incidence supplies the required flat relative
complete intersection, since its special equations remain a regular
sequence. Its coefficient equations retain the fixed curve. Thus the
extraction theorem applies after completion and any needed residue
extension. No effective nilpotence exponent is supplied.

More generally, write \(n=p^e n_0\), \(p\nmid n_0\). Applying
extraction with base equation \(H_0^{p^e}\), when \(n_0>1\), removes the prime-to-\(p\)
factor \(n_0\) from any hypothetical lift. The remaining special pair
is \((F_0,H_0^{p^e})\). For \(e>0\), this argument supplies a
degree-\(4p^e\) equation, not a quartic; it does not exclude that case.
The failure to invert \(p\) is the exact remaining limitation of this
root mechanism.

The subsequent [precision and ramification theorem](FROBENIUS_POWER_LIFTING_PRECISION.md)
shows that the maximum unramified strong precision for
\((F_p,H_4^n)\) is \(1+v_p(n)\). Any still-possible actual trait
through a \(p\)-divisible powered point must have
\(v_\pi(p)\ge p-1\). Thus the remaining pure-power problem is an
arbitrary-ramification question beyond that bound, not an unramified lift.

Other special pairs, including the non-eigen degree-\((16,p)\) source,
are outside this prescribed-power conclusion. No coverage theorem puts
every hypothetical characteristic-zero presentation in this family, and
no unrestricted increasing degree bound follows from its exclusion.

Two independent reconstructions checked the primary-ideal passage from generic
order to homogeneous polynomial membership, the actual-trait nilpotence,
generic normal-coordinate elimination, the divided-power induction, lifting
within the integral curve ideal, and the arbitrary-ramification scope.
The generalized correction space is a sum, not necessarily a direct sum;
uniqueness of the corrections is not used. Pure \(p\)-powers at the
remaining ramification indices and arbitrary special presentations remain open.
