# Effective transfer of bounded-degree STCI pairs across characteristics

**Status, October 31, 2026: independently reviewed provisional theorem.** This note
derives a quantitative consequence of the finite rank-incidence construction
in [the arithmetic degree-bound note](ARITHMETIC_EQUATIONS_AND_JET_GAPS.md).
It does not resolve the unrestricted characteristic-zero STCI question and
has not been promoted as a canonical result.

Let
\[
C_0=[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4]\subset\mathbf P^3.
\]
For a prime \(p\), let \(\delta(p)\) be the minimum of
\(\max(\deg F,\deg G)\) over pairs whose reduced projective intersection is
\(C_0\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\), as defined in the linked arithmetic
note.

## Effective bounded-degree transfer

For every integer \(D\ge2\), define
\[
L_D=\binom{D+3}{3},\quad q_D=2L_D+1,\quad
M_D=\left\lfloor\frac{D^2}{4}\right\rfloor,\quad
N_D=\binom{M_D+2}{2},\quad R_D=2L_D-1,
\]
\[
d_D=\max\{N_D,2L_D\},\qquad
h_D=\max\left\{
\log(N_D!)+N_D(\log L_D+D\log2),\ \log(R_D!+1)
\right\},
\]
and
\[
s_D=8D+3+(4DN_D+1)2^{3N_D}.
\]
Set
\[
B_D=4q_D(q_D+1)d_D^{q_D}
\left(h_D+\log s_D+(q_D+7)d_D\log(q_D+1)\right).
\tag{1}
\]
Then the following implication holds:
\[
\boxed{\quad p>\exp(B_D)\ \text{and a degree-}\le D\ \text{STCI pair for }C_0
\text{ exists over }\overline{\mathbb F}_p
\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{one exists over }\overline{\mathbb Q}.\quad}
\tag{2}
\]
This implication is unconditional. In particular, if \(C_0\) is not an STCI
in characteristic zero, then
\[
\delta(p)>D\qquad\text{for every prime }p>\exp(B_D).
\tag{3}
\]
The constants are deliberately coarse; (1) is a usable finite expression,
not an optimized estimate.
More precisely, the characteristic-zero pair in (2) can be chosen with the
same ordered degrees \((a,b)\) as the characteristic-\(p\) pair: the proof
works separately on each degree-pair incidence. This refinement permits
unconditional consequences for classes already excluded in characteristic zero.

## Proof

Fix positive degrees \(a,b\le D\). If \(4\nmid ab\), degree exhaustion
makes a supported complete-intersection pair impossible in every
characteristic. Otherwise set \(M=ab/4\), \(N=\binom{M+2}{2}\), and
\(k=N-M+1\). Use the integral transverse matrix \(T\) from the linked
arithmetic note: on \(x_0=1\), write
\[
x_1=t,\qquad x_2=t^3+u,\qquad x_3=t^4+v,
\]
and truncate transverse polynomials modulo \((u,v)^{M+1}\). The matrix has
size \(N\times2N\). On the open set of proper pairs, its rank condition
\(\operatorname{rank}_{\overline{k(t)}}T\le N-M\) is equivalent to the
intersection having reduced support exactly \(C_0\), by the degree-exhaustion
argument recorded there. The transverse length is that of the local algebra
at \((u,v)=(0,0)\), not the entire unlocalized transverse quotient.
Express this condition by setting to zero every
coefficient in \(t\) of every \(k\)-minor of \(T\). Also impose the
coefficients of \(F(s^4,s^3t,st^3,t^4)\) and
\(G(s^4,s^3t,st^3,t^4)\) to be zero. These at most \(8D+2\) equations make
containment of \(C_0\) explicit.

It remains to encode that the pair is proper. Let \(S_j\) be the vector
space of degree-\(j\) forms in four variables. The multiplication map
\[
S_b\oplus S_a\longrightarrow S_{a+b},\qquad(U,V)\longmapsto UF+VG
\]
has kernel of dimension one precisely when \(\gcd(F,G)=1\): its kernel is
generated by \((G,-F)\), and a common factor of degree \(e>0\) gives the
additional syzygies obtained by multiplying \((G/H,-F/H)\) by forms of
degree \(e\). Thus properness is equivalent to at least one nonzero minor
of size \(R=\dim S_a+\dim S_b-1\). Cover this open condition by its finitely
many minor charts. On a chart with minor \(\Delta\), introduce one variable
\(z\) and the equation \(z\Delta-1=0\). Each chart is now an affine
polynomial system over \(\mathbb Z\), with
\(q=\dim S_a+\dim S_b+1\le q_D\) variables.

If a chart system has a solution over \(\mathbb C\), the containment
equations and the rank criterion give a characteristic-zero STCI pair of
degrees \((a,b)\). Hence, whenever no such characteristic-zero pair exists,
each chart system has no common zero over \(\mathbb C\).

Apply Theorem 1 of Krick, Pardo, and Sombra, [“Sharp estimates for the
arithmetic Nullstellensatz”](https://www.maia.ub.edu/~sombra/publications/arith-nss/maino.pdf),
to each empty chart system. In their notation, for \(s\) integral equations
in \(q\) variables of degrees at most \(d\) and coefficient logarithmic
heights at most \(h\), there is a nonzero integer \(A\) in the generated
ideal with
\[
\log|A|\le4q(q+1)d^q
\bigl(h+\log s+(q+7)d\log(q+1)\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]
The parameters in (1) uniformly dominate those for every \((a,b)\) with
\(a,b\le D\) and every properness chart:

* There are at most \(q_D\) variables. The transverse coefficient equations
  have degree at most \(k\le N_D\); \(z\Delta-1\) has degree at most
  \(R+1\le2L_D\); containment equations are linear. Thus \(d\le d_D\).
* Substituting a degree-\(D\) monomial by
  \((1,t,t^3+u,t^4+v)\) has coefficient \(\ell^1\)-norm at most \(2^D\).
  Summing over at most \(L_D\) monomials bounds each entry of \(T\) by
  \(L_D2^D\). The coefficient \(\ell^1\)-norm of a \(k\)-minor is at most
  \(k!(L_D2^D)^k\). Hence every extracted integral equation has coefficient
  logarithmic height at most the first term in the maximum defining \(h_D\).
  A multiplication-map minor has coefficient \(\ell^1\)-norm at most \(R!\),
  so the chart equation has height at most \(\log(R_D!+1)\). The containment
  equations have height zero.
* Each transverse minor has degree in \(t\) at most \(4Dk\le4DN_D\).
  The number of coefficient equations from these minors is at most
  \((4DN_D+1)\binom Nk\binom{2N}k\le(4DN_D+1)2^{3N_D}\). Adding the
  containment equations and the chart equation gives at most \(s_D\)
  equations.

Thus (4) gives \(\log|A|\le B_D\). If a characteristic-\(p\) pair lies in
this chart, it solves the chart equations modulo \(p\). The identity
\(A=\sum_i g_i f_i\), with integral \(g_i\), then forces \(A=0\) in
\(\mathbb F_p\), so \(p\mid A\) and \(p\le|A|\le\exp(B_D)\). For
\(p>\exp(B_D)\), the chart must therefore have a complex solution. A
characteristic-\(p\) pair selects one of the properness charts, proving (2).
Nonemptiness of the same finite-type rational system over \(\mathbb C\)
gives a closed point over a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q\), hence a
solution over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
If characteristic zero has no pair at any degrees, the same argument applies
to every chart and every \((a,b)\), giving (3).

## Growth consequence and limits

The explicit expressions satisfy, for an absolute constant \(C>0\) and all
sufficiently large \(D\),
\[
B_D\le \exp(CD^3\log D).
\]
Indeed \(q_D=O(D^3)\), \(d_D=O(D^4)\), \(h_D=O(D^5\log D)\), and
\(\log s_D=O(D^4)\); the dominant term in \(\log B_D\) is
\(q_D\log d_D=O(D^3\log D)\). Consequently, under the additional global
hypothesis that no characteristic-zero STCI exists,
\[
\delta(p)\ \gg\
\left(\frac{\log\log p}{\log\log\log p}\right)^{1/3}
\qquad(p\to\infty),
\]
with an absolute implied constant. Constructibility by itself gives no rate;
this explicit conditional rate comes from the sizes of the integral incidence
equations and the arithmetic Nullstellensatz.

There is also an unconditional, though numerically enormous, constant lower
bound. The promoted [quartic-carrier exclusion, R-0001](../results/quartic_carrier_exclusion.md)
and its factor reduction imply that in characteristic zero both degrees of
any STCI pair for this fixed \(C_0\) must be at least five. The degree identity
\(4\mid ab\) then excludes \(\max(a,b)\le5\): the only remaining possibility
would be \((a,b)=(5,5)\), whose product is not divisible by four. Applying
(2) with \(D=5\) therefore gives
\[
\delta(p)\ge6\qquad\text{for every prime }p>\exp(B_5),
\]
without assuming the unrestricted characteristic-zero question has a negative
answer. The exact \(B_5\) is given by (1).

The global characteristic-zero hypothesis remains unresolved. This note
therefore does not assert that the displayed growth bound holds unconditionally
for \(C_0\), nor does it determine any actual minimum \(\delta(p)\). The
fixed-carrier minima and the \((4,p)\) construction for \(p\ge7\) supply
upper bounds for the unrestricted minimum and do not establish the missing
global hypothesis.

## An unconditional growing bound for every quartic carrier

The promoted quartic-carrier theorem does supply the missing
characteristic-zero hypothesis when the first equation has degree four.
Thus the degree-preserving form of (2) already gives an unconditional
growing lower bound on its mate degree, uniformly over **all** quartic
equations. Reducing the mate modulo the quartic improves the rate.

For \(D\ge4\), define \(B_D^{(4)}\) by the expression (1), replacing
\[
 q_D\text{ by }q_D^{(4)}=2D^2+37,\qquad
 M_D\text{ by }D,\qquad N_D\text{ by }N_D^{(4)}=\binom{D+2}{2}.
\]
Keep \(L_D,R_D\) as above, and recompute \(d_D,h_D,s_D\) with this
smaller \(N_D^{(4)}\). Then
\[
 \boxed{p>\exp(B_D^{(4)}),\quad
 V(F_4,G_b)_{\mathrm{red}}=C_0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b>D.}
 \tag{5}
\]
The form \(F_4\) may be reducible or nonreduced. No weight, normality,
generic normal-order, or coefficient restriction is imposed.

Fix a graded monomial order. A quartic has one of 35 possible leading
monomials. On the corresponding coefficient chart, rescale its leading
coefficient to one and set all larger quartic coefficients to zero.
This uses at most 34 free coefficients. Divide \(G_b\) by this monic
quartic and replace it by the homogeneous remainder. The ideal \((F,G)\)
is unchanged. Its remainder uses only degree-\(b\) monomials not
divisible by the leading monomial of \(F\), whose number is
\[
 \dim S_b-\dim S_{b-4}=2b^2+2\qquad(b\ge1),
\]
where \(S_j=0\) for \(j<0\). Parameterize these remainder coefficients
independently. This covers every field-valued pair after division without
substituting rational division formulas into the incidence equations.

Adding the properness-chart variable gives at most
\(34+(2D^2+2)+1=q_D^{(4)}\) variables. Since the transverse multiplicity
is now \(M=b\), its matrix size is at most \(N_D^{(4)}\). All other
bounds from the proof survive: specializing a leading coefficient to one
and other coefficients to zero does not increase the substitution norm;
the properness matrix has entries that are zero, one, or a coefficient
variable, so its determinant norm is still at most \(R_D!\).
The same containment, properness and rank equations therefore have the
displayed modified parameter bounds.

Every such characteristic-zero chart is empty. For integral \(F\), this
is R-0001 itself; for reducible or repeated \(F\), its accepted factor
reduction and the plane, quadric and cubic exclusions apply. The arithmetic
Nullstellensatz now proves (5) on each chart. There is no extra factor for
the number of leading-monomial or properness charts: a proposed pair
selects one chart, and each empty chart has the same uniform bound.

Here \(q_D^{(4)}=O(D^2)\), \(d_D=O(D^3)\),
\(h_D=O(D^3\log D)\), and \(\log s_D=O(D^2)\). Consequently
\(B_D^{(4)}\le\exp(CD^2\log D)\) for an absolute constant \(C\).
If \(\beta_4(p)\) is the minimum mate degree over all supported pairs
with a quartic first equation, then unconditionally
\[
 \boxed{\beta_4(p)\gg
 \left(\frac{\log\log p}{\log\log\log p}\right)^{1/2}}
 \qquad(p\to\infty).
 \tag{6}
\]
The known \((4,p)\) construction gives \(\beta_4(p)\le p\) for
\(p\ge7\). This all-quartic bound is much weaker than the sharp linear
bound on weight-homogeneous carriers, but it has broader carrier coverage.
It does not bound the unrestricted \(\delta(p)\) from below: a minimizing
pair could have both degrees above four. The consequence remains provisional;
it does not extend the characteristic-zero geometric case proofs to
positive characteristic.

## Independent review

An independent reconstruction on October 31, 2026 accepted the explicit
transfer (2), the conditional growth rate, and the unconditional constant
bound. It verified the integral containment equations, properness charts
including zero forms, local transverse truncation and purity, all four
parameter bounds, the primary KPS theorem, chart uniformity, algebraically
closed coefficient fields, and the R-0001 consequence. No bounded-degree
incidence was solved computationally; the proof bounds the arithmetic
certificate if the corresponding characteristic-zero incidence is empty.

The same independent reviewer subsequently reconstructed (5)–(6), including
the monic charts, homogeneous remainder and small-degree monomial counts,
unchanged determinant-height bounds, coverage of reducible quartics through
the accepted lower-degree inputs, and the absence of a chart-count factor.
The reduced variable count and the resulting square-root logarithmic rate
were accepted. This review covers the unconditional all-quartic consequence,
not unrestricted degree growth or a positive-characteristic version of the
geometric classification proof.
