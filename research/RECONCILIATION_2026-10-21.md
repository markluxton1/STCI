## Residual-cycle generalization boundary

The local Smith-normal-form determinant works for any smooth reduced support curve with a finite-flat generic-curvilinear thickening and an invertible saturated flag. For its global cycle identity, `F|Q=h rho`, `G|Q=h sigma` must define a proper residual zero-dimensional intersection on a smooth auxiliary surface `Q`, with the first class of the auxiliary equation generically nonzero in `L`. The coefficientwise first-normal divisor inequality needs the conormal quotient interpretation. The formulas are cycle/length statements, not equalities of residual schemes. Quartic-specific bidegrees and `(4,b)` consequences *do not* generalize without recomputation.

## Mathematical frontier and recommended next attack

All hypothetical actual characteristic-zero STCI equation degrees are at least five. The live cases include noncoprime or multi-generator full initial ideals; nonsaturated first kernels; reduced singular moving roots and singular formal lift surfaces; small-movement candidate rows whose actual global realization is untested; movements beyond eight; and full-conductor descent outside known excluded normalization subfamilies.

Recommended investigation: find a **provable** interface between R0016's effective nonsaturation/resultant defect cycle and R0019's pointwise saturated quasiprimitive torsion/residual intersection identity in the presence of actual global homogeneous equations. Start with a specified lci generic binary-CI class in degrees >=5 and examine successive transforms/auxiliary surfaces, proving proper residual intersection and establishing correction terms when finite-flat curvilinearity or saturation fails. Compare effective divisor degrees and supports only once a valid geometric map has been constructed. No general inequality identifying the two defect divisors is currently known; exceptional vertical components are not automatically residual curves downstairs.
