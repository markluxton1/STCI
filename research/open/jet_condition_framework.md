# Jet-condition / discrepancy framework

Status: **ACTIVE — highest priority**

## Problem

For a primitive or quasiprimitive filtration
\[
C=C_1\subset C_2\subset\cdots\subset C_m,
\]
study the successive maps from ambient hypersurface sections to the infinitesimal quotient controlling passage from \(C_j\) to \(C_{j+1}\). The key issue is not merely whether the expected number of conditions is large, but when those conditions become geometrically dependent.

The first target is the quartic order-one \(e=0,1\) frontier.

## Existing leverage

- uniform order-one bounds;
- exact e=2 primitive-fourth classification;
- quartic factorization on the e=2 obstruction locus;
- the quadric ruling/comparison divisor \(R\);
- exact first-normal symbol ranks;
- finite order-four local-cohomology reduction;
- MF6 pairing and defect constraints.

## First finite target

Write the first few containment maps intrinsically on the unique smooth quadric \(Q\cong\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) containing the rational quartic. Compare the primitive conormal quotient with the quadric conormal quotient and compute the expected versus actual ranks via exact sequences on \(Q\), \(C\), \(R\), and \(2R\).

A useful intermediate theorem would characterize rank failure through the first few jets by a finite list of special divisor/incidence configurations on \(Q\).

## Guardrails

Do not infer nonexistence merely from a negative expected condition budget: dependencies among jet conditions are the phenomenon to explain. Do not treat the e=2 factorization mechanism as automatically representative of e=0 or e=1.
